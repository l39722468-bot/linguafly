Todos hemos pensado alguna vez "si hubiera salido antes, no habría perdido el tren" o "si hubiera estudiado más, habría aprobado". Ese tipo de reflexión sobre un pasado que ya no se puede cambiar tiene una estructura muy concreta en inglés: el **third conditional**. A diferencia del second conditional (situaciones hipotéticas en el presente o futuro), el third conditional habla siempre de **el pasado**, y de un pasado que ya está cerrado: no importa cuánto lo analicemos, el resultado real ya ocurrió y no va a cambiar.

En esta unidad aprenderás la forma **if + past perfect, would have + participio**, sus variantes con *could have* y *might have*, y un vocabulario centrado en el **arrepentimiento** (*regret*) que te permitirá hablar con naturalidad sobre decisiones pasadas, errores y lo que "podría haber sido".

## Qué aprenderás

- Formar correctamente **If + past perfect, would have + past participle (V3)**.
- Usar las variantes **could have** (capacidad) y **might have** (posibilidad).

- Vocabulario para hablar de **regrets**: arrepentimiento, decisiones y consecuencias.
- Evitar el error más común de esta unidad: *if I would have known*.

## 1. El third conditional: forma y significado

### 1.1 La estructura básica

El third conditional combina una condición en **past perfect** (*had + participio*) con un resultado en **would have + past participle**. Ambas partes de la frase se sitúan claramente en el pasado, y ambas describen algo que **no ocurrió realmente**: es una manera de imaginar "qué habría pasado si las cosas hubieran sido diferentes".

| If-clause | Resultado |
| :--- | :--- |
| **If + past perfect** | **would have + past participle** |
| If I **had known**, | I **would have come**. |
| If she **had studied** harder, | she **would have passed**. |
| If we **had left** earlier, | we **would have caught** the train. |

### 1.2 Could have y might have

Igual que en los otros condicionales, el resultado no siempre necesita *would have*. **Could have** añade la idea de capacidad o posibilidad real que existió en su momento ("habría podido"), mientras que **might have** añade incertidumbre sobre si algo habría pasado o no ("puede que hubiera"):

> If he had trained harder, he **could have won** the race.  
> If the weather had been better, we **might have gone** for a walk.

### 1.3 Por qué la if-clause nunca lleva would have

El error más frecuente de esta unidad, tanto entre hispanohablantes como entre hablantes nativos informales, es escribir *if I would have known…* en lugar de *if I had known…*. Recuerda la regla de oro: **la if-clause del third conditional siempre lleva past perfect (had + participio), nunca would have**. *Would have* pertenece únicamente a la parte del resultado. Es exactamente el mismo principio que ya viste en el first conditional (donde *will* nunca va en la if-clause) y en el second (donde *would* tampoco va ahí).

## 2. Second vs third: comparando los dos condicionales del pasado y del presente

| | Second | Third |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Tiempo | Ahora / general | Pasado (ya cerrado) |
| If-clause | Past simple | Past perfect |
| Resultado | would + infinitivo | would have + participio (V3) |
| Ejemplo | If I **had** money, I**'d** travel. | If I **had had** money, I**'d have** travelled. |

Fíjate en el ejemplo del third conditional: aparece *had had*, porque el verbo "have" (tener) se combina con el auxiliar "had" del past perfect — no es un error tipográfico, es simplemente el verbo *have* conjugado en past perfect. Este doble *had* suele sorprender a los estudiantes, pero es perfectamente normal cuando el verbo principal de la frase es precisamente *have*.

La pregunta clave para elegir entre second y third es siempre la misma: **¿estoy hablando de algo que sigue siendo así ahora, o de algo que ya pasó y terminó?** Si la situación sigue siendo cierta hoy (no tengo dinero ahora), usa second. Si te refieres a un momento concreto del pasado que ya se cerró (no tuve dinero aquel día), usa third.

## 4. Errores típicos de hispanohablantes

| ❌ Incorrecto | ✅ Correcto | Por qué |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *If I would have known…* | *If I **had known**…* | La if-clause nunca lleva would have. |
| *I would passed the exam.* | *I **would have passed** the exam.* | El resultado necesita would have + participio, no would + pasado simple. |
| *If I knew about the party yesterday, I would have gone.* | *If I **had known** about the party yesterday, I would have gone.* | Pasado irreal → past perfect, no past simple. |
| *If she studied more, she would have passed.* | *If she **had studied** more, she would have passed.* | No mezcles second (past simple) con third (would have) en la misma frase. |
| *We wouldn't missed the train if we had run.* | *We **wouldn't have missed** the train if we had run.* | No olvides "have" tras would/wouldn't. |
| *If I have seen the warning, I would have stopped.* | *If I **had seen** the warning, I would have stopped.* | Past perfect necesita "had", no "have" en pasado. |
| *They might won if they had practised more.* | *They **might have won** if they had practised more.* | Might have + participio, no might + pasado simple. |
| *If we had left earlier, we don't miss the train.* | *If we had left earlier, we **wouldn't have missed** the train.* | Todo el resultado debe estar en would have + participio. |

## 5. Reading: Sam's missed train

![Regrets story](/blog/curso-b1/unit-14/regrets-story.png)

> Sam arrived late at the station, out of breath, just as the train doors closed in front of him. If he had left home earlier, he would have caught the train without any problem. If he hadn't checked his phone so many times on the way, he might have been on time after all. Looking back with hindsight, he realized his mistake: he had rushed out of the house without checking the schedule properly. If the warning on the app had been clearer, he could have avoided the whole situation. None of this changes what actually happened that morning — the train had already left, and no amount of regret could bring it back. Still, thinking through the third conditional helped him plan better for next time: tomorrow, he decided, he would leave fifteen minutes earlier, just in case.

## 6. Diálogo: Exam regrets

<audio controls preload="none" src="/audio/blog/curso-b1/unit-14/dialogue-regrets.mp3" title="🔊 Dialogue: exam regrets"></audio>

> **Emma:** How did the exam go yesterday?  
> **Liam:** Honestly, not great. If I had revised more, I would have felt more confident.  
> **Emma:** What happened exactly?  
> **Liam:** If we hadn't stayed up so late the night before, we might have slept better and thought more clearly.  
> **Emma:** I remember you said the teacher gave extra time.  
> **Liam:** Yes, and if the teacher had given us another day, we could have finished the essay properly.  
> **Emma:** Do you regret not asking for help earlier?  
> **Liam:** Definitely. If I had asked my classmates for notes, I would have understood the topic much better.  
> **Emma:** Well, it's done now. What would you do differently next time?  
> **Liam:** Next time we'll plan earlier — no more third-conditional regrets for me!  
> **Emma:** That's the right attitude. Everyone makes mistakes sometimes.  
> **Liam:** True. In hindsight, I should have taken this exam more seriously from the start.

3. **4 min — Producción oral.** Practica el diálogo de la sección 6 con otra persona, cambiando dos frases por un arrepentimiento real tuyo (¡algo pequeño y sin importancia!).
4. **3 min — Autoevaluación.** Escribe 2 frases sobre algo que habrías hecho diferente en el pasado.

## 8. Ejercicios

### Ejercicio 1 — Completa (third conditional)

1. If she ___ earlier, she would have arrived on time. (*leave*)
2. We ___ the train if we had run. (*not miss*)
3. If I had seen the warning, I ___. (*stop*)
4. They might have won if they ___ more. (*practise*)

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **had left** · 2. **wouldn't have missed** · 3. **would have stopped** · 4. **had practised**

</details>

### Ejercicio 2 — Second o third

1. If I had more time now, I'd help you.
2. If I had had more time yesterday, I'd have helped you.
3. If she studied more, she'd pass.
4. If she had studied more, she would have passed.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **second** · 2. **third** · 3. **second** · 4. **third**

</details>

### Ejercicio 3 — Corrige el error

1. *If I would have known…*
2. *I would passed.*
3. *If I knew about the party yesterday…* (pasado irreal)
4. *They might won if they had practised.*

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. If I **had known**…
2. I **would have passed**.
3. If I **had known** about the party yesterday…
4. They **might have won** if they had practised.

</details>

### Ejercicio 4 — Vocabulario

1. Perder el tren = ___
2. Arrepentirse = ___
3. Con perspectiva, mirando atrás = ___
4. Culpar = ___

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **miss the train** · 2. **regret** · 3. **hindsight** · 4. **blame**

</details>

### Ejercicio 5 — Would have, could have o might have

1. If he had trained harder, he ___ won the race. (capacidad real: *could have*)
2. If the weather had been better, we ___ gone for a walk. (incertidumbre: *might have*)
3. If she had studied, she ___ passed easily. (resultado directo: *would have*)

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **could have** · 2. **might have** · 3. **would have**

</details>

### Ejercicio 6 — Une las frases

1. If we had left earlier, — a) she would have passed the exam.
2. If she had studied more, — b) we wouldn't have missed the train.
3. If he had seen the warning, — c) he could have avoided the mistake.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1 → b · 2 → a · 3 → c

</details>

### Ejercicio 7 — Traduce al inglés

1. Si hubiéramos salido antes, no habríamos perdido el tren.
2. Si hubiera repasado más, me habría sentido más seguro.
3. Podría haber ganado la carrera si hubiera entrenado más.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **If we had left earlier, we wouldn't have missed the train.**
2. **If I had revised more, I would have felt more confident.**
3. **He could have won the race if he had trained more.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 8 — Escribe sobre ti

Escribe 3 frases con third conditional sobre algo que habrías hecho diferente (un examen, un viaje, una decisión pequeña).

**Modelo:**

> If I had studied more for my last exam, I would have got a better grade. If I hadn't rushed out that morning, I wouldn't have forgotten my umbrella. If I had asked for help sooner, I could have finished the project on time.

## Tip del profesor

Third = **pasado cerrado**. Si todavía puedes cambiar la situación hoy o mañana, no es third — probablemente necesitas second (Unidad 12) o incluso first (Unidad 11). Pregúntate siempre: "¿este momento ya pasó y ya no tiene remedio?" Si la respuesta es sí, estás en el terreno del third conditional.
