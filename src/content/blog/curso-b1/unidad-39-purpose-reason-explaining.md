      forma más corta y neutra: *I study to travel*. **In order to** es un poco más formal y enfático,

Cuando explicas por qué haces algo, en realidad estás combinando dos ideas muy distintas que en español a veces se confunden bajo la misma palabra "para": el **propósito** (la intención con la que haces algo: "estudio inglés *para* viajar") y la **causa** (el motivo por el que ocurre algo: "me quedé en casa *porque* llovía"). El inglés distingue estas dos ideas con familias de conectores completamente diferentes, y esta unidad te va a enseñar a no mezclarlas nunca más.

Para el **propósito**, aprenderás **to / in order to / so as to** (todos seguidos de infinitivo) y **so that** (seguido de una cláusula completa, ideal cuando el sujeto cambia o quieres usar un modal como *can* o *would*). Para la **causa**, aprenderás **because** (seguido de una cláusula completa) frente a **because of / due to / owing to** (seguidos de un nombre), aplicando exactamente la misma lógica gramatical que ya practicaste con although/despite en la Unidad 38 — así que esta unidad también sirve para consolidar ese patrón "cláusula vs. nombre" que aparece una y otra vez en el nivel B1.

Todo el vocabulario de esta unidad gira en torno a **explicar** (*explaining*): razones, propósitos, causas y consecuencias. Vas a practicar estas estructuras exactamente en el contexto donde más se necesitan — justificar una decisión, dar una excusa, o explicar por qué ocurrió algo.

## Qué aprenderás en la Unidad 39 (B1)

Al terminar esta unidad serás capaz de:

- Expresar propósito con **to / in order to / so as to** + infinitivo.
- Expresar propósito con **so that** + cláusula completa, incluidos los modales can/could/would.
- Expresar causa con **because** + cláusula completa.
- Expresar causa con **because of / due to / owing to** + nombre, sin confundirlo con because.
- Explicar decisiones y justificar acciones: reason, purpose, therefore, y mucho más.
- Detectar y corregir los errores más comunes de los hispanohablantes con estas estructuras.

## 1. Purpose vs. reason: el mapa completo

| Idea | Forma | Ejemplo |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Propósito | to / in order to / so as to + infinitivo | I study **to** travel. |
| Propósito | so that + cláusula | I called **so that** you wouldn't worry. |
| Causa | because + cláusula | I stayed **because** it was raining. |
| Causa | because of / due to / owing to + nombre | We stayed inside **because of** the storm. |

### 1.1 To, in order to, so as to: propósito con infinitivo

Los tres se usan cuando el sujeto que realiza la primera acción es el **mismo** que "quiere" el propósito:

> I study English **to** travel more.  
> We left early **in order to** avoid traffic.  
> She whispered **so as to** not wake the baby.

*In order to* y *so as to* son un poco más formales que el simple *to*, y son especialmente útiles en su forma negativa, porque "not to" en solitario puede sonar ambiguo:

> We left early **so as not to** be late. *(mucho más claro que "not to be late" aislado)*  
> He spoke quietly **in order not to** disturb anyone.

### 1.2 So that: propósito con cláusula completa

Usa **so that** cuando el sujeto de la segunda parte de la frase es **distinto** del sujeto de la primera, o cuando quieres incluir un verbo modal (*can, could, would, will*) para expresar la intención de forma más natural:

> I called **so that** you wouldn't worry. *(sujeto distinto: I → you)*  
> She left early **so that** she could catch the train. *(mismo sujeto, pero con modal "could")*

Fíjate en que, con *so that*, siempre hay un verbo conjugado (con o sin modal), a diferencia de to/in order to, que siempre llevan infinitivo.

### 1.3 Because: causa con cláusula completa

**Because** introduce el motivo real de algo, y siempre va seguido de una cláusula completa (sujeto + verbo):

> I stayed **because** it was raining.  
> She was tired **because** she hadn't slept well.

### 1.4 Because of, due to, owing to: causa con nombre

Estos tres conectores son sinónimos y siguen la misma regla estricta que ya conoces de despite/in spite of: van seguidos de un **nombre** o un **gerundio**, nunca de una cláusula completa:

> We stayed inside **because of** the storm.  
> The delay was **due to** the weather.  
> **Owing to** a technical problem, the flight was cancelled.

<audio controls preload="none" src="/audio/blog/curso-b1/unit-39/due-to.mp3" title="🔊 due to"></audio>

*Due to* y *owing to* son ligeramente más formales que *because of*, y son muy frecuentes en noticias, anuncios oficiales e informes escritos.

## 2. Más ejemplos en contexto

> I study English to travel more.  
> We left early in order to avoid the traffic.  
> I called so that you wouldn't worry.  
> She practises every day so that she can improve quickly.  
> We stayed inside because it was raining heavily.  
> The match was cancelled due to bad weather.  
> Owing to a strike, all trains were delayed today.

Fíjate en que estas siete frases combinan las cuatro estructuras de la unidad de forma natural: propósito con infinitivo, propósito con so that, causa con because, y causa con because of/due to/owing to.

## 4. Errores típicos de hispanohablantes

| ❌ Incorrecto | ✅ Correcto | Por qué |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *I study English for travel more.* | *I study English **to** travel more.* | Propósito con infinitivo, no "for + infinitivo". |
| *I called for that you wouldn't worry.* | *I called **so that** you wouldn't worry.* | so that, no "for that". |
| *We stayed because the storm.* | *We stayed **because of** the storm.* | because + cláusula; con nombre, usa because of. |
| *The delay was because the weather.* | *The delay was **due to** the weather.* | due to/because of + nombre, no because. |
| *We left not to be late.* | *We left **so as not to** be late.* | La forma negativa de propósito necesita so as/in order. |
| *I study to that I can travel.* | *I study **so that** I can travel.* | Con cláusula y modal, usa so that, no "to that". |
| *Owing the strike, trains were delayed.* | *Owing **to** the strike, trains were delayed.* | owing siempre necesita "to". |
| *The result of the storm, the match was cancelled.* | *As a **result of** the storm, the match was cancelled.* | Necesitas "as a result of", no solo "result of". |

## 5. Reading

![Scene](/blog/curso-b1/unit-39/explaining-scene.png)

> I decided to study English seriously in order to improve my career opportunities. My main goal was simple: to travel more confidently and to understand native speakers without constant translation. Every morning, I woke up early so that I could practise for at least thirty minutes before work — a routine I kept up mainly because of my own determination, not because anyone forced me to. Some days were difficult due to a lack of time, and once, owing to a family emergency, I missed an entire week of practice. Therefore, I created a flexible plan so that a single bad week wouldn't ruin my progress. As a result of this consistency, my speaking improved dramatically within six months. I explain this story often so that other learners understand that small, regular habits — not occasional bursts of motivation — are the real reason behind lasting progress.

## 6. Diálogo

<audio controls preload="none" src="/audio/blog/curso-b1/unit-39/dialogue-explain.mp3" title="🔊 Dialogue"></audio>

> **Ana:** Why do you study English so much?  
> **Leo:** Mainly to travel more, and also in order to get a better job.  
> **Ana:** Makes sense. Why did you leave the class early yesterday?  
> **Leo:** So that I could catch the last train home.  
> **Ana:** I see. Why was the class cancelled last week?  
> **Leo:** It was cancelled due to a problem with the classroom booking.  
> **Ana:** That's a shame. Why didn't you tell me sooner?  
> **Leo:** I called you so that you wouldn't waste your time coming.  
> **Ana:** Good thinking! And why were you late today?  
> **Leo:** Because of the traffic — there was an accident on the main road.

### Ejercicio 3 — Corrige el error

1. *I study English for travel more.*
2. *We stayed because the storm.*
3. *Owing the strike, trains were delayed.*
4. *I called for that you wouldn't worry.*

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. I study English **to** travel more.
2. We stayed **because of** the storm.
3. Owing **to** the strike, trains were delayed.
4. I called **so that** you wouldn't worry.

</details>

### Ejercicio 4 — Elige la estructura correcta

1. She left early ___ (catch / to catch) the train.

3. I whispered ___ (so as not to / not to) wake the baby.
4. He studies hard ___ (so that / for that) he can pass the exam.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **to catch** · 2. **because of** · 3. **so as not to** · 4. **so that**

## Tip del profesor

Antes de elegir un conector, hazte siempre dos preguntas en este orden. Primero: "¿estoy explicando la intención (para qué) o el motivo (por qué)?" Si es intención, ve a la familia to/in order to/so that; si es motivo, ve a la familia because/because of. Segundo: "¿lo que sigue es un verbo conjugado con su propio sujeto, o es solo un nombre?" Si es un verbo conjugado, necesitas so that o because; si es solo un nombre, necesitas to/in order to (infinitivo) o because of/due to. Esta doble pregunta resuelve prácticamente todos los casos de esta unidad.
