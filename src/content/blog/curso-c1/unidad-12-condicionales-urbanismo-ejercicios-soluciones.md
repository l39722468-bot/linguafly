Responde primero y comprueba después. La explicación está pensada para el estudiante hispanohablante.

## Parte II — Ejercicios con soluciones

### Ejercicio 1 — Identifica el tipo

Indica si cada frase es **second, third** o **mixed conditional**.

1. *If the city had invested earlier, traffic would be lighter now.*
2. *If the city invested more, traffic would be lighter.*
3. *If the city had invested earlier, traffic would have been lighter last year.*
4. *If housing were affordable, fewer families would have moved away.*

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **Mixed, pasado → presente**.
2. **Second conditional**, condición y resultado hipotéticos presentes.
3. **Third conditional**, condición y resultado pasados.
4. **Mixed, presente → pasado**: la asequibilidad es una condición estructural; la mudanza ocurrió en el pasado.

</details>

### Ejercicio 2 — Pasado hacia presente

Completa con **would, could** o **might** y la forma correcta del verbo.

1. If the council had protected the old quarter, the city ___ (have) more heritage today.
2. If the tram line had been extended, pedestrians ___ (cross) the centre more safely now.
3. If the developers had included affordable housing, the area ___ (be) more diverse today.
4. If the authority had consulted residents, the plan ___ (be) more widely accepted now.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **would have**.
2. **could cross**.
3. **might be**.
4. **might be** o **would be**, según si presentas una posibilidad o una consecuencia más lógica.

</details>

### Ejercicio 3 — Presente hacia pasado

Completa.

1. If the city were less dependent on cars, fewer people ___ ___ (suffer) from pollution during the heatwave.
2. If housing were more affordable, more families ___ ___ (stay) in the centre.
3. If the planning process were transparent, the proposal ___ ___ (attract) wider support.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **would have suffered** o, en negativo según el sentido, **would not have suffered**.
2. **could have stayed**.
3. **might have attracted**.

</details>

### Ejercicio 4 — Resultado presente o pasado

Elige la opción correcta.

1. If the city had built the metro, commuters (*would spend / would have spent*) less time travelling now.
2. If the district were more walkable, fewer residents (*would have needed / would need*) cars during last year’s disruption.
3. If the council had preserved the theatre, it (*would be / would have been*) open today.
4. If the building were structurally sound, it (*could have been / could be*) restored after the storm.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **would spend**.
2. **would have needed**.
3. **would be**.
4. **could have been** si hablamos de una decisión pasada después de la tormenta.

</details>

### Ejercicio 5 — Pasiva

Transforma.

1. If the council had protected the facade, the developers would not threaten it now.
2. If the housing scheme were inclusive, the authority could have accommodated more families.
3. If the bridge had been reinforced, pedestrians would use it today.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **If the council had protected the facade, it would not be threatened by the developers now.**
2. **If the housing scheme were inclusive, more families could have been accommodated.**
3. **If the bridge had been reinforced, it would be used by pedestrians today.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 6 — Inversión formal

Reescribe sin *if*.

1. If the city had invested in public transport, congestion would be lower now.
2. If the district were more affordable, fewer families would have moved away.
3. If the council had consulted residents, the proposal might be more acceptable today.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **Had the city invested in public transport, congestion would be lower now.**
2. **Were the district more affordable, fewer families would have moved away.**
3. **Had the council consulted residents, the proposal might be more acceptable today.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 7 — Vocabulario urbano

Completa con **gentrification, urban sprawl, smart city, mixed-use** o **pedestrianisation**.

1. The rapid spread of low-density development is known as ___ .
2. The neighbourhood has experienced ___, with rising rents and the displacement of long-term residents.
3. A ___ uses data and technology to manage urban services.
4. The development is ___, combining homes, offices and shops.
5. The ___ of the centre improved walkability but required changes to delivery routes.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **urban sprawl**; 2. **gentrification**; 3. **smart city**; 4. **mixed-use**; 5. **pedestrianisation**.

</details>

### Ejercicio 8 — Vocabulario arquitectónico

Elige el término adecuado: **retrofitting, adaptive reuse, facade, structural integrity** o **vernacular**.

1. The old factory was converted into apartments through ___ .
2. The engineers checked the building’s ___ after the earthquake.
3. The project involves ___ an office block to improve energy efficiency.
4. The front exterior of a building is its ___ .
5. The village is known for its ___ architecture and local materials.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **adaptive reuse**; 2. **structural integrity**; 3. **retrofitting**; 4. **facade**; 5. **vernacular**.

</details>

### Ejercicio 9 — Reading

Lee el texto y responde.

> The Riverside district has undergone rapid gentrification since the old warehouses were converted into mixed-use developments. The area is now more attractive to visitors, but long-term residents face rising rents and reduced access to affordable housing. If the council had introduced protections earlier, fewer families would be under pressure now. The planning committee argues that the redevelopment has improved the public realm, whereas community groups contend that the benefits have been distributed unevenly. A proposal to pedestrianise the main street might improve air quality, although local traders are concerned about deliveries. What remains unresolved is whether the district can become more liveable without losing its social fabric.

1. What caused rapid change in Riverside?
2. What problem do long-term residents face?
3. What would be different if protections had been introduced earlier?
4. What do the planning committee and community groups disagree about?
5. What remains unresolved?

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **The conversion of old warehouses into mixed-use developments.**
2. **Rising rents and reduced access to affordable housing.**
3. **Fewer families would be under pressure now.**
4. **They disagree about whether the benefits of redevelopment have been distributed fairly.**
5. **Whether the district can improve liveability without losing its social fabric.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 10 — Listening guiado

Guion: “If the city were less dependent on cars, fewer residents would have suffered during last summer’s pollution emergency. Had the council expanded the cycle network earlier, people might be cycling to work more often now. The scheme could improve walkability, but it would create trade-offs for parking and deliveries.”

Identifica los dos mixed conditionals y explica el tiempo de cada parte.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **If the city were less dependent on cars** es una condición presente; **fewer residents would have suffered** es un resultado pasado.
2. **Had the council expanded the cycle network earlier** es una condición pasada; **people might be cycling... now** es un resultado presente en progreso.

</details>

### Ejercicio 11 — Selección de colocaciones

Completa con **reduce, preserve, provide, improve** o **manage**.

1. ___ congestion
2. ___ architectural heritage
3. ___ affordable housing
4. ___ walkability
5. ___ urban density

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **reduce congestion**; 2. **preserve architectural heritage**; 3. **provide affordable housing**; 4. **improve walkability**; 5. **manage urban density**.

</details>

### Ejercicio 12 — Corrige los errores

1. *If the city would have planned better, traffic would be lighter now.*
2. *Were the district had more housing, fewer people would have left.*
3. *If the bridge had been reinforced, it would have be open today.*
4. *Had the council consulted residents, the plan will be more acceptable.*

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **If the city had planned better, traffic would be lighter now.**
2. **If the district had more housing, fewer people would have left** o **Were the district better supplied with housing, fewer people would have left.**
3. **If the bridge had been reinforced, it would be open today.**
4. **Had the council consulted residents, the plan would be more acceptable.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 13 — Análisis de trade-offs

Completa con una consecuencia lógica.

1. If the city increased density, it might ___, but it could also ___ .
2. If the council pedestrianised the centre, air quality would ___, whereas deliveries might ___ .
3. If the authority introduced smart sensors, services could ___, although privacy might ___ .

<details><summary>Modelo</summary>

1. **reduce urban sprawl; increase pressure on housing**.
2. **improve; become more complicated**.
3. **be managed more efficiently; be put at risk**.

Hay varias respuestas válidas si mantienen una relación lógica y un registro apropiado.

</details>

### Ejercicio 14 — Reformulación académica

Convierte esta afirmación absoluta en una frase prudente con mixed conditional: *The council’s earlier decision caused all the current problems.*

<details><summary>Modelo</summary>

**If the council had made a different decision earlier, some of the current problems might have been less severe, although other factors would also have contributed.**

La frase presenta una hipótesis, limita la consecuencia con *some* y reconoce que la ciudad depende de más de una causa.

</details>

### Ejercicio 15 — Writing

Escribe 150–180 palabras sobre: **Should a historic city centre be pedestrianised?** Incluye un mixed conditional, una inversión formal, dos colocaciones urbanas y un trade-off.

<details><summary>Modelo</summary>

A historic city centre should be pedestrianised gradually rather than transformed overnight. If the council had redesigned delivery routes earlier, traders would be less concerned about disruption now. Had the authority consulted local businesses from the beginning, the proposal might have attracted wider support. Pedestrianisation could reduce congestion, improve walkability and preserve the public realm, but it would also create trade-offs for residents who depend on cars and shops that require frequent deliveries. A pilot scheme would allow the council to monitor air quality, footfall and access before making the arrangement permanent. It would be essential to provide reliable public transport and accessible parking outside the restricted area. The aim should not be to exclude vehicles at any cost, but to create a safer and more liveable centre while protecting local economic activity. If the scheme were introduced with clear exemptions and regular evaluation, it could improve the area without imposing an unfair burden on particular groups.

</details>

### Ejercicio 16 — Speaking

Habla durante un minuto sobre gentrification. Usa **If the council had...**, **the neighbourhood would... now**, **whereas** y **might have**.

<details><summary>Pista y modelo</summary>

**If the council had introduced affordable-housing measures earlier, the neighbourhood would be more socially diverse now. New investment has improved some services, whereas long-term residents might have remained under less pressure if rents had been regulated. Gentrification can bring benefits, but it may also weaken the social fabric unless local people are protected.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 17 — Traducción controlada

Traduce al inglés.

1. Si la ciudad hubiera invertido antes, el tráfico sería menos intenso ahora.
2. Si la vivienda fuera más asequible, menos familias se habrían marchado el año pasado.
3. Si no fuera por el nuevo tranvía, el centro estaría mucho más congestionado.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **If the city had invested earlier, traffic would be less intense now.**
2. **If housing were more affordable, fewer families would have moved away last year.**
3. **If it weren’t for the new tram, the centre would be much more congested.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 18 — Mini-test final

Completa el texto.

> The district is under pressure. If the council ___ (protect) affordable housing earlier, fewer residents ___ (leave) now. ___ the planning process were more transparent, community groups ___ (trust) the proposal last year. The redevelopment may improve liveability, but it will create ___ for parking, deliveries and housing density.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

**had protected; would be leaving; Were; might have trusted; trade-offs.** El primer condicional conecta una acción pasada con un resultado presente en progreso. El segundo conecta una condición presente con un resultado pasado. *Trade-offs* resume los beneficios y costes que deben equilibrarse.

</details>

## Resumen de la unidad

Para formar el mixed conditional pasado → presente, usa **if + past perfect** y **would/could/might + infinitivo**: *If the city had planned better, traffic would be lighter now*. Para el patrón presente → pasado, usa **if + past simple/were** y **would/could/might have + participio**: *If housing were affordable, fewer families would have moved away*.

Distingue siempre el tiempo de la condición y el del resultado. Usa **would be** para un estado presente, **would be + -ing** para una actividad actual y **would have been + participio** para un resultado pasado en pasiva. En escritura formal puedes invertir la condición: *Had the council acted earlier...* y *Were the district more affordable...*.

El vocabulario de urbanismo exige precisión: *gentrification* no es simplemente “mejora del barrio”, *urban sprawl* no es cualquier expansión y *mixed-use* describe una combinación concreta de funciones. Cuando unas estas palabras a colocaciones como *provide affordable housing*, *improve walkability* y *preserve architectural heritage*, podrás analizar las ciudades con la complejidad temporal, léxica y argumentativa propia del nivel C1.
