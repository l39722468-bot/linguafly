## Parte I — Teoría

### 1. Qué es el subjuntivo mandativo: definición y forma

El subjuntivo mandativo es la forma verbal en base —sin la terminación de tercera persona -s— que aparece tras verbos de orden, exigencia o sugerencia: *the committee demands that he resign*, *I insist that she be present*. La marca visible es la ausencia de -s en tercera persona singular: *he resign*, no *he resigns*; *she be*, no *she is*. Esta ausencia es la pista diagnóstico que distingue el subjuntivo del indicativo y que muchos estudiantes B2 no perciben, porque en el resto de personas el subjuntivo coincide formalmente con el indicativo.

A2: After "demand" or "insist," we use the simple form of the verb.

B2: The mandative subjunctive uses the base form of the verb after verbs like demand or suggest: "I demand that he leave" (not "leaves"). The key sign is the missing -s in third person singular.

C1: The mandative subjunctive is the base form of the verb —lacking the third-person -s ending— that appears after verbs of ordering, demanding or suggesting: the committee demands that he resign; I insist that she be present. The visible mark is the absence of -s in the third singular: he resign, not he resigns; she be, not she is. That absence is the diagnostic clue that distinguishes subjunctive from indicative and that many B2 learners fail to perceive, because in the remaining persons the subjunctive is formally identical to the indicative.

### 2. Verbos que rigen el subjuntivo mandativo

Los verbos que rigen el subjuntivo mandativo forman un repertorio cerrado y reconocible: *demand*, *insist*, *require*, *request*, *suggest*, *recommend*, *propose*, *urge*, *stipulate*, *mandate*, *decree*, *order*, *advise*, *plead*. Todos comparten un núcleo semántico de mandato, recomendación o exigencia. Tras cualquiera de ellos, la cláusula subordinada con *that* admite el subjuntivo: *the board recommended that the proposal be withdrawn*. Conocer este repertorio es prerrequisito del C1, porque usar el indicativo (*that the proposal was withdrawn*) cambia el sentido: ya no es mandato sino relato de un hecho.

A2: Verbs like "suggest," "demand" and "order" can take the subjunctive.

B2: Verbs that trigger the subjunctive include demand, insist, require, suggest, recommend, propose, urge and order. After them, use the base form: "I suggest that he go."

C1: The verbs governing the mandative subjunctive form a closed and recognisable repertoire: demand, insist, require, request, suggest, recommend, propose, urge, stipulate, mandate, decree, order, advise, plead. All share a semantic core of command, recommendation or requirement. After any of them, the that-clause admits the subjunctive: the board recommended that the proposal be withdrawn. Knowing this repertoire is a C1 prerequisite, because using the indicative (that the proposal was withdrawn) shifts the sense from mandate to factual report.

### 3. La alternancia con la construcción con *should*

El subjuntivo mandativo admite una alternativa: la construcción con *should* + infinitivo. *I insist that he leave* y *I insist that he should leave* son ambas correctas y semánticamente equivalentes. La diferencia es geográfica y de registro: el subjuntivo puro es la forma preferida en inglés americano y en registro muy formal; la perístasis con *should* es más propia del inglés británico y de registro formal pero no extremo. El usuario C1 domina ambas y elige según el público y la convención. Ignorar una de las dos es perder flexibilidad estilística.

A2: You can also say "should" after these verbs: "I insist that he should leave."

B2: Two forms are possible: the subjunctive ("I insist that he leave") or "should" ("I insist that he should leave"). Both mean the same. The subjunctive is more American; "should" is more British.

C1: The mandative subjunctive admits an alternative: the should + infinitive periphrasis. I insist that he leave and I insist that he should leave are both correct and semantically equivalent. The difference is geographic and registerial: the pure subjunctive is the preferred form in American English and in very formal register; the should-periphrasis is more typical of British English and of formal but not extreme register. The C1 user masters both and selects according to audience and convention. Ignoring one of the two is to forfeit stylistic flexibility.

### 4. Distribución geográfica: inglés americano frente a británico

La distribución del subjuntivo mandativo entre variedades del inglés es uno de los hechos sociolingüísticos más relevantes para el usuario C1. En inglés americano, el subjuntivo puro es la forma dominante y se considera la opción estándar: *the court ordered that he pay restitution*. En inglés británico, el subjuntivo puro ha experimentado un renacimiento pero la construcción con *should* sigue siendo frecuente y plenamente aceptada. Un escritor C1 que redacte para un público americano debe preferir el subjuntivo; para un público británico, puede alternar. Conocer esa distribución evita sonar arcaico o, a la inversa, innecesariamente coloquial.

A2: American English uses the subjunctive more than British English.

B2: In American English, the subjunctive is the normal choice: "The court ordered that he pay." In British English, "should" is also very common: "The court ordered that he should pay."

C1: The distribution of the mandative subjunctive across English varieties is one of the most sociolinguistically relevant facts for the C1 user. In American English, the pure subjunctive is the dominant form and is considered the standard option: the court ordered that he pay restitution. In British English, the pure subjunctive has experienced a renaissance yet the should-construction remains frequent and fully accepted. A C1 writer drafting for an American audience should prefer the subjunctive; for a British audience, alternation is permissible. Knowing that distribution prevents sounding archaic or, conversely, unnecessarily colloquial.

### 5. El subjuntivo en documentos jurídicos y contractuales

El subjuntivo mandativo es ubícuo en la prosa jurídica porque condensa mandatos sin ambigüedad: *the contract stipulates that payment be made within thirty days*, *the statute requires that all applicants submit proof of identity*. En estos contextos, el subjuntivo transmite imperativo categórico sin recurrir a modales como *must* o *shall*, que añadirían redundancia. La concisión y la autoridad del subjuntivo lo hacen ideal para cláusulas, estipulaciones y condiciones. El usuario C1 que trabaja con textos jurídicos reconoce el subjuntivo como marca de registro legal y lo despliega con precisión en cláusulas condicionales y prohibitivas.

A2: Legal texts use the subjunctive a lot: "The law says that he pay."

B2: In legal English, the subjunctive is very common: "The contract stipulates that payment be made within 30 days." It gives orders clearly and briefly, without needing "must" or "shall."

C1: The mandative subjunctive is ubiquitous in legal prose because it condenses mandates without ambiguity: the contract stipulates that payment be made within thirty days; the statute requires that all applicants submit proof of identity. In such contexts, the subjunctive conveys categorical imperative without recourse to modals like must or shall, which would add redundancy. The concision and authority of the subjunctive make it ideal for clauses, stipulations and conditions. The C1 user working with legal texts recognises the subjunctive as a marker of legal register and deploys it precisely in conditional and prohibitive clauses.

### 6. Sustantivos y adjetivos que rigen el subjuntivo

El subjuntivo mandativo no se limita a verbos: ciertos sustantivos y adjetivos también lo rigen. Entre los sustantivos: *demand*, *insistence*, *requirement*, *request*, *suggestion*, *recommendation*, *proposal*, *stipulation* — *the requirement that he be present*. Entre los adjetivos: *essential*, *crucial*, *vital*, *imperative*, *necessary*, *important*, *mandatory*, *advisable* — *it is essential that she attend*. El usuario C1 reconoce que estas categorías comparten el mismo núcleo semántico de mandato o necesidad y, por tanto, admiten la misma construcción subjuntiva.

A2: Some nouns and adjectives also use the subjunctive, like "it is important that he go."

B2: Nouns like "requirement," "suggestion" and "proposal" can take the subjunctive: "the requirement that he be present." Adjectives like "essential," "crucial" and "necessary" do too: "it is essential that she attend."

C1: The mandative subjunctive is not limited to verbs: certain nouns and adjectives also govern it. Among nouns: demand, insistence, requirement, request, suggestion, recommendation, proposal, stipulation — the requirement that he be present. Among adjectives: essential, crucial, vital, imperative, necessary, important, mandatory, advisable — it is essential that she attend. The C1 user recognises that these categories share the same semantic core of command or necessity and therefore admit the same subjunctive construction.

### 7. La pasiva en el subjuntivo mandativo

El subjuntivo admite la voz pasiva: *the committee demanded that the report be published*, *the statute requires that the documents be submitted by Friday*. Aquí la marca del subjuntivo es *be* + participio, no *is/are* + participio. La pasiva subjuntiva es especialmente frecuente en registro jurídico y administrativo, donde el agente se omite y el foco recae en la acción exigida: *it is required that all forms be completed in full*. El usuario C1 maneja la pasiva subjuntiva con la misma naturalidad que la activa y no cae en el error de usar *are* o *is* en su lugar.

A2: The subjunctive can be passive too: "I demand that it be done."

B2: The subjunctive can be passive: "The committee demanded that the report be published" (not "is published"). It is common in formal and legal writing.

C1: The subjunctive admits the passive voice: the committee demanded that the report be published; the statute requires that the documents be submitted by Friday. Here the subjunctive mark is be + participle, not is/are + participle. The subjunctive passive is especially frequent in legal and administrative register, where the agent is omitted and the focus falls on the required action: it is required that all forms be completed in full. The C1 user handles the subjunctive passive with the same ease as the active and avoids the error of using are or is instead.

### 8. La negación en el subjuntivo mandativo

La negación en el subjuntivo se forma con *not* + base verbal, sin auxiliar *do*: *I insist that he not resign*, *the rule stipulates that payment not be delayed*. Nótese la ausencia de *does not* o *do not*: el *not* se antepone directamente al verbo en base. Esta construcción es una de las más difíciles para hispanohablantes, porque la tentación es escribir *that he does not resign* o *that he doesn't resign*, formas correctas en indicativo pero incorrectas en subjuntivo. El usuario C1 interioriza la regla: en subjuntivo mandativo, *not* va delante del verbo sin auxiliar.

A2: To make the subjunctive negative, put "not" before the verb: "I insist that he not go."

B2: Negation in the subjunctive uses "not" + base verb, without "do": "I insist that he not resign" (not "does not resign"). This is tricky for many learners.

C1: Negation in the subjunctive is formed with not + base verb, without the do-auxiliary: I insist that he not resign; the rule stipulates that payment not be delayed. Note the absence of does not or do not: the not is placed directly before the base verb. This construction is among the most difficult for Spanish speakers, because the temptation is to write that he does not resign or that he doesn't resign, forms correct in the indicative but incorrect in the subjunctive. The C1 user internalises the rule: in the mandative subjunctive, not precedes the verb without auxiliary.

### 9. *Should* como puente entre subjuntivo e indicativo

La construcción con *should* funciona como puente entre el subjuntivo puro y el indicativo, y es la opción que muchos escritores británicos eligen cuando dudan. *I insist that he should leave* es inequívocamente mandativo, pero evita la decisión entre subjuntivo (*leave*) e indicativo (*leaves*). Esta función de puente hace de *should* una herramienta útil para el escritor que aún no domina el subjuntivo o para el que quiere sonar formal sin arriesgar el subjuntivo en un contexto donde podría sonar arcaico. El usuario C1, sin embargo, no necesita el puente: domina el subjuntivo y lo despliega con confianza.

A2: "Should" can help when you are not sure about the subjunctive form.

B2: Using "should" is a safe choice when you are unsure: "I insist that he should leave" is clearly a command, and you avoid choosing between "leave" and "leaves."

C1: The should-construction functions as a bridge between the pure subjunctive and the indicative, and is the option many British writers choose when uncertain. I insist that he should leave is unambiguously mandative yet avoids the decision between subjunctive (leave) and indicative (leaves). This bridging function makes should a useful tool for the writer who does not yet master the subjunctive or who wishes to sound formal without risking the subjunctive in a context where it might sound archaic. The C1 user, however, does not need the bridge: they master the subjunctive and deploy it with confidence.

### 10. La fórmula *it is imperative that*

Entre las construcciones adjetivales que rigen subjuntivo, *it is imperative that* es la más enfática y formal: *it is imperative that the patient receive immediate treatment*. La palabra *imperative* introduce una necesidad categórica que el subjuntivo refuerza. Construcciones paralelas son *it is crucial that*, *it is vital that*, *it is essential that*, *it is mandatory that*. Todas comparten la misma arquitectura: *it is* + adjetivo de necesidad + *that* + sujeto + subjuntivo. El usuario C1 las usa para formular exigencias con máxima fuerza retórica sin recurrir a modales.

A2: "It is important that he go" is a strong way to say he must go.

B2: Phrases like "it is imperative that," "it is crucial that" and "it is essential that" take the subjunctive: "It is imperative that the patient receive treatment." They express strong necessity.

C1: Among the adjectival constructions governing the subjunctive, it is imperative that is the most emphatic and formal: it is imperative that the patient receive immediate treatment. The word imperative introduces a categorical necessity that the subjunctive reinforces. Parallel constructions include it is crucial that, it is vital that, it is essential that, it is mandatory that. All share the same architecture: it is + adjective of necessity + that + subject + subjunctive. The C1 user deploys them to formulate demands with maximal rhetorical force without recourse to modals.

### 11. Errores frecuentes con el subjuntivo mandativo

Los errores más frecuentes son tres. Primero, usar el indicativo donde corresponde el subjuntivo: *I demand that he leaves* en vez de *I demand that he leave*. Segundo, añadir el auxiliar *do* en la negación: *I insist that he does not resign* en vez de *I insist that he not resign*. Tercero, usar un modal donde corresponde el subjuntivo: *I suggest that he should must leave* — redundancia imposible. El usuario C1 audita su producción contra estos tres errores, porque cada uno delata un nivel intermedio no superado y un desconocimiento del registro formal avanzado.

A2: Common mistakes include using "leaves" instead of "leave" after "demand."

B2: Common errors: using the indicative ("he leaves") instead of the subjunctive ("he leave"); using "do" in negatives ("does not resign" instead of "not resign"); and adding modals where the subjunctive is needed.

C1: The most frequent errors are three. First, using the indicative where the subjunctive is required: I demand that he leaves instead of I demand that he leave. Second, adding the do-auxiliary in negation: I insist that he does not resign instead of I insist that he not resign. Third, using a modal where the subjunctive is required: I suggest that he should must leave — an impossible redundancy. The C1 user audits their production against these three errors, because each betrays an untranscended intermediate level and a disregard for advanced formal register.

### 12. El subjuntivo mandativo frente al infinitivo con *to*

Tras verbos de mandato, el inglés dispone de dos construcciones: el subjuntivo mandativo (*I demand that he leave*) y el infinitivo con *to* (*I demand him to leave*). La segunda es menos frecuente y, con algunos verbos, marginal o incorrecta: *I suggest him to leave* no es gramatical. Verbos como *advise*, *order* y *tell* admiten el infinitivo con *to* (*I advised him to leave*), pero *suggest*, *demand* e *insist* no. El usuario C1 distingue qué verbos admiten cada construcción y no confunde los patrones, porque sabe que la elección no es libre sino léxicamente determinada.

A2: Some verbs use "to": "I told him to go." Others use "that": "I demand that he go."

B2: After some verbs, you can use "to + infinitive" ("I advised him to leave"), but after others like "suggest" and "demand," you must use "that + subjunctive" ("I suggest that he leave"). "I suggest him to leave" is wrong.

C1: After verbs of command, English offers two constructions: the mandative subjunctive (I demand that he leave) and the to-infinitive (I demand him to leave). The latter is less frequent and, with some verbs, marginal or ungrammatical: I suggest him to leave is not grammatical. Verbs like advise, order and tell admit the to-infinitive (I advised him to leave), but suggest, demand and insist do not. The C1 user distinguishes which verbs admit which construction and does not conflate the patterns, knowing that the choice is not free but lexically determined.

### 13. El subjuntivo en cláusulas de finalidad y concesivas

El subjuntivo aparece también en cláusulas de finalidad con *lest* y *so that*: *he spoke quietly lest the children hear him*, *I explained it clearly so that she understand the procedure*. *Lest* es una conjunción arcaica pero viva en registro formal y jurídico que rige subjuntivo. *So that* admite subjuntivo en registro formal americano, aunque *can/could* es más frecuente. El usuario C1 reconoce *lest* como marca de registro elevado y lo reserva para contextos jurídicos, litúrgicos o muy formales, donde su arcaísmo es apropiado y no afectado.

A2: "Lest" is an old word meaning "so that not." It uses the subjunctive.

B2: "Lest" is a formal conjunction that takes the subjunctive: "He spoke quietly lest the children hear him." It means "to avoid the risk that." It is common in legal and very formal writing.

C1: The subjunctive appears also in purpose clauses with lest and so that: he spoke quietly lest the children hear him; I explained it clearly so that she understand the procedure. Lest is an archaic but living conjunction in formal and legal register that governs the subjunctive. So that admits the subjunctive in formal American register, though can/could is more frequent. The C1 user recognises lest as a marker of elevated register and reserves it for legal, liturgical or very formal contexts, where its archaism is appropriate rather than affected.

### 14. Registro administrativo y el subjuntivo

En la prosa administrativa —normas internas, reglamentos, circulares— el subjuntivo mandativo es frecuente porque formula instrucciones con autoridad concisa: *it is required that all employees submit their reports by Friday*, *management insists that attendance be mandatory*. El registro administrativo comparte con el jurídico la necesidad de formular mandatos categóricos sin ambigüedad, y el subjuntivo cumple esa función con economía. El usuario C1 que redacta documentos administrativos reconoce el subjuntivo como herramienta de registro institucional y lo usa para transmitir autoridad sin sonar agresivo ni redundante.

A2: In rules and instructions, the subjunctive is used: "It is required that everyone come."

B2: In administrative writing like rules and regulations, the subjunctive is common: "It is required that all employees submit their reports by Friday." It gives instructions clearly and with authority.

C1: In administrative prose —internal norms, regulations, circulars— the mandative subjunctive is frequent because it formulates instructions with concise authority: it is required that all employees submit their reports by Friday; management insists that attendance be mandatory. Administrative register shares with legal register the need to formulate categorical mandates without ambiguity, and the subjunctive fulfils that function economically. The C1 user drafting administrative documents recognises the subjunctive as a tool of institutional register and uses it to convey authority without sounding aggressive or redundant.

### 15. Práctica y consolidación del subjuntivo mandativo

Para consolidar el subjuntivo mandativo, el usuario C1 debe practicarlo en producción activa, no solo reconocimiento. Una técnica eficaz es redactar cláusulas jurídicas ficticias usando el repertorio completo de verbos mandativos. Otra es reescribir oraciones con modales (*he must resign*) en subjuntivo (*it is demanded that he resign*). Una tercera es auditar textos jurídicos reales identificando cada caso de subjuntivo y clasificándolo por verbo rector. La automatización del subjuntivo exige volumen de exposición y producción, porque su marca —la ausencia de -s— es sutil y fácil de pasar por alto sin práctica deliberada.

A2: To practice, write sentences with "demand that," "insist that" and "suggest that."

B2: To master the subjunctive, practice writing it actively. Try rewriting modal sentences ("he must go") as subjunctive ("it is required that he go"). Read legal texts and find examples.

C1: To consolidate the mandative subjunctive, the C1 user must practise it in active production, not merely recognition. One effective technique is drafting fictional legal clauses using the full repertoire of mandative verbs. Another is rewriting modal sentences (he must resign) into the subjunctive (it is demanded that he resign). A third is auditing real legal texts identifying each instance of the subjunctive and classifying it by governing verb. Automating the subjunctive demands volume of exposure and production, because its mark —the absence of -s— is subtle and easy to overlook without deliberate practice.

*requirement*. *The requirement that all applicants submit proof of identity is non-negotiable.* El sustantivo *requirement* rige subjuntivo igual que el verbo *require*.
</details>

**Ejercicio 9.** Reescribe con *lest*: "He spoke quietly to avoid the risk that the children would hear him."
<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*He spoke quietly lest the children hear him.* *Lest* rige subjuntivo en registro formal y arcaizante.
</details>

**Ejercicio 10.** Corrige el error: "The contract stipulates that payment is made within 30 days."
<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The contract stipulates that payment **be** made within 30 days.* Registro jurídico exige subjuntivo, no indicativo *is made*.
</details>

**Ejercicio 11.** Reescribe en subjuntivo pasivo negativo: "The rule says that the form must not be delayed."
<details>

*The rule stipulates that the form not be delayed.* Pasiva subjuntiva negativa: *not* + *be* + participio.
</details>

**Ejercicio 12.** Completa con el adjetivo rector adecuado: "It is ____ that she attend all sessions." (categorical necessity)
<details>

*imperative* (o *crucial*, *vital*, *essential*, *mandatory*). *It is imperative that she attend all sessions.* Adjetivos de necesidad + subjuntivo.
</details>

**Ejercicio 13.** Distingue americano de británico: ¿qué forma prefiere el inglés americano tras "demand that"?
<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

El inglés americano prefiere el subjuntivo puro: *I demand that he leave*. El británico admite también *I demand that he should leave*.
</details>

**Ejercicio 14.** Reescribe con el verbo *propose* en subjuntivo: "They suggested that the policy be reviewed."
<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*They proposed that the policy be reviewed.* *Propose* rige subjuntivo igual que *suggest*.
</details>

**Ejercicio 15.** Identifica el error: "It is essential that she attends the hearing."
<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*attends* es indicativo. Debe ser subjuntivo: *It is essential that she **attend** the hearing.* Adjetivo *essential* + *that* + subjuntivo.

**Ejercicio 16.** Completa la cláusula administrativa: "Management insists that attendance ____ (be) mandatory."

*be*. *Management insists that attendance be mandatory.* Subjuntivo tras *insist that* en registro administrativo.

**Ejercicio 17.** Reescribe con *mandate*: "The law requires that all vehicles pass an emissions test."
<details>

*The law mandates that all vehicles pass an emissions test.* *Mandate* rige subjuntivo y eleva el registro jurídico.

**Ejercicio 18.** Corrige la negación: "I insisted that he did not resign."
<details>

*I insisted that he **not resign**.* La negación subjuntiva no usa *did not*: *not* se antepone al verbo en base.

## Resumen de la unidad

En esta unidad hemos recorrido el subjuntivo mandativo como marca del registro formal avanzado en inglés C1. Hemos definido su forma —base verbal sin -s en tercera persona— y su función tras verbos de mandato, exigencia y sugerencia. Hemos catalogado los verbos rectores: *demand*, *insist*, *require*, *request*, *suggest*, *recommend*, *propose*, *urge*, *stipulate*, *mandate*, *decree*, *order*, *advise*. Hemos estudiado la alternancia con la perístasis de *should* y su distribución geográfica: subjuntivo puro preferido en inglés americano, *should* más frecuente en británico. Hemos extendido el subjuntivo a sustantivos y adjetivos rectores, a la voz pasiva y a la negación. Hemos visto su papel en la prosa jurídica y administrativa, donde condensa mandatos categóricos con economía. Y hemos catalogado los tres errores más frecuentes que delatan un nivel intermedio: indicativo por subjuntivo, auxiliar *do* en negación, y modal redundante. El usuario C1 domina el subjuntivo mandativo como herramienta de registro institucional y lo despliega con confianza y precisión. En la siguiente unidad abordaremos la inversión y el énfasis, donde la sintaxis se reorganiza para poner en primer plano el elemento temáticamente más saliente.
