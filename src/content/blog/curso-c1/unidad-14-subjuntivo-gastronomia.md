## Unidad 14 C1: subjuntivo, gastronomía y cultura

En inglés formal, las recomendaciones, exigencias y condiciones importantes se pueden expresar con una estructura muy precisa: **that + sujeto + forma base del verbo**. Esta forma se conoce como **mandative subjunctive** o subjuntivo mandativo.

Si llegas con la consulta **recommend that subjuntivo ejercicios**, el desarrollo está en los apartados siguientes, con ejemplos y el uso real, no como etiqueta suelta.

- *The committee recommended that the standards be revised.*
- *It is essential that every chef know the provenance of the ingredients.*
- *The regulation requires that inspections be carried out quarterly.*

La forma puede parecer sencilla porque el verbo no lleva `-s` en tercera persona y `be` aparece igual con todos los sujetos. Precisamente por eso contrasta con el inglés cotidiano, donde también son frecuentes estructuras con *should* o infinitivo. La Unidad 14 enseña a elegir el patrón según el registro y el verbo que lo introduce.

El tema es **Food, Gastronomy and Culture**. La comida no es solo nutrición: transmite identidad, memoria, pertenencia y, en ocasiones, resistencia cultural. La unidad combina la gramática formal con vocabulario como *culinary identity, cultural heritage, food insecurity, culinary appropriation, provenance, sustainability* y *food waste*. También practica la escritura de reseñas, donde hay que describir sabor, textura, técnica y experiencia sin recurrir a adjetivos vagos.

## Parte I — Teoría

### 1. El subjuntivo mandativo

El subjuntivo mandativo aparece cuando una persona o institución recomienda, exige, solicita o considera necesario que otra persona haga algo. Su estructura es:

> **verb/adjective + that + subject + base verb**

- *The chef recommended that the restaurant source its vegetables locally.*
- *The critic suggested that the menu include more seasonal dishes.*
- *It is vital that diners understand the ingredients.*

La forma base es igual para todas las personas:

- *I recommend that he attend the workshop.*
- *I recommend that she attend the workshop.*
- *I recommend that they attend the workshop.*

No añadimos `-s` en tercera persona: *I recommend that she attends* puede oírse en variedades que tratan la cláusula como indicativo, pero el subjuntivo formal es **I recommend that she attend**.

### 2. Verbos que introducen el subjuntivo

Los verbos más frecuentes son **recommend, suggest, demand, insist, request, require, propose, urge, advise** y **stipulate**. No todos aceptan exactamente los mismos complementos, así que hay que aprender el patrón completo.

- *The committee recommended that the standards be revised.*
- *The inspector demanded that the kitchen close temporarily.*
- *The law requires that food labels contain allergen information.*
- *The chef insisted that the recipe remain unchanged.*
- *The association proposed that restaurants reduce food waste.*
- *The guide urged that visitors respect local customs.*

Con **recommend, suggest, propose** también usamos **-ing** sin sujeto interno:

- *The committee recommended revising the standards.*
- *The chef suggested using seasonal ingredients.*
- *The report proposed reducing packaging.*

Si queremos mencionar quién debe realizar la acción, usamos *that + subject + base verb*:

- *The committee recommended that restaurants revise the standards.*
- *The chef suggested that the kitchen use seasonal ingredients.*

No digas normalmente *suggest someone to do*. Usa *suggest that someone do* o *suggest doing*.

### 3. Adjetivos de importancia y necesidad

El subjuntivo también aparece después de adjetivos como **essential, vital, important, necessary, crucial, imperative, advisable** y **desirable**:

- *It is essential that every chef know the provenance of the ingredients.*
- *It is vital that food be stored at the correct temperature.*
- *It is important that restaurants disclose allergens clearly.*
- *It is imperative that inspectors remain independent.*
- *It is advisable that diners book in advance.*

La estructura es:

> **It + be + adjective + that + subject + base verb**

El sujeto real aparece después de *that*. También podemos usar un infinitivo:

- *It is essential to disclose allergens.*
- *It is important for restaurants to train their staff.*

La diferencia es que **for + object + to-infinitive** hace explícito el sujeto del infinitivo, mientras que el subjuntivo utiliza una cláusula con *that*.

### 4. El verbo be en subjuntivo

El ejemplo más visible es **be**, que no cambia según el sujeto:

- *It is essential that the dish be served immediately.*
- *It is essential that the dishes be served immediately.*
- *The regulation requires that the kitchen be inspected.*
- *The regulations require that the kitchens be inspected.*

No usamos *is* o *are* dentro de esta estructura formal:

- Subjuntivo: *The rule requires that the kitchen be clean.*
- Indicativo descriptivo: *The rule requires that the kitchen is clean.*

La segunda puede aparecer en el habla y en algunas variedades, pero la primera es el patrón mandativo estándar y más claro en escritura formal.

### 5. Subjuntivo pasivo

Para expresar que una acción debe ser realizada sobre un objeto, usamos **be + past participle**:

- *The regulation requires that all inspections be carried out quarterly.*
- *The committee recommended that the standards be revised immediately.*
- *It is vital that ingredients be stored safely.*
- *The law demands that allergens be listed on the menu.*

La forma pasiva contiene el mismo `be` base para singular y plural. No añadas `-s` ni cambies a *are* dentro del subjuntivo:

- Correcto: *It is essential that the labels be checked.*
- Incorrecto para este patrón: *It is essential that the labels are checked.*

La forma con *should* también es posible:

- *It is essential that the labels should be checked.*

El subjuntivo base suele ser más conciso y formal, especialmente en normas, contratos, informes y recomendaciones profesionales.

### 6. Negación

La negación se forma con **not + base verb**:

- *The chef insisted that the recipe not be changed.*
- *It is important that customers not confuse tradition with authenticity.*
- *The inspector demanded that the restaurant not reopen until repairs were complete.*

En inglés británico también es frecuente **should not**:

- *The chef insisted that the recipe should not be changed.*

No uses *does not* o *did not* dentro del subjuntivo mandativo:

- Correcto: *The report recommends that restaurants not waste food.*
- No es el patrón: *The report recommends that restaurants do not waste food.*

La segunda puede funcionar como una cláusula indicativa en algunos contextos, pero cambia el registro y la estructura.

### 7. Should como alternativa

El patrón **that + subject + should + base verb** expresa la misma recomendación o necesidad en muchas variedades, especialmente en inglés británico:

- *The committee recommended that the standards should be revised.*
- *It is essential that every chef should know the provenance of ingredients.*
- *The inspector demanded that the kitchen should close.*

Compara:

- **Mandative subjunctive:** *They demanded that the restaurant close.*
- **Should construction:** *They demanded that the restaurant should close.*

Ambas son gramaticales. La forma base es más compacta y aparece con frecuencia en inglés formal internacional; *should* hace visible la idea de recomendación u obligación. No confundas este *should* con el consejo independiente *The restaurant should close*, que no contiene *that*.

### 8. Verbo principal frente a cláusula mandativa

El verbo que introduce la cláusula determina el patrón:

- **recommend that + subject + base:** *They recommended that he attend.*
- **recommend + -ing:** *They recommended attending.*
- **advise + object + to-infinitive:** *They advised him to attend.*
- **suggest that + subject + base:** *They suggested that he attend.*
- **suggest + -ing:** *They suggested attending.*
- **urge + object + to-infinitive:** *They urged him to attend.*
- **insist that + subject + base:** *They insisted that he attend.*
- **insist on + -ing:** *They insisted on attending.*

Los errores nacen de traducir literalmente “recomendar que” o “sugerir a alguien que”. Aprende el marco completo, no solo el verbo.

- Incorrecto: *The chef suggested the staff to change the menu.*
- Correcto: *The chef suggested that the staff change the menu.*
- Correcto: *The chef suggested changing the menu.*
- Correcto con otro verbo: *The chef advised the staff to change the menu.*

### 9. Formalidad y elección estilística

El subjuntivo mandativo es frecuente en:

- Reglamentos: *The regulation requires that all kitchens be inspected.*
- Propuestas: *We recommend that the council provide support to local producers.*
- Informes: *The review suggests that food labels include clearer information.*
- Comunicados: *It is imperative that the organisation remain transparent.*
- Lenguaje legal: *The agreement stipulates that suppliers comply with safety standards.*

En una conversación informal, es posible escuchar *The manager said that everyone should arrive early*. Esta forma es natural y no incorrecta. La elección depende del propósito. C1 implica poder pasar de una forma a otra con control:

- Formal y concisa: *The manager requested that everyone arrive early.*
- Neutral: *The manager asked everyone to arrive early.*
- Conversacional: *The manager said everyone should arrive early.*

### 10. Gastronomía como cultura

La comida puede describirse con precisión cultural y sensorial. **Cuisine** se refiere a un estilo o tradición culinaria; **dish** es un plato concreto; **recipe** son instrucciones; **ingredient** es un componente; **cuisine** no significa simplemente “comida”.

- *The region’s cuisine reflects its climate and trading history.*
- *The dish combines a smoky broth with pickled vegetables.*
- *The recipe has been passed down through several generations.*

**Culinary identity** describe el estilo distintivo de una persona o comunidad. **Cultural heritage** incluye prácticas, conocimientos y valores transmitidos. **Provenance** indica el origen documentado de un ingrediente o producto.

Colocaciones importantes son **preserve culinary heritage**, **pass down a recipe**, **source ingredients locally**, **honour a tradition**, **adapt a recipe**, **marry flavours**, **balance textures**, **showcase seasonal produce**, **develop a culinary identity** y **acknowledge a dish’s origins**.

### 11. Cultura alimentaria y ética

**Culinary appropriation** describe la adopción de prácticas o platos de otra cultura sin reconocer su origen, especialmente cuando existe una relación desigual de poder. No toda influencia culinaria es apropiación: el contexto, el reconocimiento, la compensación y la relación con la comunidad importan.

- *The restaurant acknowledged the dish’s origins and worked with local cooks.*
- *The debate concerns whether commercial success has been shared fairly.*
- *It is important that culinary traditions not be detached from their history.*

**Food insecurity** es el acceso poco fiable a alimentos suficientes, nutritivos y asequibles. Puede coexistir con **food waste** en países ricos, lo que muestra problemas de distribución, no necesariamente una falta absoluta de producción.

- *The report recommends that supermarkets donate surplus food.*
- *It is vital that food waste be reduced throughout the supply chain.*

### 12. Sabor, textura y técnica

Una reseña C1 evita repetir *good, nice, tasty* y selecciona palabras según la experiencia:

- **flavour:** sabor general o combinación de sabores.
- **taste:** percepción gustativa o sabor concreto.
- **aroma:** olor agradable o característico.
- **texture:** sensación física, como *silky, crisp, chewy, tender*.
- **aftertaste:** sabor que permanece.
- **seasoning:** uso de sal, especias y otros elementos.
- **presentation:** forma de servir y disponer el plato.
- **technique:** procedimiento culinario.
- **mouthfeel:** sensación del alimento en la boca.

Adjetivos útiles son **subtle, intense, earthy, smoky, tangy, bitter, delicate, rich, refined, balanced, overpowered, under-seasoned, impeccably presented** y **technically precise**.

- *The broth had a subtle smoky aroma and a remarkably clean aftertaste.*
- *The acidity cut through the richness of the sauce.*
- *The dish was technically precise, although its presentation felt overworked.*

### 13. Review writing

Una reseña efectiva suele incluir:

1. Contexto: lugar, ocasión y tipo de experiencia.
2. Descripción: ingredientes, técnica, textura y presentación.
3. Evaluación: fortalezas y limitaciones concretas.
4. Veredicto: para quién sería adecuada la experiencia.

La nominalisation puede ayudar: *the balance of flavours, the precision of the presentation, the originality of the pairing*. Pero alterna sustantivos y verbos para no crear una prosa rígida.

Usa lenguaje evaluativo graduado:

- **Muy positivo:** *exceptional, outstanding, accomplished, memorable*.
- **Positivo con matiz:** *promising, accomplished, largely successful, well judged*.
- **Crítico pero profesional:** *uneven, overcomplicated, underdeveloped, insufficiently balanced*.

Evita convertir una preferencia personal en hecho universal. *I found the dish too salty* es más responsable que *The dish was objectively bad*.

### 14. Errores frecuentes

- *The committee recommended that the standards are revised.* → **recommended that the standards be revised**.
- *It is essential that every chef knows the provenance.* → En subjuntivo formal: **that every chef know**.
- *The chef suggested the staff to change the recipe.* → **suggested that the staff change** o **suggested changing**.
- *The inspector demanded the kitchen to close.* → **demanded that the kitchen close**.
- *The regulation requires that inspections are carried out.* → **requires that inspections be carried out**.
- *The recipe was passed from generation to generation down.* → **was passed down from generation to generation**.
- *The flavours married together nicely.* → **The flavours married well** o **The ingredients complemented each other**.
- *Food insecurity means people don’t like the food.* → **Food insecurity means unreliable access to sufficient, nutritious food.**

## Parte II — Ejercicios con soluciones

### Ejercicio 1 — Forma base

Completa con la forma correcta del verbo.

1. The committee recommended that the standards ___ (be) revised.
2. It is essential that every chef ___ (know) the ingredients’ provenance.
3. The regulation requires that inspections ___ (be carried out) quarterly.
4. The inspector demanded that the kitchen ___ (close) temporarily.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **be**; 2. **know**; 3. **be carried out**; 4. **close**. El subjuntivo mandativo usa la forma base, también con sujetos en tercera persona.

</details>

### Ejercicio 2 — Recommend, suggest o advise

Elige la opción correcta.

1. The chef recommended that the restaurant (*source / sources*) vegetables locally.
2. The guide suggested (*using / to use*) seasonal ingredients.
3. The inspector advised the staff (*to check / checking*) the temperature.
4. The report suggested that supermarkets (*donate / donates*) surplus food.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **source**; 2. **using**; 3. **to check**; 4. **donate**. *Suggest* acepta *-ing* o *that + subject + base*; *advise* acepta objeto + infinitivo.

</details>

### Ejercicio 3 — Subjuntivo pasivo

Transforma.

1. The law requires restaurants to list allergens.
2. The committee recommended revising the safety standards.
3. It is vital for ingredients to be stored safely.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **The law requires that allergens be listed by restaurants.**
2. **The committee recommended that the safety standards be revised.**
3. **It is vital that ingredients be stored safely.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 4 — Should como alternativa

Reescribe con **should** sin cambiar el significado.

1. It is essential that the labels be checked.
2. The committee insisted that the recipe remain unchanged.
3. The regulation requires that kitchens be inspected.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **It is essential that the labels should be checked.**
2. **The committee insisted that the recipe should remain unchanged.**
3. **The regulation requires that kitchens should be inspected.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 5 — Negación

Completa con **not + verbo**.

1. The chef insisted that the recipe ___ ___ (change).
2. It is important that customers ___ ___ (confuse) tradition with authenticity.
3. The inspector demanded that the restaurant ___ ___ (reopen) before repairs were complete.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **not be changed**; 2. **not confuse**; 3. **not reopen**.

</details>

### Ejercicio 6 — Patrones de verbos

Corrige las frases.

1. *The chef suggested the staff to change the menu.*
2. *The committee recommended to reduce food waste.*
3. *The inspector demanded the restaurant to close.*
4. *The association advised that diners to book early.*

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **The chef suggested that the staff change the menu** o **suggested changing the menu**.
2. **The committee recommended reducing food waste** o **recommended that restaurants reduce food waste**.
3. **The inspector demanded that the restaurant close.**
4. **The association advised diners to book early** o **advised that diners book early**.

</details>

### Ejercicio 7 — Vocabulario culinario

2. Recipes and rituals passed between generations form part of ___ .
3. A chef’s distinctive approach to flavour and technique is their ___ .
4. Unreliable access to sufficient nutritious food is ___ .
5. Discarded edible food contributes to ___ .

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **provenance**; 2. **cultural heritage**; 3. **culinary identity**; 4. **food insecurity**; 5. **food waste**.

</details>

1. ___ culinary heritage
2. ___ ingredients locally
3. ___ flavours
4. ___ seasonal produce
5. ___ textures

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

### Ejercicio 9 — Reading

Lee el texto y responde.

> The restaurant’s new menu draws on recipes that have been passed down through three generations. The chef insists that the origins of each dish be acknowledged, particularly when ingredients or techniques come from marginalised communities. She recommends that the restaurant source produce from small local farms and proposes that surplus food be donated to community kitchens. Critics have praised the menu’s culinary identity, although some argue that the tasting format makes the food inaccessible to lower-income diners. The chef concedes that cultural heritage should not become a luxury product and says that the restaurant will introduce a more affordable lunch menu.

1. How many generations have passed down the recipes?
2. What does the chef insist on?
3. What does she recommend regarding produce?
4. What criticism has the menu received?
5. What response does the chef propose?

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **Three generations.**
2. **That the origins of each dish be acknowledged.**
3. **That the restaurant source produce from small local farms.**
4. **The tasting format may be inaccessible to lower-income diners.**
5. **Introducing a more affordable lunch menu.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 10 — Listening guiado

Guion: “The food anthropologist argues that what we eat is a performance of identity. She recommends that culinary traditions be studied in their social context and insists that researchers not treat recipes as isolated objects. Although food can create a sense of belonging, the speaker warns that commercial success should not obscure questions of power and acknowledgement.”

Identifica dos estructuras de subjuntivo, una concesión y el vocabulario ético principal.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

Subjuntivos: **recommends that culinary traditions be studied** e **insists that researchers not treat**. Concesión: **Although food can create a sense of belonging**. Vocabulario ético: **identity, social context, belonging, power, acknowledgement**.

</details>

### Ejercicio 11 — Review vocabulary

Sustituye los adjetivos vagos por una opción precisa: **subtle, smoky, tangy, silky, under-seasoned**.

1. The sauce had a slightly sour, lively flavour.
2. The broth had a delicate flavour that did not dominate.
3. The soup tasted of wood and gentle fire.
4. The purée was smooth and soft in the mouth.
5. The dish lacked sufficient salt and seasoning.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **tangy**; 2. **subtle**; 3. **smoky**; 4. **silky**; 5. **under-seasoned**.

</details>

### Ejercicio 12 — Registro de reseña

Reescribe de forma profesional: *The food was really good and the chef did cool things with old recipes.*

<details><summary>Modelo</summary>

**The meal was largely successful, and the chef demonstrated considerable skill in adapting traditional recipes without losing their cultural character.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 13 — Corrección gramatical

Corrige el párrafo.

> It is essential that every restaurant lists allergens clearly. The committee recommended that labels are revised, and the chef suggested the staff to attend a workshop. Despite the training was useful, several employees needed further practice.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

**It is essential that every restaurant list allergens clearly. The committee recommended that labels be revised, and the chef suggested that the staff attend a workshop. Although the training was useful, several employees needed further practice.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 14 — Causa y evaluación

Completa con una recomendación formal.

The menu relies on imported ingredients. It is important that the restaurant ___ (source) more produce locally, and the chef recommends that suppliers ___ (be) selected according to transparent criteria.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

**source; be**. La versión completa es: **It is important that the restaurant source more produce locally, and the chef recommends that suppliers be selected according to transparent criteria.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 15 — Reading crítico

Explica en 70–90 palabras por qué esta afirmación necesita matiz: *Cooking another culture’s food is always cultural appropriation.*

<details><summary>Modelo</summary>

The statement is too absolute because the ethical question depends on context, power and acknowledgement. Cooking another culture’s food can form part of respectful exchange when the origins are recognised, local communities are involved and benefits are shared fairly. It may become appropriation when a business removes a dish from its history, claims ownership or profits while the originating community is ignored. The same recipe can therefore have different implications in different settings.

</details>

### Ejercicio 16 — Writing

Escribe 150–180 palabras sobre: **How can restaurants preserve culinary heritage while remaining innovative?** Incluye tres estructuras de subjuntivo, dos colocaciones gastronómicas y una concesión.

<details><summary>Modelo</summary>

It is essential that restaurants acknowledge the origins of the dishes they serve. Innovation can be valuable, but it should not detach culinary heritage from the communities that created it. I recommend that chefs work with local cooks and insist that traditional techniques be explained rather than presented as anonymous discoveries. Restaurants should also source ingredients responsibly and ensure that commercial benefits are shared where appropriate. Although recipes inevitably change over time, a new interpretation can still respect the memory and social meaning of the original dish. It is important that menus provide enough context for diners to understand what has been adapted and why. The committee should require that cultural references be checked carefully, particularly when a restaurant uses a tradition from a marginalised community. In this way, chefs can marry innovation with acknowledgement instead of treating heritage as a decorative resource. Creativity is strongest when it is informed by respect, research and a willingness to credit the people behind the food.

</details>

### Ejercicio 17 — Speaking

Habla durante un minuto sobre una reseña de restaurante. Usa **I recommend that**, **it is essential that**, **although** y vocabulario de sabor y textura.

<details><summary>Pista y modelo</summary>

**I recommend that the restaurant revise the balance of its tasting menu. Although the broth had a subtle smoky aroma and the purée was silky, it is essential that the seasoning be adjusted in the main course. I also recommend that the chef provide more context about the dish’s provenance. The technique was precise, but the presentation seemed overcomplicated.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 18 — Mini-test final

Completa el texto.

> The inspector requires that all ingredients ___ (be) labelled and that allergens ___ (list) clearly. The chef recommends that the kitchen ___ (source) local produce, although she insists that the budget ___ not ___ (increase) unnecessarily. The restaurant’s new menu ___ (marry) traditional techniques with contemporary presentation.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

**be; be listed; source; not be increased; marries.** Las tres primeras formas están en subjuntivo mandativo; la cuarta es un subjuntivo pasivo negativo; *marries* pertenece a la oración principal y por eso sí lleva `-s`.

</details>

### Ejercicio 19 — Revisión de patrones

Reescribe cada recomendación usando el patrón indicado y explica qué cambia.

1. **recommend that + subject + base:** The chef wants the supplier to document the provenance.
2. **recommend + -ing:** The critic thinks restaurants should reduce packaging.
3. **advise + object + to-infinitive:** The inspector tells the manager to train the staff.
4. **insist that + subject + base:** The association wants menus to acknowledge culinary heritage.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **The chef recommends that the supplier document the provenance.**
2. **The critic recommends reducing packaging.**
3. **The inspector advises the manager to train the staff.**
4. **The association insists that menus acknowledge culinary heritage.**

Las cuatro frases expresan una recomendación o exigencia, pero cada verbo selecciona un patrón diferente. *Recommend* puede llevar una cláusula mandativa o gerundio; *advise* necesita normalmente un objeto antes del infinitivo; *insist* combina bien con *that + subject + base* o con *on + -ing*. Esta diferencia de complementación es esencial en escritura C1: no basta con traducir “recomendar” o “aconsejar” palabra por palabra.

</details>

## Resumen de la unidad

El subjuntivo mandativo se forma con **that + sujeto + verbo base** después de recomendaciones, exigencias y expresiones de importancia: *recommend that he attend*, *require that the labels be checked*, *it is essential that chefs know the provenance*. La forma no lleva `-s` en tercera persona y usa **be** igual en singular y plural.

También puedes usar **should + base**, una estructura frecuente en inglés británico, o cambiar el patrón: *recommend doing*, *advise someone to do*, *suggest that someone do* y *urge someone to do*. No traduzcas literalmente; aprende qué complemento acepta cada verbo.

En gastronomía, combina la gramática con colocaciones precisas como *preserve culinary heritage*, *source ingredients locally*, *marry flavours* y *acknowledge a dish’s origins*. Una reseña C1 no se limita a decir que algo está “bueno”: describe técnica, aroma, textura, presentación y contexto cultural, y distingue una preferencia personal de una conclusión general.
