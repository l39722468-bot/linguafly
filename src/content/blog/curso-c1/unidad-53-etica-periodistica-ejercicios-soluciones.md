## Parte II — Ejercicios

**Ejercicio 1.** Completa con el principio deontológico correcto: *accuracy*, *fairness*, *impartiality*, *independence*.

1. The duty to report facts as they occurred, verified and unembellished, is ______.
2. The obligation to represent all relevant sides of a story without stacking the deck is ______.
3. The commitment to keep editorial decisions free from commercial or political pressure is ______.
4. The aspiration to separate fact from opinion and present neither as the other is ______.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. accuracy
2. fairness
3. independence
4. impartiality

These four principles form the core of most codes of ethics, though their application often involves trade-offs: accuracy may conflict with speed, fairness with the duty to expose wrongdoing, and impartiality with the journalist's responsibility to adjudicate competing claims.
</details>

**Ejercicio 2.** Elige el verbo de atribución que mejor captura la actitud de la fuente en cada caso.

1. The minister ______ that the figures had been presented in a flattering light, but denied deliberate intent.
2. The whistleblower ______ that the programme had been operating without oversight for years.
3. The CEO ______ the allegations of fraud, calling them baseless.
4. The scientist ______ that the early results were less conclusive than initially reported.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. conceded — implies reluctant admission of a point that does not favour one's position.
2. asserted (or claimed) — signals a strong statement that the journalist is reporting but not necessarily endorsing.
3. denied — flat rejection of the allegations.
4. acknowledged — admission of a limitation, more neutral than *conceded*.

The choice of attribution verb positions the reader: *conceded* signals reluctance, *asserted* signals force without journalist endorsement, *denied* signals flat rejection, and *acknowledged* signals a measured admission.
</details>

**Ejercicio 3.** Completa con el nivel de atribución correcto: *on the record*, *off the record*, *on background*, *embargo*.

1. Information that may be published but not attributed to the source by name is ______.
2. Information that may not be published at all and only guides the journalist's understanding is ______.
3. Information that may be quoted and attributed to the named source is ______.
4. Information released in advance on condition it is not published before a stated time is under ______.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. on background (or not for attribution)
2. off the record
3. on the record
4. embargo

The levels form a gradient of attribution. A C1 journalist manages these explicitly, and a C1 reader understands that *sources said* signals background sourcing whose verification the reader cannot independently assess.
</details>

**Ejercicio 4.** Reescribe esta afirmación excesivamente categórica sobre el equilibrio periodístico con la precisión deontológica de un C1: "Journalists must always give both sides equal weight."

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*While the principle of balance \u2014 that perennial touchstone of journalistic self-regulation \u2014 may, in its aspiration to fairness, serve as a guard against the covert insertion of editorial opinion, its uncritical application risks producing a false equivalence in which the measured consensus of the scientific community and the marginal dissent of a vested interest are afforded equal weight, a practice that, in the name of impartiality, arguably does more to mislead the public than the partisanship it was designed to prevent.*

The revision distinguishes the legitimate aspiration of balance from its uncritical application, and introduces the concept of false equivalence to show that "both sides" is not always a neutral framing.
</details>

**Ejercicio 5.** Traduce al inglés C1: "El derecho de réplica, cuando se trata como una formalidad en lugar de una oportunidad genuina, se convierte en un procedimiento vacío que satisface la letra del código pero socava su espíritu."

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The right of reply, when treated as a formality to be discharged rather than a genuine opportunity for the subject to correct the record, becomes a procedural shell that satisfies the letter of the code while undermining its spirit, since a reply buried on page twelve while the accusation led page one is, in any meaningful sense, no reply at all.*

The key deontological vocabulary: *right of reply*, *formality to be discharged*, *letter of the code*, *undermining its spirit*, *due prominence* (implied by the page-twelve/front-page contrast).
</details>

**Ejercicio 6.** Completa con léxico de verificación: *corroboration*, *single-source reporting*, *triangulation*, *fact-checking*.

1. Confirming a claim through independent sources is ______.
2. Relying on one account without independent confirmation is ______.
3. Cross-referencing multiple sources and types of evidence is ______.
4. The systematic checking of factual claims before publication is ______.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. corroboration
2. single-source reporting
3. triangulation
4. fact-checking

These terms map the epistemic rigour of journalism: corroboration and triangulation strengthen a claim, single-sourcing carries acknowledged risk, and fact-checking is the systematic pre-publication discipline that distinguishes journalism from assertion.
</details>

**Ejercicio 7.** Escribe una frase en C1 que distinga *public interest* de *public curiosity*, usando vocabulario deontológico.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The tendency of certain outlets to invoke the public interest as a post hoc justification for coverage that serves principally the public curiosity is a rhetorical manoeuvre that erodes the very concept it appropriates, for if every story that attracts readers can be retroactively deemed in the public interest, the term ceases to function as a constraint and becomes instead a licence whose only limit is the market's appetite.*

The distinction does the ethical work: *public interest* is a constraint that justifies intrusion; *public curiosity* is an appetite that does not. Conflating them turns the constraint into a licence.
</details>

**Ejercicio 8.** Reescribe con precisión deontológica: "The newspaper made a small mistake but fixed it later."

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The newspaper issued a correction, though one whose due prominence \u2014 buried in a footnote on an inside page \u2014 fell considerably short of the visibility the original error had commanded, a discrepancy that, while technically discharging the obligation to rectify, arguably treated the correction as a regulatory inconvenience rather than an ethical duty to set the record as visibly as it had been distorted.*

The revision introduces *correction*, *due prominence*, and the concept that the visibility of the rectification should match the visibility of the error \u2014 a substantive, not merely procedural, understanding of accountability.
</details>

**Ejercicio 9.** Completa con léxico de independencia y conflictos de interés: *native advertising*, *disclosure*, *church-state separation*, *advertorial*.

1. The firewall between editorial and commercial operations is the ______.
2. Content that mimics editorial format but is paid for by an advertiser is ______.
3. The transparent declaration of a journalist's financial or personal interest is ______.
4. A paid promotional feature presented in the style of editorial content is an ______.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. church-state separation
2. native advertising
3. disclosure
4. advertorial

Native advertising and advertorial both blur the editorial-commercial boundary; disclosure is the ethical counterweight, and the church-state separation is the structural firewall whose erosion is one of the principal contemporary threats to editorial independence.
</details>

**Ejercicio 10.** Reescribe este titular sensacionalista con el lenguaje ético de un C1: "SHOCKING SCANDAL: Politician's secret affair revealed!"

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*While the private life of a public figure may, in certain circumstances, bear a legitimate relationship to their public role \u2014 for instance, where it bears on their advocacy of family-values legislation \u2014 the publication of details that serve no evident public interest beyond the satisfaction of curiosity raises questions about whether the story's prominence reflects a judgement of newsworthiness or a calculation of engagement, a distinction that the code of ethics exists precisely to compel.*

The revision reframes the question from sensation to the deontological test: does the private life bear a *legitimate relationship to the public role*? Without that relationship, the story serves curiosity, not interest.
</details>

**Ejercicio 11.** Escribe una frase en C1 sobre la protección de fuentes que explique por qué es una condición estructural del periodismo, no una preferencia personal.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The journalist's obligation to protect a confidential source is not a personal preference but a structural precondition of the profession, for without credible assurance that the identity of sources will not be disclosed, the flow of information on which accountability journalism depends would, over time, attenuate to the point where the watchdog function becomes largely ceremonial.*

The key move is reframing source protection from an individual choice to a *structural precondition*: the argument is not that the journalist *wants* to protect sources but that the profession's function *depends* on it.
</details>

**Ejercicio 12.** Traduce al inglés C1: "El uso de métodos de incógnito, aunque ocasionalmente indispensable para exponer irregularidades que nadie denunciaría, conlleva un coste para la credibilidad de la profesión rara vez reconocido en el momento del exclusiva."

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The use of undercover methods, while occasionally indispensable to expose wrongdoing that no source would volunteer, carries a cost to the profession's credibility that is rarely acknowledged in the moment of the scoop, since the precedent it sets \u2014 that deception is an acceptable tool when the end is sufficiently weighty \u2014 is one whose application to less meritorious cases the same logic will, in time, be invoked to justify.*

Key vocabulary: *undercover methods*, *indispensable*, *wrongdoing*, *credibility*, *scoop*, *precedent*. The hedging (*while occasionally indispensable*, *rarely acknowledged*, *in time*) sustains the tension between the immediate justification and the long-term cost.
</details>

**Ejercicio 13.** Completa con léxico de cita: *verbatim*, *ellipsis*, *out of context*, *partial quote*.

1. Reproducing a source's words exactly as spoken is quoting ______.
2. The omission of words from a quotation, marked with [...], is an ______.
3. A quotation that reproduces only part of what was said is a ______.
4. Presenting a quote without the surrounding sentences that give it meaning is quoting ______.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. verbatim
2. ellipsis
3. partial quote
4. out of context

The ethical point: a partial quote that is technically verbatim but whose meaning changes without its context functions as misrepresentation by omission, even though the words reproduced are accurate. Precision in quotation is an ethical, not merely technical, matter.
</details>

**Ejercicio 14.** Reescribe esta afirmación sobre el sesgo con la precisión de un C1: "That newspaper is totally biased, it always supports one side."

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*While the newspaper's editorial stance is, on the available evidence, consistently aligned with one end of the political spectrum, the more analytically useful distinction is not between biased and unbiased outlets \u2014 a binary that flatters the self-image of those who claim neutrality \u2014 but between those that disclose their framing and those that present a perspective as the absence of one, since transparency about standpoint is arguably a more honest service to the reader than the performance of an objectivity that no human institution fully embodies.*

The revision distinguishes *editorial stance* (disclosed) from *bias* (hidden), and argues that transparency about perspective is more honest than the performance of neutrality. This is the C1 move: from accusation to analysis.
</details>

**Ejercicio 15.** Escribe una frase en C1 sobre el efecto de las métricas de engagement en los valores editoriales, usando vocabulario deontológico.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The pressure to optimise for engagement metrics \u2014 which reward the provocative and the emotive disproportionately \u2014 introduces an incentive structure whose alignment with the duty to inform is, at best, contingent, and a newsroom that allows that incentive to shape editorial decisions without explicit counterweight has, in effect, outsourced its news values to an algorithm it did not design and whose objectives it did not set.*

Key vocabulary: *engagement metrics*, *duty to inform*, *news values*, *editorial decisions*. The argument is that the incentive structure may be *misaligned* with the deontological duty, and that *outsourcing* news values to an algorithm is an abdication of editorial responsibility.
</details>

**Ejercicio 16.** Usa conectores de tensión (*on the one hand*, *on the other hand*, *in the final analysis*, *that said*) para articular el dilema entre el deber de informar y el respeto a la privacidad.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*On the one hand, the duty to inform the public of matters of legitimate concern is a foundational principle of the profession, and the journalist who shrinks from it out of an excess of caution serves neither the public nor the truth; on the other hand, the licence that duty grants is not unbounded, and the fact that a story is interesting is not, in itself, sufficient warrant for the intrusion it may entail. That said, the line between the two is rarely self-evident in the moment of decision, and in the final analysis the test is not whether a story attracts readers but whether its publication serves a public interest weighty enough to override the privacy it intrudes upon \u2014 a judgement that, precisely because it cannot be reduced to a rule, remains the irreducible ethical core of the editor's responsibility.*

The connectors orchestrate the dilemma: *on the one hand / on the other hand* present the two poles, *that said* acknowledges the difficulty of applying them, and *in the final analysis* delivers the synthesising test. This architecture is the hallmark of C1 ethical reasoning.
</details>

**Ejercicio 17.** Reescribe usando verbos de atribución matizados en lugar de *said*: "The minister said the policy worked. The report said it didn't. The opposition said it was a lie."

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The minister *stated* that the policy had delivered the promised outcomes, while the independent report *acknowledged* that the headline figures concealed a deterioration in several underlying metrics, a characterisation the opposition *claimed* amounted to deliberate misrepresentation \u2014 a charge the government *denied* while *conceding* that the methodology would be reviewed.*

The maturation from *said* to *stated / acknowledged / claimed / denied / conceded* does the analytical work: *stated* signals official assertion, *acknowledged* signals reluctant admission, *claimed* signals a charge the journalist is reporting without endorsing, *denied* signals flat rejection, and *conceded* signals a partial yielding. Each verb positions the reader.
</details>

**Ejercicio 18.** Escribe un párrafo breve (4-5 frases) en C1 que argumente sobre si el periodismo debe ser imparcial o transparente sobre su perspectiva, usando vocabulario deontológico y al menos dos conectores de tensión.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The debate between impartiality and transparency-about-perspective is often framed as a choice between two ideals, but the more honest framing may be that they are responses to different failures. On the one hand, impartiality, as an aspiration to separate fact from opinion and to weigh evidence without favour, remains a discipline whose abandonment would leave the public with no common informational ground; on the other hand, the performance of an objectivity that no human institution fully embodies can, when it conceals a standpoint that is in fact present, become a form of covert persuasion more misleading than openly declared opinion. That said, the two are not necessarily incompatible, and in the final analysis the ethically serious position may be one that pursues impartiality in method while being transparent about the perspective that the method is applied from \u2014 a synthesis that asks more of the journalist than either pure neutrality or pure disclosure, but that arguably serves the reader better than the false choice between them.*

Deontological vocabulary: *impartiality*, *transparency*, *separate fact from opinion*, *objectivity*, *standpoint*, *perspective*. Connectors of tension: *on the one hand*, *on the other hand*, *that said*, *in the final analysis*. The paragraph models the C1 capacity to hold two principles in productive tension rather than collapsing one into the other.
</details>

**Ejercicio 19.** Escribe un párrafo breve (4-5 frases) en inglés C1 sobre un dilema ético del periodismo digital, usando al menos tres términos deontológicos y dos conectores de concesión.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The ethical challenges of digital journalism are, in several respects, continuations of old questions under new conditions rather than wholly novel dilemmas. Granted, the speed of publication and the pressure of engagement metrics have intensified the tension between accuracy and immediacy that has always defined the profession; that said, the underlying principle \u2014 that a story should be verified to the extent the timescale permits, and that the limits of that verification should be transparent to the reader \u2014 is not different in kind from the one that governed print. To be sure, the architecture of the attention economy, by rewarding the emotive and the polarising disproportionately, introduces an incentive whose misalignment with the duty to inform is, on the available evidence, more acute than anything the advertising model of the twentieth century produced. The honest position, then, is that the digital era has not invented new ethics so much as it has stress-tested the old ones \u2014 and that the newsroom whose code survives that test is the one whose commitment to accuracy, fairness and minimisation of harm is robust enough to hold even when the metrics whisper that a less scrupulous approach would, in the short term, pay.*

Deontological terms: *accuracy*, *fairness*, *minimisation of harm*, *duty to inform*, *verified*, *transparent*. Concession connectors: *granted*, *that said*, *to be sure*. The paragraph enacts the C1 stance on ethics under technological pressure: continuity of principle, recognition of intensified challenge, and insistence that the test of a code is whether it holds when the incentives push against it.
</details>

## Resumen de la unidad

En esta unidad hemos construido el andamiaje lingüístico para debatir la ética periodística con la precisión deontológica que exige el nivel C1. Has aprendido a distinguir *accuracy* de *fairness*, *off the record* de *on background*, *public interest* de *public curiosity*, y a manejar el léxico de la atribución \u2014de *stated* a *conceded*, de *claimed* a *acknowledged*\u2014, el de la verificación \u2014*corroboration*, *triangulation*, *fact-checking*\u2014 y el de la independencia \u2014*church-state separation*, *native advertising*, *disclosure*. Has incorporado el vocabulario del daño \u2014*minimisation of harm*, *grief intrusion*, *vulnerable sources*\u2014, de la corrección \u2014*due prominence*, *retraction*, *ombudsman*\u2014 y de la protección de fuentes \u2014*source confidentiality*, *shield law*, *chilling effect*. Has ejercitado la crítica del *false balance* y del *he-said-she-said journalism*, la distinción entre cita directa y paráfrasis como cuestión ética, y la articulación de los dilemas entre deber de informar y respeto a la privacidad. La destreza central de esta unidad es la argumentación deontológica: la capacidad de debatir los dilemas del periodismo reconociendo tanto las presiones estructurales como el deber de servir al público, de citar con verbos que posicionan al lector respecto a la credibilidad de la fuente, y de sostener la tensión entre principios que rara vez se alinean sin conflicto. Esa precisión ética es la marca distintiva del hablante C1 cuando se enfrenta al oficio donde la credibilidad del lenguaje es, literalmente, la moneda del intercambio con el público.
