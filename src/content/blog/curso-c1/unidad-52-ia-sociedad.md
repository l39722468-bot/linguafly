La inteligencia artificial es el debate tecnológico definitorio de nuestra era, y discutir sus implicaciones sociales exige una destreza que va más allá del vocabulario técnico: el *hedging*, esa cautela lingüística con la que los hablantes avanzados matizan la fuerza de sus afirmaciones. No basta con decir que *AI will change everything*; hay que distinguir lo que la evidencia sugiere de lo que la especulación propone, lo que ya ocurre de lo que podría ocurrir, y articular juicios con la humildad epistémica que un tema tan incierto demanda. En esta unidad del curso de inglés C1 gratis exploramos cómo los hablantes avanzados manejan el léxico de la IA \u2014desde *machine learning* hasta *alignment problem*\u2014, cómo argumentan sobre sus implicaciones con *hedging* fuerte y cómo debaten sus consecuencias con un rigor que reconoce tanto el potencial transformador como la profunda incertidumbre que lo envuelve. Es un terreno donde el lenguaje debe calibrar la certeza: *it is conceivable*, *the evidence tentatively suggests*, *there is a non-trivial risk that* no son adornos tímidos, sino instrumentos de precisión epistémica que separan la afirmación responsable de la profecía temeraria.

Para situar el nivel, observa cómo el mismo tema se expresa en tres niveles. En **A2** diríamos: *AI is very smart. It will do many things. Maybe it is dangerous.* Es claro pero plano, sin matiz ni vocabulario técnico. En **B2** subiríamos un peldaño: *Artificial intelligence is changing the world. It could help us a lot, but it might also cause problems. We don't know exactly what will happen.* Aquí ya hay problema y cautela, pero le falta densidad técnica y calibración fina. En **C1** alcanzamos la precisión necesaria: *While the evidence tentatively suggests that certain categories of routine cognitive work are susceptible to automation, extrapolating from those findings to sweeping claims about the wholesale obsolescence of human labour would be to mistake a partial trend for a settled trajectory, not least because the very notion of replacement presupposes a one-to-one mapping between tasks and jobs that the labour market's historical capacity for reconfiguration gives us reason to question.* Esta unidad te enseña a producir ese tercer nivel de forma natural.

## Parte I — Teoría

### 1. Léxico fundamental de la inteligencia artificial

La inteligencia artificial (*artificial intelligence* o *AI*) es un campo con una taxonomía que un hablante C1 debe manejar con precisión. *Artificial General Intelligence* (AGI, inteligencia artificial general, sistemas con capacidades cognitivas humanas en cualquier dominio), *narrow AI* o *weak AI* (IA estrecha, especializada en una tarea concreta), *machine learning* (aprendizaje automático, sistemas que aprenden de datos sin programación explícita), *deep learning* (aprendizaje profundo, basado en redes neuronales artificiales con múltiples capas), *neural networks* (redes neuronales), *natural language processing* (NLP, procesamiento del lenguaje natural), *computer vision* (visión artificial), *reinforcement learning* (aprendizaje por refuerzo). Un hablante C1 escribe *the conflation of narrow AI \u2014 which, for all its impressive performance within a bounded task, operates without the contextual understanding that genuine intelligence would require \u2014 with the prospect of artificial general intelligence is a category error that inflates both the promise and the threat, since the leap from pattern-matching within a domain to flexible reasoning across them is, on the current evidence, a gap that no amount of incremental scaling has yet been shown to close.* Distinguir estos niveles no es pedantería técnica sino higiene epistémica.

### 2. El hedging: la cautela lingüística del C1

El *hedging* es el recurso lingüístico mediante el cual un hablante atenúa la fuerza de sus afirmaciones para reflejar la incertidumbre epistémica. Sus instrumentos son variados: *modal verbs* (*may*, *might*, *could*, *would*), *adverbs of probability* (*probably*, *likely*, *possibly*, *presumably*), *tentative verbs* (*suggest*, *indicate*, *appear to*, *seem to*, *tend to*), *impersonal structures* (*it is conceivable that*, *there is a risk that*, *one might argue that*), *frequency adverbs* (*often*, *frequently*, *in many cases*), y *epistemic adjectives* (*possible*, *probable*, *conceivable*, *putative*). Un hablante C1 escribe *the available evidence tentatively suggests that exposure to algorithmic recommendation systems may correlate with a narrowing of content diversity, though whether that correlation reflects causation or merely the pre-existing preferences of a self-selecting user base remains an open question that the cross-sectional design of most studies is not equipped to resolve.* El hedging no es indecisión sino honestidad intelectual: afirmar con la fuerza que la evidencia justifica, ni más ni menos.

### 3. Modal verbs en predicciones sobre IA

Los *modal verbs* son el núcleo del hedging en inglés. *Will* expresa certeza (*AI will transform healthcare*); *should* probabilidad razonable (*it should reduce diagnostic errors*); *may* y *might* posibilidad (*it may displace certain roles*); *could* posibilidad con mayor incertidumbre (*it could undermine social cohesion*); *would* hipótesis (*it would require massive investment*); *must* y *have to* necesidad (*we must address the alignment problem*). Un hablante C1 escribe *while large language models will almost certainly continue to improve on benchmark tasks, whether they will translate that performance into the kind of reliable reasoning that safety-critical applications demand is a question that the frequency of hallucination in even the most capable systems gives us reason to treat with considerable caution.* La gradación modal es esencial: un hablante C1 nunca dice *will* donde la evidencia solo sostiene *might*, ni *might* donde los datos justifican *will*.

### 4. Displacement vs replacement: el debate laboral

El debate sobre el impacto laboral de la IA gira en torno a una distinción crucial: *displacement* (desplazamiento de tareas, donde la IA asume partes del trabajo) frente a *replacement* (sustitución de empleos completos). Otras nociones: *automation* (automatización), *augmentation* (aumento, la IA como complemento humano), *job polarisation* (polarización del empleo), *skill-biased technological change* (cambio tecnológico sesgado por habilidades), *routine task displacement* (desplazamiento de tareas rutinarias), *creative destruction* (destrucción creativa de Schumpeter). Un hablante C1 escribe *the tendency to frame automation in binary terms of jobs destroyed or preserved obscures the more plausible scenario in which it is tasks, rather than entire occupations, that are displaced, leaving the net effect on employment contingent on the rate at which new tasks \u2014 and the demand for human judgement within them \u2014 emerge to absorb the labour released.* Esta distinción entre tareas y empleos es analíticamente decisiva y se pierde en los titulares simplistas.

### 5. El alignment problem y la seguridad de la IA

El *alignment problem* (problema de alineación) es el desafío de asegurar que los objetivos de los sistemas de IA coincidan con los valores humanos. Su léxico incluye *value alignment* (alineación de valores), *specification problem* (problema de especificación, la dificultad de definir objetivos sin ambigüedad), *reward hacking* (hackeo del incentivo, cuando el sistema optimiza una métrica proxy en vez del objetivo real), *unintended consequences* (consecuencias no intencionadas), *instrumental convergence* (convergencia instrumental, la tendencia de agentes inteligentes a adquirir ciertos objetivos intermedios), *scalable oversight* (supervisión escalable), *corrigibility* (corregibilidad). Un hablante C1 escribe *the specification problem \u2014 the gap between what we ask a system to optimise and what we actually want it to do \u2014 is not a peripheral concern but arguably the central challenge of advanced AI, since a sufficiently capable system that pursues a mis-specified objective with single-minded efficiency could, in principle, produce outcomes that are not merely suboptimal but actively contrary to the interests its designers believed they had encoded.* El hedging es aquí especialmente necesario: hablamos de posibilidades, no de certezas.

### 6. Sesgo algorítmico y equidad

El sesgo algorítmico (*algorithmic bias*) es una de las implicaciones sociales más documentadas. *Training data bias* (sesgo en los datos de entrenamiento), *disparate impact* (impacto dispar, efectos desproporcionados sobre grupos protegidos), *fairness metrics* (métricas de equidad), *demographic parity* (paridad demográfica), *equalised odds* (probabilidades igualadas), *algorithmic discrimination* (discriminación algorítmica), *feedback loops* (bucles de retroalimentación que amplifican el sesgo), *proxy variables* (variables proxy que codifican atributos protegidos). Un hablante C1 escribe *the claim that algorithmic decisions are, by virtue of their mathematical character, inherently more objective than human ones rests on a confusion between the neutrality of the procedure and the neutrality of the inputs, for a model trained on historical data that already encodes systemic disadvantage will, with considerable reliability, reproduce and even amplify that disadvantage under the guise of data-driven objectivity.* La distinción entre neutralidad procedimental y neutralidad sustantiva es clave para no mistificar la objetividad algorítmica.

### 7. Transparencia, explicabilidad y caja negra

La opacidad de los sistemas de IA (*black box problem*) plantea problemas de *transparency* (transparencia), *explainability* o *interpretability* (explicabilidad o interpretabilidad), *accountability* (rendición de cuentas), *auditability* (auditabilidad), *model cards* (fichas de modelo, documentación de rendimiento y limitaciones), *right to explanation* (derecho a explicación). Un hablante C1 escribe *the tension between predictive performance and interpretability is not merely a technical trade-off but a governance challenge, for a model whose decisions cannot be reconstructed in terms that a human decision-maker could interrogate is one whose deployment in consequential settings \u2014 credit, criminal justice, healthcare \u2014 raises questions of accountability that the rubric of efficiency alone cannot settle.* Esta tensión entre rendimiento y explicabilidad es uno de los grandes dilemas de la gobernanza de la IA.

### 8. IA y desinformación: implicaciones democráticas

La IA tiene implicaciones para la esfera democrática: *deepfakes* (falsificaciones profundas), *synthetic media* (medios sintéticos), *automated propaganda* (propaganda automatizada), *microtargeting* (microsegmentación), *bot networks* (redes de bots), *astroturfing* (falsa base de apoyo espontáneo), *generative AI* (IA generativa). Un hablante C1 escribe *while the spectre of mass-produced deepfakes swinging elections has, on the available evidence, yet to materialise at the scale feared, the more insidious risk may lie in the erosion of the commonsense presumption that audiovisual evidence is, by default, trustworthy \u2014 a presumption whose degradation could, over time, furnish bad actors with a ready-made defence of plausible deniability regardless of whether the specific content they disseminate is fabricated.* El hedging es esencial aquí: distinguir el riesgo materializado del riesgo latente.

### 9. Concentración de poder y gobernanza

La IA plantea cuestiones de concentración de poder: *compute concentration* (concentración de capacidad computacional), *frontier models* (modelos frontera, los más avanzados), *open-source vs closed-source AI* (IA de código abierto vs cerrado), *regulatory capture* (captura regulatoria), *compute governance* (gobernanza computacional), *AI safety* (seguridad de la IA), *existential risk* (riesgo existencial), *societal-scale risks* (riesgos a escala societal). Un hablante C1 escribe *the observation that the resources required to train frontier models are concentrated in a handful of entities is sometimes cited as an argument for open-source release as a democratising counterweight, yet one might reasonably wonder whether lowering the barrier to acquiring powerful capabilities is, on balance, a democratisation of power or a multiplication of the points at which its misuse could occur.* El hedging equilibra dos argumentos plausibles sin resolver prematuramente.

### 10. IA y creatividad: el debate sobre la generación

La IA generativa y la creatividad: *generative AI* (IA generativa), *text-to-image* (texto a imagen), *large language models* (LLM), *prompt engineering* (ingeniería de prompts), *training data* (datos de entrenamiento), *copyright infringement* (infracción de copyright), *derivative works* (obras derivadas), *human-AI collaboration* (colaboración humano-IA). Un hablante C1 escribe *the question of whether outputs generated by systems trained on copyrighted corpora constitute infringement is one that the existing framework of intellectual property \u2014 designed around human authorship and direct copying \u2014 may be structurally ill-equipped to adjudicate, since the model neither reproduces the training data verbatim nor creates in the intentional sense that the law has historically presupposed.* El hedging reconoce que el marco legal existente puede no ser adecuado para los casos nuevos.

### 11. IA y privacidad: la extracción de datos

La IA y la privacidad se entrelazan en la *data extraction* (extracción de datos): *data scraping* (raspado de datos), *consent* (consentimiento), *data minimisation* (minimización de datos), *inference* (inferencia, deducir información no explicitada), *re-identification* (reidentificación de datos anonimizados), *privacy-preserving machine learning* (aprendizaje automático que preserva la privacidad), *federated learning* (aprendizaje federado). Un hablante C1 escribe *the capacity of machine learning models to infer attributes that individuals never explicitly disclosed \u2014 from shopping patterns to sexual orientation \u2014 suggests that the traditional frame of privacy as a function of what one chooses to reveal may be substantially outdated in an era where the patterns in one's behaviour can betray what one has taken care to conceal.* El hedging matiza la fuerza de la inferencia.

### 12. IA y educación: implicaciones cognitivas

La IA en educación plantea cuestiones cognitivas: *personalised learning* (aprendizaje personalizado), *adaptive systems* (sistemas adaptativos), *cognitive offloading* (descarga cognitiva), *critical thinking* (pensamiento crítico), *over-reliance* (dependencia excesiva), *metacognition* (metacognición), *scaffolding* (andamiaje pedagógico). Un hablante C1 escribe *while AI tutors could, in principle, democratise access to individualised instruction, there is a plausible risk that the convenience of instant answers may, if uncritically embraced, erode the productive struggle that cognitive science has consistently identified as a precondition for durable learning rather than mere performance.* El hedging distingue el potencial del riesgo.

### 13. IA y medio ambiente: el coste computacional

El coste ambiental de la IA: *compute intensity* (intensidad computacional), *energy consumption* (consumo energético), *carbon footprint* (huella de carbono), *training cost* (coste de entrenamiento), *inference cost* (coste de inferencia), *efficiency* (eficiencia), *green AI* (IA verde). Un hablante C1 escribe *the tendency to celebrate each new leap in model capability without acknowledging the exponential growth in compute \u2014 and therefore in energy and emissions \u2014 that those leaps have required risks normalising a trajectory whose aggregate environmental cost is, on present trends, unlikely to remain negligible relative to the sector's stated climate commitments.* El hedging reconoce la incertidumbre sobre las cifras exactas sin minimizar el problema.

### 14. Hedging fuerte: estructuras avanzadas de matización

El hedging fuerte en C1 emplea estructuras complejas: *it is conceivable that* (es concebible que), *there is a non-trivial risk that* (hay un riesgo nada trivial de que), *the evidence tentatively suggests* (la evidencia sugiere tentativamente), *one might reasonably wonder whether* (uno podría preguntarse razonablemente si), *it would be premature to conclude* (sería prematuro concluir), *such a framing risks* (tal encuadre corre el riesgo de), *to the extent that* (en la medida en que), *insofar as* (en cuanto que). Un hablante C1 escribe *to the extent that the current generation of language models exhibits reasoning-like behaviour, it would be premature to conclude that such behaviour reflects the kind of internal model-building that genuine reasoning would require, since the alternative hypothesis \u2014 that the system is performing sophisticated pattern-matching over its training distribution \u2014 remains, on the available evidence, at least as consistent with the observed performance.* Estas estructuras son la marca del hedging avanzado.

### 15. Cohesión en ensayos sobre IA y sociedad

La cohesión en ensayos sobre IA requiere conectores que articulen la incertidumbre: *granted* (concedido), *admittedly* (hay que admitirlo), *to be sure* (ciertamente), *that said* (dicho esto), *nevertheless* (sin embargo), *in fairness* (en justicia), *with the caveat that* (con la salvedad de que), *assuming that* (suponiendo que), *conditional on* (condicionado a). Un hablante C1 escribe *granted, the productivity gains that AI could unlock are, in principle, substantial; that said, the assumption that those gains will automatically translate into broadly shared prosperity presupposes a distributional mechanism that the historical record of previous technological transitions gives us no strong reason to take for granted.* Estos conectores equilibran concesión y reserva, el alma del hedging C1.

**Ejercicio 2.** Reescribe las siguientes afirmaciones excesivamente categóricas usando hedging apropiado para nivel C1.

1. AI will destroy millions of jobs.
2. AI is objective and free from bias.
3. Large language models understand language like humans do.
4. AI will solve climate change.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. *It is conceivable that AI could displace a significant number of roles, though the net effect on employment will likely depend on the rate at which new tasks emerge to absorb the labour released.*
2. *While algorithmic systems may appear objective by virtue of their mathematical character, the evidence suggests that models trained on historical data can reproduce and even amplify the biases embedded in that data.*
3. *Although large language models produce remarkably fluent text, it would be premature to conclude that they understand language in the intentional, world-grounded sense that humans do.*
4. *AI might contribute to climate solutions in domains such as energy optimisation, though framing it as a silver bullet risks underestimating both its own environmental cost and the political challenges that technology alone cannot resolve.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 3.** Elige el modal verb con la calibración epistémica correcta para cada contexto.

1. The evidence (will / may / must) suggest a correlation, but causation is unproven.
2. Frontier models (would / could / will) pose societal-scale risks if deployed without oversight.
3. It (must / might / should) be the case that fine-tuning reduces hallucination, but studies are mixed.
4. A misaligned system (could / should / may) produce outcomes contrary to human interests, in principle.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. may — the correlation is suggested but not strong enough for *will*; *must* would overstate logical necessity.
2. could — the risk is a possibility contingent on conditions, not a certainty (*will*) or an expectation (*should*).
3. might — the studies are mixed, so the weakest modal is appropriate; *must* and *should* would overclaim.
4. could — the phrase *in principle* signals a hypothetical possibility, which *could* captures precisely.

Modal calibration is the heart of hedging: matching the verb's force to the evidence's weight.
</details>

**Ejercicio 4.** Traduce al inglés C1 con hedging fuerte: "Es conceivable que la IA pueda amplificar las desigualdades existentes, aunque la evidencia sugiere tentativamente que el efecto neto depende en gran medida del marco regulador."

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*It is conceivable that AI could amplify existing inequalities, though the evidence tentatively suggests that the net effect depends to a considerable extent on the regulatory framework in place.*

Note the layered hedging: *conceivable* (possibility), *could* (contingent), *tentatively suggests* (weak evidence), *to a considerable extent* (qualification of dependence). A C1 speaker stacks these rather than choosing one.
</details>

**Ejercicio 5.** Reescribe usando una estructura impersonal de hedging: "AI will definitely make creative jobs obsolete."

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*There is a non-trivial risk that AI could render certain categories of creative work economically marginal, though to conclude that it will make creative occupations wholesale obsolete would be to extrapolate beyond what the current evidence can sustain.*

The impersonal structure (*there is a non-trivial risk that*) distances the claim from the speaker, while *certain categories* and *economically marginal* replace the absolute *definitely* and *obsolete*.
</details>

**Ejercicio 7.** Escribe una frase en C1 que distinga *displacement* de *replacement* al hablar del impacto laboral de la IA, usando hedging.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*While the tendency to frame automation in terms of wholesale job replacement makes for arresting headlines, the more analytically defensible framing is one of task displacement, in which AI assumes specific components of a role while leaving others \u2014 particularly those requiring contextual judgement and interpersonal negotiation \u2014 in human hands, a distinction whose practical importance lies in that it reframes the policy question from how to prevent job loss to how to manage a transition whose shape is, on present evidence, considerably more uneven than the binary narrative allows.*

The hedging (*on present evidence*, *considerably more uneven than*) keeps the claim calibrated, while the displacement/replacement distinction does the analytical work.
</details>

**Ejercicio 8.** Corrige el exceso de certeza en este párrafo usando al menos cuatro recursos de hedging: "AI bias is a solved problem. Once we remove biased data, algorithms will be perfectly fair. Any remaining disparity is just noise."

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*While removing biased training data may go some way towards mitigating algorithmic bias, it would be premature to regard the problem as solved, since bias can also arise from feature selection, proxy variables and the very definition of the fairness metric chosen, which suggests that residual disparities may reflect structural factors rather than mere noise. To be sure, debiasing the data is likely a necessary step; that said, treating it as sufficient risks mistaking a partial remedy for a comprehensive one.*

Hedging resources used: *may*, *would be premature to*, *can also*, *suggests that*, *may reflect*, *likely*, *that said*. The revised version acknowledges progress without overclaiming resolution.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. black box
2. interpretability (or explainability)
3. accountability
4. auditability

These four terms map the governance challenge: opacity (black box) creates a need for interpretability, which in turn underpins accountability and auditability in consequential deployments.
</details>

**Ejercicio 10.** Reescribe esta afirmación sobre deepfakes con hedging que distinga riesgo materializado de riesgo latente: "Deepfakes have already destroyed democracy."

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*While the spectre of mass-produced deepfakes swinging elections has, on the available evidence, yet to materialise at the scale feared, the more insidious risk may lie in the gradual erosion of the commonsense presumption that audiovisual evidence is trustworthy by default \u2014 a degradation whose full consequences could, over time, prove more corrosive to democratic discourse than any single fabricated clip, however sensational.*

The hedging distinguishes what has happened (*yet to materialise*) from what could happen (*could, over time, prove*), and redirects attention from the sensational to the structural.
</details>

**Ejercicio 11.** Escribe una frase en C1 sobre la concentración de poder en la IA que presente dos argumentos plausibles sin resolverlos prematuramente, usando hedging.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The observation that the resources required to train frontier models are concentrated in a handful of entities is sometimes cited as an argument for open-source release as a democratising counterweight; yet one might reasonably wonder whether lowering the barrier to acquiring powerful capabilities is, on balance, a democratisation of power or a multiplication of the points at which its misuse could occur \u2014 a tension that the current evidence does not, as yet, allow us to resolve with any confidence.*

## Resumen de la unidad

En esta unidad hemos construido el andamiaje lingüístico para hablar de la inteligencia artificial y sus implicaciones sociales con la cautela epistémica que exige el nivel C1. Has aprendido a distinguir *narrow AI* de *AGI*, *machine learning* de *deep learning*, *displacement* de *replacement*, y a manejar el léxico del *alignment problem* \u2014del *specification problem* al *reward hacking*\u2014, del sesgo algorítmico \u2014*disparate impact*, *proxy variables*, *feedback loops*\u2014 y de la gobernanza \u2014*frontier models*, *compute concentration*, *scalable oversight*. Has incorporado el vocabulario de la transparencia \u2014*black box*, *interpretability*, *accountability*\u2014 y el de las implicaciones democráticas \u2014*deepfakes*, *synthetic media*, *microtargeting*\u2014. Has ejercitado el hedging fuerte \u2014*it is conceivable that*, *the evidence tentatively suggests*, *one might reasonably wonder whether*, *it would be premature to conclude*\u2014 como instrumento de precisión epistémica, y los conectores de concesión y reserva \u2014*granted*, *that said*, *to be sure*, *with the caveat that*\u2014 como arquitectura del argumento equilibrado. La destreza central de esta unidad es la matización calibrada: la capacidad de debatir la IA reconociendo tanto su potencial transformador como la profunda incertidumbre que lo envuelve, de afirmar con la fuerza que la evidencia justifica y no más, y de resistir la tentación \u2014tan común en este debate\u2014 de convertir la incertidumbre en certeza en cualquiera de las dos direcciones. Esa elegancia epistémica es la marca distintiva del hablante C1 cuando se enfrenta al debate tecnológico donde las apuestas son más altas y el conocimiento más provisional.
