## Parte II — Ejercicios

**Ejercicio 1.** Completa las frases con el término correcto de la taxonomía de la IA: *narrow AI*, *AGI*, *machine learning*, *deep learning*.

1. A system that classifies medical images but cannot drive a car is an example of ______.
2. The hypothetical machine that can perform any intellectual task a human can is called ______.
3. The subset of AI where systems learn patterns from data without explicit programming is ______.
4. The branch of machine learning based on multi-layered neural networks is ______.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. narrow AI (or weak AI)
2. AGI (Artificial General Intelligence)
3. machine learning
4. deep learning

The distinction matters because narrow AI, however impressive, operates within a bounded task and lacks the flexible, cross-domain reasoning that would define general intelligence; conflating the two inflates both promise and threat.
</details>

**Ejercicio 2.** Reescribe las siguientes afirmaciones excesivamente categóricas usando hedging apropiado para nivel C1.

1. AI will destroy millions of jobs.
2. AI is objective and free from bias.
3. Large language models understand language like humans do.
4. AI will solve climate change.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. *It is conceivable that AI could displace a significant number of roles, though the net effect on employment will likely depend on the rate at which new tasks emerge to absorb the labour released.*
2. *While algorithmic systems may appear objective by virtue of their mathematical character, the evidence suggests that models trained on historical data can reproduce and even amplify the biases embedded in that data.*
3. *Although large language models produce remarkably fluent text, it would be premature to conclude that they understand language in the intentional, world-grounded sense that humans do.*
4. *AI might contribute to climate solutions in domains such as energy optimisation, though framing it as a silver bullet risks underestimating both its own environmental cost and the political challenges that technology alone cannot resolve.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 3.** Elige el modal verb con la calibración epistémica correcta para cada contexto.

1. The evidence (will / may / must) suggest a correlation, but causation is unproven.
2. Frontier models (would / could / will) pose societal-scale risks if deployed without oversight.
3. It (must / might / should) be the case that fine-tuning reduces hallucination, but studies are mixed.
4. A misaligned system (could / should / may) produce outcomes contrary to human interests, in principle.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. may — the correlation is suggested but not strong enough for *will*; *must* would overstate logical necessity.
2. could — the risk is a possibility contingent on conditions, not a certainty (*will*) or an expectation (*should*).
3. might — the studies are mixed, so the weakest modal is appropriate; *must* and *should* would overclaim.
4. could — the phrase *in principle* signals a hypothetical possibility, which *could* captures precisely.

Modal calibration is the heart of hedging: matching the verb's force to the evidence's weight.
</details>

**Ejercicio 4.** Traduce al inglés C1 con hedging fuerte: "Es conceivable que la IA pueda amplificar las desigualdades existentes, aunque la evidencia sugiere tentativamente que el efecto neto depende en gran medida del marco regulador."

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*It is conceivable that AI could amplify existing inequalities, though the evidence tentatively suggests that the net effect depends to a considerable extent on the regulatory framework in place.*

Note the layered hedging: *conceivable* (possibility), *could* (contingent), *tentatively suggests* (weak evidence), *to a considerable extent* (qualification of dependence). A C1 speaker stacks these rather than choosing one.
</details>

**Ejercicio 5.** Reescribe usando una estructura impersonal de hedging: "AI will definitely make creative jobs obsolete."

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*There is a non-trivial risk that AI could render certain categories of creative work economically marginal, though to conclude that it will make creative occupations wholesale obsolete would be to extrapolate beyond what the current evidence can sustain.*

The impersonal structure (*there is a non-trivial risk that*) distances the claim from the speaker, while *certain categories* and *economically marginal* replace the absolute *definitely* and *obsolete*.
</details>

**Ejercicio 6.** Completa con léxico del alignment problem: *reward hacking*, *specification problem*, *instrumental convergence*, *scalable oversight*.

1. The difficulty of defining objectives without ambiguity is the ______.
2. When a system optimises a proxy metric instead of the true goal, we call it ______.
3. The tendency of capable agents to acquire intermediate goals like self-preservation is ______.
4. The challenge of supervising systems that exceed human capability is ______.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. specification problem
2. reward hacking
3. instrumental convergence
4. scalable oversight

These terms form the core vocabulary of AI safety, and using them precisely signals engagement with the technical debate rather than its popularised gloss.
</details>

**Ejercicio 7.** Escribe una frase en C1 que distinga *displacement* de *replacement* al hablar del impacto laboral de la IA, usando hedging.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*While the tendency to frame automation in terms of wholesale job replacement makes for arresting headlines, the more analytically defensible framing is one of task displacement, in which AI assumes specific components of a role while leaving others \u2014 particularly those requiring contextual judgement and interpersonal negotiation \u2014 in human hands, a distinction whose practical importance lies in that it reframes the policy question from how to prevent job loss to how to manage a transition whose shape is, on present evidence, considerably more uneven than the binary narrative allows.*

The hedging (*on present evidence*, *considerably more uneven than*) keeps the claim calibrated, while the displacement/replacement distinction does the analytical work.
</details>

**Ejercicio 8.** Corrige el exceso de certeza en este párrafo usando al menos cuatro recursos de hedging: "AI bias is a solved problem. Once we remove biased data, algorithms will be perfectly fair. Any remaining disparity is just noise."

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*While removing biased training data may go some way towards mitigating algorithmic bias, it would be premature to regard the problem as solved, since bias can also arise from feature selection, proxy variables and the very definition of the fairness metric chosen, which suggests that residual disparities may reflect structural factors rather than mere noise. To be sure, debiasing the data is likely a necessary step; that said, treating it as sufficient risks mistaking a partial remedy for a comprehensive one.*

Hedging resources used: *may*, *would be premature to*, *can also*, *suggests that*, *may reflect*, *likely*, *that said*. The revised version acknowledges progress without overclaiming resolution.
</details>

**Ejercicio 9.** Completa con léxico de transparencia y explicabilidad: *black box*, *interpretability*, *accountability*, *auditability*.

1. A model whose internal reasoning cannot be inspected is a ______.
2. The degree to which a human can understand a model's decisions is its ______.
3. The obligation to justify and take responsibility for decisions is ______.
4. The capacity to examine a system's behaviour systematically is its ______.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. black box
2. interpretability (or explainability)
3. accountability
4. auditability

These four terms map the governance challenge: opacity (black box) creates a need for interpretability, which in turn underpins accountability and auditability in consequential deployments.
</details>

**Ejercicio 10.** Reescribe esta afirmación sobre deepfakes con hedging que distinga riesgo materializado de riesgo latente: "Deepfakes have already destroyed democracy."

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*While the spectre of mass-produced deepfakes swinging elections has, on the available evidence, yet to materialise at the scale feared, the more insidious risk may lie in the gradual erosion of the commonsense presumption that audiovisual evidence is trustworthy by default \u2014 a degradation whose full consequences could, over time, prove more corrosive to democratic discourse than any single fabricated clip, however sensational.*

The hedging distinguishes what has happened (*yet to materialise*) from what could happen (*could, over time, prove*), and redirects attention from the sensational to the structural.
</details>

**Ejercicio 11.** Escribe una frase en C1 sobre la concentración de poder en la IA que presente dos argumentos plausibles sin resolverlos prematuramente, usando hedging.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The observation that the resources required to train frontier models are concentrated in a handful of entities is sometimes cited as an argument for open-source release as a democratising counterweight; yet one might reasonably wonder whether lowering the barrier to acquiring powerful capabilities is, on balance, a democratisation of power or a multiplication of the points at which its misuse could occur \u2014 a tension that the current evidence does not, as yet, allow us to resolve with any confidence.*

The structure presents both sides with *one might reasonably wonder whether* and refuses premature resolution with *does not, as yet, allow us to resolve*. This balanced uncertainty is characteristic of C1 hedging on contested questions.
</details>

**Ejercicio 12.** Traduce al inglés C1: "En la medida en que los modelos actuales exhiben un comportamiento aparentemente racional, sería prematuro concluir que reflejan una comprensión interna del mundo."

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*To the extent that current models exhibit reasoning-like behaviour, it would be premature to conclude that such behaviour reflects an internal model of the world, since the alternative hypothesis \u2014 that the system is performing sophisticated pattern-matching over its training distribution \u2014 remains, on the available evidence, at least as consistent with the observed performance.*

The phrase *reasoning-like* (rather than *reasoning*) itself performs hedging, and *it would be premature to conclude* with *remains at least as consistent* sustains the epistemic caution.
</details>

**Ejercicio 13.** Completa con léxico de IA y privacidad: *inference*, *re-identification*, *data scraping*, *federated learning*.

1. Deducing attributes an individual never explicitly shared is called ______.
2. Recovering the identity behind "anonymised" data is ______.
3. Collecting data from the web without explicit permission is ______.
4. A training method that keeps data on users' devices is ______.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. inference
2. re-identification
3. data scraping
4. federated learning

These terms capture how AI challenges the traditional frame of privacy as control over what one reveals: inference and re-identification show that concealment is no guarantee of privacy, while federated learning represents a technical attempt to reconcile utility with data minimisation.
</details>

**Ejercicio 14.** Reescribe con hedging esta afirmación sobre IA y educación: "AI tutors will definitely improve learning for everyone and have no downsides."

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*While AI tutors could, in principle, democratise access to individualised instruction, there is a plausible risk that the convenience of instant answers may, if uncritically embraced, erode the productive struggle that cognitive science has consistently identified as a precondition for durable learning, which is to say that the net effect on genuine understanding is likely to depend less on the technology itself than on the pedagogical framework within which it is deployed.*

The hedging (*could in principle*, *plausible risk*, *may if uncritically*, *likely to depend*) replaces the certainty with calibrated possibility, and reframes the question from the technology to its deployment context.
</details>

**Ejercicio 15.** Escribe una frase en C1 sobre el coste ambiental de la IA que use hedging para reconocer la incertidumbre sin minimizar el problema.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*Although the precise figures remain a matter of ongoing estimation, the trajectory whereby each leap in model capability has required exponential growth in compute \u2014 and therefore in energy consumption and emissions \u2014 is consistent enough across generations to suggest that the aggregate environmental cost is unlikely to remain negligible relative to the sector's stated climate commitments, however inconvenient that inference may be for the narrative of unqualified progress.*

The hedging (*precise figures remain a matter of ongoing estimation*, *unlikely to remain negligible*) acknowledges uncertainty about exact numbers while sustaining the substantive concern, avoiding both overclaim and dismissal.
</details>

**Ejercicio 16.** Usa conectores de concesión y reserva (*granted*, *that said*, *to be sure*, *with the caveat that*) para equilibrar este argumento: "AI could boost productivity. But the gains won't be shared automatically."

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*Granted, the productivity gains that AI could unlock are, in principle, substantial; that said, the assumption that those gains will automatically translate into broadly shared prosperity presupposes a distributional mechanism that the historical record of previous technological transitions gives us no strong reason to take for granted \u2014 or, to put the same point with the caveat that exceptions may exist, it would be prudent to treat equitable diffusion as a policy achievement to be secured rather than a default outcome to be assumed.*

The connectors orchestrate the hedging: *granted* concedes the premise, *that said* introduces the reservation, and *with the caveat that* qualifies the generalisation. This balance of concession and reserve is the hallmark of C1 argumentation under uncertainty.
</details>

**Ejercicio 17.** Reescribe usando *tentative verbs* (*suggest*, *appear to*, *seem to*, *tend to*): "AI causes algorithmic bias. The evidence proves this."

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The available evidence suggests that algorithmic bias tends to arise when models are trained on data that already encodes systemic disadvantage, though the mechanisms by which it appears to propagate \u2014 from feature selection to proxy variables to feedback loops \u2014 seem to be more varied than a single-cause explanation would imply, which is why treating the phenomenon as proven in one dimension should not be mistaken for having understood it in full.*

The tentative verbs (*suggests*, *tends to*, *appears to*, *seem to*) replace *causes* and *proves*, calibrating the claim to the evidence while preserving its substance.
</details>

**Ejercicio 18.** Escribe un párrafo breve (4-5 frases) en C1 que argumente sobre si la IA actual constituye una amenaza existencial, usando hedging fuerte y al menos tres estructuras avanzadas.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*Whether the current generation of AI systems poses anything that could reasonably be termed an existential risk is a question on which the evidence is, to put it charitably, suggestive rather than conclusive. It is conceivable that sufficiently capable misaligned systems could, in principle, produce outcomes contrary to human interests, and one might reasonably wonder whether the rate of capability gain gives us less time to solve the alignment problem than the optimistic timelines assume. That said, it would be premature to equate the theoretical possibility of such scenarios with their practical likelihood, since the conflation of narrow-AI prowess with the prospect of general intelligence may inflate the threat as readily as it inflates the promise. To be sure, the stakes are high enough that even a low-probability risk arguably warrants serious attention; nevertheless, serious attention is not the same as settled alarm, and distinguishing the two is, in the current climate of discourse, itself a form of intellectual responsibility.*

Advanced structures used: *it is conceivable that*, *one might reasonably wonder whether*, *it would be premature to*. The paragraph models calibrated concern: neither dismissal nor panic, but measured engagement with uncertainty.
</details>

**Ejercicio 19.** Escribe un párrafo breve (4-5 frases) en inglés C1 sobre las implicaciones sociales de la IA, usando al menos cuatro recursos de hedging diferentes y dos conectores de concesión.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The implications of AI for social cohesion are, on the available evidence, more uncertain than either the utopian or the dystopian narrative tends to acknowledge. Granted, the technology could, in principle, widen access to expertise and education; that said, there is a non-trivial risk that its benefits will accrue disproportionately to those already positioned to exploit them, which is to say that the distributional question may prove more consequential than the technological one. To the extent that algorithmic mediation shapes what people see, believe and feel, the subtle erosion of shared informational common ground is a development whose cumulative effects it would be premature to forecast with confidence but imprudent to dismiss. The honest position, then, is neither celebration nor denunciation but a sustained insistence that the trajectory is not fixed \u2014 that it will be shaped, for better or worse, by decisions that are still, at this moment, ours to make.*

Hedging resources: *on the available evidence*, *could in principle*, *non-trivial risk*, *may prove*, *it would be premature to*. Concession connectors: *granted*, *that said*. The paragraph enacts the C1 stance on AI: calibrated uncertainty that nonetheless sustains ethical engagement.
</details>

## Resumen de la unidad

En esta unidad hemos construido el andamiaje lingüístico para hablar de la inteligencia artificial y sus implicaciones sociales con la cautela epistémica que exige el nivel C1. Has aprendido a distinguir *narrow AI* de *AGI*, *machine learning* de *deep learning*, *displacement* de *replacement*, y a manejar el léxico del *alignment problem* \u2014del *specification problem* al *reward hacking*\u2014, del sesgo algorítmico \u2014*disparate impact*, *proxy variables*, *feedback loops*\u2014 y de la gobernanza \u2014*frontier models*, *compute concentration*, *scalable oversight*. Has incorporado el vocabulario de la transparencia \u2014*black box*, *interpretability*, *accountability*\u2014 y el de las implicaciones democráticas \u2014*deepfakes*, *synthetic media*, *microtargeting*\u2014. Has ejercitado el hedging fuerte \u2014*it is conceivable that*, *the evidence tentatively suggests*, *one might reasonably wonder whether*, *it would be premature to conclude*\u2014 como instrumento de precisión epistémica, y los conectores de concesión y reserva \u2014*granted*, *that said*, *to be sure*, *with the caveat that*\u2014 como arquitectura del argumento equilibrado. La destreza central de esta unidad es la matización calibrada: la capacidad de debatir la IA reconociendo tanto su potencial transformador como la profunda incertidumbre que lo envuelve, de afirmar con la fuerza que la evidencia justifica y no más, y de resistir la tentación \u2014tan común en este debate\u2014 de convertir la incertidumbre en certeza en cualquiera de las dos direcciones. Esa elegancia epistémica es la marca distintiva del hablante C1 cuando se enfrenta al debate tecnológico donde las apuestas son más altas y el conocimiento más provisional.
