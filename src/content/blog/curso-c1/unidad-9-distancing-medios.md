# Unidad 9 C1: distancing, medios y clefts avanzadas

En periodismo, comunicación institucional y análisis académico, no siempre podemos presentar una información como un hecho confirmado. A veces existe una fuente anónima, una declaración interesada, un rumor o una interpretación que todavía debe comprobarse. La Unidad 9 C1 enseña a manejar esa distancia mediante **distancing language**, construcciones impersonales y **cleft sentences** avanzadas.

Compara estas frases:

- *The footage was staged.*
- *It is alleged that the footage was staged.*
- *The footage is alleged to have been staged.*
- *The footage, which was circulated online, is supposedly authentic.*

La primera afirma directamente. Las otras atribuyen, distancian o marcan la información como no confirmada. Esa diferencia es esencial: un texto responsable debe dejar claro qué se ha observado, qué se ha dicho y qué se infiere.

Las clefts añaden otra herramienta. En lugar de presentar todos los elementos con el mismo peso, podemos decir:

- *It was the timing that raised questions.*
- *What raised questions was the timing.*
- *The timing was what raised questions.*

El hecho básico es parecido, pero cambia el foco. En una noticia o un ensayo, controlar ese foco ayuda a guiar al lector hacia la información más relevante.

## Parte I — Teoría

### 1. La idea de distancia

La distancia lingüística separa al escritor de una afirmación. No significa necesariamente que la información sea falsa; indica que la fuente, la evidencia o el grado de confirmación deben tenerse en cuenta.

- **Hecho presentado como confirmado:** *The station corrected the report.*
- **Información atribuida:** *The station is reported to have corrected the report.*
- **Rumor o afirmación no verificada:** *The station supposedly corrected the report.*
- **Interpretación cautelosa:** *The correction appears to have followed public pressure.*

En la práctica, un periodista puede distinguir entre lo que sabe directamente y lo que procede de terceros. Un investigador puede distinguir entre el resultado medido y la explicación propuesta. El inglés C1 ofrece recursos precisos para esas diferencias.

### 2. Allegedly, reportedly y supposedly

Estos adverbios se colocan normalmente antes del verbo principal o después de un auxiliar:

- *The company allegedly manipulated the figures.*
- *The company is allegedly manipulating the figures.*
- *The company has reportedly withdrawn the advertisement.*
- *The presenter supposedly knew about the error.*

**Allegedly** suele asociarse con una acusación que no ha sido probada, especialmente en contextos legales o periodísticos. **Reportedly** indica que una fuente o varios medios han informado de algo, sin que el escritor lo confirme personalmente. **Supposedly** puede significar “supuestamente” y a veces transmite escepticismo o ironía.

- *The minister allegedly accepted gifts.* Es una acusación atribuida.
- *The minister reportedly accepted gifts.* Los medios han informado de ello.
- *The minister supposedly accepted gifts.* Se presenta como una versión cuestionable o no comprobada.

No trates estos adverbios como sinónimos perfectos. La elección comunica actitud. Si quieres evitar una insinuación excesivamente negativa, puedes usar *according to the report*, *the available evidence suggests* o *the claim remains unverified*.

### 3. Impersonal passive reporting

Una estructura muy frecuente es:

> **It + be + past participle + that-clause**

- *It is alleged that the footage was staged.*
- *It is believed that the account was hacked.*
- *It has been reported that several witnesses were contacted.*
- *It is widely assumed that the headline was misleading.*

Los verbos más habituales son **say, believe, think, report, claim, allege, assume, expect, understand, know**. El adverbio puede indicar la extensión o la fuente: *widely, generally, officially, reportedly, privately*.

El tiempo del auxiliar sitúa la atribución:

- *It is believed that the policy is changing.*
- *It was believed that the policy would change.*
- *It has been claimed that the figures were altered.*
- *It had been reported that the station would close.*

La estructura impersonal permite evitar una fuente vaga como *people say*. No obstante, si la fuente es importante, nómbrala: *The regulator stated that...* es más informativo que *It was stated that...*.

### 4. Estructura personal con infinitivo

La misma información puede expresarse con un sujeto enfocado y **be + participle + to-infinitive**:

> **Subject + be + past participle + to-infinitive**

- *The dossier is said to contain fabricated evidence.*
- *The presenter is believed to have been misquoted.*
- *The staff are reported to have been briefed with a doctored translation.*
- *The CEO is alleged to have concealed the conflict of interest.*

Usa **to + infinitive** cuando la acción es simultánea o posterior al verbo de reporte:

- *The outlet is believed to be expanding.*
- *The outlet is expected to publish a correction.*

Usa **to have + past participle** para una acción anterior:

- *The outlet is believed to have published a misleading headline.*
- *The outlet is reported to have removed the article.*

Para una situación continua anterior aparece **to have been + -ing**:

- *The team is thought to have been monitoring the account.*

La transformación entre las dos estructuras es mecánica:

- *It is believed that the CEO concealed the conflict.*
- *The CEO is believed to have concealed the conflict.*

No mezcles las formas: *It is believed the CEO to have concealed...* no es el patrón estándar. Elige *it + that-clause* o *subject + passive reporting + infinitive*.

### 5. There is thought to be

Las construcciones impersonales también pueden combinarse con **there**:

- *There is thought to be widespread unease about synthetic media.*
- *There are believed to be several errors in the translation.*
- *There appears to be little consensus on how to label AI-generated images.*

La concordancia depende del sustantivo posterior: *there is thought to be a problem*, pero *there are thought to be several problems*. En estilo periodístico, estas formas permiten presentar la existencia de algo sin atribuir la opinión a una persona concreta.

Con *appear* es posible usar una construcción más directa:

- *There appears to be a conflict of interest.*
- *There appear to be three conflicting accounts.*

No digas *there is appearing to be* en este uso. *Appear* funciona normalmente en simple.

### 6. Distancing con so-called y what is described as

**So-called** puede ser neutral o crítico. Presenta una etiqueta que quizá no aceptamos completamente:

- *The so-called reform failed to change the ownership structure.*
- *The so-called experts had no relevant qualifications.*

**What is described as**, **what has been presented as** y **what was billed as** atribuyen una caracterización:

- *What was described as a technical update became a major redesign.*
- *The event, billed as a public debate, allowed no questions.*
- *The campaign has been presented as a transparency measure.*

Estas estructuras crean distancia entre el escritor y la etiqueta. Si el tono debe ser neutral, puedes usar *the measure described by the ministry as...*. Si quieres señalar discrepancia, *so-called* lo hace de forma más evidente.

### 7. Impersonal constructions en medios

Además de la pasiva de reporte, aparecen estructuras con **one, people, it** y **there**:

- *One tends to assume that information is neutral.*
- *People are known to share headlines without reading them.*
- *It is often difficult to distinguish satire from misinformation.*
- *There is little evidence to support the claim.*

**One** es formal y generalizador, aunque puede sonar distante o rígido. **People** es más natural, pero menos impersonal. **It is** permite presentar una evaluación; **there is/are** presenta existencia o ausencia de evidencia.

Para no generalizar demasiado, usa *tend to, are likely to, may, can sometimes*:

- *Readers may interpret a headline differently depending on context.*
- *Users tend to share emotionally striking content.*

### 8. Cleft sentences para el foco

Una cleft divide una oración en dos partes para destacar un elemento:

> **It is/was + focus + that/who + clause**

- *It was the timing that raised questions.*
- *It was the editor who approved the headline.*
- *It is the lack of context that makes the clip misleading.*
- *It was after the correction that the story gained attention.*

La parte focalizada puede ser una persona, objeto, tiempo, lugar o causa. **Who** se usa habitualmente con personas; **that** puede usarse con objetos y situaciones. En una cleft negativa o interrogativa, el verbo conserva la forma correspondiente:

- *Was it the source that the editor had failed to check?*
- *It was not the headline that caused the dispute.*

La cleft no añade información nueva por sí sola. Cambia la distribución del énfasis y puede contrastar un elemento con otros.

### 9. What-clefts y pseudo-clefts

La estructura **what-cleft** es:

> **What + clause + be + focus**

- *What surprised viewers was the speed of the correction.*
- *What the investigation revealed was a pattern of undisclosed sponsorship.*
- *What matters is whether the evidence can be independently verified.*
- *What the public wants to know is who approved the campaign.*

El verbo dentro de la *what-clause* sigue el orden declarativo: *what the investigation revealed*, no *what did the investigation reveal*. La parte posterior identifica el foco.

También es posible invertir el orden:

- *The speed of the correction was what surprised viewers.*
- *Who approved the campaign is what the public wants to know.*

La versión con *what* al principio suele presentar una pregunta implícita o un tema que se desarrolla. Es útil para organizar una explicación: *What the report does not establish is whether the error was deliberate.*

### 10. The reason why y the thing that

Otras clefts avanzadas son:

- *The reason why the story spread is that it confirmed existing fears.*
- *The reason the story spread was its emotional appeal.*
- *The thing that concerned viewers was the absence of a correction.*
- *The point that the editor was making was that context matters.*

En muchos casos, *the reason why* es correcto, aunque *the reason* puede ser más conciso. Evita mezclar dos estructuras: *The reason why the story spread was because...* aparece en el habla, pero en escritura formal suele preferirse *The reason why the story spread was that...*.

### 11. Clefts frente a inversión y fronting

Estas tres técnicas cambian el foco de maneras distintas:

- **Cleft:** *It was the lack of context that misled readers.* Divide la oración y destaca la causa.
- **What-cleft:** *What misled readers was the lack of context.* Presenta una relación “lo que... fue...”.
- **Fronting:** *The lack of context, the editor failed to address.* Coloca el objeto al inicio, con una estructura marcada.

La cleft suele ser la opción más clara para un ensayo o una presentación. El fronting puede sonar literario o enfático y requiere más control. No uses una técnica marcada simplemente para parecer avanzado: el foco debe mejorar la comunicación.

### 12. Vocabulario de media and information

- **dossier:** expediente o conjunto de documentos.
- **footage:** imágenes grabadas, normalmente de vídeo.
- **eyewitness:** testigo presencial.
- **doctored translation:** traducción manipulada.
- **chyron:** texto superpuesto en pantalla.
- **synthetic media:** contenido generado o alterado digitalmente.
- **misinformation:** información falsa o inexacta sin prueba de intención.
- **disinformation:** información falsa difundida deliberadamente.
- **source verification:** comprobación de una fuente.
- **editorial oversight:** supervisión editorial.
- **conflict of interest:** conflicto de intereses.
- **undisclosed sponsorship:** patrocinio no revelado.
- **media literacy:** alfabetización mediática.
- **public broadcaster:** radiodifusor público.
- **regulatory body:** organismo regulador.
- **correction:** rectificación.
- **retraction:** retirada formal de un artículo o afirmación.
- **public backlash:** reacción pública negativa.
- **clickbait:** contenido diseñado para atraer clics mediante titulares llamativos.
- **fact-checking:** verificación de hechos.

Colocaciones importantes son **verify a source**, **publish a correction**, **issue a retraction**, **distort the context**, **fabricate evidence**, **broadcast footage**, **circulate a claim**, **disclose sponsorship**, **raise questions**, **trigger a backlash**, **challenge a narrative**, **assess credibility** y **combat disinformation**.

### 13. Cómo escribir de forma responsable

Un texto de medios debe distinguir tres capas:

1. **Observación:** *The video was uploaded at 9:14.*
2. **Atribución:** *The account is reported to have removed it later.*
3. **Interpretación:** *The timing may suggest an attempt to limit scrutiny.*

No conviertas la tercera capa en la primera. *The timing proves censorship* es mucho más fuerte que la evidencia descrita. Una redacción responsable puede decir: *The timing appears to have raised questions, although it does not establish deliberate censorship.*

También debes distinguir entre **alleged**, que marca una acusación, y **confirmed**, que afirma que una autoridad o investigación ha verificado algo. *The alleged error* y *the confirmed error* no tienen la misma carga. Si una persona podría resultar dañada por una afirmación, atribuye la información y especifica su estado.

### 14. Errores frecuentes

- *The dossier is said having been compiled.* → **The dossier is said to have been compiled.**
- *It is alleged the footage to be staged.* → **It is alleged that the footage was staged.**
- *There are thought to be a problem.* → **There is thought to be a problem.**
- *Rarely the source is verified.* → Si se busca inversión: **Rarely is the source verified.**
- *What did the report reveal was...* → **What the report revealed was...**
- *It was the editor which approved the headline.* → **It was the editor who approved the headline.**
- *The reason why it spread was because it was emotional.* → En registro formal: **The reason why it spread was that it was emotional.**
- *The so-called verified account* cuando solo quieres informar de su etiqueta oficial. → **The account described as verified** puede ser más neutral.

## Parte II — Ejercicios con soluciones

### Ejercicio 1 — Passive reporting

Completa con **is alleged to have been**, **is alleged having been** o **alleged to be**.

1. The footage ___ staged before the leak.
2. The presenter ___ misquoted in the headline.
3. The account is ___ controlled by an overseas company.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **is alleged to have been** — la puesta en escena es anterior a la atribución.
2. **is alleged to have been** — *misquoted* ocurrió antes del momento actual.
3. **alleged to be** — después de *is* ya aparece la estructura personal; la frase completa es **The account is alleged to be controlled...**.

</details>

### Ejercicio 2 — Impersonal o personal

Transforma.

1. It is believed that the dossier contains fabricated evidence.
2. It was reported that the staff had been briefed with a doctored translation.
3. It is thought that there is little consensus about the label.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **The dossier is believed to contain fabricated evidence.**
2. **The staff were reported to have been briefed with a doctored translation.**
3. **There is thought to be little consensus about the label.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 3 — Tiempo del infinitivo

Elige **to be**, **to have been** o **to have + participle**.

1. The outlet is believed ___ expanding rapidly.
2. The outlet is believed ___ removed the article.
3. The outlet is believed ___ under pressure before the correction.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **to be** — situación simultánea o en desarrollo.
2. **to have removed** — acción anterior.
3. **to have been** — estado anterior.

</details>

### Ejercicio 4 — Adverbios de distancia

Completa con **allegedly, reportedly, supposedly** o **according to the report**.

1. The minister ___ accepted gifts from the company.
2. The broadcaster has ___ withdrawn the programme.
3. The ___ independent review was funded by the organisation itself.
4. ___, several witnesses were contacted before publication.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **allegedly** — acusación no probada.
2. **reportedly** — información difundida por medios o fuentes.
3. **supposedly** — etiqueta que el escritor mira con escepticismo.
4. **According to the report** — atribución explícita a una fuente documental.

</details>

### Ejercicio 5 — There is/are thought to be

Completa con la concordancia correcta.

1. There ___ thought to be a conflict of interest.
2. There ___ believed to be several errors in the translation.
3. There ___ appears to be little evidence for the claim.
4. There ___ appear to be three conflicting accounts.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **is**; 2. **are**; 3. **appears**; 4. **appear**. El verbo concuerda con el sustantivo posterior en las estructuras con *there*.

</details>

### Ejercicio 6 — It-clefts

Reescribe destacando el elemento entre paréntesis.

1. The timing raised questions. (the timing)
2. The editor approved the headline. (the editor)
3. The story gained attention after the correction. (after the correction)
4. The lack of context made the clip misleading. (the lack of context)

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **It was the timing that raised questions.**
2. **It was the editor who approved the headline.**
3. **It was after the correction that the story gained attention.**
4. **It was the lack of context that made the clip misleading.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 7 — What-clefts

Completa con **What** y el orden correcto.

1. ___ surprised viewers ___ the speed of the correction.
2. ___ the investigation revealed ___ a pattern of undisclosed sponsorship.
3. ___ matters ___ whether the evidence can be independently verified.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **What surprised viewers was**.
2. **What the investigation revealed was**.
3. **What matters is**.

La cláusula con *what* mantiene el orden declarativo: *what the investigation revealed*, no *what did the investigation reveal*.

</details>

### Ejercicio 8 — Distancing y tono

Explica qué cambia en el tono.

1. *The footage was staged.*
2. *The footage is alleged to have been staged.*
3. *The footage may have been staged.*
4. *The footage appears to have been staged.*

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. Presenta la afirmación como hecho.
2. Atribuye una acusación a una fuente o contexto externo.
3. Presenta una posibilidad abierta.
4. Presenta una interpretación basada en indicios, con distancia moderada.

</details>

### Ejercicio 9 — Vocabulario

2. Inaccurate content shared without clear intent is ___ .
3. The journal issued a formal ___ after discovering fabricated evidence.
4. A sensational headline designed to attract clicks is ___ .

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **disinformation**; 2. **misinformation**; 3. **retraction**; 4. **clickbait**; 5. **media literacy**.

</details>

### Ejercicio 10 — Reading

Lee y responde.

What surprised viewers was not the speed of the upload but the absence of an immediate correction. There appears to be little consensus about whether the error resulted from editorial oversight or deliberate disinformation. The broadcaster has issued a statement and is believed to be conducting an internal review. As for the audience, media-literacy groups have warned users not to share the clip without checking its context.

1. When was the account reportedly created?
2. What is alleged about the footage?
3. What surprised viewers?
4. What remains uncertain?
5. What has the broadcaster done?

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **Shortly before the video circulated widely.**
2. **It is alleged to have come from an unrelated event.**
3. **The absence of an immediate correction.**
4. **Whether the error resulted from editorial oversight or deliberate disinformation.**
5. **It has issued a statement and is believed to be conducting an internal review.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 11 — Listening guiado

Guion: “The campaign was billed as a public debate, but it supposedly allowed no questions from the audience. It is widely believed that the format was designed to protect the speakers from scrutiny. What the organisers have not explained is why the transcript was published three days late. There are thought to be several discrepancies between the recording and the written version.”

Identifica una expresión de distancia, una impersonal, una what-cleft y una construcción con *there*.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

Distancia: **supposedly** o **was billed as**. Impersonal: **It is widely believed that...**. What-cleft: **What the organisers have not explained is...**. There-construction: **There are thought to be several discrepancies...**.

</details>

1. *It is alleged the video to be manipulated.*
2. *The CEO is believed having concealed the conflict.*
3. *What did the investigation reveal was a sponsorship pattern.*
4. *It was the reporter which found the discrepancy.*

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **It is alleged that the video was manipulated.**
2. **The CEO is believed to have concealed the conflict.**
3. **What the investigation revealed was a sponsorship pattern.**
4. **It was the reporter who found the discrepancy.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 13 — Neutraliza la acusación

Reescribe con un registro periodístico responsable: *The broadcaster deliberately hid the error.*

<details><summary>Modelo</summary>

**The broadcaster is alleged to have withheld information about the error, although the available evidence does not establish whether the omission was deliberate.**

La versión atribuye la acusación y añade el límite de la evidencia.

</details>

### Ejercicio 14 — Writing

Escribe 150–180 palabras sobre: **How should news organisations handle an unverified viral video?** Incluye una impersonal passive, una estructura personal con infinitivo perfecto, una cleft y tres términos de medios.

<details><summary>Modelo</summary>

When an unverified video goes viral, it is essential that news organisations distinguish evidence from speculation. The footage may be authentic, but it may also have been taken out of context or digitally altered. It is often reported that the first account to share a clip has already disappeared; however, this does not prove who created the material. The account is sometimes believed to have circulated the video for political purposes, although that interpretation requires independent verification. What matters most is whether the source, date and location can be confirmed. Editors should therefore delay publication until they have conducted source verification and checked the footage against reliable records. If an error occurs, the organisation should issue a visible correction rather than quietly changing the headline. Media literacy also matters: readers need enough context to recognise clickbait and distinguish misinformation from deliberate disinformation. A cautious process may reduce speed, but it protects editorial credibility and public trust.

</details>

### Ejercicio 15 — Speaking

Habla durante un minuto sobre una noticia controvertida. Usa **allegedly**, **reportedly**, **what matters is** y una cleft con *who* o *that*.

<details><summary>Pista y modelo</summary>

**The company allegedly funded the campaign, although the payment was reportedly made through an intermediary. What matters is whether the documents can be verified. It was the lack of disclosure that raised questions, rather than the campaign’s content itself. The organisation may have breached the rules, but the available evidence is not yet conclusive.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 16 — Traducción controlada

Traduce al inglés.

1. Se cree que el expediente contiene pruebas fabricadas.
2. Lo que sorprendió a los espectadores fue la rapidez de la rectificación.
3. Hay varios errores en la traducción, según se informa.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **The dossier is believed to contain fabricated evidence.**
2. **What surprised viewers was the speed of the correction.**
3. **There are reportedly several errors in the translation.** También: **There are said to be several errors in the translation.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 17 — Edición avanzada

Mejora este párrafo para que sea formal, claro y prudente:

> The so-called independent report proves that the broadcaster lied. The reporter which wrote it is believed having received money. What did the report show was a big conflict.

<details><summary>Modelo</summary>

**The report, described as independent, claims that the broadcaster misrepresented several facts. The reporter who wrote it is alleged to have received undisclosed funding. What the report appears to show is a significant conflict of interest, although further verification is required.**

Se evita presentar las acusaciones como hechos confirmados, se corrigen *which* y el infinitivo, y se forma correctamente la what-cleft.

</details>

### Ejercicio 18 — Mini-test final

Completa el texto.

> The footage ___ ___ ___ have been edited before publication. ___ is alleged that the original file was shorter. ___ surprised investigators was the unexplained delay in the correction. There ___ thought to be several discrepancies, ___ the broadcaster maintains that the error was accidental.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

**is alleged to; It; What; are; although.** La versión completa es: **The footage is alleged to have been edited before publication. It is alleged that the original file was shorter. What surprised investigators was the unexplained delay in the correction. There are thought to be several discrepancies, although the broadcaster maintains that the error was accidental.**

</details>

## Resumen de la unidad

Usa **allegedly, reportedly, supposedly, it is claimed** y las estructuras con **is said/believed/reported to** para marcar que una información procede de una fuente o no está completamente confirmada. Si la acción es anterior, utiliza **to have + participio**; si la situación es simultánea, usa **to be** o **to be + -ing**.

Las construcciones con **there is/are thought to be** presentan la existencia de una opinión, problema o discrepancia. Las clefts con **it was X that/who** y **what... was...** reorganizan el foco y ayudan a responder preguntas implícitas: qué ocurrió, quién lo hizo, cuándo sucedió o qué aspecto resulta relevante.

En medios e información, la gramática debe ir unida a la responsabilidad. Distingue hechos, atribuciones e interpretaciones; no conviertas una alegación en una certeza. Cuando combines esa precisión con colocaciones como *verify a source*, *issue a correction*, *assess credibility* y *combat disinformation*, podrás escribir textos C1 claros, matizados y fiables.
