## Parte II — Ejercicios

**Ejercicio 1.** Completa con la posición filosófica correcta: *compatibilism*, *hard determinism*, *libertarianism*, *incompatibilism*.

1. The thesis that free will and determinism are compatible is ______.
2. The thesis that determinism is true and free will is therefore an illusion is ______.
3. The thesis that free will exists and determinism is therefore false is ______.
4. The thesis that free will and determinism cannot both be true is ______.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. compatibilism
2. hard determinism
3. libertarianism (philosophical, not political)
4. incompatibilism

Note that *incompatibilism* is the broader category: it holds that free will and determinism are incompatible, and both *hard determinism* (which accepts determinism and rejects free will) and *libertarianism* (which accepts free will and rejects determinism) are species of it. The compatibilist is the one who denies incompatibilism.
</details>

**Ejercicio 2.** Completa el argumento con el conector lógico correcto: *it follows that*, *from which it follows*, *the corollary is that*, *this entails*.

1. If every action is determined by prior causes, ______ the agent could not have done otherwise.
2. The agent could not have done otherwise, ______, in turn, that responsibility is without foundation.
3. Responsibility is without foundation; ______ the institutions of blame would need reconstruction.
4. Determinism removes alternate possibilities, ______ that moral desert is undermined.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. it follows that
2. from which it follows
3. the corollary is that
4. this entails

These connectors make the logical chain explicit: *it follows that* moves from premise to conclusion, *from which it follows* chains conclusions, *the corollary is that* names a necessary consequence, and *this entails* signals entailment. Each does different work in displaying the inference structure.
</details>

**Ejercicio 3.** Reescribe esta afirmación simple como un argumento C1 con conectores lógicos explícitos: "If everything is determined, we are not free. So we are not responsible."

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*If, as the hard determinist contends, every action is the inevitable consequence of prior causes that the agent neither authored nor controlled, it follows that the agent could not have done otherwise, from which it follows, in turn, that the conventional attribution of moral responsibility \u2014 which presupposes that very ability \u2014 rests on a capacity that the deterministic framework rules out by definition, the corollary being that the institutions of blame and punishment would require reconstruction on grounds other than desert.*

The rewrite replaces the flat conditional with a chained argument: *it follows that... from which it follows, in turn... the corollary being that*. Each step is made explicit, and the intermediate premise (responsibility presupposes the ability to do otherwise) is supplied, turning a slogan into a structured inference.
</details>

**Ejercicio 4.** Traduce al inglés C1 con conectores de concesión y refutación: "Concedido que el determinismo elimina la capacidad absoluta de actuar de otro modo, el compatibilista replicará que la libertad relevante es la ausencia de coacción, no la violación de la ley causal."

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*Granted that the deterministic premise removes the ability to do otherwise in the absolute sense, the compatibilist will counter that the kind of freedom moral responsibility requires is not the ability to have acted contrary to causal law but the ability to act in accordance with one's own motivations without external coercion, a reformulation that, if accepted, dissolves the incompatibilist inference without denying the truth of determinism.*

Key connectors: *granted that* (concession), *the compatibilist will counter that* (reply), *a reformulation that, if accepted, dissolves...* (the strategic effect). The sentence models the concessive-refutative structure that philosophical argument requires: concede the premise, then reframe to dissolve the inference.
</details>

**Ejercicio 5.** Reescribe usando un condicional hipotético (*were it the case that* / *assuming that*): "If compatibilism is true, then the incompatibilist's argument must rest on a stronger claim."

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*Were it the case that the truth of determinism is logically compatible with the kind of freedom that responsibility requires \u2014 as the compatibilist maintains \u2014 then the incompatibilist's case would have to rest not on the mere truth of determinism but on the stronger claim that determinism is incompatible with freedom, a claim whose acceptance would, in turn, require the libertarian to demonstrate that free will is not merely possible but actual.*

The inversion *were it the case that... then... would* is a hallmark of C1 hypothetical reasoning: it marks the conditional as a counterfactual exploration rather than a simple causal if-then, and the modal *would* throughout sustains the subjunctive register. The structure isolates the logical variables: if compatibilism holds, the burden shifts.
</details>

**Ejercicio 6.** Completa con léxico del consequence argument: *transfer of powerlessness*, *the past is fixed*, *the laws of nature are fixed*, *inevitable*.

1. No one has power over the facts of the past, for ______.
2. No one has power over the laws of nature, for ______.
3. If the present follows necessarily from past and laws, then the present is ______.
4. The argument proceeds by a ______: from powerlessness over the inputs to powerlessness over the output.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. the past is fixed
2. the laws of nature are fixed
3. inevitable
4. transfer of powerlessness

The consequence argument's force lies in the *transfer of powerlessness*: if you have no power over the past and no power over the laws, and the present follows necessarily from both, then you have no power over the present \u2014 including your own actions. The transfer is what makes the conclusion follow from the premises.
</details>

**Ejercicio 7.** Escribe una frase en C1 que presente el problema de la suerte contra el libertarismo, usando conectores lógicos.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The libertarian who posits that the agent could have done otherwise in the very same circumstances faces the problem of luck: if nothing in the prior state of the agent or the world settles whether A or B is chosen, then the outcome is, from the agent's perspective, a matter of luck, which is to say that the very indeterminism that was meant to secure freedom has reintroduced it in a form \u2014 randomness \u2014 that is the antithesis of the control that responsibility would seem to require.*

The logical structure: the libertarian's premise (could have done otherwise in identical circumstances) leads, via *if nothing... settles... then... is a matter of luck*, to a consequence (*which is to say that...*) that contradicts the libertarian's own requirement (control). The argument turns the libertarian's resource (indeterminism) into a liability (randomness).
</details>

**Ejercicio 8.** Reescribe con abstracción filosófica y precisión conceptual: "We are free if we do what we want and nobody forces us."

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The compatibilist's reformulation of freedom \u2014 from the libertarian's ability to have done otherwise to the ability to act in accordance with one's own motivations, uncoerced and responsive to reasons \u2014 is not, as the incompatibilist often charges, a capitulation dressed as a definition, but a substantive claim that the kind of freedom we actually value and the kind that moral responsibility presupposes are closer to the latter than to the former, a claim whose acceptance would dissolve the incompatibilist inference at the level of the premise rather than the conclusion.*

The rewrite abstracts from the everyday formulation (*do what we want, nobody forces us*) to the conceptual vocabulary (*act in accordance with one's own motivations, uncoerced and responsive to reasons*), and embeds it in the debate by anticipating and rebutting the incompatibilist's charge of capitulation. The everyday claim becomes a philosophical thesis with a strategic function.
</details>

**Ejercicio 9.** Completa con léxico de la autoría y el yo: *sourcehood*, *ultimate source*, *identification with*, *alienation from*.

1. The condition that the agent be the origin of her actions is the ______ condition.
2. The incompatibilist demands that the agent be the ______ of her actions, tracing back no further.
3. The compatibilist holds that ______ one's motives suffices for responsibility.
4. ______ one's desires, by contrast, undermines the attribution of the action to the self.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. sourcehood
2. ultimate source
3. identification with
4. alienation from

The sourcehood debate turns on whether the agent must be the *ultimate* source (incompatibilist demand) or only the *proximate* source via identification (compatibilist offer). Frankfurt's hierarchy of desires \u2014 identification versus alienation \u2014 provides the compatibilist's framework for distinguishing the agent's true self from impulses she disowns.
</details>

**Ejercicio 10.** Reescribe esta afirmación sobre la neurociencia con la cautela inferencial de un C1: "Brain scans show our decisions are made before we know. So free will is fake."

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The appeal to Libet's readiness potential as evidence against free will rests on a contentious inference: from the finding that an unconscious brain signal precedes the conscious awareness of the decision to act, it does not follow that the decision was not the agent's, for the move from a neural precursor to a denial of agency requires bridging the gap between the timing of an urge and the attribution of a deliberated choice, a gap that the experiment, on most interpretations, does not close.*

The C1 move: *rests on a contentious inference*, *it does not follow that*, *requires bridging the gap between... and...*. The rewrite distinguishes what the experiment shows (a neural precursor precedes awareness) from what it would need to show to refute free will (that the decision was not the agent's), and argues that the inference between them is not warranted by the data alone.
</details>

**Ejercicio 11.** Escribe una frase en C1 que explique por qué la indeterminación cuántica no rescata el libre albedrío libertario, usando conectores lógicos.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The invocation of quantum indeterminacy as a liberation from determinism is, on examination, of limited use to the libertarian, for the kind of freedom that moral responsibility would seem to require is not the absence of determinism but the presence of control, and the replacement of deterministic laws with probabilistic ones introduces randomness rather than agency, a substitution that, if anything, exacerbates the problem of luck rather than solving it.*

The logical structure: *for... is not... but...* (reframes the requirement), *and the replacement... introduces X rather than Y* (shows the substitution fails), *a substitution that... exacerbates... rather than solving* (shows it makes things worse). The argument is that indeterminacy swaps one problem (determinism) for a worse one (randomness), since freedom requires control, not merely the absence of determination.
</details>

**Ejercicio 12.** Traduce al inglés C1 con abstracción: "La noción de que el yo, en cuanto agente, es fuente de sus acciones es desafiada por el determinismo, no negando que los agentes actúan, sino negando que sean el origen último."

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The notion that the self is, qua agent, the source of its actions in a sense strong enough to ground responsibility is one that the deterministic framework challenges not by denying that agents act but by denying that they are the ultimate origin of the springs of their action, a distinction between proximate and ultimate sourcehood that the compatibilist exploits and the incompatibilist insists is disqualifying.*

Key abstracting vocabulary: *qua agent*, *in a sense strong enough to ground*, *the springs of their action*, *proximate and ultimate sourcehood*. The structure *challenges not by denying X but by denying Y* isolates precisely what the deterministic challenge denies, which is not agency as such but ultimate sourcehood \u2014 the distinction on which the whole debate turns.
</details>

**Ejercicio 13.** Completa con conectores de reformulación: *more precisely*, *that is to say*, *to be clear*, *in the relevant sense*.

1. The claim, ______, is not that freedom and determinism are compatible in every sense.
2. They are compatible ______ that moral responsibility requires.
3. ______, the compatibilist narrows the claim to avoid equivocation.
4. The freedom at stake is, ______, the absence of coercion, not the violation of causal law.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. more precisely
2. in the relevant sense
3. to be clear
4. that is to say (or in other words)

These reformulators do precision work: *more precisely* narrows a claim, *in the relevant sense* scopes it, *to be clear* preempts a misreading, and *that is to say* unpacks an abstraction. In philosophical argument they are not ornament but necessity, for the debates often turn on whether a term is being used in the same sense by both sides.
</details>

**Ejercicio 14.** Reescribe esta afirmación sobre el castigo con la abstracción de un C1: "If we don't have free will, we can't punish people for revenge."

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The practical question of whether the institutions of blame and punishment would survive the acceptance of hard determinism is one that Strawson's appeal to the participant stance addresses by arguing that our reactive attitudes \u2014 resentment, gratitude, forgiveness \u2014 are not, in practice, hostage to the truth of determinism, for they are constitutive of interpersonal life in a way that a theoretical conviction, however well-argued, is unlikely to dislodge, which is to say that even if the retributive justification of punishment falters, the practices it rationalised may persist on the independent ground of the reactive attitudes that sustain them.*

The rewrite abstracts from *punish people for revenge* to *the institutions of blame and punishment* and *the retributive justification*, introduces Strawson's framework (*participant stance*, *reactive attitudes*), and argues that the practices may survive the collapse of their retributive rationale by resting on a different, non-theoretical ground. The everyday claim becomes a philosophical position embedded in the literature.
</details>

**Ejercicio 15.** Escribe una frase en C1 que articule el desafío de Frankfurt al principio de posibilidades alternativas, usando conectores lógicos.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*Frankfurt's challenge to the principle of alternate possibilities \u2014 through cases in which the agent could not have done otherwise yet seems morally responsible \u2014 if successful, would detach moral responsibility from the ability to do otherwise, which would, in turn, undercut the incompatibilist's central premise without requiring the compatibilist to deny the truth of determinism, a result whose strategic importance for the compatibilist case is difficult to overstate, for it would dissolve the inference at the level of the premise rather than contesting the truth of the conclusion.*

The logical structure: Frankfurt's cases *if successful would detach* responsibility from alternate possibilities, *which would in turn undercut* the incompatibilist premise, *without requiring* the compatibilist to deny determinism, *for it would dissolve the inference at the level of the premise*. The argument shows that Frankfurt's cases, if they work, let the compatibilist win on a logical rather than an empirical point.
</details>

**Ejercicio 16.** Usa la estructura cohesiva *the argument proceeds as follows: given that... it follows that... yet the reply is that... to which the counter is... the upshot being that* para articular el debate entre incompatibilismo y compatibilismo.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The argument proceeds as follows: given that every action is determined by prior causes, and granted that responsibility requires the ability to do otherwise, it follows that responsibility is incompatible with determinism \u2014 yet the compatibilist, on this view, will reply that the second premise is false, to which the incompatibilist's counter is that redefining freedom to fit determinism is not to solve the problem but to change the subject, the upshot being that the debate turns less on the truth of determinism than on the analysis of the concept of freedom itself.*

The cohesive structure displays the full architecture of the exchange: *the argument proceeds as follows* opens, *given that... granted that... it follows that* lays out the incompatibilist inference, *yet the compatibilist will reply* introduces the rebuttal, *to which the incompatibilist's counter is* introduces the counter-rebuttal, and *the upshot being that* delivers the synthesising conclusion. The sentence is a miniature of the debate in one coherent movement.
</details>

**Ejercicio 17.** Reescribe esta afirmación categórica con la cautela y la abstracción de un C1: "Neuroscience has proven we have no free will."

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*While certain neuroscientific findings \u2014 most notably the Libet-style experiments on the readiness potential \u2014 have been invoked as evidence against free will, the inference from an unconscious neural precursor to the denial of agency is, on examination, more contentious than the popular framing suggests, for it requires bridging the gap between the timing of an urge and the attribution of a deliberated choice, a gap that the experimental data, on most interpretations, do not close, which is to say that neuroscience has, at most, complicated rather than refuted the case for free will.*

The C1 moves: *while certain findings... have been invoked as evidence* (grounds the claim in the literature rather than assertion), *the inference... is more contentious than the popular framing suggests* (challenges the inference, not the data), *it requires bridging the gap between... and...* (identifies the logical gap), *which is to say that... has complicated rather than refuted* (calibrates the conclusion). The rewrite replaces proof-claim with calibrated epistemic assessment.
</details>

**Ejercicio 18.** Escribe un párrafo breve (4-5 frases) en C1 que presente el núcleo del debate entre compatibilismo e incompatibilismo, usando al menos cuatro conectores lógicos y abstracción conceptual.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The debate between compatibilism and incompatibilism turns, in the final analysis, less on the truth of determinism than on the analysis of the concept of freedom that moral responsibility is taken to require. Granted that determinism, if true, removes the ability to have done otherwise in the absolute sense, it follows that the incompatibilist's case is secure if and only if that ability is a necessary condition of responsibility \u2014 a conditional that places the burden squarely on the analysis of freedom itself. The compatibilist, on this view, will reply that the kind of freedom we value and the kind that responsibility presupposes are better captured by the absence of coercion and the presence of reasons-responsiveness than by the libertarian's ability to have acted contrary to causal law, a reformulation that, if accepted, dissolves the incompatibilist inference at the level of the premise. To which the incompatibilist's counter is that such a reformulation, however practically appealing, changes the subject rather than solving the problem, for the question was whether we are the ultimate sources of our actions, not merely whether we act without interference. The upshot, then, is that the dispute is unlikely to be settled by empirical findings about the brain, for it is, at bottom, a conceptual dispute about what we mean by freedom \u2014 and about whether the meaning we settle on is one that the deterministic framework, if true, can accommodate.*

Logical connectors: *in the final analysis*, *granted that... it follows that... if and only if*, *on this view... will reply that*, *to which the counter is*, *the upshot, then, is that*. Conceptual abstraction: *analysis of the concept of freedom*, *necessary condition of responsibility*, *reasons-responsiveness*, *ultimate sources*, *conceptual dispute*. The paragraph enacts the C1 capacity to display an argument's logical architecture while sustaining the conceptual abstraction the subject demands.
</details>

**Ejercicio 19.** Escribe un párrafo breve (4-5 frases) en inglés C1 que argumente sobre las implicaciones prácticas del debate (derecho, castigo, ética), usando conectores lógicos y abstracción filosófica.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*If the incompatibilist's inference is sound and determinism does, in fact, rule out the kind of freedom that moral desert presupposes, it follows that the retributive justification of punishment \u2014 the one that rests on the agent's deserving blame for what she could have avoided doing \u2014 is, strictly speaking, without foundation, however inconvenient that conclusion may be for the institutions it underwrites. That said, the collapse of the retributive rationale does not, by implication, entail the collapse of punishment as such, for the consequentialist justification \u2014 which rests on deterrence, rehabilitation and protection rather than desert \u2014 remains available to ground the practice on grounds that the deterministic premise does not undermine. Granted that this reconceptualisation asks the legal system to surrender the intuition that blame is deserved, yet the Strawsonian point that our reactive attitudes are constitutive of interpersonal life suggests that the practical persistence of blame may survive the theoretical collapse of its strongest justification, for what people actually do in holding one another responsible is not, in the main, hostage to the metaphysics their philosophers settle. The upshot, then, is not that hard determinism abolishes responsibility but that it relocates its foundation \u2014 from the backward-looking desert of the retributive frame to the forward-looking purposes of the consequentialist one \u2014 a relocation that is, admittedly, a larger intellectual adjustment than the everyday practice of blame is likely to register, but one whose coherence the philosophical argument, if accepted, would require. The honest position, in the final analysis, is that the practical question is not whether we can go on holding people responsible \u2014 for in all likelihood we will \u2014 but whether the framework we offer to justify that practice is one that can survive the truth, if truth it be, of the deterministic premise on which the incompatibilist's inference rests.*

Logical connectors: *if... it follows that*, *that said*, *by implication, does not entail*, *granted that... yet*, *the upshot, then, is that*, *in the final analysis*. Conceptual abstraction: *moral desert*, *retributive justification*, *consequentialist justification*, *reactive attitudes*, *backward-looking vs forward-looking*. The paragraph moves from inference to practical consequence to Strawsonian qualification to relocation of foundation to honest conclusion \u2014 the full arc of a C1 argument that integrates logical rigour with philosophical abstraction and practical implication.
</details>

## Resumen de la unidad

En esta unidad hemos construido el andamiaje lingüístico para argumentar sobre el libre albedrío y el determinismo con la abstracción conceptual y la precisión lógica que exige el nivel C1. Has aprendido a distinguir *compatibilism* de *incompatibilism*, *hard determinism* de *libertarianism*, y a manejar el léxico de la filosofía de la acción \u2014*agency*, *autonomy*, *sourcehood*, *moral desert*, *reasons-responsiveness*\u2014, el del *consequence argument* \u2014*transfer of powerlessness*, *the past is fixed*, *inevitable*\u2014 y el del *problem of luck* \u2014*indeterminism*, *randomness*, *agent causation*. Has incorporado los conectores lógicos de implicación \u2014*it follows that*, *this entails*, *the corollary is that*, *from which it follows*\u2014, los de concesión y refutación \u2014*granted that*, *the reply is that*, *to which the counter is*\u2014 y los de reformulación \u2014*more precisely*, *in the relevant sense*, *that is to say*, *qua*. Has ejercitado la abstracción filosófica \u2014*the notion that*, *the thesis that*, *in a sense strong enough to ground*\u2014, la cautela inferencial ante la evidencia neurocientífica, y la articulación de las implicaciones prácticas del debate para el derecho y la ética. La destreza central de esta unidad es el razonamiento abstracto transparente: la capacidad de construir cadenas argumentales donde cada conector señala al lector la relación lógica entre las ideas, de manejar el lenguaje conceptual con la precisión que evita la equivocación, y de sostener la tensión entre posiciones sin colapsar el debate en una falsa resolución. Esa elegancia lógica es la marca distintiva del hablante C1 cuando se enfrenta a uno de los debates donde la claridad del lenguaje no es un adorno sino la condición misma de la inteligibilidad del argumento.
