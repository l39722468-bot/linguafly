El debate sobre el libre albedrío y el determinismo es uno de los territorios más exigentes del discurso filosófico, y argumentar en él con propiedad exige una destreza doble: el manejo del lenguaje abstracto \u2014*agency*, *autonomy*, *moral responsibility*, *the self*\u2014 y el dominio de los conectores lógicos que articulan las relaciones de implicación entre las ideas. No basta con decir que *people can choose* o que *everything is determined*; hay que distinguir *compatibilism* de *incompatibilism*, *hard determinism* de *libertarianism*, y construir cadenas argumentales donde cada paso se siga lógicamente del anterior \u2014*it follows that*, *this entails*, *by implication*, *the corollary is that*. En esta unidad del curso de inglés C1 gratis exploramos cómo los hablantes avanzados manejan el léxico de la filosofía de la acción, cómo usan conectores lógicos con la precisión que el razonamiento abstracto demanda y cómo argumentan sobre una de las cuestiones más antiguas de la filosofía con el rigor que su dificultad merece. Es un terreno donde el lenguaje debe transparentar la lógica: cada conector no decora, sino que señala al lector exactamente cómo se relacionan las premisas con la conclusión.

Para situar el nivel, observa cómo el mismo debate se expresa en tres niveles. En **A2** diríamos: *People can choose what to do. But maybe everything is decided before. It is difficult to know.* Es claro pero plano, sin vocabulario filosófico ni estructura lógica. En **B2** subiríamos un peldaño: *Some philosophers think we have free will. Others think that everything is determined by causes. There is a debate about whether these two ideas are compatible.* Aquí ya hay posiciones y debate, pero le falta densidad conceptual y conexión lógica explícita. En **C1** alcanzamos la precisión necesaria: *If, as the hard determinist contends, every action is the inevitable consequence of prior causes that the agent neither authored nor controlled, it follows that the conventional attribution of moral responsibility \u2014 which presupposes that the agent could have done otherwise \u2014 rests on a capacity that the deterministic framework rules out by definition, a conclusion that, whatever its practical inconvenience for the institutions of blame and punishment, is one that the compatibilist must rebut not by denying the logic of the inference but by contesting the premise from which it is derived, namely that the ability to do otherwise is a necessary condition of moral responsibility.* Esta unidad te enseña a producir ese tercer nivel de forma natural.

## Parte I — Teoría

### 1. Léxico fundamental del debate

El debate sobre el libre albedrío se articula en torno a un léxico fundamental: *free will* (libre albedrío), *determinism* (determinismo), *compatibilism* (compatibilismo, la tesis de que libre albedrío y determinismo son compatibles), *incompatibilism* (incompatibilismo, la tesis de que no lo son), *hard determinism* (determinismo duro, no hay libre albedrío porque todo está determinado), *libertarianism* (libertarismo filosófico, hay libre albedrío, luego el determinismo es falso), *agency* (agencia, capacidad de actuar), *autonomy* (autonomía), *moral responsibility* (responsabilidad moral). Un hablante C1 escribe *the taxonomy of positions in the free will debate \u2014 compatibilism, hard determinism and libertarianism \u2014 turns on two questions that are logically distinct but often conflated: whether determinism is true, and whether the truth of determinism would be incompatible with the kind of freedom that moral responsibility requires, a conflation that much of the philosophical literature exists precisely to disentangle.* Distinguir estas posiciones con precisión es la condición previa de cualquier argumento serio.

### 2. Conectores lógicos de implicación

Los conectores de implicación articulan cómo una premisa conduce a una conclusión: *it follows that* (se sigue que), *this entails* (esto entraña), *by implication* (por implicación), *the corollary is that* (la consecuencia lógica es que), *which means that* (lo que significa que), *from which it follows* (de lo que se sigue), *a necessary consequence* (una consecuencia necesaria), *logically, then* (lógicamente, entonces). Un hablante C1 escribe *if every action is causally determined by prior events over which the agent had no control, it follows that the agent could not have done otherwise, from which it follows, in turn, that the attribution of responsibility \u2014 which presupposes that very ability \u2014 is, on the deterministic premise, without foundation, the corollary being that the institutions of blame and punishment would require reconstruction on grounds other than desert.* Estos conectores hacen explícita la cadena lógica que el argumento recorre.

### 3. Conectores de concesión y refutación

La argumentación filosófica requiere concesiones y refutaciones explícitas: *granted that* (concedido que), *admittedly* (hay que admitirlo), *to be sure* (ciertamente), *that said* (dicho esto), *yet* (sin embargo), *nevertheless* (no obstante), *the objection runs that* (la objeción sostiene que), *the reply is that* (la respuesta es que), *the compatibilist will counter* (el compatibilista replicará). Un hablante C1 escribe *granted that the deterministic premise removes the ability to do otherwise in the absolute sense, the compatibilist will counter that the kind of freedom moral responsibility requires is not the ability to have acted contrary to causal law but the ability to act in accordance with one's own motivations without external coercion, a reformulation that, if accepted, dissolves the incompatibilist inference without denying the truth of determinism.* La concesión y la réplica estructuran el debate entre posiciones.

### 4. Conectores condicionales y hipotéticos

El razonamiento filosófico emplea estructuras condicionales: *if... then* (si... entonces), *supposing that* (suponiendo que), *were it the case that* (si fuera el caso que), *assuming that* (asumiendo que), *on the hypothesis that* (sobre la hipótesis de que), *in the event that* (en el caso de que), *only if* (solo si), *if and only if* (si y solo si). Un hablante C1 escribe *were it the case that the truth of determinism is logically compatible with the kind of freedom that responsibility requires \u2014 as the compatibilist maintains \u2014 then the incompatibilist's case would have to rest not on the mere truth of determinism but on the stronger claim that determinism is incompatible with freedom, a claim whose acceptance would, in turn, require the libertarian to demonstrate that free will is not merely possible but actual, a burden that the problem of luck makes notoriously difficult to discharge.* Los condicionales no solo hipotetizan: aislan las variables lógicas del argumento.

### 5. El argumento del consecuente

El *consequence argument* (argumento del consecuente) de van Inwagen es uno de los argumentos centrales contra el compatibilismo: su léxico incluye *inevitable* (inevitable), *no one has power over* (nadie tiene poder sobre), *the past is fixed* (el pasado es fijo), *the laws of nature are fixed* (las leyes de la naturaleza son fijas), *transfer of powerlessness* (transferencia de impotencia), *could not have done otherwise* (no podría haber actuado de otra manera). Un hablante C1 escribe *the consequence argument proceeds by a transfer of powerlessness: if no one has power over the facts of the past and no one has power over the laws of nature, and if the present follows necessarily from the past and the laws, then no one has power over the facts of the present, which includes one's own actions \u2014 from which it follows, by the logic of the transfer, that the ability to do otherwise, on which the incompatibilist insists, is one that determinism rules out by structure rather than by contingent fact.* La transferencia de impotencia es el motor lógico del argumento.

### 6. Compatibilismo: libertad como ausencia de coacción

El compatibilismo redefine el libre albedrío: *freedom as absence of coercion* (libertad como ausencia de coacción), *acting in accordance with one's will* (actuar conforme a la propia voluntad), *second-order desires* (deseos de segundo orden, de Frankfurt), *hierarchical motivation* (motivación jerárquica), *reasons-responsiveness* (respuesta a razones), *mesh theory* (teoría del encaje), *sourcehood* (ser fuente de la acción). Un hablante C1 escribe *the compatibilist's reformulation of freedom \u2014 from the libertarian's ability to have done otherwise to the ability to act in accordance with one's own motivations, uncoerced and responsive to reasons \u2014 is not, as the incompatibilist often charges, a capitulation dressed as a definition, but a substantive claim that the kind of freedom we actually value and the kind that moral responsibility presupposes are closer to the latter than to the former, a claim whose acceptance would dissolve the incompatibilist inference at the level of the premise rather than the conclusion.* La redefinición es una maniobra filosófica sustantiva, no verbal.

### 7. El problema de la suerte (problem of luck)

El *problem of luck* (problema de la suerte) ataca al libertarismo: si en una situación idéntica el agente podría elegir A o B, entonces cuál elige es cuestión de suerte, lo que socava el control que el libre albedrío exige. Léxico: *indeterminism* (indeterminismo), *randomness* (azar), *agent causation* (causación del agente), *event causation* (causación de eventos), *control condition* (condición de control), *explanation gap* (brecha explicativa), *eliminative worry* (preocupación eliminativista). Un hablante C1 escribe *the libertarian who posits that the agent could have done otherwise in the very same circumstances faces the problem of luck: if nothing in the prior state of the agent or the world settles whether A or B is chosen, then the outcome is, from the agent's perspective, a matter of luck, which is to say that the very indeterminism that was meant to secure freedom has reintroduced it in a form \u2014 randomness \u2014 that is the antithesis of the control that responsibility would seem to require.* El problema de la suerte es el dilema interno del libertarismo.

### 8. Responsabilidad moral y el principio de "could have done otherwise"

El principio de que la responsabilidad moral requiere que el agente *could have done otherwise* (podría haber actuado de otra manera) es central: *principle of alternate possibilities* (principio de posibilidades alternativas, PAP), *moral desert* (mérito moral), *backward-looking justification* (justificación retrospectiva), *forward-looking justification* (justificación prospectiva), *retributivism* (retribucionismo), *consequentialism* (consecuencialismo). Un hablante C1 escribe *Frankfurt's challenge to the principle of alternate possibilities \u2014 through cases in which the agent could not have done otherwise yet seems morally responsible \u2014 if successful, would detach moral responsibility from the ability to do otherwise, which would, in turn, undercut the incompatibilist's central premise without requiring the compatibilist to deny the truth of determinism, a result whose strategic importance for the compatibilist case is difficult to overstate.* El ataque al PAP es estratégicamente decisivo para el debate.

### 9. El yo y la autoría de las acciones

La cuestión de la *authorship* (autoría) y el *self* (yo): *source of one's actions* (fuente de las propias acciones), *ultimate source* (fuente última), *origination* (originación), *self-constitution* (autoconstitución), *identification with one's motives* (identificación con los propios motivos), *alienation from one's desires* (alienación respecto de los propios deseos), *volitional necessity* (necesidad volicional). Un hablante C1 escribe *the question of whether the agent is the source of her actions in a sense strong enough to ground responsibility \u2014 the sourcehood condition \u2014 is one that the determinist answers negatively, on the grounds that the chain of causes traces back beyond the agent, and the compatibilist answers affirmatively, on the grounds that sourcehood need not be ultimate but only proximate, provided the agent identifies with the motives from which she acts and is not alienated from them.* La autoría es el terreno donde se libra la batalla entre las posiciones.

### 10. Neurociencia y libre albedrío

La neurociencia aporta evidencia al debate: *Libet experiments* (experimentos de Libet), *readiness potential* (potencial de preparación), *unconscious precursors* (precursores inconscientes), *veto power* (poder de veto), *neural determinism* (determinismo neural), *empirical challenge* (desafío empírico), *neuroscientific evidence* (evidencia neurocientífica). Un hablante C1 escribe *the appeal to Libet's readiness potential as evidence against free will rests on a contentious inference: from the finding that an unconscious brain signal precedes the conscious awareness of the decision to act, it does not follow that the decision was not the agent's, for the move from a neural precursor to a denial of agency requires bridging the gap between the timing of an urge and the attribution of a deliberated choice, a gap that the experiment, on most interpretations, does not close.* La inferencia desde los datos neurocientíficos a la conclusión filosófica es controvertida.

### 11. Determinismo físico y cuántico

El debate sobre el determinismo físico: *classical determinism* (determinismo clásico), *quantum indeterminacy* (indeterminación cuántica), *probabilistic laws* (leyes probabilísticas), *randomness is not freedom* (el azar no es libertad), *macro-level determinism* (determinismo a nivel macro), *chaos theory* (teoría del caos), *epistemic vs ontic determinism* (determinismo epistémico vs óntico). Un hablante C1 escribe *the invocation of quantum indeterminacy as a liberation from determinism is, on examination, of limited use to the libertarian, for the kind of freedom that moral responsibility would seem to require is not the absence of determinism but the presence of control, and the replacement of deterministic laws with probabilistic ones introduces randomness rather than agency, a substitution that, if anything, exacerbates the problem of luck rather than solving it.* La indeterminación cuántica no rescata el libre albedrío libertario.

### 12. Abstracción y lenguaje conceptual

La abstracción exige un lenguaje conceptual preciso: *the concept of* (el concepto de), *the notion that* (la noción de que), *the thesis that* (la tesis de que), *the framework within which* (el marco dentro del cual), *the conditions for* (las condiciones para), *in the relevant sense* (en el sentido relevante), *as such* (como tal), *qua* (en cuanto). Un hablante C1 escribe *the notion that the self is, qua agent, the source of its actions in a sense strong enough to ground responsibility is one that the deterministic framework challenges not by denying that agents act but by denying that they are the ultimate origin of the springs of their action, a distinction between proximate and ultimate sourcehood that the compatibilist exploits and the incompatibilist insists is disqualifying.* El lenguaje conceptual (*qua*, *in the relevant sense*, *as such*) permite la precisión que la abstracción exige.

### 13. Conectores de reformulación y clarificación

La argumentación abstracta reformula y clarifica: *that is to say* (es decir), *in other words* (en otras palabras), *to put it differently* (para decirlo de otro modo), *the point is that* (el punto es que), *what this means is* (lo que esto significa es), *more precisely* (más precisamente), *to be clear* (para ser claro), *the distinction is between* (la distinción es entre). Un hablante C1 escribe *the compatibilist's claim, more precisely, is not that determinism and freedom are compatible in every sense one might attach to the latter, but that they are compatible in the specific sense that moral responsibility requires \u2014 namely, the absence of coercion and the presence of reasons-responsiveness \u2014 a narrowing of the claim that, if successful, avoids the libertarian's charge of equivocation.* La reformulación precisa es esencial para evitar la equivocación en el debate.

### 14. Implicaciones prácticas: derecho, castigo y ética

Las implicaciones prácticas del debate: *justification of punishment* (justificación del castigo), *retributive justice* (justicia retributiva), *rehabilitation* (rehabilitación), *diminished responsibility* (responsabilidad disminuida), *mens rea* (intención criminal), *actus reus* (acto criminal), *moral reactive attitudes* (actitudes reactivas morales, de Strawson), *participant stance* (postura de participante). Un hablante C1 escribe *the practical question of whether the institutions of blame and punishment would survive the acceptance of hard determinism is one that Strawson's appeal to the participant stance addresses by arguing that our reactive attitudes \u2014 resentment, gratitude, forgiveness \u2014 are not, in practice, hostage to the truth of determinism, for they are constitutive of interpersonal life in a way that a theoretical conviction, however well-argued, is unlikely to dislodge.* Las actitudes reactivas son la respuesta de Strawman a la cuestión práctica.

### 15. Cohesión en ensayos filosóficos

La cohesión en ensayos filosóficos combina conectores de implicación, concesión y reformulación en cadenas lógicas explícitas: *the argument proceeds as follows* (el argumento procede así), *given that* (dado que), *it follows that* (se sigue que), *granted that, yet* (concedido que, sin embargo), *the reply is that* (la respuesta es que), *to which the counter is* (a lo que la réplica es), *on this view* (en esta visión), *the upshot is that* (el resultado es que). Un hablante C1 escribe *the argument proceeds as follows: given that every action is determined by prior causes, and granted that responsibility requires the ability to do otherwise, it follows that responsibility is incompatible with determinism \u2014 yet the compatibilist, on this view, will reply that the second premise is false, to which the incompatibilist's counter is that redefining freedom to fit determinism is not to solve the problem but to change the subject, the upshot being that the debate turns less on the truth of determinism than on the analysis of the concept of freedom itself.* La cohesión filosófica hace visible la arquitectura del argumento.

3. The compatibilist holds that ______ one's motives suffices for responsibility.
4. ______ one's desires, by contrast, undermines the attribution of the action to the self.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. sourcehood
2. ultimate source
3. identification with
4. alienation from

The sourcehood debate turns on whether the agent must be the *ultimate* source (incompatibilist demand) or only the *proximate* source via identification (compatibilist offer). Frankfurt's hierarchy of desires \u2014 identification versus alienation \u2014 provides the compatibilist's framework for distinguishing the agent's true self from impulses she disowns.

**Ejercicio 10.** Reescribe esta afirmación sobre la neurociencia con la cautela inferencial de un C1: "Brain scans show our decisions are made before we know. So free will is fake."

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The appeal to Libet's readiness potential as evidence against free will rests on a contentious inference: from the finding that an unconscious brain signal precedes the conscious awareness of the decision to act, it does not follow that the decision was not the agent's, for the move from a neural precursor to a denial of agency requires bridging the gap between the timing of an urge and the attribution of a deliberated choice, a gap that the experiment, on most interpretations, does not close.*

The C1 move: *rests on a contentious inference*, *it does not follow that*, *requires bridging the gap between... and...*. The rewrite distinguishes what the experiment shows (a neural precursor precedes awareness) from what it would need to show to refute free will (that the decision was not the agent's), and argues that the inference between them is not warranted by the data alone.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. more precisely
2. in the relevant sense
3. to be clear
4. that is to say (or in other words)

These reformulators do precision work: *more precisely* narrows a claim, *in the relevant sense* scopes it, *to be clear* preempts a misreading, and *that is to say* unpacks an abstraction. In philosophical argument they are not ornament but necessity, for the debates often turn on whether a term is being used in the same sense by both sides.

**Ejercicio 14.** Reescribe esta afirmación sobre el castigo con la abstracción de un C1: "If we don't have free will, we can't punish people for revenge."

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The practical question of whether the institutions of blame and punishment would survive the acceptance of hard determinism is one that Strawson's appeal to the participant stance addresses by arguing that our reactive attitudes \u2014 resentment, gratitude, forgiveness \u2014 are not, in practice, hostage to the truth of determinism, for they are constitutive of interpersonal life in a way that a theoretical conviction, however well-argued, is unlikely to dislodge, which is to say that even if the retributive justification of punishment falters, the practices it rationalised may persist on the independent ground of the reactive attitudes that sustain them.*

The rewrite abstracts from *punish people for revenge* to *the institutions of blame and punishment* and *the retributive justification*, introduces Strawson's framework (*participant stance*, *reactive attitudes*), and argues that the practices may survive the collapse of their retributive rationale by resting on a different, non-theoretical ground. The everyday claim becomes a philosophical position embedded in the literature.

**Ejercicio 16.** Usa la estructura cohesiva *the argument proceeds as follows: given that... it follows that... yet the reply is that... to which the counter is... the upshot being that* para articular el debate entre incompatibilismo y compatibilismo.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The argument proceeds as follows: given that every action is determined by prior causes, and granted that responsibility requires the ability to do otherwise, it follows that responsibility is incompatible with determinism \u2014 yet the compatibilist, on this view, will reply that the second premise is false, to which the incompatibilist's counter is that redefining freedom to fit determinism is not to solve the problem but to change the subject, the upshot being that the debate turns less on the truth of determinism than on the analysis of the concept of freedom itself.*

The cohesive structure displays the full architecture of the exchange: *the argument proceeds as follows* opens, *given that... granted that... it follows that* lays out the incompatibilist inference, *yet the compatibilist will reply* introduces the rebuttal, *to which the incompatibilist's counter is* introduces the counter-rebuttal, and *the upshot being that* delivers the synthesising conclusion. The sentence is a miniature of the debate in one coherent movement.

**Ejercicio 18.** Escribe un párrafo breve (4-5 frases) en C1 que presente el núcleo del debate entre compatibilismo e incompatibilismo, usando al menos cuatro conectores lógicos y abstracción conceptual.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The debate between compatibilism and incompatibilism turns, in the final analysis, less on the truth of determinism than on the analysis of the concept of freedom that moral responsibility is taken to require. Granted that determinism, if true, removes the ability to have done otherwise in the absolute sense, it follows that the incompatibilist's case is secure if and only if that ability is a necessary condition of responsibility \u2014 a conditional that places the burden squarely on the analysis of freedom itself. The compatibilist, on this view, will reply that the kind of freedom we value and the kind that responsibility presupposes are better captured by the absence of coercion and the presence of reasons-responsiveness than by the libertarian's ability to have acted contrary to causal law, a reformulation that, if accepted, dissolves the incompatibilist inference at the level of the premise. To which the incompatibilist's counter is that such a reformulation, however practically appealing, changes the subject rather than solving the problem, for the question was whether we are the ultimate sources of our actions, not merely whether we act without interference. The upshot, then, is that the dispute is unlikely to be settled by empirical findings about the brain, for it is, at bottom, a conceptual dispute about what we mean by freedom \u2014 and about whether the meaning we settle on is one that the deterministic framework, if true, can accommodate.*

Logical connectors: *in the final analysis*, *granted that... it follows that... if and only if*, *on this view... will reply that*, *to which the counter is*, *the upshot, then, is that*. Conceptual abstraction: *analysis of the concept of freedom*, *necessary condition of responsibility*, *reasons-responsiveness*, *ultimate sources*, *conceptual dispute*. The paragraph enacts the C1 capacity to display an argument's logical architecture while sustaining the conceptual abstraction the subject demands.

**Ejercicio 19.** Escribe un párrafo breve (4-5 frases) en inglés C1 que argumente sobre las implicaciones prácticas del debate (derecho, castigo, ética), usando conectores lógicos y abstracción filosófica.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*If the incompatibilist's inference is sound and determinism does, in fact, rule out the kind of freedom that moral desert presupposes, it follows that the retributive justification of punishment \u2014 the one that rests on the agent's deserving blame for what she could have avoided doing \u2014 is, strictly speaking, without foundation, however inconvenient that conclusion may be for the institutions it underwrites. That said, the collapse of the retributive rationale does not, by implication, entail the collapse of punishment as such, for the consequentialist justification \u2014 which rests on deterrence, rehabilitation and protection rather than desert \u2014 remains available to ground the practice on grounds that the deterministic premise does not undermine. Granted that this reconceptualisation asks the legal system to surrender the intuition that blame is deserved, yet the Strawsonian point that our reactive attitudes are constitutive of interpersonal life suggests that the practical persistence of blame may survive the theoretical collapse of its strongest justification, for what people actually do in holding one another responsible is not, in the main, hostage to the metaphysics their philosophers settle. The upshot, then, is not that hard determinism abolishes responsibility but that it relocates its foundation \u2014 from the backward-looking desert of the retributive frame to the forward-looking purposes of the consequentialist one \u2014 a relocation that is, admittedly, a larger intellectual adjustment than the everyday practice of blame is likely to register, but one whose coherence the philosophical argument, if accepted, would require. The honest position, in the final analysis, is that the practical question is not whether we can go on holding people responsible \u2014 for in all likelihood we will \u2014 but whether the framework we offer to justify that practice is one that can survive the truth, if truth it be, of the deterministic premise on which the incompatibilist's inference rests.*

Logical connectors: *if... it follows that*, *that said*, *by implication, does not entail*, *granted that... yet*, *the upshot, then, is that*, *in the final analysis*. Conceptual abstraction: *moral desert*, *retributive justification*, *consequentialist justification*, *reactive attitudes*, *backward-looking vs forward-looking*. The paragraph moves from inference to practical consequence to Strawsonian qualification to relocation of foundation to honest conclusion \u2014 the full arc of a C1 argument that integrates logical rigour with philosophical abstraction and practical implication.

## Resumen de la unidad

En esta unidad hemos construido el andamiaje lingüístico para argumentar sobre el libre albedrío y el determinismo con la abstracción conceptual y la precisión lógica que exige el nivel C1. Has aprendido a distinguir *compatibilism* de *incompatibilism*, *hard determinism* de *libertarianism*, y a manejar el léxico de la filosofía de la acción \u2014*agency*, *autonomy*, *sourcehood*, *moral desert*, *reasons-responsiveness*\u2014, el del *consequence argument* \u2014*transfer of powerlessness*, *the past is fixed*, *inevitable*\u2014 y el del *problem of luck* \u2014*indeterminism*, *randomness*, *agent causation*. Has incorporado los conectores lógicos de implicación \u2014*it follows that*, *this entails*, *the corollary is that*, *from which it follows*\u2014, los de concesión y refutación \u2014*granted that*, *the reply is that*, *to which the counter is*\u2014 y los de reformulación \u2014*more precisely*, *in the relevant sense*, *that is to say*, *qua*. Has ejercitado la abstracción filosófica \u2014*the notion that*, *the thesis that*, *in a sense strong enough to ground*\u2014, la cautela inferencial ante la evidencia neurocientífica, y la articulación de las implicaciones prácticas del debate para el derecho y la ética. La destreza central de esta unidad es el razonamiento abstracto transparente: la capacidad de construir cadenas argumentales donde cada conector señala al lector la relación lógica entre las ideas, de manejar el lenguaje conceptual con la precisión que evita la equivocación, y de sostener la tensión entre posiciones sin colapsar el debate en una falsa resolución. Esa elegancia lógica es la marca distintiva del hablante C1 cuando se enfrenta a uno de los debates donde la claridad del lenguaje no es un adorno sino la condición misma de la inteligibilidad del argumento.
