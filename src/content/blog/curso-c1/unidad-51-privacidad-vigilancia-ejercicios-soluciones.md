## Parte II — Ejercicios

**Ejercicio 1.** Distingue *targeted surveillance* y *mass surveillance* en una frase C1 que muestre sus diferentes bases legales.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*Whereas targeted surveillance, conducted under judicial warrant and focused on individualised suspicion, fits within the traditional framework of proportionate intrusion, mass surveillance inverts that logic by gathering first and filtering later, a sequence that shifts the burden onto the state to justify retroactively what it has already collected — a structure that the proportionality framework was never designed to accommodate.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 2.** Explica la distinción *content* vs *metadata* y por qué se ha debilitado éticamente.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The distinction between content — what is said in a communication — and metadata — who contacted whom, when, from where and for how long — rests on the premise that the latter reveals nothing of substance. Yet the empirical reality of pattern analysis has substantially undermined that premise, since the mosaic of communication patterns can reconstruct an intimate portrait of a person's associations, movements and habits that many an intercepted message could not match — a gap between the legal distinction and the analytical reality that calls into question whether metadata still deserves the lesser protection it has historically received.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 3.** Completa con léxico del GDPR: *The distinction between ________ and ________ determines where legal accountability lies, a line that cloud arrangements have complicated, since a service that merely stores data at the controller's direction occupies a different position from one that actively mines it for its own purposes.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*data controller* y *data processor* — el *data controller* decide cómo y por qué se tratan los datos y soporta la responsabilidad principal; el *data processor* los trata por cuenta del controller. Los arreglos en la nube difuminan esta línea cuando el proveedor extrae valor de los datos por su cuenta, aproximándose funcionalmente a un controller.
</details>

**Ejercicio 4.** Reescribe con léxico de cifrado y el argumento técnico contra las puertas traseras: *The government wants to read encrypted messages, but tech people say it's impossible to do safely.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The persistent pressure for "exceptional access" to encrypted communications rests on the assumption that a backdoor accessible only to legitimate authorities can be engineered, a premise that the technical community has consistently argued is mathematically untenable, since any deliberately inserted vulnerability is, by definition, accessible to whoever finds it, legitimate or otherwise.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 5.** Completa con el marco de proporcionalidad: *The requirement that any intrusion be ________, ________ and the ________ means available sets a standard that mass collection, by its very nature, struggles to meet.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*necessary*, *proportionate*, *least intrusive* — estos tres criterios conforman el marco de proporcionalidad del derecho de los derechos humanos: la medida debe ser necesaria para un objetivo legítimo, proporcional al coste en derechos y el medio menos intrusivo que logre el fin. La recogida masiva, que opera sin sospecha individualizada, difícilmente satisface estos requisitos.
</details>

**Ejercicio 6.** Reescribe con léxico del capitalismo de vigilancia: *Companies collect data about what we do and sell predictions about us.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The extraction of behavioural surplus — the detailed record of what users do, for how long and in what sequence, harvested as raw material for the prediction markets that fund the platforms — represents a form of surveillance that operates not for security but for profit, a logic that the framework of state oversight is only beginning to grapple with.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 7.** Escribe una frase que argumente a favor del derecho a cifrar como corolario de la libertad de expresión.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The advocacy for a right to encrypt, often framed as a corollary of freedom of expression, rests on the premise that the ability to communicate privately is a precondition for free expression in any meaningful sense, since the knowledge that one is observed chills the very speech that the right is meant to protect.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 8.** Reescribe con léxico de whistleblowers: *Snowden told everyone about the secret programs, and now people can debate them.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The Snowden revelations, by making visible the scale of bulk collection that had previously been denied or obscured, catalysed a public debate that the classification of the programmes had effectively foreclosed, raising the question of whether, in a democratic society, the public's capacity to debate the powers exercised in its name can legitimately depend on the courage of a single whistleblower.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 9.** Completa con léxico del derecho al olvido: *The right to be forgotten balances the individual's interest in shedding a digital past against the public's interest in access to information, a balance that is notoriously hard to strike, since what counts as ________ or ________ is itself contestable and context-dependent.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*outdated* (o *stale*) y *irrelevant* — el derecho al olvido se activa cuando los datos son inadecuados, irrelevantes o excesivos respecto a los fines del tratamiento, pero qué cuenta como obsoleto o irrelevante es inherentemente contextual, lo que hace del derecho un equilibrio difícil de fijar de manera uniforme.
</details>

**Ejercicio 10.** Reescribe con léxico de vigilancia algorítmica: *Facial recognition cameras identify everyone in the street, which is like mass surveillance.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The deployment of facial recognition in public spaces collapses the traditional distinction between targeted and mass surveillance, since a system that identifies everyone who passes through its field of view operates, by design, on the entire population, a scope that the proportionality framework was not built to accommodate and that biometric mass surveillance bans are increasingly seeking to foreclose.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 11.** Escribe una frase que critique el consentimiento como mecanismo agotado, usando *consent fatigue* o *dark patterns*.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The ubiquity of consent banners has produced a paradox in which the formal mechanism designed to empower users — informed, granular, freely given consent — has degenerated into a ritual of button-clicking compounded by dark patterns, delivering neither information nor genuine choice, a gap between the legal form and the lived reality that the framework of consent, however well-intentioned, has struggled to close.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 12.** Completa con léxico de vigilancia laboral: *The ________ of power in the employment context renders the notion of freely given consent largely ________, since an employee who declines to authorise monitoring may face consequences that make the refusal, in practice, unavailable.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*asymmetry* y *formal* (o *nominal*) — la asimetría de poder convierte el consentimiento laboral en un acto más formal que sustantivo, porque el coste de rechazar la vigilancia puede ser el empleo mismo, una dinámica que cuestiona si el consentimiento en este contexto puede sostener éticamente el peso que el marco legal le pide cargar.
</details>

**Ejercicio 13.** Desmonta el falso dilema seguridad vs privacidad en una frase C1 que presente las tecnologías de privacidad como medidas de seguridad.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The framing of privacy and security as a zero-sum trade-off obscures the extent to which robust encryption and privacy-enhancing technologies are themselves security measures, protecting not only individual liberty but the integrity of the communications infrastructure on which the economy, the state and the dissidents under authoritarian regimes all equally depend.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 14.** Distingue *delisting* y *erasure* en el contexto del derecho al olvido.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*Delisting* (o *dereferencing*) consiste en retirar un enlace de los resultados de un buscador, de modo que la información, aunque sigue existiendo en su fuente original, se hace sustancialmente menos accesible. *Erasure* (o *right to erasure*) es la supresión de los datos mismos en origen. La distinción es jurídicamente significativa: el delisting protege la privacidad sin borrar la información de interés público, mientras que la erasure puede tensionar más directamente con la libertad de información y la memoria histórica.
</details>

**Ejercicio 15.** Reescribe con léxico de privacidad diferencial o anonimización: *If you add noise to the data, you can protect people while still learning things in general.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*Differential privacy, by injecting calibrated noise into datasets in a way that preserves their statistical utility while mathematically bounding the information that can be inferred about any individual, exemplifies a privacy-enhancing technology that refuses the zero-sum framing — enabling aggregate insight without compromising the privacy of the contributors on whose data the insight rests.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 16.** Completa con léxico de contexto y privacidad relacional: *The erosion of ________ — whereby data gathered in one context flows freely into others for which it was never intended — captures a form of privacy loss that the traditional frame of individual consent is ill-equipped to address.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*contextual integrity* — la integridad contextual (de Helen Nissenbaum) sostiene que la privacidad se respeta cuando la información circula según las normas del contexto al que pertenece, y se vulnera cuando fluye hacia contextos para los que no fue concebida, un daño que el marco del consentimiento individual, centrado en cada divulgación aislada, no puede captar porque el perjuicio reside en la agregación transversal.
</details>

**Ejercicio 17.** Reescribe esta conclusión con cohesión y matiz: *We need to watch people for security. But we need privacy too. It's about balance.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*Ultimately, what the surveillance debate amounts to is not a choice between security and privacy but a question of proportionality: which intrusions, under what oversight, for what purposes, and with what safeguards against the foreseeable abuse of powers that, however well-intentioned at their inception, history gives us little reason to assume will always remain so.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 18.** Explica en 2-3 frases, en inglés C1, por qué la distinción legal entre *content* y *metadata* resulta insostenible a la luz del análisis de patrones.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The legal distinction between content and metadata rests on the assumption that the latter — who contacted whom, when, from where and for how long — reveals nothing worth protecting as intensely as the words exchanged. Yet pattern analysis has shown that a mosaic of metadata can reconstruct a person's associations, movements, medical consultations and religious attendance with a granularity that many an intercepted message could not match. The distinction, in other words, holds in theory but collapses in practice, which is why treating metadata as categorically less sensitive than content is a legal convention that the analytical reality has substantially overtaken.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 19.** Escribe un párrafo breve (4-5 frases) en inglés C1 que argumente sobre el equilibrio entre vigilancia estatal y privacidad, usando al menos tres elementos del léxico de esta unidad (proporcionalidad, tipos de vigilancia, cifrado o metadatos).

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*While the state's interest in preventing serious harm is legitimate, the case for targeted surveillance conducted under judicial warrant is a categorically different proposition from the case for bulk collection of everyone's data on the off chance that some of it may one day prove useful. The proportionality framework — requiring that any intrusion be necessary, proportionate and the least intrusive means available — was built precisely to mark that difference, and mass surveillance, by operating without individualised suspicion, struggles by its very nature to meet it. Compounding the concern, the persistent pressure for "exceptional access" to encrypted communications asks the public to accept a backdoor whose security the technical community has consistently argued is mathematically untenable, since any deliberately inserted vulnerability is accessible to whoever finds it. To be sure, no framework can eliminate every risk, and the state retains a genuine interest in targeted, proportionate intrusion under independent oversight. Yet the lesson of the past two decades is that powers granted for one purpose tend to migrate to others, which is why the burden of proof must rest squarely on those who would expand surveillance, not on those who would guard the privacy that an open society requires to remain open.*
</details>

## Resumen de la unidad

En esta unidad hemos construido el andamiaje lingüístico para debatir privacidad y vigilancia con la precisión legal e informática que exige el nivel C1. Has aprendido a distinguir *targeted* de *mass surveillance*, *content* de *metadata*, *data controller* de *data processor*, y a manejar el léxico del GDPR —del *right to be forgotten* a la *data protection by design*. Has incorporado el vocabulario del cifrado —*end-to-end encryption*, *backdoor*, *exceptional access*— y el del capitalismo de vigilancia —*behavioural surplus*, *data broking*, *targeted advertising*. Has explorado el marco de proporcionalidad —*necessary*, *proportionate*, *least intrusive*— como estándar para juzgar las medidas, el de los *whistleblowers* y las *revelations* como catalizadores del debate democrático, y el de la vigilancia algorítmica —*facial recognition*, *predictive policing*— como frontera que desafía los marcos existentes. Has ejercitado la crítica del consentimiento como mecanismo agotado, del falso dilema seguridad-privacidad y de la asimetría de poder en la vigilancia laboral. La destreza central de esta unidad es la argumentación proporcional: la capacidad de debatir vigilancia reconociendo las preocupaciones legítimas de seguridad sin conceder que toda intrusión se justifica por ellas, y de anclar el juicio en un léxico técnico que distingue lo que es proporcionado de lo que es desproporcionado. Esa elegancia argumentativa es la marca distintiva del hablante C1 cuando se enfrenta a uno de los debates donde el derecho y la tecnología se entrelazan con más fuerza y con mayores consecuencias para la libertad.
