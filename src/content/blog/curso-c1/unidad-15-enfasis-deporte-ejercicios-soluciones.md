## Parte II — Ejercicios con soluciones

### Ejercicio 1 — Do y did enfáticos

Completa con **do, does** o **did**.

1. She ___ train incredibly hard for the marathon.
2. The team ___ deserve recognition for its recovery.
3. I ___ understand the coach’s concern.
4. The strategy ___ work under certain conditions.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **did**; 2. **does**; 3. **do**; 4. **does**. El verbo principal permanece en forma base después del auxiliar.

</details>

### Ejercicio 2 — Corrige el verbo

1. *She did trained all winter.*
2. *The athlete does performs consistently.*
3. *They did improved after the coaching change.*

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **She did train all winter.**
2. **The athlete does perform consistently.**
3. **They did improve after the coaching change.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 3 — Only + inversión

Reescribe.

1. She achieved her ambition only through years of sacrifice.
2. The team improved only after the coach changed the strategy.
3. Athletes can sustain performance only by managing recovery.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **Only through years of sacrifice did she achieve her ambition.**
2. **Only after the coach changed the strategy did the team improve.**
3. **Only by managing recovery can athletes sustain performance.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 4 — Not until y not only

Completa.

1. Not until the final lap ___ the runner overtake her rival.
2. Not only ___ the training improve endurance, but it also reduced injury risk.
3. Not only is the sport commercialised, but it ___ also increasingly unequal.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **did**; 2. **did**; 3. **is**. La inversión aparece después del elemento restrictivo y el paralelismo se mantiene en la segunda parte.

</details>

### Ejercicio 5 — Fronting sin inversión

Elige la opción correcta.

1. *In the final, the team played / did the team play cautiously.*
2. *Only in the final played the team / did the team play cautiously.*
3. *After the injury, the runner changed / did the runner change her technique.*

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **the team played**; 2. **did the team play**; 3. **the runner changed**. *In* y *after* son complementos frontales neutros; *only* provoca inversión.

</details>

### Ejercicio 6 — Clefts

Reescribe destacando el elemento indicado.

1. Her mental resilience set her apart. (mental resilience)
2. The coach changed the strategy. (the coach)
3. The lack of recovery time caused the injury. (lack of recovery time)
4. The race changed during the final lap. (during the final lap)

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **It was her mental resilience that set her apart.**
2. **It was the coach who changed the strategy.**
3. **It was the lack of recovery time that caused the injury.**
4. **It was during the final lap that the race changed.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 7 — What-clefts

Completa.

1. ___ set her apart ___ her mental resilience.
2. ___ the team lacked ___ tactical awareness.
3. ___ matters ___ the ability to recover under pressure.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **What; was**; 2. **What; was**; 3. **What; is**.

</details>

### Ejercicio 8 — Vocabulario

Completa con **flow, momentum, mental resilience, marginal gains** o **level playing field**.

1. The athlete entered a state of total absorption known as ___ .
2. Small improvements in sleep and nutrition can produce ___ .
3. The team gained ___ after scoring twice in quick succession.
4. A fair competition requires a reasonably ___ .
5. The ability to recover from pressure demonstrates ___ .

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **flow**; 2. **marginal gains**; 3. **momentum**; 4. **level playing field**; 5. **mental resilience**.

</details>

### Ejercicio 9 — Reading

Lee el texto y responde.

> The pursuit of sporting excellence increasingly resembles an arms race. Wealthier clubs invest in marginal gains through nutrition, sleep monitoring, specialist coaching and psychological support. These improvements may seem individually minor, but together they can determine whether an athlete performs consistently under pressure. Critics argue that the system entrenches inequality because raw talent is developed differently depending on access to resources. The language of meritocracy therefore requires qualification: effort matters, but effort alone cannot create a level playing field. Only when young athletes receive comparable facilities and recovery support can competition become genuinely fairer.

1. What do wealthy clubs invest in?
2. Why do small improvements matter?
3. What criticism do opponents make?
4. Why does meritocracy require qualification?
5. What condition could make competition fairer?

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **Nutrition, sleep monitoring, specialist coaching and psychological support.**
2. **Together they can determine consistent performance under pressure.**
3. **That the system entrenches inequality through unequal access to resources.**
4. **Because effort alone cannot create a level playing field when resources differ.**
5. **Comparable facilities and recovery support for young athletes.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 10 — Listening guiado

Guion: “The athlete did work hard, but it was the quality of her recovery programme that transformed her results. Only after she had reduced her training load did her endurance improve. What separated her from other competitors was not the absence of anxiety, but her ability to reframe it as energy.”

Identifica un `did` enfático, una it-cleft, una inversión con `only` y una what-cleft.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

`Did` enfático: **did work hard**. It-cleft: **it was the quality of her recovery programme that transformed her results**. Inversión: **Only after she had reduced her training load did her endurance improve**. What-cleft: **What separated her from other competitors was...**.

</details>

### Ejercicio 11 — Fronting y sentido

Explica la diferencia entre:

1. *The team improved only after the coach changed the strategy.*
2. *Only after the coach changed the strategy did the team improve.*

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

El significado básico es el mismo, pero la segunda versión pone la condición temporal en primer plano y utiliza inversión enfática. Es más marcada y adecuada cuando el momento del cambio es el punto central del argumento.

</details>

### Ejercicio 12 — Corrección avanzada

Corrige el párrafo.

> She did trained hard, but rarely the coach recognised her progress. Not only the team improved, but also reduced injuries. What did matter was the access to recovery support.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

**She did train hard, but rarely did the coach recognise her progress. Not only did the team improve, but it also reduced injuries. What mattered was access to recovery support.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 13 — Registro argumentativo

Reescribe con énfasis y matiz: *Sport rewards talent equally, but resources are different.*

<details><summary>Modelo</summary>

**It is not talent alone that determines sporting success; only when athletes have access to comparable coaching, facilities and recovery support can competition approach a level playing field.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 14 — Writing

Escribe 150–180 palabras sobre: **Does competitive sport build character?** Incluye un `did` enfático, una inversión con `only`, una cleft y vocabulario de rendimiento.

<details><summary>Modelo</summary>

Competitive sport can build character, but its effects depend heavily on the environment in which competition takes place. Athletes do develop discipline and mental resilience through demanding training, yet pressure can also produce anxiety or unhealthy perfectionism. It is not victory alone that builds character; rather, it is the way coaches and teammates respond to setbacks. Only when competitors are encouraged to reflect on failure can they develop genuine composure and self-belief. Elite sport may also expose unequal access to nutrition, facilities and psychological support. Wealthier athletes can accumulate marginal gains, whereas others may struggle to recover between sessions. The language of meritocracy therefore needs qualification. Effort does matter, and athletes do deserve recognition for sustained commitment, but personal dedication cannot create a level playing field by itself. Competitive sport is most likely to foster resilience when it combines high expectations with responsible coaching, adequate recovery and respect for the person behind the performance.

</details>

### Ejercicio 15 — Speaking

Habla durante un minuto sobre la presión competitiva. Usa `What matters is`, `It is... that`, `only when` y `did` enfático.

<details><summary>Pista y modelo</summary>

**What matters is how athletes interpret pressure. It is not the absence of anxiety that produces consistent performance, but the ability to manage it. Only when competitors receive appropriate support can they recover effectively. The athlete did work hard, but her progress also depended on coaching and access to facilities.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 16 — Traducción controlada

Traduce al inglés.

1. Sí que entrenó muchísimo para el maratón.
2. Solo mediante años de sacrificio consiguió su objetivo.
3. Fue la resiliencia mental lo que la distinguió.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **She did train incredibly hard for the marathon.**
2. **Only through years of sacrifice did she achieve her ambition.**
3. **It was her mental resilience that set her apart.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 17 — Edición de una reseña

Mejora esta frase: *The athlete was very good and she tried hard, so she won.* Incluye una cleft y una colocación de rendimiento.

<details><summary>Modelo</summary>

**It was the athlete’s ability to maintain focus under pressure, combined with her exceptional physical endurance, that enabled her to reach peak performance.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 18 — Mini-test final

Completa el texto.

> The runner ___ train consistently, but ___ after the injury ___ she changed her recovery plan. ___ was her mental resilience that kept her competing, and ___ matters most ___ the ability to manage pressure rather than the absence of anxiety.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

**did; only; did; It; what; is.** El texto completo es: **The runner did train consistently, but only after the injury did she change her recovery plan. It was her mental resilience that kept her competing, and what matters most is the ability to manage pressure rather than the absence of anxiety.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 19 — Integra el argumento

Combina estas ideas en un párrafo formal de 70–90 palabras. Incluye un `did` enfático para corregir una afirmación, una inversión con **only**, una it-cleft y una concesión.

> The athlete trained consistently. Her progress was not caused by training alone. She received specialist recovery support. This support became available after she joined an elite programme. Sport can build resilience, but access to resources is unequal.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

Una respuesta posible es: **The athlete did train consistently, but her progress was not caused by training alone. It was specialist recovery support that made the greatest difference after she joined an elite programme. Only when athletes have access to comparable resources can a competition approach a level playing field. Although sport can build resilience, access to coaching, nutrition and recovery facilities remains unequal.**

El párrafo utiliza `did train` para reforzar una afirmación, la it-cleft para destacar el apoyo, la inversión con `only` para expresar una condición y `although` para introducir una concesión. El contenido también evita explicar el éxito mediante una única causa.

</details>

Al revisar cualquier respuesta de esta unidad, comprueba tres aspectos. Primero, confirma que `do`, `does` o `did` van seguidos del verbo base. Segundo, localiza la inversión: después de `only` debe aparecer el auxiliar antes del sujeto, mientras que un complemento frontal neutro no exige ese cambio. Por último, pregunta qué factor quieres destacar. Si el énfasis no corrige, contrasta ni organiza una idea, la oración normal suele ser la opción más natural.

## Resumen de la unidad

Usa **do/does/did + verbo base** para reforzar una afirmación, confirmar una acción o contradecir una negación. Recuerda que el verbo principal no lleva `-s` ni pasado después del auxiliar: *She does perform*, *she did perform*.

Usa **only, not until, never, rarely** y otras expresiones restrictivas al principio para crear fronting e inversión. Si el complemento frontal es neutro, como *in the final* o *after the injury*, normalmente no necesitas inversión. Las clefts con **it was... that/who** y las what-clefts con **what... was...** distribuyen el foco de otra manera.

En debates deportivos, estas estructuras ayudan a evitar explicaciones demasiado simples. El esfuerzo importa, pero también cuentan la recuperación, el entrenamiento, la nutrición y el acceso a recursos. Cuando combines *mental resilience*, *marginal gains*, *perform under pressure* y *level playing field* con énfasis gramatical, podrás escribir y hablar sobre rendimiento con la precisión argumentativa propia del nivel C1.
