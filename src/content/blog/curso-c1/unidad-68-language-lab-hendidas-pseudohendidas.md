## Parte I — Teoría

### 1. Qué es una oración hendida y por qué el escritor C1 la necesita

Una oración hendida (it-cleft) divide una oración canónica en dos cláusulas para focalizar un elemento: *John broke the window* se convierte en *it was John who broke the window*. La estructura es *it is/was* + elemento focal + *that/who* + cláusula restante. La función es focalizar: el escritor indica al lector qué elemento debe tratar como nuevo o destacado. La hendida no añade información: reorganiza la existente para asignar prominencia. El escritor C1 la necesita porque la prosa avanzada no solo transmite contenido sino que señala al lector cómo procesarlo, qué elemento merece atención especial.

A2: "It is John who came" emphasizes that John is the one who came.

B2: A cleft sentence splits a normal sentence to focus on one part: "It was John who broke the window" (from "John broke the window"). The structure is "It is/was + focus + that/who + rest."

C1: An it-cleft divides a canonical clause into two to focalise an element: John broke the window becomes it was John who broke the window. The structure is it is/was + focal element + that/who + remainder. The function is focalisation: the writer signals to the reader which element should be treated as new or prominent. The cleft adds no information: it reorganises existing information to assign prominence. The C1 writer needs it because advanced prose not only conveys content but signals to the reader how to process it, which element merits special attention.

### 2. La estructura de la hendida: *it is/was* + foco + *that/who*

La estructura canónica de la hendida es rígida: *it is/was* + elemento focal + *that* (o *who* para personas) + cláusula restante. El elemento focal puede ser un sujeto (*it was John who broke the window*), un objeto (*it was the window that John broke*), un adverbio temporal (*it was yesterday that the incident occurred*) o una frase preposicional (*it was in Paris that they met*). La flexibilidad reside en qué elemento se elige como foco, no en la estructura misma. El escritor C1 selecciona el foco según qué elemento del mensaje quiere destacar en cada contexto retórico.

A2: The pattern is "It is [focus] that [rest]."

B2: The cleft structure is fixed: "It is/was + focus + that/who + rest." The focus can be a subject, object, time or place: "It was yesterday that it happened," "It was in London that they met."

C1: The canonical structure of the cleft is rigid: it is/was + focal element + that (or who for persons) + remainder. The focal element can be a subject (it was John who broke the window), an object (it was the window that John broke), a temporal adverb (it was yesterday that the incident occurred) or a prepositional phrase (it was in Paris that they met). The flexibility lies in which element is chosen as focus, not in the structure itself. The C1 writer selects the focus according to which element of the message they wish to highlight in each rhetorical context.

### 3. Hendidas de contraste y corrección

Las hendidas son especialmente útiles para contrastar o corregir: *it is not the method but the application that matters*, *it was not the policy itself but its implementation that failed*. La estructura *it is not X but Y that* permite formular un contraste con máxima claridad focal. Igualmente, *it was X, not Y, who* sirve para corregir una atribución errónea. El escritor C1 usa estas hendidas de contraste en prosa argumentativa para precisar matices, rechazar interpretaciones equivocadas y dirigir la atención del lector hacia el elemento correcto del análisis.

A2: "It is not A but B that matters" shows contrast.

B2: Clefts are useful for contrast and correction: "It is not the method but the application that matters," "It was the policy, not the implementation, that failed." They make the contrast very clear.

C1: Clefts are especially useful for contrasting and correcting: it is not the method but the application that matters; it was not the policy itself but its implementation that failed. The structure it is not X but Y that allows formulating a contrast with maximal focal clarity. Equally, it was X, not Y, who serves to correct a mistaken attribution. The C1 writer uses these contrastive clefts in argumentative prose to precisify nuances, reject mistaken interpretations and direct the reader's attention to the correct element of the analysis.

### 4. Hendidas con adverbios temporales y de lugar

Las hendidas con adverbios temporales y locativos focalizan el cuándo y el dónde: *it was in 1989 that the wall fell*, *it was at the conference that the breakthrough was announced*. Estas hendidas son frecuentes en prosa narrativa e histórica porque permiten dar relieve a los marcos temporales y espaciales de los eventos. El escritor C1 las despliega cuando el contexto temporal o espacial es decisivo para la interpretación del evento, y las evita cuando el marco es accesorio, porque una hendida innecesaria distrae al lector con un foco que no carga peso interpretativo.

A2: "It was in 1989 that the wall fell" emphasizes the year.

B2: Clefts can focus on time or place: "It was in 1989 that the wall fell," "It was at the conference that the announcement was made." Common in narrative and historical writing.

C1: Clefts with temporal and locative adverbs focalise the when and the where: it was in 1989 that the wall fell; it was at the conference that the breakthrough was announced. These clefts are frequent in narrative and historical prose because they allow giving prominence to the temporal and spatial frames of events. The C1 writer deploys them when the temporal or spatial context is decisive for the interpretation of the event, and avoids them when the frame is accessory, because an unnecessary cleft distracts the reader with a focus that carries no interpretive weight.

### 5. La pseudohendida: estructura con *what*

La pseudohendida (wh-cleft) usa *what* para focalizar: *what I need is a vacation* (en vez de *I need a vacation*). La estructura es *what* + cláusula + *is/was* + elemento focal. A diferencia de la hendida, que pone el foco al principio, la pseudohendida pone el foco al final, tras *is/was*. Esta diferencia posiciona tiene consecuencias retóricas: la pseudohendida crea expectativa —el lector espera el foco— y lo entrega como clímax. Es por ello más natural en discurso fluyente y conversacional, mientras que la hendida es más enfática y formal.

A2: "What I need is a rest" emphasizes "a rest."

B2: A pseudocleft uses "what" to focus: "What I need is a vacation" (instead of "I need a vacation"). The structure is "What + clause + is/was + focus." The focus comes at the end.

C1: The wh-cleft uses what to focalise: what I need is a vacation (instead of I need a vacation). The structure is what + clause + is/was + focal element. Unlike the it-cleft, which places the focus at the front, the wh-cleft places it at the end, after is/was. This positional difference has rhetorical consequences: the wh-cleft creates expectation —the reader awaits the focus— and delivers it as climax. It is therefore more natural in flowing and conversational discourse, whereas the it-cleft is more emphatic and formal.

### 6. Pseudohendidas inversas: foco al principio

Existe una variante inversa de la pseudohendida que pone el foco al principio: *a vacation is what I need* (en vez de *what I need is a vacation*). Esta inversión de la pseudohendida es menos frecuente pero útil cuando el foco ya es conocido o presupuesto y lo que se añade es la cláusula de *what*. El escritor C1 distingue la pseudohendida canónica (*what X is Y*) de la inversa (*Y is what X*) y elige según qué elemento —el foco o la cláusula— merezca posición final, que en inglés es la posición de máxima prominencia informacional.

A2: You can also say "A vacation is what I need."

B2: An inverted pseudocleft puts the focus first: "A vacation is what I need" (instead of "What I need is a vacation"). This is less common but useful when the focus is already known.

C1: There exists an inverse variant of the wh-cleft that places the focus at the front: a vacation is what I need (instead of what I need is a vacation). This inversion of the wh-cleft is less frequent but useful when the focus is already known or presupposed and what is added is the what-clause. The C1 writer distinguishes the canonical wh-cleft (what X is Y) from the inverted (Y is what X) and selects according to which element —the focus or the clause— merits final position, which in English is the position of maximal informational prominence.

### 7. Hendidas con *all* y *the thing*

Las hendidas pueden usar otros introductores además de *it* y *what*: *all I want is a quiet life*, *the thing that bothers me is his attitude*, *the person who called was John*. Estas variantes con *all*, *the thing*, *the person*, *the place*, *the reason* funcionan como pseudohendidas lexicalizadas. Son frecuentes en registro hablado y en prosa informal-cuidada. El escritor C1 las reconoce como miembros de la familia hendida y las usa cuando el registro admite una formulación menos rígida que la hendida canónica con *it is*.

A2: "All I want is a rest" is like a cleft with "all."

B2: Clefts can use other words: "All I want is a quiet life," "The thing that bothers me is his attitude," "The reason I came is to help." These are common in speech and semi-formal writing.

C1: Clefts may use other introducers besides it and what: all I want is a quiet life; the thing that bothers me is his attitude; the person who called was John. These variants with all, the thing, the person, the place, the reason function as lexicalised wh-clefts. They are frequent in spoken register and in careful-informal prose. The C1 writer recognises them as members of the cleft family and deploys them when the register admits a less rigid formulation than the canonical it is cleft.

### 8. Hendidas con *because* para focalizar causa

Las hendidas pueden focalizar la causa con *because*: *it was because he was exhausted that he made the mistake*. Aquí la cláusula causal entera se convierte en el foco. Esta hendida es útil en prosa analítica donde la explicación causal es el punto decisivo del argumento. El escritor C1 la reserva para los momentos donde identificar la causa correcta entre varias posibles es el movimiento retórico clave, porque la hendida señala al lector: de todas las explicaciones posibles, esta es la que debes retener.

A2: "It was because he was tired that he failed" emphasizes the reason.

B2: Clefts can focus on a reason with "because": "It was because he was exhausted that he made the mistake." The whole reason clause becomes the focus.

C1: Clefts can focalise cause with because: it was because he was exhausted that he made the mistake. Here the entire causal clause becomes the focus. This cleft is useful in analytical prose where the causal explanation is the decisive point of the argument. The C1 writer reserves it for moments where identifying the correct cause among several candidates is the key rhetorical move, because the cleft signals to the reader: of all possible explanations, this is the one you should retain.

### 9. Concordancia en las pseudohendidas

Un punto sutil de las pseudohendidas es la concordancia del verbo *be* con el foco: *what I want is more time* (singular), *what I want are more opportunities* (plural). La concordancia se establece con el foco, no con la cláusula de *what*. Algunos hablantes prefieren la concordancia con la cláusula (*what I want are more time* es rechazado por puristas pero se oye). El usuario C1 conoce la norma prescriptiva —concordancia con el foco— y la sigue en registro formal, pero reconoce que en registro hablado la concordancia con la cláusula es tolerada y a veces preferida por euphony.

A2: "What I want is more time" — the verb agrees with "time."

B2: In pseudoclefts, the verb "be" agrees with the focus, not the "what" clause: "What I want is more time" (singular), "What I want are more opportunities" (plural).

C1: A subtle point of wh-clefts is the agreement of the verb be with the focus: what I want is more time (singular); what I want are more opportunities (plural). Agreement is established with the focus, not with the what-clause. Some speakers prefer agreement with the clause (what I want are more time is rejected by purists but is heard). The C1 user knows the prescriptive norm —agreement with the focus— and follows it in formal register, yet recognises that in spoken register agreement with the clause is tolerated and sometimes preferred for euphony.

### 10. Hendidas y prosodia: el foco fónico

En la lengua hablada, la hendida se acompaña de una prominencia fónica sobre el elemento focal: *it was **John** who broke the window*. La prosodia refuerza la focalización sintáctica. En la lengua escrita, esa prominencia se transmite tipográficamente con cursiva o negrita en contextos donde la focalización debe ser inequívoca. El escritor C1 es consciente de que la hendida es, en origen, un recurso fónico que la escritura representa sintácticamente, y por eso la usa con mesura: una hendida en lengua escrita equivale a un énfasis entonativo en lengua hablada, y abusar de ella satura el texto de énfasis que el lector percibe como artificioso.

A2: When speaking, we stress the focused word in a cleft.

B2: In speech, the focus in a cleft gets stress: "It was JOHN who broke the window." In writing, you can use italics or bold for the same effect.

C1: In spoken language, the cleft is accompanied by phonological prominence on the focal element: it was John who broke the window. Prosody reinforces the syntactic focalisation. In written language, that prominence is transmitted typographically with italics or bold in contexts where focalisation must be unambiguous. The C1 writer is aware that the cleft is, in origin, a phonological resource that writing represents syntactically, and therefore uses it with measure: a cleft in written language equates to an intonational emphasis in spoken language, and overusing it saturates the text with emphasis the reader perceives as artificial.

### 11. Hendidas en preguntas y negaciones

Las hendidas aparecen también en preguntas (*was it John who broke the window?*) y en negaciones (*it was not John who broke the window*). En las preguntas hendidas, la inversión del auxiliar se aplica al *it is/was*: *is it X that* o *was it X that*. En las negaciones, el *not* recae sobre *it is/was*: *it is not X that*. Estas variantes amplían el repertorio hendido y permiten focalizar en contextos interrogativos y polares. El escritor C1 las maneja cuando necesita formular una pregunta focal o una negación focal con la misma precisión sintáctica que la hendida afirmativa.

A2: We can ask "Was it John who did it?" or say "It was not John who did it."

B2: Clefts appear in questions ("Was it John who broke the window?") and negatives ("It was not John who broke the window"). In questions, the auxiliary inverts: "Is it X that...?"

C1: Clefts appear also in questions (was it John who broke the window?) and in negations (it was not John who broke the window). In cleft questions, auxiliary inversion applies to the it is/was: is it X that or was it X that. In negations, the not falls on it is/was: it is not X that. These variants extend the cleft repertoire and allow focalisation in interrogative and polar contexts. The C1 user handles them when needing to formulate a focal question or a focal negation with the same syntactic precision as the affirmative cleft.

### 12. Pseudohendidas con *the reason why* y *the way*

Las pseudohendidas lexicalizadas con *the reason why* y *the way* son frecuentes en registro cuidado: *the reason why he resigned is that the pressure was unbearable*, *the way he handled the crisis was exemplary*. Estas construcciones focalizan la causa y el modo respectivamente, y funcionan como pseudohendidas con núcleo nominal lexicalizado. El escritor C1 las reconoce como recursos de focalización económica y las distingue de las pseudohendidas con *what* puras, porque su núcleo nominal (*reason*, *way*) ya preespecifica el tipo de relación focalizada, a diferencia del *what* que es semánticamente neutro.

A2: "The reason why he left is that he was tired" focuses on the reason.

B2: Phrases like "the reason why" and "the way" create pseudoclefts: "The reason why he resigned is that the pressure was unbearable," "The way he handled the crisis was exemplary."

C1: Lexicalised wh-clefts with the reason why and the way are frequent in careful register: the reason why he resigned is that the pressure was unbearable; the way he handled the crisis was exemplary. These constructions focalise cause and manner respectively and function as wh-clefts with a lexicalised nominal head. The C1 writer recognises them as resources of economical focalisation and distinguishes them from pure what-clefts, because their nominal head (reason, way) pre-specifies the type of relation focalised, unlike the semantically neutral what.

### 13. Errores frecuentes con hendidas y pseudohendidas

Los errores más frecuentes son tres. Primero, omitir *that* o *who* en la hendida: *it was John broke the window* en vez de *it was John who broke the window*. Segundo, usar *what* donde corresponde *that*: *it was what John broke the window* — incorrecto. Tercero, romper la concordancia en la pseudohendida: *what I want are more time* en registro formal. El usuario C1 audita su producción contra estos tres errores, porque cada uno delata un control incompleto de la estructura hendida y de las restricciones que la rigen.

A2: Common mistakes include forgetting "that" or "who" in clefts.

B2: Common errors: omitting "that/who" ("It was John broke the window"); using "what" instead of "that" ("It was what John broke"); and wrong agreement in pseudoclefts ("What I want are more time").

C1: The most frequent errors are three. First, omitting that or who in the cleft: it was John broke the window instead of it was John who broke the window. Second, using what where that is required: it was what John broke the window — incorrect. Third, breaking agreement in the wh-cleft: what I want are more time in formal register. The C1 user audits their production against these three errors, because each betrays incomplete control of the cleft structure and of the restrictions governing it.

### 14. Distribución y registro de las hendidas

Las hendidas con *it is* son más frecuentes en registro escrito formal y en prosa argumentativa; las pseudohendidas con *what* son más naturales en discurso hablado y en prosa fluyente. El escritor C1 calibra la elección según el registro: en un ensayo académico, prefiere la hendida canónica para focalizar contrastes decisivos; en un artículo divulgativo, puede usar la pseudohendida para crear ritmo y expectativa. Conocer esa distribución es parte de la competencia pragmática C1, que no se limita a producir estructuras correctas sino a elegir la estructura que mejor se adecúa al género y al público.

A2: Clefts are common in writing; "what" clefts are common in speech.

B2: It-clefts are more common in formal writing; wh-clefts are more natural in speech and flowing prose. Choose based on your register and audience.

C1: It-clefts are more frequent in formal written register and in argumentative prose; wh-clefts are more natural in spoken discourse and flowing prose. The C1 writer calibrates the choice according to register: in an academic essay, they prefer the canonical cleft to focalise decisive contrasts; in a popular article, they may use the wh-cleft to create rhythm and expectation. Knowing that distribution is part of C1 pragmatic competence, which is not limited to producing correct structures but to selecting the structure that best fits the genre and the audience.

### 15. Práctica y consolidación de las hendidas

Para consolidar las hendidas, el usuario C1 debe practicar la transformación de oraciones canónicas en hendidas y pseudohendidas: *John broke the window* => *it was John who broke the window* => *what John broke was the window*. Otra técnica es redactar oraciones de contraste usando la estructura *it is not X but Y that*. Una tercera es auditar textos argumentativos identificando cada hendida y clasificándola por tipo (it-cleft, wh-cleft, lexicalizada). La automatización exige practicar la transformación bidireccional —canónica a hendida y hendida a canónica— porque solo así se interioriza la relación entre la estructura plana y la focalizada.

A2: To practice, change normal sentences into clefts: "John did it" => "It was John who did it."

B2: To master clefts, practice transforming normal sentences: "John broke the window" => "It was John who broke the window" => "What John broke was the window." Also practice contrast: "It is not A but B that matters."

C1: To consolidate clefts, the C1 user must practise the transformation of canonical sentences into clefts and pseudoclefts: John broke the window => it was John who broke the window => what John broke was the window. Another technique is drafting contrast sentences using the structure it is not X but Y that. A third is auditing argumentative texts identifying each cleft and classifying it by type (it-cleft, wh-cleft, lexicalised). Automation demands practising bidirectional transformation —canonical to cleft and cleft to canonical— because only thus is the relation between flat and focalised structure internalised.

*The reason why he resigned is that the pressure was unbearable.* Pseudohendida lexicalizada con núcleo nominal *reason*.
</details>

**Ejercicio 13.** Corrige el error: "It was what John broke the window."
<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*What* no es válido en la hendida; debe ser *that* o *who*. Corrección: *It was the window **that** John broke.* (foco en el objeto)
</details>

**Ejercicio 14.** Transforma en hendida locativa: "They met in Paris." (foco en el lugar)
<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*It was in Paris that they met.* Hendida con frase preposicional locativa como foco.
</details>

**Ejercicio 15.** Reescribe con pseudohendida con *the way*: "He handled the crisis exemplarily."
<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The way he handled the crisis was exemplary.* Pseudohendida lexicalizada con núcleo nominal *way*.
</details>

**Ejercicio 16.** Transforma en pseudohendida: "I really enjoyed that book." (foco en that book)
<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*What I really enjoyed was that book.* Pseudohendida: *what* + cláusula + *was* + foco.
</details>

**Ejercicio 17.** Reescribe con hendida de objeto: "John broke the window." (foco en the window)
<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*It was the window that John broke.* Hendida con el objeto como foco.
</details>

**Ejercicio 18.** Identifica foco y tipo: "All she wanted was a chance to prove herself."
<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

Pseudohendida lexicalizada con *all*. El foco es *a chance to prove herself*, colocado al final tras *was*. La cláusula *all she wanted* va al principio.
</details>

**Ejercicio 19.** Redacta una oración de contraste usando una hendida y otra de foco causal usando una pseudohendida, sobre el tema del cambio climático.

<summary>Ver solución</summary>

Hendida de contraste: *It is not the absence of policy but the inconsistency of its application that has hampered progress on emissions reduction.* Pseudohendida causal: *The reason why meaningful action has been delayed is that short-term economic interests have consistently outweighed long-term environmental considerations.*
</details>

## Resumen de la unidad

En esta unidad hemos recorrido las oraciones hendidas y pseudohendidas como herramientas de reorganización de la estructura informativa del inglés C1. Hemos definido la hendida (*it is/was* + foco + *that/who* + cláusula) y su función focalizadora. Hemos trabajado las hendidas de contraste (*it is not X but Y that*), temporales, locativas, causales, interrogativas y negativas. Hemos estudiado la pseudohendida (*what* + cláusula + *is/was* + foco) y su variante inversa. Hemos visto las pseudohendidas lexicalizadas con *all*, *the thing*, *the reason why*, *the way*. Hemos abordado la concordancia del verbo *be* con el foco en las pseudohendidas. Hemos relacionado la hendida con la prosodia fónica y con la distribución de registro: hendidas más formales y escritas, pseudohendidas más naturales y habladas. Y hemos catalogado los tres errores más frecuentes: omisión del relativo, uso de *what* por *that*, y ruptura de concordancia. El usuario C1 domina las hendidas como herramientas de arquitectura informacional y las despliega para enfatizar, contrastar y corregir con precisión sintáctica. En la siguiente unidad abordaremos el hedging y la postura, donde la atenuación académica converge con el posicionamiento autoral.
