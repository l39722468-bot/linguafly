## Parte II — Ejercicios

### Ejercicio 1 — Elipsis en cláusulas coordinadas

Omite el elemento recuperable para evitar repetición.

1. The first image is sharp; the second image is blurred.
2. Some photographs document; others photographs interpret.
3. He shot in black and white; she shot in colour.
4. The foreground is in focus; the background is not in focus.
5. The first frame is tight; the second frame is loose.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. The first image is sharp; the second**,** blurred. (se omite *is*)
2. Some photographs document; others**,** interpret. (se omite *photographs*; el verbo se sobreentiende por paralelismo)
3. He shot in black and white; she**,** in colour. (se omite *shot*)
4. The foreground is in focus; the background**,** not. (se omite *is in focus*)
5. The first frame is tight; the second**,** loose. (se omite *is*)

La elipsis omite lo recuperable por el contexto paralelo; densifica el texto sin ambigüedad.

</details>

### Ejercicio 2 — Sustitución con one/ones

Sustituye el sustantivo repetido por *one/ones*.

1. This portrait is stark; the portrait beside it is softer.
2. He favours muted tones; I prefer vibrant tones.
3. The first crop is tight; the second crop is loose.
4. I prefer the black-and-white image to the colour image.
5. These frames are sharp; those frames are blurred.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. This portrait is stark; the **one** beside it is softer.
2. He favours muted tones; I prefer vibrant **ones**.
3. The first crop is tight; the second **one** is loose.
4. I prefer the black-and-white image to the colour **one**.
5. These frames are sharp; those **ones** are blurred.

*One* sustituye un sustantivo contable singular; *ones*, plural. Evita la repetición manteniendo la referencia.

</details>

### Ejercicio 3 — Sustitución con do so / so

Sustituye con *do so* o *so*.

1. She cropped tightly; he cropped tightly rarely.
2. —Is the image ambiguous? —I think it is ambiguous.
3. He frames decisively; she frames decisively too.
4. —Does the photo imply a departure? —It implies a departure.
5. He works in low-key; she works in low-key as well.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. She cropped tightly; he **did so** rarely.
2. —Is the image ambiguous? —I think **so**.
3. He frames decisively; she **does so** too.
4. —Does the photo imply a departure? —It **does so** (o *Yes, it does*).
5. He works in low-key; she **does so** as well.

*Do so* sustituye un predicado verbal; *so* sustituye una cláusula completa. Cohesionan sin repetición.

</details>

### Ejercicio 4 — Inferencia visual-textual

Reescribe describiendo lo visible e infiriendo lo ausente con un verbo de inferencia.

1. The room is empty and it looks like someone left fast.
2. The light is cool and it looks like early morning.
3. The figure is out of focus and it seems like a witness we don't see.
4. The grain is coarse and it feels like a memory.
5. The glass is tilted and it seems someone put it down in a hurry.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. The empty room **implies** a hasty departure.
2. The cool light **suggests** early morning.
3. The out-of-focus figure **hints at** an unseen witness.
4. The coarse grain **evokes** a memory rather than a record.
5. The tilted glass **implies** a hand set down in haste.

Verbos de inferencia (*imply, suggest, hint at, evoke*) separan lo visible de lo deducido con cautela.

</details>

### Ejercicio 5 — Grados de certeza en la inferencia

Elige el verbo adecuado según el grado de certeza (*shows / implies / hints at / invites*).

1. The photo ___ an empty chair. (hecho visible)
2. The empty chair ___ a departure. (inferencia probable)
3. The grain ___ a memory. (inferencia débil)
4. The image ___ the viewer to infer a story. (interpretación abierta)
5. The tilted glass ___ haste. (inferencia probable)

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. The photo **shows** an empty chair. (hecho visible, certeza)
2. The empty chair **implies** a departure. (inferencia probable)
3. The grain **hints at** a memory. (inferencia débil)
4. The image **invites** the viewer to infer a story. (interpretación abierta)
5. The tilted glass **implies** (o *suggests*) haste. (inferencia probable)

*Shows* = certeza visible; *implies/suggests* = inferencia probable; *hints at/evokes* = inferencia débil; *invites* = interpretación abierta al espectador.

</details>

### Ejercicio 6 — Vocabulario fotográfico (definiciones)

Empareja cada término con su definición.

Términos: *depth of field / bokeh / leading lines / juxtaposition / grain / long exposure*
1. Zona de la imagen que aparece nítida, delante y detrás del foco.
2. Calidad estética del desenfoque en las zonas fuera de foco.
3. Líneas de la composición que dirigen la mirada hacia el sujeto.
4. Aproximación de dos elementos contrastados en el mismo encuadre.
5. Textura granulada visible en la película o en alto ISO.
6. Captura con obturador abierto largo tiempo, ideal para luz débil o estelas.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **depth of field** — Zona de la imagen que aparece nítida, delante y detrás del foco.
2. **bokeh** — Calidad estética del desenfoque en las zonas fuera de foco.
3. **leading lines** — Líneas de la composición que dirigen la mirada hacia el sujeto.
4. **juxtaposition** — Aproximación de dos elementos contrastados en el mismo encuadre.
5. **grain** — Textura granulada visible en la película o en alto ISO.
6. **long exposure** — Captura con obturador abierto largo tiempo, ideal para luz débil o estelas.

</details>

### Ejercicio 7 — Adjetivos evaluativos fotográficos

Sustituye el adjetivo B2 por el C1 adecuado.

1. The photo is nice and quiet. → It is ___ and ___.
2. The image is bad and confused. → It is ___ and ___.
3. The light is strong and clear. → It is ___.
4. The photo is edited too much. → It is ___.
5. The picture is full of light. → It is ___.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. It is **evocative** and **hushed**.
2. It is **muddled** (o *inert*) and **derivative**.
3. It is **stark** (o *precise*).
4. It is **over-processed**.
5. It is **luminous**.

Los adjetivos C1 nombran el modo del efecto visual, no solo si gusta.

</details>

### Ejercicio 8 — Colocaciones del discurso fotográfico

Completa con la colocación adecuada.

1. The empty room ___ a departure. (insinúa una partida)
2. The shallow depth of field ___ the subject. (aísla al sujeto)
3. The grain ___ a memory. (evoca un recuerdo)
4. The composition ___ the eye to the figure. (dirige la mirada)
5. The image ___ the decisive instant. (captura el instante decisivo)
6. The photo ___ open the question of who left. (deja abierta)

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. The empty room **implies** a departure.
2. The shallow depth of field **isolates** the subject.
3. The grain **evokes** a memory.
4. The composition **draws** the eye to the figure.
5. The image **captures** the decisive instant.
6. The photo **leaves** open the question of who left.

*Imply a departure, isolate the subject, evoke a memory, draw the eye, capture the decisive instant, leave open* son colocaciones habituales de la crítica fotográfica.

</details>

### Ejercicio 9 — Corrige los errores

Cada frase tiene un error de registro. Corrígelo.

1. The photo shows sadness. (emoción como hecho)
2. The first image is sharp and the second image is blurred. (repetición)
3. The photo is good and interesting. (demasiado genérico)
4. The portrait one beside it is softer. (sustitución redundante)
5. The depth of field are shallow. (concordancia)

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. The photo **implies** (o *suggests / evokes*) sadness (verbo de inferencia, no *shows* que afirmaría la emoción como hecho visible).
2. The first image is sharp; the second**,** blurred (elipsis para evitar repetición).
3. The photo is **evocative and hushed** (adjetivos C1 concretos).
4. The **portrait** beside it is softer (si se introduce por primera vez, no se sustituye; *one* solo sustituye un sustantivo ya mencionado: *this portrait is stark; the one beside it...*).
5. The depth of field **is** shallow (sustantivo singular, verbo singular).

</details>

### Ejercicio 10 — Transformación (CAE-style)

Reescribe con la palabra dada.

1. The first image is sharp; the second is blurred. (SHARP) → The first ___; the second, ___
2. This portrait is stark; the one beside it is softer. (ONE) → This portrait is stark; the ___
3. The empty room implies a departure. (IMPLIES) → A departure ___
4. The photo is ambiguous, I think. (SO) → I think ___
5. She cropped tightly; he rarely did. (DID) → She cropped tightly; he ___

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. The first **is sharp**; the second**,** **blurred** (elipsis).
2. This portrait is stark; the **one** beside it is softer.
3. A departure **is implied by** the empty room (pasiva) o *what the empty room implies is a departure* (cleft).
4. I think **so**.
5. She cropped tightly; he **rarely did so** (o *he did so rarely*).

Cada reescritura conserva el significado usando elipsis, sustitución o reordenamiento objetivo.

</details>

### Ejercicio 11 — Vocabulario fotográfico (gap-fill)

Completa con: *aperture / contrast / negative space / crop / shutter speed / focal point*.

1. A wide ___ produces a shallow depth of field.
2. The high ___ between light and shadow dramatises the scene.
3. The vast ___ around the figure underscores her isolation.
4. A tight ___ removes all distraction and centres the eye.
5. A fast ___ freezes motion; a slow one blurs it.
6. The leading lines converge on the ___ in the lower third.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. A wide **aperture** produces a shallow depth of field.
2. The high **contrast** between light and shadow dramatises the scene.
3. The vast **negative space** around the figure underscores her isolation.
4. A tight **crop** removes all distraction and centres the eye.
5. A fast **shutter speed** freezes motion; a slow one blurs it.
6. The leading lines converge on the **focal point** in the lower third.

</details>

### Ejercicio 13 — Reading

Lee el fragmento y responde.

> *The image is hushed: an empty chair, a glass tilted at an angle that implies a hand set down in haste, and beyond the window, a street drained of light. The shallow depth of field isolates the chair against a soft, low-key background, while the grain —coarse, almost tactile— evokes a memory rather than a record. What the frame excludes, the figure who has just left, is as legible as what it shows; the photograph does not document a departure so much as invite the viewer to infer one.*

1. ¿Qué recurso cohesionador aparece en *an empty chair, a glass tilted...*?
2. ¿Qué verbo de inferencia relaciona el vaso inclinado con una partida apresurada?
3. ¿Qué dos recursos técnicos aislan la silla del fondo?
4. ¿Qué verbo de grado de certeza introduce la interpretación abierta final?
5. ¿Qué papel juega lo que el marco excluye según el texto?

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **Elipsis**: la serie *an empty chair, a glass tilted...* omite verbos y artículos por paralelismo, densificando la descripción.
2. *implies* (*a glass tilted at an angle that implies a hand set down in haste*): inferencia probable a partir de un detalle visible.
3. La **shallow depth of field** y el **low-key background** (fondo suave y de tonos apagados) aíslan la silla.
4. *invite* (*invite the viewer to infer one*): verbo de interpretación abierta, que deja la deducción al espectador.
5. Lo excluido (*the figure who has just left*) es **tan legible como lo mostrado**: en fotografía, lo que el marco omite es parte del relato, y el buen texto lo alude para completar la inferencia.

</details>

### Ejercicio 14 — Listening (paráfrasis)

Imagina que escuchas a un crítico decir: *"It's what's not there that does the work — the empty chair, the tilted glass. The frame doesn't show a departure; it lets you infer one, and that's far more powerful."* Parafrasea en una frase con elipsis y un verbo de inferencia.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

Modelo: *The hushed image —an empty chair, a tilted glass— implies a departure the frame declines to show, inviting the viewer to infer what it withholds.*

Elementos: elipsis (*an empty chair, a tilted glass*), verbo de inferencia (*implies, inviting to infer*), registro evaluativo (*hushed, declines to show, withholds*).

</details>

### Ejercicio 15 — Writing

Escribe una descripción e inferencia breves (120-150 palabras) de una fotografía real o imaginada que incluya: una elipsis, una sustitución con *one/ones*, un verbo de inferencia y un adjetivo evaluativo C1.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

Modelo (146 palabras):

*The image is hushed: an empty chair, a glass tilted at an angle that implies a hand set down in haste, and beyond the window, a street drained of light. The shallow depth of field isolates the chair against a soft, low-key background, while the grain —coarse, almost tactile— evokes a memory rather than a record. The first frame is tight; the second, looser, and this one lets the negative space do the interpretive work. What the frame excludes, the figure who has just left, is as legible as what it shows; the photograph does not document a departure so much as invite the viewer to infer one. If the crop of the third image teeters on the sentimental, it is a minor flaw in a series that commands attention through what it withholds. A stark, luminous set that lingers.*

Rúbrica: elipsis (*an empty chair, a glass tilted...*; *the first is tight; the second, looser*), sustitución (*this one*), verbo de inferencia (*implies, evokes, invite to infer*), adjetivos C1 (*hushed, stark, luminous, coarse, low-key*).

</details>

### Ejercicio 16 — Speaking (monólogo)

Habla durante dos minutos valorando una fotografía que conozcas, usando al menos: una elipsis, un verbo de inferencia y dos adjetivos evaluativos C1. Grábate y comprueba que describes lo visible e infieres lo ausente, sin sobreexplicar.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

Modelo de respuesta oral:

*The image is hushed: an empty chair, a tilted glass, and beyond the window, a street with no one on it. The shallow depth of field isolates the chair — it's almost tactile, the grain is coarse. The first thing you notice is what's not there. The frame implies a departure, it doesn't show one, and that's the whole point. I'd call it a stark, luminous picture. Where a documentary shot would record the moment, this one invites you to infer it, and the negative space does half the work. If anything, the crop borders on the sentimental, but the restraint of the lighting saves it. What lingers is the absence — the figure who's just left is as legible as the chair they left behind.*

Criterios de la rúbrica: elipsis (*an empty chair, a tilted glass*), verbo de inferencia (*implies, invites you to infer*), adjetivos C1 (*hushed, stark, luminous, tactile, restrained*) y descripción visible + inferencia ausente (*what's not there, the figure who's just left*) sin sobreexplicar.

</details>

### Ejercicio 17 — Registro: pie de foto vs. ensayo crítico

Reescribe el pie de foto en estilo de ensayo crítico (más nominalizado, inferencial, sin primera persona).

Pie de foto: *Empty chair, Paris, 1962. Someone left in a hurry.*

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

Modelo de ensayo: *The empty chair, isolated by the shallow depth of field and framed against a hushed, low-key street, implies a departure the photographer declines to document; the tilted glass, coarse grain and drained light conspire to invite the viewer to infer a narrative the frame withholds, thereby elevating absence into the image's governing motif.*

Cambios: paso de dato factual a interpretación; nominalizaciones (*the shallow depth of field, the tilted glass, drained light*); verbos de inferencia (*implies, invite to infer*); registro más abstracto (*elevating absence into the image's governing motif*).

</details>

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

2. **implies** (o *suggests*)
3. **isolates** (o *separates*)
4. **memory** (o *recollection*)
5. **looser** (elipsis: se omite *is*)
6. **one** (sustitución de *frame*)

El párrafo integra elipsis, sustitución, verbo de inferencia y vocabulario técnico.

</details>

### Ejercicio 19 — Reescribe el párrafo con registro C1

Eleva el registro del párrafo neutro con una elipsis, un verbo de inferencia y una sustitución con *one/ones*.

> *The photo shows a room. The room is empty and there is a chair and a glass. It looks like someone left fast. The first photo is tight and the second photo is loose.*

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

Modelo: *The hushed image —an empty chair, a tilted glass— implies a hasty departure; the first frame is tight, the second looser, and this one lets the shallow depth of field isolate the chair against a low-key background, inviting the viewer to infer what the frame withholds.*

Rúbrica: elipsis (*an empty chair, a tilted glass*; *the first is tight, the second looser*), verbo de inferencia (*implies, inviting to infer*), sustitución (*this one*), adjetivos C1 (*hushed, low-key*) y eliminación de *shows, looks like* en favor de descripción visible + inferencia ausente.

</details>

## Resumen de la unidad

- La fotografía es el **arte de la sugerencia**: fija un instante y omite el resto, de modo que el discurso sobre ella es un ejercicio de **inferencia** —describir lo visible y dejar que el lector deduzca lo ausente—.
- La **elipsis** omite elementos recuperables por el contexto (*the first is sharp; the second, blurred*) y la **sustitución** los reemplaza por proformas (*one, ones, do so, so*); juntas cohesionan y densifican el texto, marca de C1 frente a la repetición de B2.
- La **inferencia visual-textual** nombra un detalle visible y extrae una conclusión cautelosa con verbos de grado de certeza: *shows* (hecho), *implies/suggests* (probable), *hints at/evokes* (débil), *invites* (interpretación abierta).
- El **léxico evaluativo** nombra el modo del efecto visual: adjetivos matizados (*stark, luminous, hushed, evocative, ambiguous, over-processed, flat*) y colocaciones (*imply a departure, isolate the subject, evoke a memory, draw the eye, capture the decisive instant, leave open*).
- El **vocabulario fotográfico** (*composition, exposure, depth of field, framing, juxtaposition, focal point, negative space, grain, contrast, leading lines, shutter speed, aperture, bokeh*) constituye el sustrato técnico de toda descripción precisa; aludir a lo que el marco excluye completa el relato visual que la imagen solo sugiere.
