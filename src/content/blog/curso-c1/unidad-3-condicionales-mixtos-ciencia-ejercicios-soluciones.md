## Parte II — Ejercicios con soluciones

### Ejercicio 1 — Identifica el tiempo del resultado

Indica si cada frase es **past to present** o **present to past**.

1. *If she had accepted the grant, she would be leading the laboratory now.*
2. *If the ethics review were stricter, fewer papers would have been published last year.*
3. *If we had backed up the dataset, we would not be rebuilding it today.*
4. *If the algorithm were transparent, users would have trusted it more.*

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **Past to present**: aceptar la beca pertenece al pasado; dirigir el laboratorio es una situación actual.
2. **Present to past**: la revisión actual o hipotética se conecta con publicaciones pasadas.
3. **Past to present**: la copia de seguridad no se hizo; la reconstrucción ocurre ahora.
4. **Present to past**: la transparencia es una condición hipotética; la confianza se refiere al pasado.

</details>

### Ejercicio 2 — Completa el mixed conditional

1. If I had studied biomedical engineering, I ___ ___ a clinical trial coordinator today.
2. If we had calibrated the sensors earlier, the model ___ ___ drifting now.
3. If the laboratory were better funded, it ___ ___ completed the trial on time.
4. If the protocol had been preregistered, we ___ ___ facing this crisis now.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **would be**.
2. **would not be**.
3. **could have**.
4. **would not be**.

Las dos primeras frases tienen resultado presente; la tercera tiene resultado pasado; la cuarta vuelve al presente con *now*.

</details>

### Ejercicio 3 — Elige la opción correcta

1. If the team had shared the code, other researchers (*would reproduce / could be reproducing / would have reproduced*) the results now.
2. If the device were more reliable, it (*would not have failed / would not fail / will not fail*) during last year’s trial.
3. If she had taken the fellowship, she (*would work / would be working / would have worked*) in Geneva today.
4. If the company had protected its patent, it (*would own / would have owned*) the technology now.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **could be reproducing** — capacidad hipotética en curso.
2. **would not have failed** — resultado pasado.
3. **would be working** — actividad presente.
4. **would own** — posesión actual.

</details>

### Ejercicio 4 — Corrige los errores

1. *If she would have received the grant, she would be a professor now.*
2. *If the sensors had been calibrated, the model would have been stable now.*
3. *If the review process was stricter, fewer studies would be rejected last year.*
4. *Had the team shared the logs, we can diagnose the fault now.*

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **If she had received the grant, she would be a professor now.**
2. **If the sensors had been calibrated, the model would be stable now.**
3. **If the review process were stricter, fewer studies would have been rejected last year.**
4. **Had the team shared the logs, we could diagnose the fault now.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 5 — Tercer condicional o mixed

Elige la continuación adecuada.

1. If they had tested the prototype properly, the machine ___ (would not have broken / would not be breaking) during yesterday’s demonstration.
2. If they had tested the prototype properly, the machine ___ (would not have broken / would not be breaking) down every week now.
3. If the researchers had used a larger sample, the findings ___ (would be more reliable now / would have been more reliable in the original study).
4. If the researchers had used a larger sample, the findings ___ (would be more reliable now / would have been more reliable in the original study) last year.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **would not have broken** — *yesterday* marca un resultado pasado.
2. **would not be breaking** — *now* marca una consecuencia presente.
3. **would be more reliable now** — mixed, pasado hacia presente.
4. **would have been more reliable in the original study** — third conditional.

</details>

### Ejercicio 6 — Modales de consecuencia

Completa con **would**, **could** o **might** y explica el matiz.

1. If the code had been documented, new developers ___ understand it more easily now.
2. If the study were replicated, the conclusion ___ become more credible.
3. If the equipment had arrived earlier, the team ___ have completed the experiment.
4. If the data had been anonymised, the breach ___ not have occurred.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **could** — capacidad actual.
2. **might** — posibilidad futura o hipotética.
3. **could** — oportunidad o capacidad pasada.
4. **would** — consecuencia presentada como lógica del escenario.

En varios contextos puede haber más de una opción si cambia la intención. La explicación debe coincidir con el modal elegido.

</details>

### Ejercicio 7 — Vocabulario científico

Completa con **dataset, peer review, bias, prototype, breakthrough**.

1. The team tested an early ___ before manufacturing the final device.
2. Independent experts examine a paper during ___ .
3. A distorted sampling method can introduce ___ .
4. The researchers cleaned the ___ before analysing it.
5. The discovery was described as a major ___ .

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **prototype**; 2. **peer review**; 3. **bias**; 4. **dataset**; 5. **breakthrough**.

</details>

### Ejercicio 8 — Colocaciones

Elige el verbo correcto: **conduct, collect, draw, secure, replicate**.

1. ___ a clinical trial
2. ___ data
3. ___ a conclusion
4. ___ research funding
5. ___ a finding

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **conduct a clinical trial**.
2. **collect data**.
3. **draw a conclusion**.
4. **secure research funding**.
5. **replicate a finding**.

</details>

### Ejercicio 9 — Reading

Lee el texto y responde.

> The Northbridge team developed a low-cost sensor for monitoring water quality. The prototype worked well in controlled conditions, but it produced inconsistent readings in the field. The researchers had not calibrated it for sudden temperature changes, and they had not preregistered the testing protocol. As a result, they are now repeating part of the experiment and facing questions about the reliability of the original data. If the team had calibrated the sensors earlier, it would be analysing a more stable dataset now. If the protocol were more transparent, independent laboratories could have assessed the method last year. The team has released the code and plans a new trial with a larger sample.

1. What problem did the sensor have in the field?
2. What two preparation steps had the researchers missed?
3. What are they doing now?
4. Write the two mixed conditionals from the text in your own words.
5. What changes are planned for the new trial?

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **It produced inconsistent readings when conditions changed.**
2. **They had not calibrated it for temperature changes and had not preregistered the protocol.**
3. **They are repeating part of the experiment and responding to questions about reliability.**
4. **If they had calibrated the sensors, they would be analysing a more stable dataset now; if the protocol were more transparent, independent laboratories could have assessed it last year.**
5. **They have released the code and plan to use a larger sample.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 10 — Listening guiado

Guion: “If the research team had shared the training logs, we would understand why the model is drifting now. The system might have performed better last year if the data had been balanced. However, if the engineers were less concerned with speed, they could have tested the safeguards more thoroughly before deployment.”

Identifica la condición y el resultado en cada frase, y señala si el resultado es presente o pasado.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **Had shared the training logs** es la condición pasada; **would understand** es el resultado presente.
2. **Had been balanced** es la condición pasada; **might have performed** es el resultado pasado.
3. **Were less concerned with speed** es la condición presente; **could have tested** es el resultado pasado.

</details>

### Ejercicio 11 — Reformulación con inversión

Reescribe usando **Had** o **Were**.

1. If the protocol had been registered, the criticism would be less severe now.
2. If the data were complete, the conclusion would have been stronger.
3. If the engineers had checked the logs, the fault would be easier to diagnose.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **Had the protocol been registered, the criticism would be less severe now.**
2. **Were the data complete, the conclusion would have been stronger.**
3. **Had the engineers checked the logs, the fault would be easier to diagnose.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 12 — Reading de registro académico

Transforma *The experiment failed because the scientists made a bad decision* en una formulación más cautelosa con un mixed conditional.

<details><summary>Modelo</summary>

**If the researchers had selected a different design, the experiment might be producing more reliable results now.**

La versión no acusa directamente y presenta una consecuencia alternativa como posibilidad, no como hecho demostrado.

</details>

### Ejercicio 13 — Selección de evidencia

¿Cuál es más rigurosa?

A. *If the algorithm had been fair, nobody would be complaining now.*

B. *If the algorithm had been audited more thoroughly, fewer users might be reporting problems now.*

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

**B.** Es más rigurosa porque especifica una acción verificable, limita la cantidad con *fewer* y usa *might* en lugar de garantizar un resultado absoluto.

</details>

### Ejercicio 14 — Writing

Escribe 140–170 palabras sobre: **“Should scientific code be open to the public?”** Incluye un mixed conditional, dos colocaciones científicas y una limitación.

<details><summary>Modelo</summary>

Scientific code should generally be available for inspection, although access may need to be managed when privacy or security is involved. If research teams had shared their code more consistently, independent experts would be able to replicate more findings now. Open code can reveal hidden assumptions, reduce certain forms of bias and make peer review more meaningful. It can also help early-career researchers learn how complex analyses are conducted. However, publication alone does not guarantee reliable research. If a dataset contains personal information, the code might have been shared without the raw data, provided that the documentation were sufficiently detailed. Moreover, some models are difficult to reproduce because they depend on proprietary hardware. Open science is therefore a strong principle, but it should be combined with data protection, clear licensing and realistic documentation.

</details>

### Ejercicio 15 — Speaking

Habla durante un minuto sobre una tecnología que habría sido diferente si se hubiera tomado otra decisión. Usa **If + past perfect**, **would be/could be/might be** y vocabulario de la unidad.

<details><summary>Pista y modelo</summary>

**If the platform had been designed with privacy in mind from the beginning, it would be safer now. The developers might be collecting less personal data, and users could be more confident about the system. However, the current problems may also have resulted from later commercial decisions.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 16 — Traducción controlada

Traduce al inglés.

1. Si hubieran protegido la privacidad, ahora tendrían más confianza de los usuarios.
2. Si el modelo fuera menos sesgado, habría producido resultados más justos el año pasado.
3. Si hubiéramos compartido el conjunto de datos, otros investigadores podrían estar replicando el estudio ahora.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **If they had protected privacy, they would have more user trust now.**
2. **If the model were less biased, it would have produced fairer results last year.**
3. **If we had shared the dataset, other researchers could be replicating the study now.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 17 — Revisión de tiempos

Completa el párrafo.

> The laboratory is under pressure. If it ___ (receive) more funding in the past, it ___ (have) better equipment now. If its review process ___ (be) more transparent, an independent team ___ (replicate) the experiment last year. The director admits that the group ___ (should / preregister) the study before collecting data.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

**had received; would have; were; could have replicated; should have preregistered.** La primera oración es pasado hacia presente; la segunda, presente hacia pasado; la última expresa una crítica sobre una acción pasada.

</details>

### Ejercicio 18 — Mini-test final

Elige la opción correcta.

1. If she had studied computing, she ___ a software engineer now. (*would be / would have been*)
2. If the device were safer, fewer users ___ injured in the previous trial. (*would be / would have been*)
3. ___ the code been documented, we could maintain the system now. (*Had / Were*)
4. If the sample had been larger, the findings ___ more reliable today. (*would be / would have been*)
5. If the algorithm were transparent, researchers ___ it last year. (*could have audited / could audit*)

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **would be**.
2. **would have been**.
3. **Had**.
4. **would be**.
5. **could have audited**.

</details>

## Resumen de la unidad

Para construir un mixed conditional, separa siempre los dos relojes de la frase. Si la condición pertenece al pasado y la consecuencia al presente, usa **if + past perfect** y **would/could/might + base verb**: *If the team had acted earlier, it would be safer now*. Si la condición presente explica un resultado pasado, usa **if + past simple/were** y **would/could/might have + V3**: *If the system were safer, fewer people would have been affected*.

Después comprueba cuatro puntos: el participio de la cláusula *if*, el tiempo del resultado, el modal que expresa el grado de certeza y la colocación científica que acompaña al verbo. La estructura será más útil si la empleas para analizar evidencia sin presentar una hipótesis como hecho. Así, los mixed conditionals permiten hablar de ciencia y tecnología con precisión temporal, rigor académico y una voz propia de C1.
