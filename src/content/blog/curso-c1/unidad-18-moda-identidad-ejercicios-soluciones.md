Este cuaderno reúne los **ejercicios de la Unidad 18 del curso C1** con soluciones. Haz cada bloque sin mirar la respuesta.

> **Guía teórica:** [Inversión para Énfasis en Inglés C1](/blog/curso-c1/unidad-18-moda-identidad)
> **Más unidades de este nivel:** [Curso C1](/blog/curso-c1)

### Cómo usar este cuaderno

Responde primero y comprueba después. La explicación está pensada para el estudiante hispanohablante.

## Parte II — Ejercicios

### Ejercicio 1 — Inversión con adverbios negativos

Reescribe empezando por el adverbio negativo, con inversión.

1. She had never witnessed such a spectacle.
2. Critics rarely agree so completely.
3. They little knew that the line would be withdrawn.
4. The contradiction is nowhere clearer than in luxury marketing.
5. Consumers seldom question the supply chain.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **Never had she witnessed such a spectacle.**
2. **Rarely do critics agree so completely.**
3. **Little did they know that the line would be withdrawn.**
4. **Nowhere is the contradiction clearer than in luxury marketing.**
5. **Seldom do consumers question the supply chain.**

Con tiempos simples se introduce *do/does/did*; con tiempos compuestos, el auxiliar propio se adelanta al sujeto.

</details>

### Ejercicio 2 — No sooner / Hardly

Completa con la forma correcta (inversión + conjunción).

1. No sooner ___ the collection ___ (launch) than it sold out.
2. Hardly ___ the show ___ (end) when the criticism began.
3. Scarcely ___ the model ___ (step) off the runway when the backlash started.
4. No sooner ___ the brand ___ (apologise) than a new leak emerged.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **No sooner had the collection launched than it sold out.**
2. **Hardly had the show ended when the criticism began.**
3. **Scarcely had the model stepped off the runway when the backlash started.**
4. **No sooner had the brand apologised than a new leak emerged.**

*No sooner* → *than*; *hardly/scarcely* → *when*. La cláusula invertida va en past perfect.

</details>

### Ejercicio 3 — Not only ... but also

Une las ideas con inversión en la primera cláusula.

1. The brand copied the design. It also denied any wrongdoing.
2. Fast fashion is cheap. It is also ruinously expensive for the planet.
3. The campaign perpetuates stereotypes. It frames them as empowerment.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **Not only did the brand copy the design, but it also denied any wrongdoing.**
2. **Not only is fast fashion cheap, but it is also ruinously expensive for the planet.**
3. **Not only does the campaign perpetuate stereotypes, but it also frames them as empowerment.**

Solo se invierte la primera cláusula; la segunda mantiene el orden normal con *but (also)*.

</details>

### Ejercicio 4 — Not until

Reescribe con *Not until* al inicio.

1. Consumers grasped the true cost only after the documentary aired.
2. Streetwear entered high fashion only in the 1990s.
3. The labels opened their books only when regulators threatened fines.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **Not until the documentary aired did consumers grasp the true cost.**
2. **Not until the 1990s did streetwear enter high fashion.**
3. **Not until regulators threatened fines did the labels open their books.**

La subordinada tras *not until* conserva el orden; la cláusula principal se invierte.

</details>

### Ejercicio 5 — Only by / Only when

Transforma con inversión.

1. Consumers can force change only by boycotting the brand.
2. The scale of the waste emerged only when the labels opened their books.
3. She grasped the cynicism of the campaign only then.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **Only by boycotting the brand can consumers force change.**
2. **Only when the labels opened their books did the scale of the waste emerge.**
3. **Only then did she grasp the cynicism of the campaign.**

La inversión afecta a la cláusula principal, no a la subordinada de *only when*.

</details>

### Ejercicio 6 — So / Such ... that

Reescribe con inversión para intensificar.

1. The trend was so pervasive that no wardrobe remained untouched.
2. The backlash was such that the brand withdrew the line within a week.
3. The style spread so quickly that it became a uniform within months.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **So pervasive was the trend that no wardrobe remained untouched.**
2. **Such was the backlash that the brand withdrew the line within a week.**
3. **So quickly did the style spread that within months it had become a uniform.**

*So + adj/adverb* o *such + sintagma nominal* se adelantan; el auxiliar precede al sujeto.

</details>

### Ejercicio 7 — Inversión condicional

Reescribe omitiendo *if*.

1. If the brand had disclosed its supply chain, the scandal would never have erupted.
2. If I were to design it again, I would choose organic fibres.
3. If you should wish to return the item, a full refund is available.
4. If we had known the human cost, we would not have bought it.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **Had the brand disclosed its supply chain, the scandal would never have erupted.**
2. **Were I to design it again, I would choose organic fibres.**
3. **Should you wish to return the item, a full refund is available.**
4. **Had we known the human cost, we would not have bought it.**

*Had* (pasado), *were* (presente irreal) y *should* (remota posibilidad) sustituyen a *if* y se adelantan al sujeto.

</details>

### Ejercicio 8 — Corrige los errores

Cada frase tiene un error de inversión; corrígelo.

1. Never she had seen such waste.
2. Seldom a trend causes such debate.
3. No sooner it had launched than it sold out.
4. Not only the design is derivative, but it is also unethical.
5. Only by we boycott the brand can we force change.
6. Hardly had the show ended than the criticism began.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **Never had she seen such waste.**
2. **Seldom does a trend cause such debate.**
3. **No sooner had it launched than it sold out.**
4. **Not only is the design derivative, but it is also unethical.**
5. **Only by boycotting the brand can we force change.**
6. **Hardly had the show ended when the criticism began.** (*hardly* → *when*, no *than*).

</details>

### Ejercicio 9 — Transformación (CAE-style)

Reescribe la frase manteniendo el sentido, usando la palabra entre paréntesis.

1. The brand only admitted the problem after the protest. (NOT)
2. As soon as the show finished, the reviews appeared. (NO)
3. I had never seen such a cynical campaign. (NEVER)
4. If the company were transparent, trust would return. (WERE)

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **Not until the protest did the brand admit the problem.** (o *Not until the protest had taken place did...*)
2. **No sooner had the show finished than the reviews appeared.**
3. **Never had I seen such a cynical campaign.**
4. **Were the company transparent, trust would return.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 10 — Vocabulario de moda

Elige el término adecuado.

1. A garment that stands out and "declares" something is a ___ (staple / statement) piece.
2. Fashion that is cheap and rotates quickly is ___ (slow / fast) fashion.
3. A design that imitates others without originality is ___ (groundbreaking / derivative).
4. The spirit of an era captured by fashion is the ___ (zeitgeist / silhouette).
5. Taking elements from a marginalised culture without context is ___ (homage / cultural appropriation).

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **statement piece**; 2. **fast fashion**; 3. **derivative**; 4. **zeitgeist**; 5. **cultural appropriation**.

</details>

### Ejercicio 11 — Colocaciones

Empareja cada verbo con su complemento.

1. set / follow / tap into / push / lay bare / perpetuate / subvert / frame ... as
2. a. the boundaries b. a trend c. the zeitgeist d. stereotypes e. the human cost f. something g. a trend h. expectations

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

- **set** a trend (g) / **follow** a trend (b) / **tap into** the zeitgeist (c) / **push** the boundaries (a) / **lay bare** the human cost (e) / **perpetuate** stereotypes (d) / **subvert** expectations (h) / **frame** something as (f).

</details>

### Ejercicio 12 — Lenguaje evaluativo

Reescribe con tono evaluativo más maduro (usa *arguably, tellingly, perpetuate, subvert, lay bare* según convenga).

1. The campaign shows that the brand is not original.
2. The documentary makes the human cost visible.
3. The brand calls it empowerment, but it is really conformity.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. *The campaign arguably reveals the brand's derivative streak.*
2. *The documentary lays bare the human cost of the industry.*
3. *Tellingly, what the brand frames as empowerment amounts to little more than conformity.*

El uso de *arguably*, *lays bare* y *frames ... as* convierte una opinión llana en crítica matizada.

</details>

### Ejercicio 13 — Combinar inversión y crítica

Escribe una frase que combine: *Not only* + inversión + *but also* + una idea de coste social.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

Modelo: *Not only does fast fashion produce garments at breakneck speed, but it also externalises the environmental cost onto the very communities it markets to.*

La inversión refuerza la primera carga y el *but also* acumula la segunda, lo que da al juicio un ritmo creciente.

</details>

### Ejercicio 14 — Reading

Lee el fragmento y responde.

> *Rarely has a season laid bare the contradictions of the industry so plainly. Not only does the collection borrow motifs from working-class communities, but it repackages them at prices those communities could never meet. Arguably, what is sold as self-expression is conformity in a statement-piece disguise.*

1. ¿Qué dos efectos retóricos produce la primera frase?
2. ¿Qué denuncia el autor con *but it repackages them*?
3. ¿Qué función cumple *arguably*?

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. La inversión con *rarely* da énfasis solemne y sitúa la rareza del hecho al frente, atrayendo la atención.
2. Que la marca se apropia estéticas ajenas y las revende inasequibles a quienes las originaron (apropiación y exclusión económica).
3. Modula la certeza (hedging): presenta la interpretación como razonable pero no como hecho probado, protegiendo al autor de afirmaciones excesivas.

</details>

### Ejercicio 15 — Listening (paráfrasis)

Imagina un fragmento de audio: *"The brand only changed its policy once the boycott began."* Reescribe lo que oíste con inversión y con tu propias palabras.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

Modelo: *Not until the boycott began did the brand change its policy.* / *Only when the boycott got underway did the label revise its stance.*

El ejercicio entrena la reexpresión con inversión, clave para parafrasear inputs orales en C1.

</details>

### Ejercicio 16 — Writing

Escribe un párrafo (60–80 palabras) que critique una campaña de moda, usando al menos: una inversión con *rarely/never*, un *not only... but also* invertido y una condicional invertida con *were*.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

Modelo: *Rarely has a campaign exposed the industry's cynicism so clearly. Not only does the brand market conformity as rebellion, but it also hides the true cost of production behind a sustainability label. Were consumers to look past the slogans, the backlash might at last force genuine reform.* (44 palabras; amplíala con un ejemplo concreto para llegar a 60–80.)

Criterios: inversión correcta, *not only* invertido solo en la primera cláusula, condicional con *were* sin *if*, y coherencia argumentativa.

</details>

### Ejercicio 17 — Speaking (monólogo)

Prepara un monólogo de 2 minutos respondiendo: *"Is fashion a form of self-expression or of conformity?"* Incluye al menos una inversión y una condicional invertida.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

Modelo de guion: *I'd argue that fashion is both. Seldom has a single garment meant only one thing. On one hand, a statement piece can signal belonging to a subculture; on the other, mass production turns novelty into uniformity within months. Were we to judge by marketing alone, we'd conclude it is pure self-expression; but the data suggests otherwise.*

Practica la entonación descendente tras la inversión y la pausa antes de la condicional.

</details>

### Ejercicio 18 — Consolidación

Identifica en el siguiente texto todas las inversiones y clasifícalas (adverbio negativo / not only / only / condicional / so-such).

> *Never had the label faced such scrutiny. Not only did it appropriate working-class imagery, but it also priced the imitations beyond reach. Only by releasing its supplier list could it hope to recover trust. So intense was the backlash that three retailers dropped the line. Were it to repeat the strategy, the brand would not survive.*

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

- *Never had the label faced* → adverbio negativo (*never*).
- *Not only did it appropriate* → *not only ... but also* (inversión en primera cláusula).
- *Only by releasing ... could it hope* → *only by* (inversión en cláusula principal).
- *So intense was the backlash* → *so + adj* (inversión con *so*).
- *Were it to repeat* → condicional invertida (*were*, omisión de *if*).

</details>

## Resumen de la unidad

- La **inversión** adelanta el auxiliar al sujeto tras elementos negativos o restrictivos (*never, rarely, seldom, hardly, no sooner, not only, not until, only by, only when, so/such*) y en condicionales formales sin *if* (*had, were, should*).
- Con tiempos simples se introduce *do/does/did*; con tiempos compuestos, el auxiliar propio se adelanta.
- *No sooner* se completa con *than*; *hardly/scarcely*, con *when*.
- En *not until* y *only when*, la subordinada mantiene el orden y la cláusula principal se invierte.
- La **crítica social de moda** combina inversión, lenguaje evaluativo (*derivative, groundbreaking, perpetuate, subvert, lay bare*), colocaciones (*set/follow a trend, tap into the zeitgeist*) y **hedging** (*arguably, tellingly*) para argumentar con matiz y contundencia.
