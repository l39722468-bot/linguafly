La filosofía de la mente es uno de los territorios más fascinantes del pensamiento contemporáneo, y argumentar en él con propiedad exige una destreza singular: el manejo de la metáfora con precisión conceptual. No basta con decir que *the mind is like a computer* o que *the brain produces thoughts*; hay que distinguir *dualism* de *physicalism*, *qualia* de *functional states*, *intentionality* de *aboutness*, y usar las metáforas explicativas \u2014the mind as a machine, consciousness as an emergent property\u2014 con la conciencia de sus límites que el rigor filosófico demanda. En esta unidad del curso de inglés C1 gratis exploramos cómo los hablantes avanzados manejan el léxico de la filosofía de la mente, cómo emplean la metáfora como instrumento de explicación sin confundirla con la teoría y cómo argumentan sobre la consciencia y el problema cuerpo-mente con la precisión que uno de los debates más difíciles de la filosofía exige. Es un terreno donde el lenguaje debe ser a la vez imaginativo y riguroso: la metáfora ilumina, pero el hablante C1 sabe dónde deja de iluminar.

Para situar el nivel, observa cómo el mismo debate se expresa en tres niveles. En **A2** diríamos: *The brain thinks. The mind is inside the head. It is hard to understand.* Es claro pero plano, sin vocabulario filosófico ni matices. En **B2** subiríamos un peldaño: *Some philosophers think the mind and the body are different. Others think they are the same. Consciousness is still a mystery.* Aquí ya hay posiciones y problema, pero le falta densidad conceptual y conciencia metalingüística. En **C1** alcanzamos la precisión necesaria: *While the metaphor of the mind as a computer has proven heuristically indispensable in organising the functionalist's account of cognition, its very fruitfulness carries a risk that the philosophically careful user must keep in view: that the analogy between software and mental states, however illuminating as a model of information processing, may seduce us into treating as solved the question that the hard problem of consciousness actually poses \u2014 namely, why there is anything it is like to be in such a state at all, a question that no amount of functional decomposition, however complete, appears even to address, let alone to answer.* Esta unidad te enseña a producir ese tercer nivel de forma natural.

## Parte I — Teoría

### 1. Léxico fundamental de la filosofía de la mente

La filosofía de la mente se articula en torno a un léxico fundamental: *mind* (mente), *brain* (cerebro), *consciousness* (consciencia), *mental states* (estados mentales), *qualia* (qualia, las cualidades subjetivas de la experiencia), *intentionality* (intencionalidad, la propiedad de estar "acerca de" algo), *subjective experience* (experiencia subjetiva), *phenomenal character* (carácter fenoménico), *the what-it-is-like* (el "cómo se siente"). Un hablante C1 escribe *the distinction between the functional aspects of a mental state \u2014 what it does, what role it plays in the cognitive economy \u2014 and its phenomenal character \u2014 what it is like, from the inside, to be in that state \u2014 is the distinction that the hard problem of consciousness exploits, for the former admits, in principle, of a complete functional account while the latter, by its very nature, seems to resist any account that does not, at some point, presuppose the very thing it sets out to explain.* Distinguir estos niveles de descripción es la condición previa de cualquier argumento serio.

### 2. El problema cuerpo-mente

El *mind-body problem* (problema cuerpo-mente) es la cuestión central: *substance dualism* (dualismo de sustancias, mente y cuerpo son sustancias distintas, de Descartes), *property dualism* (dualismo de propiedades, una sustancia con propiedades físicas y mentales), *physicalism* o *materialism* (fisicalismo o materialismo, todo es físico), *monism* (monismo, una sola sustancia), *epiphenomenalism* (epifenomenalismo, los estados mentales existen pero no causan), *interaction problem* (problema de la interacción, cómo interactúan sustancias distintas). Un hablante C1 escribe *the interaction problem that has dogged substance dualism since Descartes \u2014 how two substances as different in kind as mind and matter could causally affect one another \u2014 is not merely a technical difficulty but a structural one, for the very intelligibility of causal interaction seems to presuppose some common currency between cause and effect that the radical heterogeneity of the two substances appears to rule out by definition.* El problema de la interacción es el talón de Aquiles del dualismo cartesiano.

### 3. El hard problem de la consciencia

El *hard problem of consciousness* (problema duro de la consciencia) de Chalmers distingue los *easy problems* (explicar funciones cognitivas: atención, integración de información, control) del *hard problem*: por qué hay experiencia subjetiva en absoluto. Léxico: *explanatory gap* (brecha explicativa), *zombie argument* (argumento del zombi, un ser funcionalmente idéntico sin experiencia), *the knowledge argument* (argumento del conocimiento, de Mary la neurocientífica que ve color por primera vez), *Mary's room* (la habitación de Mary). Un hablante C1 escribe *the hard problem, as Chalmers frames it, is not the difficulty of explaining any particular cognitive function \u2014 those are the easy problems, challenging but in principle tractable \u2014 but the question of why the performance of those functions should be accompanied by subjective experience at all, a question whose very formulation suggests that no account framed entirely in functional terms, however complete, will touch the explanandum, for the explanandum is precisely the non-functional residue that the functional account, by its nature, leaves out.* El hard problem apunta a lo que el funcionalismo, por definición, no aborda.

### 4. Funcionalismo y sus variantes

El *functionalism* (funcionalismo) define los estados mentales por su función, no por su material: *functional role* (papel funcional), *multiple realisability* (realizabilidad múltiple, el mismo estado mental en sustratos distintos), *computational theory of mind* (teoría computacional de la mente), *machine functionalism* (funcionalismo de máquina), *psychofunctionalism* (psicofuncionalismo), *representational theory* (teoría representacional). Un hablante C1 escribe *the functionalist's appeal to multiple realisability \u2014 the thought that a mental state is individuated by its causal role rather than by the physical substrate that implements it, so that pain in a human, a Martian and a silicon system could, in principle, be the same state \u2014 is at once the theory's greatest strength and the source of its most serious difficulty, for in severing the mental from any particular physical realisation it risks severing it from the physical altogether, a consequence that the physicalist functionalist must somehow prevent without abandoning the very consideration that made functionalism attractive.* La realizabilidad múltiple es el arma de doble filo del funcionalismo.

### 5. La metáfora en filosofía de la mente: el modelo y la realidad

El uso de la metáfora (*metaphor*) en filosofía de la mente exige distinguir el *model* (modelo explicativo) de la *reality modelled* (la realidad modelada): *the mind as a computer* (la mente como ordenador), *the brain as a machine* (el cerebro como máquina), *consciousness as an emergent property* (la consciencia como propiedad emergente), *the mind as a theatre* (la mente como teatro, del Cartesian theatre de Dennett), *the brain as a prediction machine* (el cerebro como máquina de predicción). Un hablante C1 escribe *the metaphor of the Cartesian theatre \u2014 the picture, which Dennett attributes to our intuitive self-understanding, of a homunculus seated in the mind observing the stream of experience projected on an inner screen \u2014 is not a theory anyone explicitly endorses but a picture that, Dennett argues, covertly structures much of our thinking about consciousness, which is why exposing it as a metaphor rather than a model is a necessary preliminary to any account that would dispense with the inner spectator it presupposes.* La conciencia metalingüística sobre la metáfora es la marca del C1 en este campo.

### 6. Conectores de precisión conceptual

La precisión conceptual emplea conectores que calibran la afirmación: *to be precise* (para ser preciso), *in the strict sense* (en sentido estricto), *properly speaking* (en propiedad), *in the relevant sense* (en el sentido relevante), *strictly construed* (estrictamente interpretado), *as distinct from* (a diferencia de), *not to be confused with* (no debe confundirse con). Un hablante C1 escribe *consciousness, in the strict sense that the hard problem targets, is not to be confused with the cluster of cognitive functions \u2014 attention, access, reportability \u2014 that the easy problems address, for while those functions may be necessary conditions of conscious experience in the creatures we know, they are not identical with it, and the tendency to slide between the two is one that the philosophical literature exists in large part to resist.* Estos conectores previenen la equivocación que arruinaría el argumento.

### 7. Intencionalidad y aboutness

La *intentionality* (intencionalidad) es la *aboutness* (el estar "acerca de") de los estados mentales: *intentional state* (estado intencional), *representational content* (contenido representacional), *directedness* (direccionalidad hacia un objeto), *intentional inexistence* (inexistencia intencional, de Brentano), *propositional attitude* (actitud proposicional: creer que, desear que), *mental representation* (representación mental). Un hablante C1 escribe *the Brentanian thesis that intentionality is the mark of the mental \u2014 that what distinguishes mental states from physical ones is their directedness towards an object, their being about something \u2014 raises the question of whether intentionality can be naturalised, that is, explained in terms that do not themselves presuppose the mentality they set out to account for, a question whose difficulty is compounded by the existence of states, like beliefs about non-existent entities, that have intentionality without an object.* La naturalización de la intencionalidad es uno de los grandes desafíos.

### 8. Qualia y el argumento del conocimiento

El debate sobre los *qualia* (cualidades subjetivas): *qualia* (qualia), *raw feel* (sensación bruta), *phenomenal properties* (propiedades fenoménicas), *inverted qualia* (qualia invertidos: mi rojo es tu verde), *the knowledge argument* (argumento del conocimiento), *what Mary didn't know* (lo que Mary no sabía), *phenomenal concepts* (conceptos fenoménicos). Un hablante C1 escribe *the knowledge argument, through the thought experiment of Mary \u2014 the colour scientist who knows every physical fact about colour vision but has never seen colour, and who, upon first seeing red, appears to learn something new \u2014 purports to show that there are facts about experience that escape the physicalist's inventory, for if Mary knew all the physical facts and yet learns something on leaving the room, then what she learns is, by construction, non-physical, a conclusion the physicalist must rebut by arguing that what Mary acquires is not new knowledge but a new way of accessing knowledge she already had.* El argumento de Mary ataca al fisicalismo desde la primera persona.

### 9. Emergencia y reducción

El debate entre *emergence* (emergencia) y *reduction* (reducción): *emergent property* (propiedad emergente), *reductionism* (reduccionismo), *supervenience* (superveniencia, los cambios mentales dependen de cambios físicos), *identity theory* (teoría de la identidad, los estados mentales son estados cerebrales), *anomalous monism* (monismo anómalo, de Davidson), *reductive vs non-reductive physicalism* (fisicalismo reductivo vs no reductivo). Un hablante C1 escribe *the doctrine of supervenience \u2014 that there can be no mental difference without a physical difference, so that the mental depends on the physical without, in the non-reductive physicalist's view, being reducible to it \u2014 occupies an attractive but unstable position between reductive physicalism, which it is too weak to entail, and property dualism, from which it must struggle to distinguish itself, for if the mental supervenes on the physical but is not reducible to it, one owes an account of what the irreducibility consists in that does not collapse into the dualism it was meant to avoid.* La superveniencia es una posición intermedia y discutida.

### 10. Eliminativismo y relajación del vocabulario mental

El *eliminativism* (eliminativismo) cuestiona el vocabulario mental mismo: *folk psychology* (psicología popular, las creencias y deseos del sentido común), *eliminative materialism* (materialismo eliminativo), *propositional attitudes* (actitudes proposicionales), *theory theory* (teoría de la teoría, la psicología popular como teoría), *simulation theory* (teoría de la simulación). Un hablante C1 escribe *the eliminativist's claim that folk psychology is a theory \u2014 and a theory that, like phlogiston or caloric, may turn out to be radically mistaken about its domain \u2014 is not the modest observation that we sometimes misattribute beliefs but the radical thesis that the categories of belief and desire may not carve the cognitive reality at its joints, a thesis whose plausibility depends on whether the predictive and explanatory success of folk psychology is the kind of success that a fundamentally mistaken theory could enjoy.* El eliminativismo es una de las posiciones más radicales del debate.

### 11. Conectores de matización metafórica

La matización metafórica emplea conectores que señalan los límites de la analogía: *in a sense* (en cierto sentido), *up to a point* (hasta cierto punto), *with the caveat that* (con la salvedad de que), *the analogy breaks down at* (la analogía se rompe en), *the metaphor should not be pressed too far* (la metáfora no debe tomarse demasiado literalmente), *only in a qualified sense* (solo en un sentido cualificado), *to the extent that* (en la medida en que). Un hablante C1 escribe *the computational metaphor is illuminating, up to a point, insofar as it captures the way in which mental states can be individuated by their functional roles; with the caveat, however, that the analogy breaks down at the point where consciousness is concerned, for while a computer processes information, there is nothing it is like, from the inside, to be that processing \u2014 a disanalogy that the metaphor, if pressed too far, obscures precisely where the philosophically interesting question lies.* Estos conectores calibran la extensión de la metáfora.

### 12. La primera persona y el acceso privilegiado

El debate sobre la *first-person perspective* (perspectiva de primera persona) y el *privileged access* (acceso privilegiado): *introspection* (introspección), *first-person authority* (autoridad de primera persona), *incorrigibility* (incorregibilidad), *transparency of the mental* (transparencia de lo mental), *self-knowledge* (autoconocimiento), *the subjective stance* (postura subjetiva). Un hablante C1 escribe *the first-person authority that we ordinarily accord to a subject's avowals of her own mental states \u2014 the presumption that, if she sincerely says she is in pain, she is, barring rare exceptions, right \u2014 is a feature of our epistemic practice that any philosophy of mind must accommodate, though the explanation of that authority is itself contested, with some accounts grounding it in a special mode of access and others in the constitutive role that the avowal plays in fixing the state avowed.* El acceso privilegiado es un dato que toda teoría debe explicar.

### 13. Consciencia de acceso y consciencia fenoménica

La distinción entre *access consciousness* (consciencia de acceso, la disponibilidad para el control verbal y la acción) y *phenomenal consciousness* (consciencia fenoménica, la experiencia subjetiva): *access consciousness* (Block), *phenomenal consciousness* (Block), *global workspace theory* (teoría del espacio de trabajo global, de Baars), *integrated information theory* (teoría de la información integrada, de Tononi), *higher-order theories* (teorías de orden superior). Un hablante C1 escribe *the distinction between access and phenomenal consciousness, if sound, allows the functionalist to account for the former \u2014 which is, by construction, a matter of information availability \u2014 while conceding that the latter poses the hard problem, a division of labour that is attractive to the functionalist precisely because it confines the explanandum that resists functional analysis to a domain that the functionalist can, without inconsistency, leave to one side.* La distinción permite una división del trabajo explicativo.

### 14. Encarnación y cognición extendida

El *embodied cognition* (cognición encarnada) y la *extended mind* (mente extendida): *embodiment* (encarnación), *extended mind thesis* (tesis de la mente extendida, de Clark y Chalmers), *cognitive extension* (extensión cognitiva), *active externalism* (externalismo activo), *coupled system* (sistema acoplado), *4E cognition* (cognición 4E: embodied, embedded, enacted, extended). Un hablante C1 escribe *the extended mind thesis \u2014 that cognitive processes can, under certain conditions, loop out into the environment, so that a notebook used reliably for memory counts, by parity of reasoning, as part of the mind \u2014 challenges the tacit assumption that the boundaries of the cognitive are coextensive with the boundaries of the skin, though whether the parity argument establishes that the external resource is part of the mind or merely that the boundary between mind and world is less sharp than we assumed is a question its proponents and critics continue to contest.* La mente extendida desafía la localización tradicional de lo cognitivo.

### 15. Cohesión en ensayos de filosofía de la mente

La cohesión en ensayos de filosofía de la mente combina precisión conceptual, matización metafórica y conectores lógicos: *the question, properly framed, is* (la pregunta, bien formulada, es), *the distinction that matters is between* (la distinción que importa es entre), *on one construal* (en una interpretación), *on another* (en otra), *the upshot is that* (el resultado es que), *the metaphor is apt insofar as* (la metáfora es adecuada en la medida en que). Un hablante C1 escribe *the question, properly framed, is not whether the mind is like a computer \u2014 a metaphor whose aptness is, in any case, contestable \u2014 but whether the computational model, insofar as it captures the functional individuation of mental states, can address the phenomenal residue that the hard problem identifies, and the upshot is that it can, at best, clarify the former while leaving the latter precisely where it was, which is why the metaphor, however useful as a heuristic, should not be mistaken for a solution to the problem it so elegantly reorganises.* La cohesión filosófica hace visible tanto la estructura del argumento como los límites de la metáfora que lo articula.
