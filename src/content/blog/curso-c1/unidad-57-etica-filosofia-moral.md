La ética no es solo decidir entre el bien y el mal: es el estudio riguroso de por qué consideramos buenas o malas ciertas acciones, qué principios rigen nuestra conducta y cómo justificamos moralmente nuestras decisiones. En el nivel C1, razonar sobre ética en inglés exige precisión léxica, capacidad de construir argumentos con estructura lógica y habilidad para plantear y responder a contraejemplos. Esta unidad te prepara para discutir filosofía moral con el mismo rigor con el que lo haría un estudiante universitario angloparlante.

A2: Ethics is about what is right and wrong. Some people think we must follow rules. Other people think we must think about what happens after our actions. Both ideas are important.

B2: Ethics examines how we decide what is morally right or wrong. Deontologists argue that we must follow universal duties, whereas consequentialists judge actions by their outcomes. Each framework offers a different lens through which to evaluate moral dilemmas.

C1: Ethics, as a branch of philosophy, interrogates the very grounds on which we adjudicate between right and wrong. Deontological frameworks ground moral obligation in universalisable maxims, whereas consequentialist theories evaluate actions purely in terms of their aggregate outcomes. The tension between duty-based and outcome-based reasoning generates the enduring dialectic at the heart of moral philosophy.

## Parte I — Teoría: Ética y filosofía moral

### 1. Las tres grandes tradiciones éticas

La filosofía moral occidental se articula en torno a tres tradiciones principales: la deontología, el consecuencialismo y la ética de las virtudes. Cada una responde de manera distinta a la pregunta fundamental: ¿qué hace que una acción sea moralmente correcta?

A2: There are three main ways to think about ethics. One says follow the rules. Another says look at the results. The third says be a good person.

B2: Three major ethical traditions dominate Western moral philosophy. Deontology focuses on duties and rules, consequentialism evaluates outcomes, and virtue ethics emphasises character. Each tradition offers a coherent but competing account of moral rightness.

C1: Western moral philosophy is conventionally triangulated around three paradigms: deontology, which grounds moral rightness in adherence to universalisable duties; consequentialism, which adjudicates actions solely by their aggregate outcomes; and virtue ethics, which relocates the locus of moral evaluation from acts to character. These paradigms are not merely complementary perspectives but rival frameworks whose internal logic often yields incompatible verdicts on the same dilemma.

### 2. Deontología: el imperativo categórico de Kant

Immanuel Kant sostiene que la moralidad no depende de las consecuencias sino del deber. Su imperativo categórico ordena actuar solo según máximas que uno podría querer que se convirtieran en ley universal. Mentir es siempre inmoral, incluso si la mentira salva una vida, porque universalizar la mentira destruiría la confianza que hace posible la comunicación misma.

A2: Kant says we must always tell the truth. We must do our duty because it is the right thing, not because of what happens after.

B2: For Kant, moral duty is unconditional. The categorical imperative demands that we act only on principles we could will to become universal laws. This means lying is always wrong, even when it might save a life, because a universal duty to lie would undermine trust itself.

C1: Kant's deontology posits that moral obligation is categorically unconditional, deriving not from contingent outcomes but from the rational form of the maxim itself. The categorical imperative commands that one act only on that maxim which one can simultaneously will to be a universal law. This formulation yields a stringent prohibition on lying irrespective of consequences, on the grounds that universalising deceit would self-destructively erode the very fabric of communicative trust upon which rational agency depends.

### 3. Consecuencialismo y utilitarismo

El consecuencialismo sostiene que solo los resultados importan moralmente. El utilitarismo, su variante más influyente, desarrollada por Jeremy Bentham y John Stuart Mill, defiende que la acción correcta es aquella que produce la mayor felicidad para el mayor número de personas. La distinción entre utilitarismo de acto y utilitarismo de regla es crucial: el primero evalúa cada acción individualmente, el segundo evalúa la utilidad de seguir reglas generales.

A2: Utilitarianism says we should do what makes the most people happy. If an action helps many people, it is good.

B2: Utilitarianism, developed by Bentham and Mill, holds that the right action is the one that maximises overall well-being. Act utilitarianism evaluates each action individually, whereas rule utilitarianism assesses the consequences of following general rules.

C1: Consequentialism, in its utilitarian variant elaborated by Bentham and Mill, maintains that the sole morally relevant consideration is the aggregate welfare produced by an action. The distinction between act utilitarianism, which calibrates each individual action by its marginal contribution to overall utility, and rule utilitarianism, which evaluates the expected value of internalising general rules, is pivotal for resolving the theory's most stubborn objections.

### 4. Ética de las virtudes: Aristóteles y la eudaimonia

Aristóteles desplaza el foco ético desde la acción hacia el carácter. La pregunta no es qué debo hacer, sino qué persona debo ser. La eudaimonia, traducida imperfectamente como florecimiento o bienestar, es el fin último de la vida humana. Se alcanza cultivando virtudes mediante la práctica habitual hasta que se vuelven disposiciones estables del carácter.

A2: Aristotle says we should try to be a good person. A good person has good habits. When we practice good things, we become good.

B2: Aristotle's virtue ethics shifts the focus from actions to character. Rather than asking what we should do, it asks what kind of person we should become. Eudaimonia, or flourishing, is achieved by cultivating virtues through habitual practice until they become stable dispositions.

C1: Aristotle's virtue ethics effects a decisive shift from the act-centred framing of deontology and consequentialism toward a character-centred moral ontology. The governing question is not which action to perform but which dispositions to cultivate, with eudaimonia, inadequately rendered as happiness or flourishing, posited as the telos of human life, attained through the habitual exercise of virtuous capacities until they consolidate into stable traits of character.

### 5. El caso del tranvía: dilema clásico

El trolley problem, formulado por Philippa Foot y desarrollado por Judith Jarvis Thomson, es el dilema ético más discutido del siglo XX. Un tranvía sin control se dirige hacia cinco personas. Puedes desviar la vía para que mate a una persona en su lugar. ¿Es correcto hacerlo? El dilema revela la tensión entre el consecuencialismo, que autoriza el desvío, y la deontología, que prohíbe usar a una persona como medio.

A2: The trolley problem is a famous story. A train will hit five people. You can change the track so it hits one person instead. Is that okay?

B2: The trolley problem asks whether it is permissible to divert a runaway trolley, killing one person to save five. Consequentialists say yes, because five lives outweigh one. Deontologists often say no, because actively killing one person treats them as a mere means to an end.

C1: The trolley problem, inaugurated by Foot and refined by Thomson, functions as the exemplary stress test for competing ethical frameworks. The consequentialist verdict, that diverting the trolley is obligatory given the aggregate utility of five lives preserved over one, collides with the deontological prohibition on instrumentalising a person as a mere means, thereby exposing the structural fault line between outcome-based and duty-based reasoning.

### 6. Contraejemplos: la navaja metodológica de la ética

Un contraejemplo es un caso que desafía una teoría mostrando una situación donde la teoría produce una conclusión contraria a nuestra intuición moral. El trolley problem es un contraejemplo del consecuencialismo ingenuo. El caso del tranvía gordo y el violinista de Thomson son contraejemplos diseñados para mostrar los límites de teorías éticas específicas.

A2: A counterexample is a story that shows a theory might be wrong. If the theory says something we feel is bad, maybe the theory has a problem.

B2: A counterexample challenges a theory by presenting a case where its conclusion clashes with our moral intuition. Philosophers construct counterexamples deliberately to expose the limits of a framework. The trolley problem and Thomson's violinist case are classic instances.

C1: A counterexample operates as a methodological scalpel in moral philosophy, isolating a case in which a theory's prescribed verdict diverges sharply from considered moral judgement. The deliberate construction of counterexamples, exemplified by the fat man variant and Thomson's violinist thought experiment, serves not to refute a theory outright but to map its counterintuitive implications and thereby force its refinement or abandonment.

### 7. El violinista de Thomson y el argumento del aborto

Judith Jarvis Thomson propone un experimento mental: despiertas conectada a un violinista famoso que necesita tus riñones para sobrevivir nueve meses. ¿Estás obligada a mantenerlo conectado? Thomson argumenta que no, aunque reconozcas su derecho a la vida. Este caso pretende mostrar que el derecho a la vida no implica el derecho a usar el cuerpo de otra persona, un argumento influyente en el debate sobre el aborto.

A2: Thomson asks: if someone needs your body to live, must you help them? She says maybe you are kind if you help, but you do not have to.

B2: Thomson's violinist thought experiment imagines you waking up connected to a famous violinist who needs your kidneys for nine months. She argues that even if the violinist has a right to life, he has no right to use your body without your consent, a parallel she draws to debates about abortion.

C1: Thomson's violinist case constructs a carefully calibrated analogy: you awaken medically attached to an unconscious violinist whose survival depends on nine months of circulatory support from your body. The case is engineered to demonstrate that the right to life, even when conceded, does not entail a derivative right to commandeer another person's bodily resources, thereby furnishing a prima facie justification for permissive abortion policy that bypasses the contested status of the foetus.

### 8. El dilema de Heinz: las etapas morales de Kohlberg

Lawrence Kohlberg usa el dilema de Heinz, un hombre que roba medicamento para salvar a su esposa, para estudiar el desarrollo moral. Identifica seis etapas en tres niveles: preconvencional, convencional y postconvencional. Carol Gilligan critica que el modelo privilegia el razonamiento ético masculino basado en la justicia sobre el femenino centrado en el cuidado.

A2: Kohlberg tells a story about a man who steals medicine for his wife. He asks people if the man was right. He finds people think about morality in different ways as they grow up.

B2: Kohlberg used the Heinz dilemma to map six stages of moral development across three levels: preconventional, conventional, and postconventional. Carol Gilligan objected that his framework privileged a justice-oriented reasoning stereotypically male over a care-oriented perspective stereotypically female.

C1: Kohlberg's Heinz dilemma furnished the empirical scaffolding for a six-stage model of moral development stratified into preconventional, conventional and postconventional levels. Gilligan's critique exposed the model's gendered bias, arguing that its hierarchical valorisation of justice-based reasoning over care-based reasoning systematically marginalised the relational moral voice predominantly articulated by women.

### 9. Ética aplicada: bioética, ética empresarial y ambiental

La ética aplicada traslada los principios teóricos a problemas concretos. La bioética aborda la eutanasia, la experimentación con animales y la justicia en la distribución de recursos sanitarios. La ética empresarial examina la responsabilidad corporativa y los conflictos de interés. La ética ambiental cuestiona si tenemos obligaciones morales hacia seres no humanos y ecosistemas futuros.

A2: Applied ethics looks at real problems. It asks questions like: is it okay to help someone die? Should companies be fair? Should we protect nature?

B2: Applied ethics translates abstract principles into concrete dilemmas. Bioethics addresses euthanasia and animal research. Business ethics examines corporate responsibility. Environmental ethics asks whether we owe moral duties to non-human beings and to future generations.

C1: Applied ethics mediates between abstract normative theory and concrete practical deliberation. Bioethics adjudicates disputes ranging from euthanasia and animal experimentation to the equitable allocation of scarce medical resources. Business ethics interrogates the scope of corporate moral agency, whilst environmental ethics interrogates the very extension of the moral community to encompass non-human organisms and as-yet-unrealised future generations.

### 10. El relativismo moral frente al universalismo

El relativismo moral sostiene que la moralidad es culturalmente relativa: lo correcto en una sociedad puede ser incorrecto en otra. El universalismo moral defiende que existen principios éticos válidos para todos los seres humanos independientemente de la cultura. El debate tiene profundas implicaciones para los derechos humanos y la justicia internacional.

A2: Some people think what is right depends on your country. Other people think some things are right or wrong everywhere.

B2: Moral relativism holds that right and wrong are culturally determined, so no universal standard exists. Moral universalism insists that certain ethical principles hold for all humans regardless of culture. This debate shapes how we think about human rights.

C1: Moral relativism contends that normative standards are irreducibly culture-bound, precluding any cross-cultural adjudication of moral claims. Moral universalism, by contrast, maintains that certain ethical principles possess trans-cultural validity derivable from rational or human nature itself. The dispute carries far-reaching implications for the legitimacy of universal human rights regimes and the coherence of international justice.

### 11. El problema del es y el debe: la guillotina de Hume

David Hume observó que muchos filósofos pasan inadvertidamente de descripciones fácticas (el es) a prescripciones normativas (el debe) sin justificación lógica. Esta guillotina de Hume establece que no se puede derivar un ought puramente de un is, separando el plano descriptivo del normativo, una distinción fundacional de la metaética contemporánea.

A2: Hume said we cannot jump from what is true to what we should do. Just because something is a certain way does not mean it should be that way.

B2: Hume observed that philosophers often slide unnoticed from descriptive claims about what is the case to normative claims about what ought to be. The is-ought gap insists that no purely logical inference can bridge the descriptive and the normative.

C1: Hume's guillotine, articulated in the Treatise, diagnoses an endemic fallacy whereby philosophers imperceptibly transition from descriptive premises concerning what is the case to normative conclusions concerning what ought to be. The resultant is-ought distinction enforces a logical barrier between the descriptive and the normative, a demarcation that remains constitutive of contemporary metaethical debate.

### 12. Intuicionismo y escepticismo moral

El intuicionismo moral sostiene que conocemos ciertas verdades morales básicas por intuición directa, análogas a la percepción sensorial. El escepticismo moral niega que existan hechos morales objetivos. El no cognitivismo, una variante escéptica, defiende que los juicios morales no expresan creencias verdaderas o falsas, sino actitudes o prescripciones.

A2: Some people think we just know what is right by feeling it. Other people think there are no real moral facts, only opinions.

B2: Moral intuitionism claims we grasp basic moral truths through direct intuition. Moral scepticism denies that objective moral facts exist. Non-cognitivism, a sceptical variant, argues that moral judgements express attitudes rather than truth-apt beliefs.

C1: Moral intuitionism, in its contemporary revival, posits that certain foundational moral truths are apprehended through non-inferential intellectual seeming, analogous to perceptual acquaintance. Moral scepticism, in its non-cognitivist incarnation, denies the truth-aptness of moral discourse altogether, construing moral utterances as expressions of conative attitudes or prescriptions rather than as propositions susceptible to truth-conditional analysis.

### 13. Justicia y equidad: Rawls y el velo de ignorancia

John Rawls propone un experimento mental para fundamentar la justicia: el velo de ignorancia. Si diseñaras los principios de la sociedad sin saber qué posición ocuparías en ella, elegirías reglas que protegieran incluso a los más desfavorecidos. Sus dos principios de justicia priorizan las libertades básicas y permiten desigualdades solo si benefician a los menos aventajados.

A2: Rawls asks: if you did not know if you would be rich or poor, what rules would you choose? You would probably choose fair rules.

B2: Rawls's veil of ignorance asks us to design society's rules without knowing our future position. Rational agents behind the veil would choose principles that protect the worst off. His two principles of justice prioritise basic liberties and permit inequality only if it benefits the least advantaged.

C1: Rawls's original position, operationalised through the veil of ignorance, solicits the principles of justice that rational agents would converge upon under a deliberate deprivation of knowledge regarding their eventual social placement. The resultant two principles lexically prioritise equal basic liberties and authorise socio-economic inequalities only insofar as they maximise the position of the least advantaged, a constraint dubbed the difference principle.

### 14. Lenguaje y argumentación ética en inglés C1

A nivel C1, debes construir argumentos morales con estructuras precisas. Expresiones como it might be objected that, on the contrary, this reasoning is flawed because, a counterexample would be, the implication of this view is que permiten articular objeciones y refutaciones. Los conectores lógicos, por tanto, son cruciales: nevertheless, moreover, insofar as, notwithstanding, provided that.

A2: When we talk about ethics, we can use phrases like "I think this is wrong because" and "but other people say."

B2: At an advanced level, you can structure moral arguments with phrases like it might be objected that, this reasoning is flawed because, and a counterexample would be. These expressions help you present and respond to objections clearly.

C1: At C1, ethical argumentation demands mastery of meta-discursive markers such as it might be objected that, this reasoning rests on a dubious premise, a decisive counterexample would be, and the implication of this view is. Logical connectors including nevertheless, moreover, insofar as, notwithstanding and provided that enable the precise articulation of concessive, additive and conditional relations essential to rigorous moral reasoning.

### 15. Colocaciones y léxico filosófico-moral

El léxico ético del inglés C1 incluye colocaciones estables que debes dominar: to act on principle, to weigh the consequences, to draw a distinction, to mount an objection, to concede a point, to rebut an argument, to uphold a duty, to breach a moral rule, to universalise a maxim, to maximise aggregate welfare, to cultivate a virtue, to possess moral standing, to derive an ought from an is.

A2: Here are some useful phrases: to do the right thing, to think about what happens, to be a good person.

B2: Useful collocations include to act on principle, to weigh the consequences, to mount an objection and to universalise a maxim. Learning these fixed expressions makes your ethical vocabulary sound natural and precise.

C1: The C1 ethical lexicon comprises entrenched collocations whose acquisition is indispensable for idiomatic philosophical discourse: to act on principle, to weigh the consequences, to draw a distinction, to mount an objection, to concede a point, to rebut an argument, to uphold a duty, to breach a moral rule, to universalise a maxim, to maximise aggregate welfare, to cultivate a virtue, to possess moral standing, and to derive an ought from an is.

## Resumen de la unidad

En esta unidad has explorado las tres grandes tradiciones de la filosofía moral: la deontología de Kant, el consecuencialismo utilitarista y la ética de las virtudes de Aristóteles. Has aprendido a construir y a responder a contraejemplos morales como el trolley problem y el violinista de Thomson, a distinguir el es del debe según Hume y a razonar sobre justicia con el velo de ignorancia de Rawls. A nivel lingüístico, has consolidado colocaciones filosóficas como to mount an objection, to universalise a maxim y to derive an ought from an is, junto con conectores lógicos avanzados como insofar as y notwithstanding. Con los 19 ejercicios has practicado la argumentación ética en inglés C1 con la precisión léxica y la estructuración lógica que exige el nivel avanzado. En la siguiente unidad, la 58, trasladarás esta capacidad de razonamiento cauteloso al futuro de la humanidad, donde la especulación prudente se convierte en la herramienta central.
