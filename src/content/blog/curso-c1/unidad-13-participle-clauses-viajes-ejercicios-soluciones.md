### Cómo usar este cuaderno

Responde primero y comprueba después. La explicación está pensada para el estudiante hispanohablante.

## Parte II — Ejercicios con soluciones

### Ejercicio 1 — Present participle

Combina cada par con una cláusula en **-ing**.

1. He walked through the market. He noticed the contrast between old and new shops.
2. She lived abroad for a year. She developed a deeper appreciation of her own culture.
3. The guide looked at the map. She realised the group was lost.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **Walking through the market, he noticed the contrast between old and new shops.**
2. **Living abroad for a year, she developed a deeper appreciation of her own culture.**
3. **Looking at the map, the guide realised the group was lost.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 2 — Perfect participle

Completa con **having + participio**.

1. ___ (travel) extensively, he understood the cultural nuances.
2. ___ (read) the travel advisory, the family postponed the trip.
3. ___ never ___ (experience) reverse culture shock, she underestimated the difficulty of returning home.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **Having travelled**.
2. **Having read**.
3. **Having never experienced**.

La acción del participio es anterior a la acción principal.

</details>

### Ejercicio 3 — Pasiva

Elige la forma correcta.

1. ___ (Having warned / Having been warned) about overtourism, the visitors avoided the busiest district.
2. ___ (Having invited / Having been invited) by the host community, the researchers attended the ceremony.
3. ___ (Having delayed / Having been delayed) by a strike, the train arrived after midnight.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **Having been warned**; 2. **Having been invited**; 3. **Having been delayed**. En los tres casos, el sujeto recibe la acción.

</details>

### Ejercicio 4 — Participio pasado o -ing

Completa con **travelling** o **travelled**.

1. The students ___ through Peru kept a journal.
2. The route ___ by early traders is now a hiking trail.
3. Visitors ___ today should register at the desk.
4. The route ___ by visitors is difficult in winter.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **travelling** — los estudiantes realizan la acción.
2. **travelled** — la ruta recibe la acción.
3. **arriving** sería la forma natural en esta frase: *Visitors arriving today...*.
4. **travelled** — la ruta es recorrida.

</details>

### Ejercicio 5 — Concesión

Reescribe con **adjective + though + subject + be**.

1. Although the travellers were exhausted, they continued.
2. Although the journey was difficult, it changed her perspective.
3. Although the customs seemed unfamiliar, the visitors adapted.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **Exhausted though the travellers were, they continued.**
2. **Difficult though the journey was, it changed her perspective.**
3. **Unfamiliar though the customs seemed, the visitors adapted.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 6 — Tiempo

Combina con una participle clause.

1. After she arrived in Tokyo, she felt excited.
2. While he travelled abroad, he kept a language journal.
3. Before they returned home, they completed the survey.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **On arriving in Tokyo, she felt excited.**
2. **While travelling abroad, he kept a language journal.**
3. **Before returning home, they completed the survey.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 7 — Causa o resultado

Indica si la cláusula expresa causa o resultado.

1. *Having misunderstood the instructions, the visitors missed the meeting point.*
2. *The airline cancelled the flight, leaving hundreds of passengers stranded.*
3. *Feeling unwell, she returned to the hotel.*
4. *The route was closed, forcing the group to change its itinerary.*

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **Causa**: el malentendido explica por qué perdieron el punto de encuentro.
2. **Resultado**: los pasajeros quedaron varados como consecuencia.
3. **Causa**: sentirse mal explica el regreso.
4. **Resultado**: el cierre provocó un cambio de itinerario.

</details>

### Ejercicio 8 — Dangling modifiers

Corrige las frases ilógicas.

1. *Having travelled for twelve hours, the hotel looked inviting.*
2. *Walking through the market, the smells became overwhelming.*
3. *After visiting the museum, the rain began.*

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **Having travelled for twelve hours, the travellers found the hotel inviting.**
2. **Walking through the market, I found the smells overwhelming.**
3. **After the tourists visited the museum, the rain began.**

En las versiones originales, el sujeto principal no podía realizar la acción del participio.

</details>

### Ejercicio 9 — Vocabulario cultural

Completa con **acculturation, assimilation, overtourism, reverse culture shock** o **cultural conditioning**.

1. The gradual adoption of elements of a host culture while retaining aspects of one’s own is ___ .
2. The complete adaptation to another culture, sometimes involving loss of the original identity, is ___ .
3. The difficulty of returning home after years abroad is ___ .
4. Excessive visitor numbers that a destination cannot manage sustainably constitute ___ .
5. Unconscious values absorbed through upbringing are part of ___ .

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **acculturation**; 2. **assimilation**; 3. **reverse culture shock**; 4. **overtourism**; 5. **cultural conditioning**.

</details>

### Ejercicio 10 — Reading

Lee el texto y responde.

> Having lived in Singapore for five years, Elena expected returning to her home country to feel simple. Instead, she experienced reverse culture shock. Walking through the streets of her old neighbourhood, she noticed that many shops had disappeared and that social conversations seemed less open than she remembered. Although welcomed by her family, she felt that she no longer fitted easily into the routines she had left behind. Her experience taught her that acculturation is not a one-way process: adapting to a host culture can change the expectations with which a person later views home. Elena now advises returning residents to allow themselves time to adjust rather than assuming that familiarity will remove every difficulty.

1. How long had Elena lived in Singapore?
2. What did she experience on returning?
3. What had changed in her neighbourhood?
4. What did she learn about acculturation?
5. What advice does she give returning residents?

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **Five years.**
2. **Reverse culture shock.**
3. **Many shops had disappeared and conversations seemed less open.**
4. **That acculturation can change a person’s expectations of home; it is not one-way.**
5. **They should allow themselves time to adjust rather than assuming familiarity will remove every difficulty.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 11 — Listening guiado

Guion: “Having been warned about overtourism, the visitors avoided the old town at midday. Travelling by train instead, they reached a smaller village, where they were welcomed by a host community that had developed sustainable tourism projects. Exhausted though they were after the journey, they attended a local workshop.”

Identifica una pasiva perfecta, una cláusula en -ing y una concesión.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

Pasiva perfecta: **Having been warned about overtourism**. Cláusula en -ing: **Travelling by train instead**. Concesión: **Exhausted though they were after the journey**.

</details>

### Ejercicio 12 — Transformación

Reescribe con una participle clause.

1. Because she was unfamiliar with local customs, she asked a guide for advice.
2. After the researchers had completed the interviews, they analysed the responses.
3. Although he was surrounded by unfamiliar languages, he remained calm.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **Unfamiliar with local customs, she asked a guide for advice.**
2. **Having completed the interviews, the researchers analysed the responses.**
3. **Though surrounded by unfamiliar languages, he remained calm.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 13 — Registro y precisión

Reescribe de forma más formal: *The volunteers went to the village and then realised that their project was not useful.*

<details><summary>Modelo</summary>

**Having consulted the host community, the volunteers realised that their initial project was not sufficiently relevant to local priorities.**

La versión sustituye una secuencia simple por un participio perfecto y añade vocabulario más preciso.

</details>

### Ejercicio 14 — Writing

Escribe 150–180 palabras sobre: **How can travellers avoid behaving paternalistically?** Incluye una participle clause de anterioridad, una pasiva, una concesión y vocabulario de intercambio cultural.

<details><summary>Modelo</summary>

Having learned about the host community before travelling, visitors are more likely to recognise local priorities and avoid imposing their own solutions. The people living in the destination should be consulted before volunteer projects are designed, and their agency must be respected throughout the process. Welcomed by a community does not mean that every activity is automatically beneficial; the impact should be evaluated rather than assumed. Although travellers may arrive with good intentions, they can still behave paternalistically if they believe that their education or resources make them better qualified to decide what others need. Culturally sensitive visitors should therefore listen before proposing, support locally led initiatives and learn enough about local customs to avoid causing unnecessary offence. Having been invited to contribute, they should remain accountable to the host organisation and accept criticism when a project does not work. Exchange becomes meaningful when both sides share knowledge and make decisions, rather than when one side treats the other as a passive recipient of help.

</details>

### Ejercicio 15 — Speaking

Habla durante un minuto sobre una experiencia de intercambio cultural. Usa **Having travelled**, **while living**, **though**, **culturally sensitive** y **host community**.

<details><summary>Pista y modelo</summary>

**Having travelled through several countries, I have learned that cultural sensitivity requires more than memorising customs. While living with a host community, I had to ask questions and reconsider assumptions. Though some habits initially seemed unfamiliar, I gradually understood their context. The experience made me more careful about describing other cultures in simple terms.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 16 — Traducción controlada

Traduce al inglés.

1. Habiendo viajado mucho, comprendía los matices culturales.
2. Aunque estaban agotados, los viajeros continuaron explorando la ciudad.
3. Rodeada de montañas, la aldea atrae visitantes todo el año.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **Having travelled extensively, he understood the cultural nuances.**
2. **Exhausted though they were, the travellers continued exploring the city.**
3. **Surrounded by mountains, the village attracts visitors all year round.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 17 — Edición avanzada

Corrige el párrafo.

> Having been never abroad, she was nervous. Walking through the airport, the announcements seemed confusing. After arriving, the luggage was collected by the guide. Exhausted though were the travellers, they attended the ceremony.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

**Having never been abroad, she was nervous. Walking through the airport, she found the announcements confusing. After arriving, the travellers collected their luggage from the guide. Exhausted though the travellers were, they attended the ceremony.**

Se corrige la posición de *never*, el dangling modifier, el sujeto implícito de *arriving* y el orden de la concesión.

</details>

### Ejercicio 18 — Mini-test final

Completa el texto.

> ___ (live) abroad for a year, Maya developed greater cultural sensitivity. ___ (arrive) in the host country, she initially misunderstood several customs. ___ though she was, she continued asking respectful questions. The project ___ (design) by the host community helped her understand that acculturation is a mutual process.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

**Having lived; On arriving; Uncertain though; designed.** El texto completo es: **Having lived abroad for a year, Maya developed greater cultural sensitivity. On arriving in the host country, she initially misunderstood several customs. Uncertain though she was, she continued asking respectful questions. The project designed by the host community helped her understand that acculturation is a mutual process.**

</details>

## Resumen de la unidad

Usa **-ing** para acciones simultáneas o causas cuando el sujeto realiza la acción. Usa **having + participio** para marcar que una acción terminó antes de la principal. Usa **having been + participio** cuando el sujeto recibe una acción anterior y un participio pasado para relaciones pasivas o estados.

Las estructuras concesivas como **exhausted though they were** y **difficult though the journey was** aportan un registro formal y compacto. Antes de reducir una cláusula, comprueba que el sujeto implícito sea el mismo que el sujeto de la oración principal. Si no coincide, escribe una cláusula completa.

El vocabulario también exige precisión: **acculturation** no es exactamente **assimilation**, **reverse culture shock** no es simple jet lag y **culturally sensitive** implica respeto contextual, no una colección de reglas superficiales. Cuando combines estas distinciones con colocaciones como *support host communities*, *preserve cultural heritage* y *develop intercultural competence*, podrás hablar de viajes e intercambio cultural con la riqueza y el control propios del C1.
