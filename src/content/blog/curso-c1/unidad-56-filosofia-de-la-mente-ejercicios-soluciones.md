## Parte II — Ejercicios

**Ejercicio 1.** Completa con el término correcto del léxico fundamental: *qualia*, *intentionality*, *phenomenal character*, *mental states*.

1. The subjective "what-it-is-like" quality of an experience is its ______.
2. The property of mental states of being "about" something is ______.
3. The felt qualities of experience, such as the redness of red, are called ______.
4. Beliefs, desires and sensations are collectively referred to as ______.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. phenomenal character
2. intentionality (or aboutness)
3. qualia
4. mental states

These four terms map the foundational vocabulary: *mental states* is the genus, *intentionality* is one mark of the mental (directedness), *phenomenal character* is another (subjective feel), and *qualia* are the individual felt qualities that compose the phenomenal character. Distinguishing them is the precondition of clear argument in this domain.
</details>

**Ejercicio 2.** Completa con la posición en el problema cuerpo-mente: *substance dualism*, *physicalism*, *property dualism*, *epiphenomenalism*.

1. The thesis that mind and body are two distinct substances is ______.
2. The thesis that everything that exists is physical is ______.
3. The thesis that there is one substance with two kinds of properties is ______.
4. The thesis that mental states exist but have no causal power is ______.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. substance dualism
2. physicalism (or materialism)
3. property dualism
4. epiphenomenalism

Note the relationships: *property dualism* is a compromise between *substance dualism* and *physicalism* \u2014 it keeps one substance but two kinds of property. *Epiphenomenalism* can be combined with property dualism: mental properties are real but causally inert, like the steam from a train's whistle that does not affect the train's motion.
</details>

**Ejercicio 3.** Reescribe esta afirmación simple como un argumento C1 con precisión conceptual: "The mind is like a computer. So consciousness is just information processing."

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*While the metaphor of the mind as a computer has proven heuristically indispensable in organising the functionalist's account of cognition, its very fruitfulness carries a risk that the philosophically careful user must keep in view: that the analogy between software and mental states, however illuminating as a model of information processing, may seduce us into treating as solved the question that the hard problem of consciousness actually poses \u2014 namely, why there is anything it is like to be in such a state at all, a question that no amount of functional decomposition, however complete, appears even to address, let alone to answer.*

The rewrite distinguishes the heuristic value of the metaphor from its explanatory limits: *heuristically indispensable* concedes the usefulness, *its very fruitfulness carries a risk* signals the danger, and *seduce us into treating as solved the question that... actually poses* names the specific error. The final clause \u2014 *no amount of functional decomposition... appears even to address* \u2014 explains why the metaphor cannot do the work the simple version asks of it.
</details>

**Ejercicio 4.** Traduce al inglés C1 con precisión conceptual: "La distinción entre los aspectos funcionales de un estado mental y su carácter fenoménico es la que explota el problema duro de la consciencia."

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The distinction between the functional aspects of a mental state \u2014 what it does, what role it plays in the cognitive economy \u2014 and its phenomenal character \u2014 what it is like, from the inside, to be in that state \u2014 is the distinction that the hard problem of consciousness exploits, for the former admits, in principle, of a complete functional account while the latter, by its very nature, seems to resist any account that does not, at some point, presuppose the very thing it sets out to explain.*

Key vocabulary: *functional aspects*, *phenomenal character*, *the hard problem of consciousness*, *a complete functional account*, *presuppose the very thing it sets out to explain*. The structure distinguishes the two levels of description and explains why the hard problem targets the second: the functional level is tractable, the phenomenal level seems to presuppose consciousness in its own explanation.
</details>

**Ejercicio 5.** Reescribe usando conectores de precisión conceptual (*in the strict sense*, *not to be confused with*, *as distinct from*): "Consciousness is just attention. They are the same thing."

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*Consciousness, in the strict sense that the hard problem targets, is not to be confused with attention \u2014 as distinct from the cluster of access functions that attention involves \u2014 for while attention may be a necessary condition of certain forms of conscious experience in the creatures we know, it is not identical with the phenomenal character that consciousness, in the relevant sense, denotes, and the tendency to slide between the two is one that the philosophical literature exists in large part to resist.*

The connectors do precision work: *in the strict sense* scopes the term, *not to be confused with* flags the distinction, *as distinct from* elaborates what attention actually is. The rewrite distinguishes a necessary condition (attention) from the phenomenon itself (phenomenal consciousness), preventing the conflation that the simple version commits.
</details>

**Ejercicio 6.** Completa con léxico del funcionalismo: *functional role*, *multiple realisability*, *machine functionalism*, *computational theory of mind*.

1. The idea that a mental state is individuated by what it does, not what it is made of, is the ______.
2. The thesis that the same mental state can be implemented in different substrates is ______.
3. The view that the mind is a computer running programmes is the ______.
4. The broader thesis that cognition is computation is the ______.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. functional role
2. multiple realisability
3. machine functionalism
4. computational theory of mind

These terms form a nested hierarchy: *functional role* is the core idea, *multiple realisability* is its key implication, *machine functionalism* is one specific implementation (the mind as a Turing machine), and the *computational theory of mind* is the broader thesis that cognition is computation. Understanding their relations is essential for arguing within the functionalist framework.
</details>

**Ejercicio 7.** Escribe una frase en C1 que presente el argumento del zombi contra el fisicalismo, usando precisión conceptual y conectores lógicos.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The zombie argument \u2014 the thought experiment of a being functionally and physically identical to a conscious human yet devoid of any inner experience \u2014 purports to show that phenomenal consciousness is not entailed by the physical facts, for if such a being is conceivable without contradiction, then the physical facts do not, by logical necessity, include the phenomenal ones, from which it follows, if the conceivability is genuine, that physicalism is false, a conclusion the physicalist must rebut by arguing either that the zombie is not genuinely conceivable or that conceivability does not entail possibility.*

The logical structure: the zombie is *conceivable without contradiction* \u2014 *if... then the physical facts do not... include the phenomenal ones* \u2014 *from which it follows... that physicalism is false*. The final clause (*the physicalist must rebut by arguing either... or...*) identifies the two lines of response. The argument turns conceivability into a challenge to physicalist entailment.
</details>

**Ejercicio 8.** Reescribe con matización metafórica, señalando dónde la analogía se rompe: "The brain is a prediction machine. It works just like a computer predicting things."

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The metaphor of the brain as a prediction machine is illuminating, up to a point, insofar as it captures the way in which neural processing can be modelled as the minimisation of prediction error across hierarchical levels; with the caveat, however, that the analogy breaks down at the point where the question of why the prediction is accompanied by experience is concerned, for while a prediction machine processes error signals, there is nothing it is like, from the inside, to be that processing \u2014 a disanalogy that the metaphor, if pressed too far, obscures precisely where the philosophically interesting question lies.*

The maturation: *illuminating, up to a point, insofar as* concedes the heuristic value; *with the caveat, however, that the analogy breaks down at* signals the limit; *there is nothing it is like... to be that processing* names the disanalogy; *obscures precisely where the philosophically interesting question lies* explains the danger. The rewrite uses the metaphor without being used by it.
</details>

**Ejercicio 9.** Completa con léxico de qualia y el argumento del conocimiento: *qualia*, *the knowledge argument*, *what Mary didn't know*, *phenomenal concepts*.

1. The thought experiment about the colour scientist who learns something new on seeing red is ______.
2. The subjective qualities of experience that Mary appears to gain knowledge of are ______.
3. The argument that there are facts about experience that escape the physical inventory is ______.
4. Concepts that pick out experiences by their felt quality, not their physical basis, are ______.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. what Mary didn't know (or Mary's room)
2. qualia
3. the knowledge argument
4. phenomenal concepts

The argument's structure: Mary knows all the physical facts (*the knowledge argument*), yet on seeing red she appears to learn about *qualia*; this is *what Mary didn't know*; the physicalist's reply is that she gains *phenomenal concepts* (a new mode of access) rather than new facts. Understanding the argument and the reply is essential for engaging with the physicalism debate.
</details>

**Ejercicio 10.** Reescribe esta afirmación sobre el eliminativismo con la precisión y la abstracción de un C1: "Beliefs aren't real. Folk psychology is wrong, like old science."

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The eliminativist's claim that folk psychology is a theory \u2014 and a theory that, like phlogiston or caloric, may turn out to be radically mistaken about its domain \u2014 is not the modest observation that we sometimes misattribute beliefs but the radical thesis that the categories of belief and desire may not carve the cognitive reality at its joints, a thesis whose plausibility depends on whether the predictive and explanatory success of folk psychology is the kind of success that a fundamentally mistaken theory could enjoy, or whether that success is, rather, evidence that the categories it deploys correspond, at least roughly, to real features of the cognitive system.*

The rewrite distinguishes the modest thesis (we sometimes misattribute) from the radical one (the categories may not carve reality at its joints), and frames the evaluation as turning on a specific epistemological question: could a fundamentally mistaken theory enjoy the predictive success folk psychology enjoys? The everyday claim becomes a philosophical thesis embedded in the debate about theoretical success and reference.
</details>

**Ejercicio 11.** Escribe una frase en C1 que explique el problema de la interacción del dualismo cartesiano, usando conectores lógicos.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The interaction problem that has dogged substance dualism since Descartes \u2014 how two substances as different in kind as mind and matter could causally affect one another \u2014 is not merely a technical difficulty but a structural one, for the very intelligibility of causal interaction seems to presuppose some common currency between cause and effect that the radical heterogeneity of the two substances appears to rule out by definition, which is to say that the difficulty is not that we have not yet found the mechanism of interaction but that the very notion of a mechanism, as something that operates in physical space, is one that the immateriality of the mind puts beyond reach.*

The logical structure: the problem is *not merely a technical difficulty but a structural one* (escalation), *for... seems to presuppose some common currency... that the... heterogeneity... appears to rule out by definition* (the structural reason), *which is to say that...* (reformulation that deepens). The argument shows the problem is not empirical (we haven't found the mechanism) but conceptual (the notion of mechanism is incompatible with immateriality).
</details>

**Ejercicio 12.** Traduce al inglés C1: "La tesis de la mente extendida desafía el supuesto tácito de que los límites de lo cognitivo coinciden con los de la piel."

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The extended mind thesis \u2014 that cognitive processes can, under certain conditions, loop out into the environment, so that a notebook used reliably for memory counts, by parity of reasoning, as part of the mind \u2014 challenges the tacit assumption that the boundaries of the cognitive are coextensive with the boundaries of the skin, though whether the parity argument establishes that the external resource is part of the mind or merely that the boundary between mind and world is less sharp than we assumed is a question its proponents and critics continue to contest.*

Key vocabulary: *extended mind thesis*, *loop out into the environment*, *parity of reasoning*, *coextensive with the boundaries of the skin*. The structure presents the thesis, identifies what it challenges, and then complicates the evaluation: does parity show the external is *part of the mind*, or merely that the *boundary is less sharp*? This refusal to resolve the interpretive question is characteristic of C1 philosophical writing.
</details>

**Ejercicio 13.** Completa con léxico de emergencia y reducción: *supervenience*, *emergent property*, *identity theory*, *anomalous monism*.

1. The thesis that mental states are identical to brain states is the ______.
2. The dependence of the mental on the physical without reducibility is ______.
3. A property of a system that is not a property of its parts is an ______.
4. Davidson's view that every mental event is physical but mental descriptions are irreducible is ______.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. identity theory (type identity theory)
2. supervenience
3. emergent property
4. anomalous monism

The relationships: *identity theory* is the strongest reductive claim (mental = physical), *supervenience* is weaker (mental depends on physical but may not be reducible), *emergent properties* are system-level properties not present in the parts, and *anomalous monism* (Davidson) holds that every event is physical (monism) but there are no strict psychophysical laws (anomalous), making it a form of non-reductive physicalism. These positions form a spectrum of commitment to reduction.
</details>

**Ejercicio 14.** Reescribe esta afirmación sobre el acceso privilegiado con la precisión de un C1: "I know my own mind perfectly. No one can correct me about what I feel."

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The first-person authority that we ordinarily accord to a subject's avowals of her own mental states \u2014 the presumption that, if she sincerely says she is in pain, she is, barring rare exceptions, right \u2014 is a feature of our epistemic practice that any philosophy of mind must accommodate, though the explanation of that authority is itself contested, with some accounts grounding it in a special mode of access and others in the constitutive role that the avowal plays in fixing the state avowed, a divergence that turns, in the final analysis, on whether self-knowledge is a form of observation or a form of creation.*

The rewrite replaces the absolute claim (*I know my mind perfectly*) with the calibrated notion of *first-person authority* as a feature of epistemic practice, acknowledges that it admits of *rare exceptions*, and frames the explanation as a contested question (is self-knowledge observation or creation?). The everyday certainty becomes a philosophical datum with competing theoretical explanations.
</details>

**Ejercicio 15.** Escribe una frase en C1 que distinga consciencia de acceso de consciencia fenoménica, explicando por qué la distinción es estratégica para el funcionalista.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The distinction between access and phenomenal consciousness, if sound, allows the functionalist to account for the former \u2014 which is, by construction, a matter of information availability for verbal report and rational control \u2014 while conceding that the latter poses the hard problem, a division of explanatory labour that is attractive to the functionalist precisely because it confines the explanandum that resists functional analysis to a domain that the functionalist can, without inconsistency, leave to one side, though whether the distinction is sound \u2014 whether access and phenomenal consciousness can in fact come apart, as the dissociation cases are invoked to show \u2014 is a question that the functionalist's critics contest precisely because the strategy's success depends on it.*

The structure: *the distinction... if sound, allows* (the strategic benefit), *a division of explanatory labour that is attractive... because it confines... to a domain the functionalist can leave aside* (why it's attractive), *though whether the distinction is sound... is a question that... critics contest precisely because the strategy's success depends on it* (the vulnerability). The sentence explains both why the functionalist wants the distinction and why it is the point at which critics attack.
</details>

**Ejercicio 16.** Usa la estructura cohesiva *the question, properly framed, is not X but Y; the metaphor is apt insofar as Z; the upshot is that W* para articular el límite de la metáfora computacional.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The question, properly framed, is not whether the mind is like a computer \u2014 a metaphor whose aptness is, in any case, contestable \u2014 but whether the computational model, insofar as it captures the functional individuation of mental states, can address the phenomenal residue that the hard problem identifies; the metaphor is apt insofar as it illuminates the functional level of description, where the analogy between software and mental states organises a genuine insight about role-based individuation; the upshot, however, is that the model can, at best, clarify that functional level while leaving the phenomenal residue precisely where it was, which is why the metaphor, however useful as a heuristic, should not be mistaken for a solution to the very problem it so elegantly reorganises.*

The cohesive structure performs three moves: *the question, properly framed, is not... but...* (reframes the question from the metaphor's aptness to its explanatory reach), *the metaphor is apt insofar as* (concedes the legitimate scope), *the upshot, however, is that* (delivers the limit). The sentence uses the metaphor as a heuristic while marking precisely where it ceases to illuminate \u2014 the C1 stance on explanatory metaphors.
</details>

**Ejercicio 17.** Reescribe esta afirmación sobre la intencionalidad con la abstracción filosófica de un C1: "Thoughts are about things. But how does the brain do that?"

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The Brentanian thesis that intentionality is the mark of the mental \u2014 that what distinguishes mental states from physical ones is their directedness towards an object, their being about something \u2014 raises the question of whether intentionality can be naturalised, that is, explained in terms that do not themselves presuppose the mentality they set out to account for, a question whose difficulty is compounded by the existence of states, like beliefs about non-existent entities, that have intentionality without an object, which is to say that the very feature that marks the mental also generates the puzzle of how a physical system can be about something at all.*

The rewrite moves from the everyday observation (*thoughts are about things*) to the Brentanian thesis (*intentionality as the mark of the mental*), formulates the philosophical question (*can intentionality be naturalised?*), defines the challenge (*without presupposing the mentality it sets out to explain*), and deepens it with the case of *intentionality without an object* (beliefs about non-existent entities). The simple question becomes a layered philosophical problem.
</details>

**Ejercicio 18.** Escribe un párrafo breve (4-5 frases) en C1 que articule el debate entre fisicalismo reductivo, no reductivo y dualismo de propiedades, usando precisión conceptual y conectores lógicos.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The spectrum of positions on the mind-body problem \u2014 from reductive physicalism, through non-reductive physicalism, to property dualism \u2014 turns, in the final analysis, on a single question: whether the mental, if it supervenes on the physical, is thereby reducible to it, or whether supervenience leaves a residue that resists reduction without amounting to the distinct substance of the Cartesian dualist. The reductive physicalist holds that the mental just is the physical, properly described, so that a complete physical description would, in principle, leave nothing mental unaccounted for; the non-reductive physicalist concedes the dependence but denies the identity, holding that the mental supervenes on the physical without being reducible to it. The difficulty for the non-reductive position is to specify what the irreducibility consists in: if it is merely epistemic \u2014 a matter of our descriptive categories \u2014 it threatens to collapse into the reductive view the non-reductivist meant to resist, whereas if it is ontic \u2014 a matter of the properties themselves \u2014 it threatens to collapse into the property dualism it was meant to avoid. The upshot, then, is that the non-reductive physicalist occupies an attractive but unstable middle ground, one whose coherence depends on drawing a distinction \u2014 between epistemic and ontic irreducibility \u2014 that is, on the available arguments, more easily asserted than established, which is why the debate, for all its technical refinement, remains as live as it is.*

Conceptual vocabulary: *reductive physicalism*, *non-reductive physicalism*, *property dualism*, *supervenience*, *reducible*, *epistemic vs ontic irreducibility*. Logical connectors: *in the final analysis*, *if... then... whereas if... then...*, *the upshot, then, is that*. The paragraph maps the spectrum, explains the middle position's difficulty, and identifies the distinction on which its coherence turns.
</details>

**Ejercicio 19.** Escribe un párrafo breve (4-5 frases) en inglés C1 que argumente sobre el papel de la metáfora en filosofía de la mente, usando matización metafórica, precisión conceptual y conectores de cohesión.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The role of metaphor in the philosophy of mind is, to put the matter carefully, both indispensable and dangerous \u2014 indispensable because the subject matter, being in part the subjective character of experience, resists the literal vocabulary that suffices for the physical sciences, and dangerous because a metaphor that illuminates at one level of description can obscure at another, seducing us into mistaking the model for the reality modelled. The computational metaphor, to take the paradigmatic case, is apt insofar as it captures the functional individuation of mental states \u2014 the insight that what makes a state the state it is, is the role it plays rather than the material that realises it \u2014 yet the analogy breaks down, with the caveat that this is not a minor disanalogy but a decisive one, at the point where the question of phenomenal consciousness is concerned, for while a computer processes information, there is nothing it is like, from the inside, to be that processing, and the metaphor, if pressed beyond its legitimate scope, obscures precisely the explanandum \u2014 the subjective residue \u2014 that the hard problem identifies. The upshot is not that the metaphor should be abandoned, for its heuristic value at the functional level is genuine, but that it should be used with the awareness that its illumination is, in the relevant sense, partial: it organises one level of description while leaving another untouched, and the philosophically serious user is the one who can deploy it for what it illuminates without letting it dictate what it cannot. This discipline \u2014 the capacity to use a metaphor and, at the same time, to see where it ceases to apply \u2014 is, in the final analysis, not merely a stylistic virtue but an epistemic one, for the history of the philosophy of mind is in no small part the history of confusions that arose when a useful picture was mistaken for a settled theory. The honest position, then, is that we cannot do without metaphors in this domain, but we can \u2014 and the C1 speaker must \u2014 do with them consciously, marking their scope, signalling their limits, and resisting the perennial temptation to let the elegance of the model substitute for the rigour of the argument it was meant to organise.*

Matización metafórica: *apt insofar as*, *the analogy breaks down, with the caveat that*, *if pressed beyond its legitimate scope*, *partial illumination*. Precisión conceptual: *functional individuation*, *phenomenal consciousness*, *the explanandum*, *the subjective residue*. Conectores de cohesión: *to put the matter carefully*, *in the relevant sense*, *the upshot is not that... but that*, *in the final analysis*, *the honest position, then, is that*. The paragraph enacts the very discipline it advocates: it uses the computational metaphor to illustrate a point about metaphor while rigorously marking where that illustration ceases to apply.
</details>

## Resumen de la unidad

En esta unidad hemos construido el andamiaje lingüístico para argumentar sobre la filosofía de la mente con la precisión conceptual y la conciencia metalingüística que exige el nivel C1. Has aprendido a distinguir *dualism* de *physicalism*, *qualia* de *functional states*, *access consciousness* de *phenomenal consciousness*, y a manejar el léxico del problema cuerpo-mente \u2014*substance dualism*, *property dualism*, *epiphenomenalism*\u2014, el del hard problem \u2014*explanatory gap*, *zombie argument*, *the knowledge argument*\u2014 y el del funcionalismo \u2014*functional role*, *multiple realisability*, *computational theory of mind*. Has incorporado el vocabulario de la intencionalidad \u2014*aboutness*, *propositional attitudes*, *mental representation*\u2014, de la emergencia \u2014*supervenience*, *emergent property*, *identity theory*\u2014 y de la cognición encarnada \u2014*embodied cognition*, *extended mind*, *4E cognition*. Has ejercitado la matización metafórica \u2014*apt insofar as*, *the analogy breaks down at*, *if pressed too far*\u2014 como disciplina epistémica, la precisión conceptual \u2014*in the strict sense*, *not to be confused with*, *as distinct from*\u2014 como higiene argumentativa, y la cohesión filosófica que hace visible tanto la estructura del argumento como los límites de la metáfora que lo articula. La destreza central de esta unidad es el uso consciente de la metáfora: la capacidad de emplear los modelos explicativos \u2014la mente como ordenador, la consciencia como propiedad emergente\u2014 para lo que iluminan, al tiempo que se señala con precisión dónde dejan de iluminar, resistiendo la tentación, tan común en este campo, de confundir la elegancia del modelo con la solución del problema. Esa elegancia metalingüística es la marca distintiva del hablante C1 cuando se enfrenta a uno de los debates donde el lenguaje debe ser a la vez imaginativo y riguroso, y donde la diferencia entre una metáfora que ilumina y una que engaña es, literalmente, la diferencia entre filosofía y confusión.
