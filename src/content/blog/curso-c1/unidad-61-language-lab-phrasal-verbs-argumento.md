## Parte I — Teoría: Phrasal verbs en argumento

### 1. La anatomía del phrasal verb

Un phrasal verb combina un verbo léxico con una partícula (preposición o adverbio) cuyo significado conjunto no se deduce literalmente de sus partes. To bring about significa causar, no traer alrededor; to put forward significa proponer, no poner adelante. Esta no composicionalidad es la marca definitoria del phrasal verb y la razón por la que deben aprenderse como unidades léxicas completas.

A2: A phrasal verb is a verb plus a small word. Together they mean something new.

B2: A phrasal verb combines a lexical verb with a particle whose joint meaning is not literal. To bring about means to cause, not to bring around. This non-compositionality is what makes phrasal verbs learned as whole units.

C1: The phrasal verb fuses a lexical verb with a particle, prepositional or adverbial, whose composite semantics are irreducible to the sum of the constituent meanings. To bring about denotes causation rather than literal circumferential conveyance; to put forward denotes propositional advancement rather than literal spatial relocation. This semantic non-compositionality is the defining diacritic of the phrasal verb and the rationale for its acquisition as an indivisible lexical unit.

### 2. Phrasal verbs separables e inseparables

Los phrasal verbs separables permiten intercalar el objeto entre el verbo y la partícula: turn the offer down o turn down the offer. Los inseparables no lo permiten: look into the matter (no look the matter into). Con pronombres, los separables obligan a la separación: turn it down, nunca turn down it. Conocer esta distinción evita errores sintácticos que delatan un nivel inferior.

A2: Some phrasal verbs can be split: "turn down the offer" or "turn the offer down." With words like "it," you must split: "turn it down."

B2: Separable phrasal verbs allow the object between the verb and the particle: turn the offer down or turn down the offer. Inseparable ones do not: look into the matter. With pronouns, separables must split: turn it down, never turn down it.

C1: Separable phrasal verbs license the interpolation of the direct object between the verb and the particle, yielding alternants such as turn the offer down and turn down the offer. Inseparable phrasal verbs preclude such interpolation, look into the matter being the only admissible ordering. A further constraint is that pronominal objects obligatorily trigger separation in separable verbs, turn it down being grammatical and turn down it ungrammatical, a distinction whose mastery precludes a syntactic error that would conspicuously signal sub-C1 competence.

### 3. Phrasal verbs de tres partes

Algunos phrasal verbs constan de verbo más dos partículas: to come up with (idear), to get away with (salirse con la suya), to look down on (despreciar), to put up with (tolerar), to get back to (volver a un tema). Estos son siempre inseparables y suelen ser de registro informal o neutro, por lo que su uso en ensayos académicos debe ponderarse.

A2: Some phrasal verbs have two small words: "come up with" means to think of an idea.

B2: Three-part phrasal verbs consist of verb plus two particles: to come up with, to get away with, to look down on, to put up with, to get back to. They are always inseparable and tend to be informal or neutral, so their use in academic essays should be weighed carefully.

C1: A subset of phrasal verbs comprises verb plus two particles, to come up with, to get away with, to look down on, to put up with, to get back to, and these are categorically inseparable. Their register distribution skews toward the informal or neutral, and consequently their inclusion in academic prose warrants judicious calibration, with formal Latinate alternatives preferred where the stylistic stakes are high.

### 4. Phrasal verbs formales para la argumentación

Ciertos phrasal verbs son de registro formal y encajan en ensayos y debates: to account for (explicar), to bring about (causar), to put forward (proponer), to rule out (descartar), to set out (exponer), to stem from (derivarse de), to point to (indicar), to boil down to (reducirse a), to spell out (explicitar), to bear out (confirmar). Su uso adecuado en contextos argumentativos es un indicador fiable del C1.

A2: Some phrasal verbs sound formal and are good for essays, like "to bring about" (cause) and "to put forward" (propose).

B2: Certain phrasal verbs are formal and fit essays and debates: to account for, to bring about, to put forward, to rule out, to set out, to stem from, to point to, to boil down to, to spell out, to bear out. Using them correctly in argumentation signals C1.

C1: A delineable subset of phrasal verbs possesses the register elevation requisite for argumentative and expository prose: to account for, to bring about, to put forward, to rule out, to set out, to stem from, to point to, to boil down to, to spell out and to bear out. Their idiomatic integration within argumentative discourse functions as a diagnostically reliable index of C1 competence, their conspicuous absence conversely indicative of a lexical plateau beneath the target proficiency.

### 5. Alternancia con sinónimos latinos: la dialéctica del registro

El inglés C1 exige saber alternar entre phrasal verbs y sus sinónimos latinos según el registro. To bring about / to cause; to put forward / to propose; to rule out / to exclude; to stem from / to originate in; to point to / to indicate; to spell out / to elucidate. En un ensayo denso, el sinónimo latino puede ser más apropiado; en un debate oral, el phrasal verb aporta fluidez y naturalidad.

A2: Many phrasal verbs have longer words that mean the same thing. "Bring about" and "cause" are similar.

B2: C1 English requires alternating between phrasal verbs and their Latinate synonyms by register. To bring about / to cause; to put forward / to propose; to rule out / to exclude; to stem from / to originate in; to point to / to indicate; to spell out / to elucidate. In a dense essay, the Latinate synonym may be more apt; in oral debate, the phrasal verb adds fluency.

C1: C1 English demands the register-sensitive alternation between phrasal verbs and their Latinate equivalents. The paradigmatic pairings, to bring about and to cause, to put forward and to propose, to rule out and to exclude, to stem from and to originate in, to point to and to indicate, to spell out and to elucidate, instantiate a stylistic duality whose exploitation is itself a marker of proficiency. In lexically dense expository prose, the Latinate variant may be preferred for its morphological compactness; in extemporaneous oral debate, the phrasal verb confers a fluency and idiomaticity that the Latinate alternative cannot replicate.

### 6. Colocaciones argumentativas con phrasal verbs

La colocación es la combinación habitual del phrasal verb con ciertos sustantivos. To mount an argument, to put forward a proposal, to account for a discrepancy, to rule out a possibility, to bring about a change, to set out the reasons, to point to evidence, to bear out a hypothesis. Aprender estas combinaciones fijas es lo que separa el uso correcto del uso idiomático.

A2: Some words go together naturally. We say "put forward a proposal," not "put forward a cat."

B2: Collocation is the habitual combination of a phrasal verb with certain nouns. To mount an argument, to put forward a proposal, to account for a discrepancy, to rule out a possibility, to bring about a change, to set out the reasons, to point to evidence, to bear out a hypothesis. Learning these fixed combinations separates correct from idiomatic usage.

C1: Collocation denotes the statistically conventionalised co-occurrence of a phrasal verb with a delimited class of nominal complements. The entrenched pairings, to mount an argument, to put forward a proposal, to account for a discrepancy, to rule out a possibility, to bring about a change, to set out the reasons, to point to evidence and to bear out a hypothesis, constitute the idiomatic bedrock of argumentative phrasal usage. Their assimilation is what bifurcates the merely correct from the authentically idiomatic C1 user.

### 7. To bring about: causación y cambio

To bring about significa causar o provocar un cambio, especialmente significativo. Se coloca con change, reform, transformation, revolution, shift, collapse. Su sinónimo latino es to cause o to effect. En un ensayo sobre política, to bring about lasting reform es más idiomático que to cause lasting reform, aunque ambos son correctos.

A2: "Bring about" means to make something happen, like a big change.

B2: To bring about means to cause or provoke a significant change. It collocates with change, reform, transformation, revolution, shift and collapse. Its Latinate synonym is to cause or to effect. In a politics essay, to bring about lasting reform is more idiomatic than to cause lasting reform.

C1: To bring about encodes the causation of a significant change or transformation, and collocates with change, reform, transformation, revolution, shift and collapse. Its Latinate equivalents are to cause and to effect, the latter a markedly formal variant. In political expository prose, to bring about lasting reform exhibits an idiomaticity that to cause lasting reform, though grammatically impeccable, lacks, illustrating the registeral subtlety that distinguishes C1 lexical selection.

### 8. To put forward: proposición y argumentación

To put forward significa proponer o presentar una idea, argumento o candidatura. Se coloca con proposal, argument, theory, candidate, suggestion, hypothesis. Su sinónimo latino es to propose, to present o to advance. En un debate, to put forward a compelling argument es la formulación natural; en un artículo académico denso, to advance a compelling argument puede ser preferible.

A2: "Put forward" means to suggest an idea or a plan.

B2: To put forward means to propose or present an idea, argument or candidacy. It collocates with proposal, argument, theory, candidate, suggestion and hypothesis. Its Latinate synonym is to propose, to present or to advance. In a debate, to put forward a compelling argument is natural; in a dense academic article, to advance a compelling argument may be preferable.

C1: To put forward encodes the propositional or presentational advancement of an idea, argument or candidacy, collocating with proposal, argument, theory, candidate, suggestion and hypothesis. Its Latinate congeners are to propose, to present and to advance. In oral debate, to put forward a compelling argument constitutes the unmarked formulation; in lexically dense academic prose, to advance a compelling argument may be stylistically preferred, a registeral alternation that the proficient C1 user navigates with reflexive ease.

### 9. To rule out: exclusión y refutación

To rule out significa descartar o excluir una posibilidad. Se coloca con possibility, option, scenario, explanation, candidate. Su sinónimo latino es to exclude o to preclude. En argumentación, to rule out an alternative explanation es una jugada retórica poderosa que estrecha el espacio de hipótesis competidoras.

A2: "Rule out" means to say something is not possible.

B2: To rule out means to discard or exclude a possibility. It collocates with possibility, option, scenario, explanation and candidate. Its Latinate synonym is to exclude or to preclude. In argumentation, to rule out an alternative explanation is a powerful rhetorical move that narrows the space of competing hypotheses.

C1: To rule out encodes the eliminative exclusion of a possibility, collocating with possibility, option, scenario, explanation and candidate. Its Latinate equivalents are to exclude and to preclude, the latter encoding a stronger logical preemption. In argumentative discourse, to rule out an alternative explanation constitutes a rhetorically potent manoeuvre that contracts the space of competing hypotheses, a discursive function central to the logic of refutation at C1.

### 10. To account for: explicación y rendición de cuentas

To account for significa explicar o dar razón de algo, especialmente una discrepancia, un fenómeno o un resultado. Se coloca con discrepancy, phenomenon, variance, behaviour, outcome, failure. Su sinónimo latino es to explain o to elucidate. Es uno de los phrasal verbs más rentables en ensayos académicos.

A2: "Account for" means to explain why something happened.

B2: To account for means to explain or give a reason for something, especially a discrepancy, phenomenon or result. It collocates with discrepancy, phenomenon, variance, behaviour, outcome and failure. Its Latinate synonym is to explain or to elucidate. It is one of the most useful phrasal verbs in academic essays.

C1: To account for encodes the provision of an explanatory rationale for a phenomenon, particularly a discrepancy, variance or outcome, collocating with discrepancy, phenomenon, variance, behaviour, outcome and failure. Its Latinate equivalents are to explain and to elucidate, the latter imparting a more formal explanatory register. It ranks among the most discursively productive phrasal verbs in academic expository prose, its versatility rendering it indispensable to the C1 argumentative repertoire.

### 11. To stem from y to boil down to: origen y esencia

To stem from significa derivarse de o tener su origen en. Se coloca con problem, criticism, confusion, inequality, discrepancy. To boil down to significa reducirse a o consistir esencialmente en. Se coloca con question, issue, matter, debate. Ambos son formales y útiles para la síntesis argumentativa.

A2: "Stem from" means to come from. "Boil down to" means to be really about one main thing.

B2: To stem from means to derive from or originate in. It collocates with problem, criticism, confusion, inequality and discrepancy. To boil down to means to reduce to or essentially consist in. It collocates with question, issue, matter and debate. Both are formal and useful for argumentative synthesis.

C1: To stem from encodes derivational origin, collocating with problem, criticism, confusion, inequality and discrepancy, and admits the Latinate equivalent to originate in. To boil down to encodes reductive essentialisation, the compression of a complex matter to its constitutive core, collocating with question, issue, matter and debate. Both occupy the formal register and are instrumentally valuable for argumentative synthesis, the former tracing aetiology, the latter distilling essence.

### 12. To set out y to spell out: exposición y explicitación

To set out significa exponer o presentar de manera estructurada. Se coloca con reasons, arguments, proposals, plan, stall. To spell out significa explicitar o detallar punto por punto. Se coloca with implications, consequences, details, terms. Ambos son formales y propios de la prosa académica y administrativa.

A2: "Set out" means to explain something in a clear order. "Spell out" means to explain in a lot of detail.

B2: To set out means to lay out or present in a structured manner. It collocates with reasons, arguments, proposals and plan. To spell out means to make explicit or detail point by point. It collocates with implications, consequences, details and terms. Both are formal and typical of academic and administrative prose.

C1: To set out encodes the structured exposition of content, collocating with reasons, arguments, proposals, plan and stall, and admits the Latinate equivalent to expound. To spell out encodes the explicit articulation of content point by point, collocating with implications, consequences, details and terms, and admits the Latinate equivalent to elucidate. Both inhabit the formal register characteristic of academic and administrative prose, the former supplying structural scaffolding, the latter supplying granular explicitness.

### 13. Phrasal verbs que deben evitarse en registro formal

Ciertos phrasal verbs son demasiado informales para la prosa académica: to chuck out (descartar), to put up with (tolerar), to bottle up (reprimir), to boss around (mandonear), to chicken out (acobardarse), to pig out (atracarse). En su lugar, el C1 prefiere to discard, to tolerate, to repress, to dominate, to withdraw, to overeat. La conciencia de esta frontera de registro es esencial.

A2: Some phrasal verbs are too casual for formal writing. Use longer words instead.

B2: Certain phrasal verbs are too informal for academic prose: to chuck out, to put up with, to bottle up, to boss around, to chicken out, to pig out. C1 prefers to discard, to tolerate, to repress, to dominate, to withdraw, to overeat. Awareness of this register boundary is essential.

C1: A delimitable class of phrasal verbs is registerally inappropriate for academic prose: to chuck out, to put up with, to bottle up, to boss around, to chicken out and to pig out all occupy the informal register and would, if deployed in scholarly writing, constitute a registeral infraction. The C1 user accordingly deploys their Latinate equivalents, to discard, to tolerate, to repress, to dominate, to withdraw and to overeat, a registeral policing whose reflexive execution is a diagnostic of advanced competence.

### 14. Phrasal verbs en el speaking formal: debate y presentación

En el speaking formal, como un debate o una presentación académica, los phrasal verbs aportan naturalidad y fluidez sin sacrificar el registro. To put forward a point, to build on an argument, to draw on evidence, to put across an idea, to follow through on a commitment son formulaciones idiomáticas que suenan persuasivas. La clave es combinar phrasal verbs formales con estructuras complejas.

A2: When you speak formally, phrasal verbs can make you sound natural and clear.

B2: In formal speaking, like a debate or academic presentation, phrasal verbs add naturalness and fluency without sacrificing register. To put forward a point, to build on an argument, to draw on evidence, to put across an idea and to follow through on a commitment are idiomatic and persuasive. Combine formal phrasal verbs with complex structures.

C1: In formal oral discourse, such as competitive debate or academic presentation, phrasal verbs confer idiomatic naturalness and fluency without compromising registeral elevation. The formulations to put forward a point, to build on an argument, to draw on evidence, to put across an idea and to follow through on a commitment instantiate the persuasive idiomaticity that characterises proficient oral argumentation. The strategic synthesis of formal phrasal verbs with complex syntactic structures, inversion, mixed conditionals and concessive clauses, is what distinguishes the C1 oral performance.

### 15. Estrategias de aprendizaje y consolidación de phrasal verbs

La consolidación de phrasal verbs exige agruparlos por registro y por campo semántico, no alfabéticamente. Crear tarjetas con el phrasal verb, su sinónimo latino, su colocación y una frase de ejemplo en contexto argumentativo es eficaz. Practicar la reformulación, parafraseando un texto reemplazando sinónimos latinos por phrasal verbs y viceversa, entrena la alternancia de registro. La exposición abundante a prosa académica auténtica es insustituible.

A2: To learn phrasal verbs, group them by topic. Make cards with an example sentence.

B2: Consolidating phrasal verbs requires grouping them by register and semantic field, not alphabetically. Create cards with the phrasal verb, its Latinate synonym, its collocation and an example sentence in argumentative context. Practice reformulation, paraphrasing a text by swapping Latinate synonyms for phrasal verbs and vice versa. Abundant exposure to authentic academic prose is irreplaceable.

C1: The consolidation of phrasal verbs necessitates their taxonomic organisation by register and semantic field rather than alphabetical ordering, a structuring that mirrors their functional distribution. The construction of flashcards encoding the phrasal verb, its Latinate synonym, its canonical collocation and a contextually argumentative exemplar sentence is empirically efficacious. The practice of bidirectional reformulation, paraphrasing a source text by substituting Latinate synonyms for phrasal verbs and conversely, trains the registeral alternation that is the hallmark of C1 lexical agility. Abundant immersion in authentic academic prose remains the irreplaceable substrate of acquisition.

## Resumen de la unidad

En esta unidad del Language Lab has entrenado el uso de phrasal verbs de registro formal en contextos argumentativos. Has aprendido la anatomía del phrasal verb, la distinción entre separables e inseparables, la existencia de phrasal verbs de tres partes y la frontera entre phrasal verbs formales e informales. Has consolidado colocaciones como to bring about a change, to put forward a proposal, to rule out a possibility, to account for a discrepancy y to stem from a problem. Has practicado la alternancia con sinónimos latinos (to bring about / to cause, to put forward / to propose, to rule out / to exclude) según el registro, una habilidad distintiva del C1. Los 19 ejercicios te han entrenado en la reformulación y el uso idiomático. En la siguiente unidad, la 62, el Language Lab se centra en los patrones preposicionales de verbo y adjetivo, otro territorio donde la precisión C1 se gana o se pierde.
