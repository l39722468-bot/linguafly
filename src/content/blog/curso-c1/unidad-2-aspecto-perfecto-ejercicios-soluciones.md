Este cuaderno reúne los **ejercicios de la Unidad 2 del curso C1** con soluciones. Haz cada bloque sin mirar la respuesta.

> **Guía teórica:** [Teoría unidad 2 C1: Aspecto Perfecto en Inglés C1](/blog/curso-c1/unidad-2-aspecto-perfecto)

### Cómo usar este cuaderno

Responde primero y comprueba después. La explicación está pensada para el estudiante hispanohablante.

## Parte II — Ejercicios con soluciones

Responde antes de abrir cada solución. En las tareas abiertas, se ofrece un modelo posible: otras respuestas son válidas si respetan el tiempo, el significado y el registro.

### Ejercicio 1 — Resultado o actividad

Elige la opción más adecuada.

1. I ___ five messages this morning. (*have sent / have been sending*)
2. I ___ messages since 8 a.m. and I need a break. (*have sent / have been sending*)
3. The researchers ___ the final version. It is now online. (*have published / have been publishing*)
4. The researchers ___ the article all week, but it is still not finished. (*have published / have been revising*)

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **have sent** — destaca la cantidad.
2. **have been sending** — destaca la actividad y su duración.
3. **have published** — el resultado está disponible.
4. **have been revising** — el proceso continúa y no está terminado.

</details>

### Ejercicio 2 — For y since

Completa con **for** o **since**.

1. We have been discussing the proposal ___ three hours.
2. She has worked in media ___ 2019.
3. I have known this expression ___ university.
4. They have been exchanging voice notes ___ the train left.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **for three hours** — periodo de duración.
2. **since 2019** — punto de inicio.
3. **since university** — momento de inicio.
4. **since the train left** — acontecimiento que marca el comienzo.

</details>

### Ejercicio 3 — Marcadores de tiempo

Corrige las frases si es necesario.

1. *I have received the message yesterday.*
2. *We have completed three interviews this week.*
3. *She sent the report already.*
4. *When have you first used this platform?*

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **I received the message yesterday.** *Yesterday* es un momento terminado.
2. **We have completed three interviews this week.** Es correcto si la semana aún continúa.
3. **She has already sent the report.** El presente perfecto conecta el envío con el presente.
4. **When did you first use this platform?** *When* pide un momento concreto y terminado.

</details>

### Ejercicio 4 — Verbos de estado

Elige la forma correcta.

1. I (*have known / have been knowing*) her since the conference.
2. They (*have believed / have been believing*) the claim for years.
3. He (*has considered / has been considering*) changing his research topic.
4. We (*have understood / have been understanding*) the implications from the start.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **have known**; 2. **have believed**; 4. **have understood**. Son estados y no suelen usarse en continuo. En 3, **has been considering** es natural porque presenta un proceso de deliberación.

</details>

### Ejercicio 5 — Perfect infinitive

Completa con la forma correcta del verbo.

1. The speaker seems ___ (misunderstand) the question.
2. The journalist claims ___ (check) the original recording.
3. The participants are believed ___ (prefer) private interviews.
4. I am sorry ___ (interrupt) you.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **to have misunderstood**.
2. **to have checked**.
3. **to have preferred**.
4. **to have interrupted**.

En todos los casos, la acción del infinitivo es anterior al verbo principal o a la reacción del hablante.

</details>

### Ejercicio 6 — Perfect gerund

Completa con **having + participio**.

1. She denied ___ (alter) the transcript.
2. He regrets ___ (ignore) the editor’s advice.
3. They admitted ___ (publish) an inaccurate figure.
4. ___ (finish) the interview, the researcher checked the notes.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **having altered**.
2. **having ignored**.
3. **having published**.
4. **Having finished**.

Después de *deny, regret* y *admit* usamos normalmente gerundio. Al inicio de la última frase, *Having finished* muestra una acción anterior a *checked*.

</details>

### Ejercicio 7 — Simple o continuo con significado

Explica la diferencia entre cada par.

1. *She has written three articles.* / *She has been writing articles all month.*
2. *The team has discussed the complaint.* / *The team has been discussing the complaint.*
3. *I have read the book.* / *I have been reading the book.*

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. La primera destaca una producción concreta; la segunda, una actividad prolongada.
2. La primera puede presentar la discusión como hecho o resultado; la segunda enfatiza el proceso, posiblemente aún abierto.
3. La primera normalmente implica que he terminado el libro; la segunda indica que he estado leyéndolo, sin afirmar necesariamente que lo haya acabado.

</details>

### Ejercicio 8 — Reading

Lee el texto y responde.

> The community radio station has changed the way it communicates with listeners. It has introduced a weekly question-and-answer programme and has received hundreds of messages since January. The presenters have been experimenting with shorter interviews because many listeners have been accessing the programme on mobile phones. One presenter seems to have misunderstood the audience data at first, but the team has gradually developed a clearer picture of its listeners. They have not abandoned long-form discussions; instead, they have been combining them with shorter updates.

1. What has the station introduced?
2. How many messages has it received?
3. Why have the presenters been experimenting with shorter interviews?
4. What happened to one presenter?
5. Have they abandoned long-form discussions?

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **A weekly question-and-answer programme.**
2. **Hundreds of messages since January.**
3. **Because many listeners have been accessing the programme on mobile phones.**
4. **The presenter seems to have misunderstood the audience data at first.**
5. **No. They have been combining long discussions with shorter updates.**

El simple resume cambios y resultados; el continuo presenta experimentación y actividad en desarrollo; *seems to have misunderstood* usa el infinitivo perfecto para una acción anterior a la conclusión actual.

</details>

### Ejercicio 9 — Listening guiado

Guion: “I have worked in public relations for eight years, but I have been focusing on crisis communication since last autumn. We have handled four major incidents this year. Our latest client seems to have underestimated the speed at which a rumour can spread. Fortunately, the team has been monitoring social media continuously, so we have responded before the story became dominant.”

Identifica una forma de cada tipo: present perfect simple, present perfect continuous y perfect infinitive. Explica su función.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

**Have worked** expresa experiencia acumulada; **have been focusing** destaca una actividad o especialización que continúa desde un punto de inicio; **seems to have underestimated** presenta una acción anterior a la valoración actual. También aparecen **have handled** como cantidad de incidentes y **has been monitoring** como actividad prolongada.

</details>

### Ejercicio 10 — Transformación

Reformula sin cambiar el significado.

1. It appears that the editor has changed the headline. → The editor appears ___ .
2. They started analysing the comments in March and continue now. → They ___ since March.
3. The report was finished before the meeting began. → By the time the meeting began, the team ___ .
4. He says he checked the source. → He claims ___ .

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **to have changed the headline**.
2. **have been analysing the comments since March**.
3. **had finished the report**.
4. **to have checked the source**.

</details>

### Ejercicio 11 — Elige el aspecto por intención

Escribe una respuesta breve para cada situación.

1. Quieres informar de cuántas entrevistas has completado.
2. Quieres explicar por qué estás cansado después de una mañana de entrevistas.
3. Quieres decir que conoces a tu tutor desde 2021.
4. Quieres atribuir con cautela un error anterior a un portavoz.

<details><summary>Modelo</summary>

1. **I have completed six interviews.**
2. **I have been conducting interviews all morning.**
3. **I have known my tutor since 2021.**
4. **The spokesperson appears to have misunderstood the figures.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 12 — Registro académico

Convierte esta afirmación demasiado directa en una frase académica: *The researcher got the participants’ answers wrong.*

<details><summary>Modelo</summary>

**The researcher appears to have misinterpreted some of the participants’ responses.**

El perfect infinitive introduce distancia y anterioridad; *appears to* evita presentar la interpretación como un hecho indiscutible.

</details>

### Ejercicio 13 — Detección de errores

Corrige el párrafo.

> I have been knowing Maya since 2020. This week, she has sent me three drafts and has been revising the introduction since Monday. Yesterday, she has discovered a problem in the data. She seems to understood the cause, but she denies to have ignored the warning.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

**I have known Maya since 2020. This week, she has sent me three drafts and has been revising the introduction since Monday. Yesterday, she discovered a problem in the data. She seems to have understood the cause, but she denies having ignored the warning.**

Los cambios son: verbo de estado en simple, *yesterday* con past simple, perfect infinitive después de *seems* y perfect gerund después de *denies*.

</details>

### Ejercicio 14 — Writing

Escribe 140–170 palabras sobre esta pregunta: **How has digital communication changed the way people maintain relationships?** Incluye dos presentes perfectos simples, dos continuos, un infinitivo perfecto y una limitación.

<details><summary>Modelo</summary>

Digital communication has transformed the way people maintain relationships. Many users have created networks that would have been impossible to sustain through face-to-face contact alone, and messaging platforms have made it easier to remain in touch across long distances. At the same time, people have been negotiating new expectations about availability. Some users have been checking messages throughout the day, while others have deliberately limited their notifications. This change seems to have increased the speed of communication, but it has not necessarily improved its quality. A short message can maintain a connection, yet it may also replace a more meaningful conversation. Researchers have been examining whether constant contact strengthens relationships or merely creates an impression of closeness. The evidence has not produced a single answer, since the effect depends on the users, the relationship and the purpose of the exchange.

</details>

### Ejercicio 15 — Speaking

Habla durante un minuto sobre una habilidad comunicativa que has desarrollado. Usa *I have learned*, *I have been practising*, *I seem to have improved* y una expresión de duración.

<details><summary>Pista y modelo</summary>

**I have learned to summarise complex ideas more clearly. I have been practising this skill for several months, especially in seminars. I seem to have improved because I can now identify the main point before giving details. I have also received useful feedback, although I still need to work on responding spontaneously.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 16 — Contraste final

Completa el texto con las formas correctas.

> The media team ___ (work) on the campaign since May. So far, it ___ (produce) twelve short videos, but it ___ (not / finish) the longer documentary yet. The director seems ___ (change) the central message after the first audience test. She says the team ___ (learn) a great deal from the feedback.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

**has been working; has produced; has not finished; to have changed; has learned / has learnt.** El primer verbo presenta una actividad prolongada; el segundo, una cantidad; el tercero, un resultado pendiente; el cuarto, una acción anterior a la conclusión; y el último, conocimiento adquirido con relevancia actual.

</details>

### Ejercicio 17 — Revisión de matiz

Explica por qué estas frases pueden ser apropiadas en contextos distintos:

1. *We have investigated the complaint.*
2. *We have been investigating the complaint.*
3. *We had investigated the complaint before the interview.*
4. *We appear to have investigated the complaint thoroughly.*

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. Presenta la investigación como un hecho o resultado actual.
2. Destaca el proceso y puede sugerir que aún continúa.
3. Sitúa la investigación antes de otro momento pasado.
4. Presenta una conclusión cautelosa sobre una investigación anterior y añade la valoración *thoroughly*.

</details>

### Ejercicio 18 — Mini-test final

Elige la opción correcta en cada caso.

1. *I ___ this podcast three times this week.* (*have listened to / have been listening to*)
2. *I ___ to this podcast all afternoon and still have two episodes left.* (*have listened / have been listening*)
3. *She is said ___ the original statement.* (*to have deleted / to deleted*)
4. *They ___ the same misconception since school.* (*have believed / have been believing*)
5. *We met after he ___ the final email.* (*had sent / has sent*)

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **have listened to** — cantidad de veces.
2. **have been listening** — actividad prolongada e incompleta.
3. **to have deleted** — perfect infinitive.
4. **have believed** — verbo de estado.
5. **had sent** — acción anterior a *met*.

</details>

## Resumen de la unidad

El aspecto perfecto ayuda a ordenar y enfocar la información:

1. Usa **present perfect simple** para resultados, cantidades y experiencias conectadas con el presente.
2. Usa **present perfect continuous** para duración, actividad y huellas visibles del proceso.
3. Usa **past perfect** para señalar anterioridad respecto a otro momento pasado.
4. Usa **perfect infinitive** para presentar una acción anterior a un verbo de apariencia, reporte, afirmación o valoración.
5. Usa **perfect gerund** después de preposiciones y verbos como *deny, admit* y *regret*.
6. Comprueba los marcadores: *yesterday* pide normalmente past simple; *since*, *for*, *already* y *yet* suelen conectar con el presente.

La elección correcta no depende solo de si una acción “ha terminado”. Pregúntate qué quieres que vea el lector: el resultado, la cantidad, la actividad, la duración o la relación temporal con otro hecho. Esa decisión es la que convierte una frase gramaticalmente correcta en una frase precisa y verdaderamente C1.
