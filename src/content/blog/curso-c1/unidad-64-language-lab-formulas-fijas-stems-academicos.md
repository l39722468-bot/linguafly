## Parte I — Teoría

### 1. Qué es una fórmula fija y por qué el escritor C1 la necesita

Una fórmula fija es una secuencia fraseológica relativamente invariable que cumple una función retórica recurrente en el discurso académico. No se trata de meras collocations —combinaciones habituales como *heavy rain* o *strong argument*— sino de bloques más largos y funcionalmente especializados: *it is widely acknowledged that*, *this paper seeks to*, *the findings indicate that*. Su utilidad es triple: economizan esfuerzo cognitivo, señalan registro y organizan la progresión temática. El escritor C1 las maneja no como decoración sino como infraestructura.

A2: Some phrases are always the same in formal writing. We use them a lot.

B2: A fixed formula is a phrase that stays the same and has a special job in academic writing, like "it is generally accepted that." These phrases save time and make your text sound formal.

C1: A fixed formula is a relatively invariable phraseological sequence that fulfils a recurrent rhetorical function in academic discourse. It is not a mere collocation but a longer, functionally specialised chunk. Its utility is threefold: it economises cognitive effort, signals register and organises thematic progression. The C1 writer wields it as infrastructure, not ornament.

### 2. Fórmulas de apertura y enmarque

Las fórmulas de apertura sitúan al lector en el territorio del discurso antes de que aparezca el argumento sustantivo. Las más frecuentes incluyen *it is generally accepted that*, *it has long been argued that*, *scholars have increasingly recognised that*, *a growing body of literature suggests that*. Estas secuencias cumplen una función atributiva: el autor no se adjudica la afirmación sino que la sitúa en un consenso o en una tradición investigadora. El error típico del estudiante B2 es sustituirlas por "I think" o "in my opinion", lo cual colapsa el registro.

A2: To start a formal text, we say "it is known that."

B2: Opening formulas like "it is generally accepted that" place the reader in the topic before the main argument. They show that the idea comes from a consensus, not just from the writer.

C1: Opening formulas seat the reader in the territory of discourse before the substantive argument appears. Sequences such as it is generally accepted that, it has long been argued that and a growing body of literature suggests that fulfil an attributive function: the writer does not appropriate the claim but situates it within a consensus or a research tradition. The typical B2 error is to substitute them with "I think," which collapses the register entirely.

### 3. Fórmulas de posicionamiento del autor

Cuando el autor sí quiere atribuirse una postura, dispone de otro repertorio: *this paper argues that*, *the present study contends that*, *it is our contention that*, *we maintain that*. Nótese la alternancia entre la primera persona plural (*we maintain*) y las construcciones impersonales con *it* (*it is our contention*). Ambas estrategias elevan el registro, pero la impersonal es más cautelosa y propia de las ciencias sociales; la primera persona plural es más habitual en humanidades. Conocer esa diferencia de convención disciplinar forma parte de la competencia C1.

A2: When you give your opinion, you can say "this text says that."

B2: To show your own position, use phrases like "this paper argues that" or "we maintain that." The choice between "we" and impersonal "it" depends on the discipline.

C1: When the author wishes to attribute a stance, a separate repertoire is available: this paper argues that, the present study contends that, it is our contention that, we maintain that. The alternation between first-person plural and impersonal it-constructions both elevates register, yet the impersonal is more cautious and typical of the social sciences, while the first-person plural is more habitual in the humanities. Grasping that disciplinary convention is part of C1 competence.

### 4. Fórmulas de evidencia y reporting

El discurso académico vive de citar y reportar. Las fórmulas canónicas son *according to X*, *as X observes*, *X argues that*, *X's findings suggest that*, *in the words of X*. El verbo de reporting no es indiferente: *argue*, *contend*, *maintain* y *assert* presuponen una posición tomada, mientras que *note*, *observe*, *point out* y *acknowledge* presentan el contenido como menos combativo. *Claim* introduce escepticismo implícito. Elegir el verbo de reporting correcto es una de las marcas más finas del registro C1.

A2: When we quote someone, we say "X says that."

B2: Academic writing uses reporting verbs like "argues," "notes" or "claims." The verb you choose shows how you feel about the source's idea.

C1: Academic discourse lives by citing and reporting. The reporting verb is not indifferent: argue, contend, maintain and assert presuppose a taken stance, whereas note, observe, point out and acknowledge present the content as less combative. Claim introduces implicit scepticism. Selecting the correct reporting verb is one of the finest markers of C1 register, for it calibrates the dialogic distance the writer constructs with each cited source.

### 5. Hedges y mitigación asertiva

Un hedge es un recurso que modula la fuerza de una afirmación para proteger al autor de sobregeneralizaciones. Los hedges académicos más comunes son *it may be argued that*, *it could be contended that*, *the evidence would seem to suggest*, *arguably*, *to a certain extent*, *in all likelihood*, *there is a tendency for*. Su función no es cobardía retórica sino prudencia epistémica: el investigador C1 sabe que rara vez dispone de pruebas absolutas y modula en consecuencia. El abuso, sin embargo, diluye la tesis; el equilibrio es la marca del escritor maduro.

A2: Sometimes we make our sentences softer, like "maybe" or "perhaps."

B2: Hedges make claims less strong. Words like "arguably" or "to a certain extent" protect the writer from overgeneralising. But using too many hedges makes the argument weak.

C1: A hedge is a resource that modulates the force of an assertion to protect the author from overgeneralisation. Common academic hedges include it may be argued that, the evidence would seem to suggest, arguably and in all likelihood. Their function is not rhetorical cowardice but epistemic prudence: the C1 researcher knows that absolute proof is rare and modulates accordingly. Overuse, however, dilutes the thesis; equilibrium is the hallmark of the mature writer.

### 6. Stems académicos: definición y mecánica

Un stem académico es una plantilla fraseológica parcialmente abierta que el escritor completa con contenido disciplinar. Ejemplos canónicos: *the extent to which X*, *the role of Y in Z*, *the relationship between X and Y*, *the extent of X's influence on Y*, *the degree to which X accounts for Y*. A diferencia de las fórmulas fijas puras, los stems admiten ranuras variables, pero su armazón sintáctico es estable. El escritor C1 los usa para empaquetar relaciones conceptuales complejas en una sola unidad fraseológica.

A2: Some sentence patterns have gaps you fill in, like "the role of X in Y."

B2: Academic stems are sentence templates with blanks. You fill the blanks with your topic. "The extent to which X" is a very common one in academic writing.

C1: An academic stem is a partially open phraseological template that the writer completes with disciplinary content. Canonical examples include the extent to which X, the role of Y in Z and the degree to which X accounts for Y. Unlike pure fixed formulas, stems admit variable slots, yet their syntactic armature is stable. The C1 writer uses them to package complex conceptual relations into a single phraseological unit, thereby maintaining syntactic precision without improvising the architecture each time.

### 7. El stem *the extent to which*

Este stem es probablemente el más productivo del inglés académico. Permite formular grados y proporciones sin recurrir a cuantificadores torpes: *the extent to which government policy shapes public opinion*, *the extent to which linguistic input determines acquisition*. Su peligrosidad reside en que muchos estudiantes B2 lo sustituyen por "how much", lo cual rebaja el registro inmediatamente. Otro error frecuente es escribir *the extent which* sin la preposición *to*: la preposición es obligatoria y su omisión es marca inequívoca de nivel intermedio.

A2: "How much" can be said as "the extent to which" in formal writing.

B2: "The extent to which" is a formal way to talk about degrees. Many students wrongly drop the word "to." Always keep it.

C1: The stem the extent to which is arguably the most productive in academic English. It formulates degrees and proportions without clumsy quantifiers: the extent to which government policy shapes public opinion. Its danger lies in that many B2 learners substitute "how much," which immediately lowers the register. Another frequent error is writing the extent which without the preposition to: the preposition is obligatory and its omission is an unambiguous marker of intermediate level.

### 8. El stem *the role of X in Y*

Este stem enmarca relaciones de causalidad o contribución sin comprometerse con un mecanismo explicativo completo: *the role of motivation in second language acquisition*, *the role of institutions in economic development*. Es más neutro que *the cause of* o *the effect of*, porque *role* admite matices —puede ser principal, secundario, mediador, catalizador— sin fijarlos de antemano. Por eso es tan frecuente en títulos de tesis y en introducciones de artículos. El error típico del B2 es escribir *the role of X on Y* con la preposición equivocada: el stem exige *in*, no *on*.

A2: "The role of X in Y" talks about how X affects Y.

B2: "The role of X in Y" is a neutral way to describe a relationship. It is common in thesis titles. Note the preposition "in," not "on."

C1: This stem frames causal or contributory relations without committing to a full explanatory mechanism: the role of motivation in second language acquisition. It is more neutral than the cause of or the effect of, because role admits nuances —central, secondary, mediating, catalytic— without fixing them in advance. Hence its frequency in thesis titles and article introductions. The typical B2 error is writing the role of X on Y with the wrong preposition: the stem requires in, not on.

### 9. Stems de comparación y contraste

Los stems comparativos permiten contrastar dos entidades en una sola arquitectura: *whereas X emphasises A, Y foregrounds B*, *in contrast to X, which prioritises A, Y centres on B*, *while X focuses on A, Y is concerned with B*. La estructura paralela —dos cláusulas con la misma forma sintáctica— es lo que da elegancia al contraste. Romper la paralelidad (*whereas X emphasises A, Y is about B*) destruye el efecto y delata un escritor que aún no controla la fraseología académica.

A2: We compare two things with "whereas" or "while."

B2: Comparative stems like "whereas X emphasises A, Y foregrounds B" contrast two ideas in one structure. Keep both sides parallel for a clean contrast.

C1: Comparative stems contrast two entities within a single architecture: whereas X emphasises A, Y foregrounds B. The parallel structure —two clauses with identical syntactic form— is what lends the contrast its elegance. Breaking parallelism (whereas X emphasises A, Y is about B) destroys the effect and betrays a writer who does not yet control academic phraseology. The C1 writer plans parallelism before drafting and ensures both verbs belong to the same semantic field.

### 10. Fórmulas de transición y progresión temática

La escritura académica avanza por transiciones explícitas. Las fórmulas más útiles son *having established X, we now turn to Y*, *this raises the question of*, *it follows from this that*, *a further consideration is*, *with this in mind*. La transición no es decorativa: indica al lector qué paso del argumento acaba de completarse y cuál viene después. Un escritor C1 planifica sus transiciones antes de redactar, porque sabe que son el esqueleto visible del razonamiento.

A2: To move to a new point, we say "now we will talk about."

B2: Transition phrases like "having established X, we now turn to Y" guide the reader through the argument. They show what step is done and what comes next.

C1: Academic writing advances through explicit transitions. The most useful formulas are having established X, we now turn to Y; this raises the question of; it follows from this that. The transition is not decorative: it indicates to the reader which step of the argument has just been completed and which comes next. A C1 writer plans transitions before drafting, knowing they are the visible skeleton of the reasoning.

### 11. Fórmulas de conclusión y cierre

El cierre académico tiene su propia fraseología: *in light of the above*, *taken together, these findings suggest*, *the evidence presented here supports the view that*, *it may therefore be concluded that*. Estas fórmulas condensan y proyectan: resumen lo argumentado y lo conectan con una inferencia. El error clásico del B2 es cerrar con "in conclusion" sin más, una fórmula tan genérica que no orienta al lector sobre la naturaleza del cierre.

A2: To finish, we say "in conclusion."

B2: Closing phrases like "in light of the above" or "taken together, these findings suggest" summarise and draw a conclusion. "In conclusion" alone is too generic.

C1: The academic close has its own phraseology: in light of the above; taken together, these findings suggest; the evidence presented here supports the view that; it may therefore be concluded that. These formulas condense and project: they summarise what has been argued and connect it with an inference. The classic B2 error is to close with "in conclusion" alone, a formula so generic that it does not orient the reader to the nature of the close.

### 12. Collocations institucionalizadas del discurso académico

Ciertas combinaciones verbo-sustantivo son tan frecuentes en el registro académico que funcionan casi como fórmulas: *draw a distinction*, *cast doubt on*, *shed light on*, *lay the groundwork for*, *call into question*, *bring to bear*, *give rise to*, *pave the way for*. Estas collocations institucionalizadas empaquetan operaciones intelectuales completas en dos o tres palabras. El escritor C1 las acumula como vocabulario de cargo y las despliega con precisión, evitando paráfrasis más largas y menos elegantes.

A2: Some word pairs are always used together, like "shed light on."

B2: Collocations like "cast doubt on," "shed light on" and "pave the way for" are very common in academic writing. They pack a whole idea into a few words.

C1: Certain verb-noun combinations are so frequent in academic register that they function almost as formulas: draw a distinction, cast doubt on, shed light on, lay the groundwork for, call into question, give rise to, pave the way for. These institutionalised collocations package complete intellectual operations into two or three words. The C1 writer accumulates them as cargo vocabulary and deploys them with precision, avoiding longer and less elegant paraphrases.

### 13. La distinción entre fórmula fija y cliché

No toda secuencia recurrente es buena fórmula. Hay clichés académicos desgastados —*at the end of the day*, *needless to say*, *it goes without saying*— que el escritor C1 evita porque señalan pereza retórica. La diferencia entre fórmula productiva y cliché estéril reside en la densidad informativa: la fórmula productiva hace trabajo conceptual; el cliché rellena espacio. El escritor maduro audita sus propias fórmulas y elimina las que no cargan significado.

A2: Some common phrases are too old or lazy, like "needless to say." Avoid them.

B2: Not every common phrase is good. Clichés like "at the end of the day" or "since the dawn of time" are lazy. Good formulas do real work; clichés just fill space.

C1: Not every recurrent sequence is a good formula. Worn academic clichés —at the end of the day, needless to say, it goes without saying, since the dawn of time— are avoided by the C1 writer because they signal rhetorical laziness. The difference between a productive formula and a sterile cliché lies in informational density: the productive formula does conceptual work; the cliché fills space. The mature writer audits their own formulas and eliminates those that carry no meaning.

### 14. Variación disciplinar de las fórmulas

Las fórmulas académicas no son universales: varían según la disciplina. Las ciencias duras prefieren fórmulas de procedimiento (*the experiment was conducted in order to*, *the results were obtained by means of*); las ciencias sociales, fórmulas de debate (*it has been contended that*, *a counterargument to this view is*); las humanidades, fórmulas hermenéuticas (*this reading suggests that*, *the text can be interpreted as*). El escritor C1 que se mueve entre disciplinas calibra su repertorio fraseológico en función del público.

A2: Different subjects use different formal phrases.

B2: Formulas change by discipline. Science uses procedural phrases; social sciences use debate phrases; humanities use interpretation phrases. Match your phrases to your audience.

C1: Academic formulas are not universal: they vary by discipline. The hard sciences prefer procedural formulas (the experiment was conducted in order to); the social sciences, debate formulas (it has been contended that); the humanities, hermeneutic formulas (this reading suggests that). The C1 writer who moves between disciplines calibrates their phraseological repertoire in function of the audience and the research tradition.

### 15. Cómo practicar las fórmulas fijas sin sonar mecánico

El riesgo del trabajo con fórmulas es que la prosa suene ensamblada. Para evitarlo: primero, alterna la posición de la fórmula —no siempre al inicio de la frase—; segundo, intercala frases sin fórmula entre las fórmulas, para que el lector perciba ritmo; tercero, personaliza los stems con contenido específico y no genérico; cuarto, varía los hedges para que no se repita siempre el mismo. El objetivo es que el lector note la estructura, no el molde.

A2: Don't use the same phrase too much. Mix it up.

B2: If you use too many fixed phrases, your writing sounds robotic. Vary their position, mix in normal sentences, and personalise the stems with your own content.

C1: The risk of working with formulas is that prose sounds assembled. To avoid it: first, alternate the position of the formula —not always at sentence-initial position—; second, interleave formula-free sentences so the reader perceives rhythm; third, personalise stems with specific rather than generic content; fourth, vary hedges so the same one does not recur. The objective is that the reader notices the structure, not the mould. The C1 writer treats formulas as joinery tools, not ornament: deploys them where they structure the argument and withdraws them where they impede fluency.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

**Ejercicio 4.** Identifica el verbo de reporting que introduce escepticismo implícito en: "Smith (2020) ____ that the policy reduced inequality."
<details>

*claims*. *Smith (2020) claims that the policy reduced inequality* sugiere que el autor no se compromete plenamente con la afirmación de Smith.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

</details>

**Ejercicio 6.** Completa el stem comparativo manteniendo paralelismo: "Whereas behavioural economics emphasises heuristics, ____."

</details>

**Ejercicio 7.** Reescribe "In conclusion, my essay showed that" con una fórmula de cierre académica.
<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*In light of the above, the evidence presented here supports the view that...* o *Taken together, these findings suggest that...*
</details>

**Ejercicio 8.** Sustituye el cliché "needless to say" por una formulación más densa conceptualmente.
<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*It scarcely needs emphasising that* o, mejor aún, suprimir el cliché e introducir directamente la afirmación con una fórmula de evidencia: *the literature consistently indicates that...*
</details>

**Ejercicio 9.** Completa la collocation institucionalizada: "The new data ____ doubt on the established theory."
<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*casts doubt on*. *The new data casts doubt on the established theory.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 10.** Reescribe usando el stem *the role of X in Y*: "Motivation matters a lot for learning languages."
<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The role of motivation in second language acquisition has been extensively documented.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 11.** Identifica y corrige el error en: "The extent which income inequality affects health is well documented."
<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

Falta la preposición *to*. Corrección: *The extent **to** which income inequality affects health is well documented.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 12.** Reescribe la transición "Now I will talk about" con una fórmula académica.
<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*Having established the theoretical framework, we now turn to the empirical evidence.* o *This raises the question of...*
</details>

**Ejercicio 13.** Completa el stem: "the degree ____ which early intervention accounts ____ later outcomes."
<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*the degree **to** which early intervention accounts **for** later outcomes.* Dos preposiciones obligatorias en un mismo stem.
</details>

**Ejercicio 14.** Sustituye "X says that" por un verbo de reporting que presente el contenido como menos combativo.
<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*X notes that* o *X observes that* o *X points out that*. Estos verbos presentan la afirmación como observación, no como posicionamiento combativo.
</details>

**Ejercicio 15.** Reescribe con la fórmula *it has long been argued that*: "People have always said that cities make people lonely."
<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*It has long been argued that urban environments contribute to social isolation.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 16.** Completa la collocation: "The pilot study ____ the groundwork for future research."
<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*lays the groundwork for*. *The pilot study lays the groundwork for future research.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 17.** Reescribe "This shows that" con una fórmula de evidencia más formal.
<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The findings indicate that* o *the evidence presented here supports the view that* o *these results would seem to suggest that.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 18.** Completa el stem comparativo sin romper la paralelidad: "In contrast to qualitative research, which prioritises meaning, ____."
<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*In contrast to qualitative research, which prioritises meaning, quantitative research centres on measurement.* Paralelismo: *which prioritises* / *centres on* + sustantivo abstracto.
</details>

**Ejercicio 19.** Redacta una oración de cierre para un ensayo sobre el impacto de las redes sociales en el debate público, usando al menos dos fórmulas fijas.
<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*Taken together, the evidence presented here supports the view that social media platforms, to a certain extent, have reshaped the contours of public debate; it may therefore be concluded that their role in contemporary democratic life warrants sustained critical attention.*
</details>

## Resumen de la unidad

En esta unidad hemos recorrido el andamiaje fraseológico del inglés académico C1. Hemos distinguido las fórmulas fijas —secuencias relativamente invariables con función retórica recurrente— de los stems académicos —plantillas parcialmente abiertas que el escritor completa con contenido disciplinar. Hemos clasificado las fórmulas por función: apertura y enmarque, posicionamiento del autor, evidencia y reporting, hedges y mitigación, transición, conclusión. Hemos trabajado stems productivos como *the extent to which*, *the role of X in Y* y los comparativos paralelos. Hemos advertido contra el cliché estéril y contra la sonoridad mecánica que produce el uso indiscriminado de fórmulas. Y hemos subrayado la variación disciplinar: las fórmulas no son universales, sino que se calibran según el público y la tradición investigadora. El escritor C1 no acumula fórmulas como ornamento: las despliega como infraestructura invisible que sostiene el argumento y orienta al lector. En la siguiente unidad daremos el salto a los condicionales avanzados —mixtos, invertidos—, donde la sintaxis y la fraseología académica convergen en estructuras de máxima densidad lógica.
