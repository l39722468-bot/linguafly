## Parte II — Ejercicios con soluciones

### Ejercicio 1 — Inversión con adverbiales negativos

Completa con el auxiliar adecuado.

1. Rarely ___ the Security Council been so divided.
2. Seldom ___ governments acknowledge the long-term cost.
3. Never ___ I seen such a fragile ceasefire.
4. Little ___ the negotiators realise how serious the dispute was.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **has**; 2. **do**; 3. **have**; 4. **did**. Cuando no hay auxiliar en presente o pasado simple, añadimos *do/does/did* y el verbo vuelve a la forma base.

</details>

### Ejercicio 2 — Not only

Reescribe con **not only... but also**.

*The drought reduced harvests. It increased food prices.*

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

**Not only did the drought reduce harvests, but it also increased food prices.** La inversión aparece después de *not only* y la segunda cláusula mantiene el paralelismo.

</details>

### Ejercicio 3 — Not until y only after

Completa.

1. Not until the treaty was signed ___ the ceasefire hold.
2. Only after the corridor reopened ___ aid reach the displaced communities.
3. Only when reliable data become available ___ the policy be evaluated.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **did**; 2. **did**; 3. **can**. La cláusula temporal mantiene el orden normal; la inversión está en la principal.

</details>

### Ejercicio 4 — Fronting o cleft

Elige la estructura más adecuada para destacar el elemento indicado.

1. Destacar *climate finance*: *It was climate finance that / Climate finance it was that dominated the summit.*
2. Destacar la necesidad de regulación: *What the planet needs is / That the planet needs is enforceable regulation.*
3. Destacar la región: *It was in the border region that / In the border region it was that the shortage became severe.*

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **It was climate finance that dominated the summit.**
2. **What the planet needs is enforceable regulation.**
3. **It was in the border region that the shortage became severe.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 5 — As for y with regard to

Completa con la expresión adecuada.

1. ___ migration, the report recommends regional coordination.
2. ___ debt relief, the proposal remains under discussion.
3. ___ the refugees, their immediate safety must remain the priority.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

Son posibles **with regard to** y **as for** en los tres casos, con diferencias leves de registro. *With regard to* es más formal; *as for* introduce un cambio o delimitación de tema más marcado.

</details>

### Ejercicio 6 — Contraste

Elige **whereas, despite, by contrast** o **conversely**.

1. ___ developed nations have stabilised inflation, emerging economies face debt distress.
2. ___ economic growth, inequality widened.
3. The first region improved access. ___, the second saw little change.
4. Lower rates may support borrowers; ___, they can reduce returns for savers.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **Whereas**; 2. **Despite**; 3. **By contrast**; 4. **conversely**. *Whereas* introduce una cláusula; *despite* un grupo nominal; los adverbios enlazan dos resultados contrastados.

</details>

### Ejercicio 7 — Corrige la concesión

Corrige estas frases.

1. *Despite the economy grew, inequality widened.*
2. *Whereas developed nations stabilised, but emerging markets struggled.*
3. *The policy was expensive, however it improved access.*

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **Although the economy grew, inequality widened** o **Despite economic growth, inequality widened.**
2. **Whereas developed nations stabilised, emerging markets struggled.**
3. **The policy was expensive; however, it improved access.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 8 — Especulación presente

Elige **might well, could conceivably, is likely to** o **is unlikely to**.

1. Without funding, the programme ___ fail.
2. A negotiated settlement ___ be reached this year, although several obstacles remain.
3. Prices ___ rise if humanitarian access is restricted.
4. The measure ___ solve the underlying cause on its own.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **might well**; 2. **could conceivably**; 3. **are likely to**; 4. **is unlikely to**. Cada expresión gradúa la hipótesis de forma distinta.

</details>

### Ejercicio 9 — Especulación pasada

Completa con el modal perfecto correcto.

1. The negotiators ___ ___ ___ (underestimate) public opposition.
2. The delay ___ ___ ___ (result) from a shortage of fuel.
3. The figures ___ ___ ___ (manipulate).
4. The agreement ___ ___ ___ (address) every concern.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **may/might have underestimated**.
2. **could have resulted**.
3. **may/might/could have been manipulated**.
4. **can’t have addressed** si las pruebas muestran que quedan muchas preocupaciones; **might not have addressed** si solo se plantea como posibilidad.

</details>

### Ejercicio 10 — Vocabulario global

Completa con **ceasefire, displacement, accountability, debt relief** o **humanitarian access**.

1. The parties negotiated a temporary ___ .
2. The conflict caused mass ___ across the region.
3. Aid agencies need secure ___ to reach civilians.
4. The report calls for greater governmental ___ .
5. Several countries requested urgent ___ .

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **ceasefire**; 2. **displacement**; 3. **humanitarian access**; 4. **accountability**; 5. **debt relief**.

</details>

### Ejercicio 11 — Reading

Lee el texto y responde.

> Rarely has a climate summit produced such a sharp contrast between public ambition and financial commitments. Whereas wealthy states have announced ambitious emissions targets, several emerging economies continue to face debt distress and limited access to climate finance. Only after a new funding mechanism was proposed did negotiators begin to discuss implementation in detail. The mechanism might well improve adaptation capacity, but it is unlikely to succeed unless funding is predictable and local institutions are involved. As for vulnerable communities, the agreement states that their participation must be protected at every stage.

1. What contrast does the text identify?
2. When did negotiators discuss implementation in detail?
3. What might the mechanism improve?
4. What conditions are necessary for success?
5. What does the agreement say about vulnerable communities?

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **There is a contrast between wealthy states’ public ambition and the financial commitments available to emerging economies.**
2. **Only after a new funding mechanism was proposed.**
3. **It might improve adaptation capacity.**
4. **Funding must be predictable and local institutions must be involved.**
5. **Their participation must be protected at every stage.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 12 — Listening guiado

Guion: “Under no circumstances should the humanitarian corridor be treated as permanent. The agreement could conceivably hold for several weeks, but it might well collapse if aid deliveries are delayed. By contrast, local ceasefires have tended to last longer when community leaders are involved. Only then can trust develop between the parties.”

Identifica una inversión negativa, dos expresiones de especulación y un conector de contraste.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

Inversión negativa: **Under no circumstances should the humanitarian corridor be treated as permanent.** Especulación: **could conceivably hold** y **might well collapse**. Contraste: **By contrast**. La última frase usa inversión con *only then*: **Only then can trust develop**.

</details>

### Ejercicio 13 — Reformulación

Reescribe con un registro más académico: *The sanctions will fix the crisis, but they may make things worse.*

<details><summary>Modelo</summary>

**The sanctions are unlikely to address the underlying cause on their own; conversely, they might well intensify economic pressure on vulnerable populations.**

La reformulación evita una promesa absoluta, usa una colocación precisa y añade un contraste lógico.

</details>

### Ejercicio 14 — Writing

Escribe 150–180 palabras sobre: **Should international organisations impose conditions on humanitarian aid?** Incluye una inversión, dos conectores de contraste y tres expresiones de especulación.

<details><summary>Modelo</summary>

It is often argued that aid should be conditional on improvements in governance. However, such conditions may delay assistance when vulnerable populations are already facing food insecurity. Only when accountability mechanisms are transparent can conditionality be evaluated fairly. Whereas some donors might well consider strict requirements necessary, local organisations could conceivably view them as an obstacle to humanitarian access. By contrast, unrestricted funding is likely to reach communities more quickly, although it may not guarantee responsible implementation. Under no circumstances should civilians lose access to emergency support because political negotiations have stalled. International agencies should therefore distinguish between conditions designed to protect public resources and requirements that merely express donor preferences. A carefully monitored compromise might provide funding immediately while requiring public reporting, independent audits and community participation. Such a system is unlikely to satisfy every actor, but it could balance speed, accountability and local ownership more effectively than a single inflexible rule.

</details>

### Ejercicio 15 — Speaking

Habla durante un minuto sobre un problema global. Usa **whereas**, **by contrast**, **might well**, **could conceivably** y **as for**.

<details><summary>Pista y modelo</summary>

**Whereas some countries can invest in climate adaptation, others face debt distress. By contrast, communities with strong local institutions might well respond more effectively to extreme weather. A regional funding mechanism could conceivably reduce the disparity. As for vulnerable families, their immediate access to food and shelter must remain the priority.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 16 — Traducción controlada

Traduce al inglés.

1. Raramente ha sido tan difícil negociar un alto el fuego.
2. No fue hasta que se firmó el tratado cuando se mantuvo la tregua.
3. El acuerdo podría fracasar si no se garantiza el acceso humanitario.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **Rarely has it been so difficult to negotiate a ceasefire.**
2. **Not until the treaty was signed did the ceasefire hold.**
3. **The agreement could fail if humanitarian access is not guaranteed.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 17 — Corrección avanzada

Corrige el párrafo.

> Rarely governments have agreed on climate finance. Despite the proposal was ambitious, it did not address debt distress; however several states supported it. The mechanism could conceivably will improve adaptation, but under no circumstances local communities should be excluded.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

**Rarely have governments agreed on climate finance. Although the proposal was ambitious, it did not address debt distress; however, several states supported it. The mechanism could conceivably improve adaptation, but under no circumstances should local communities be excluded.**

Se corrige la inversión tras *rarely*, la concesión con *although*, la puntuación de *however*, la ausencia de *will* después de *could* y la inversión tras *under no circumstances*.

</details>

### Ejercicio 18 — Mini-test final

Completa el texto.

> ___ only after the corridor reopened ___ aid reach the displaced population. ___ contrast, the rural route remains unsafe, ___ local leaders have requested additional monitoring. A negotiated settlement ___ well be possible, but it is ___ to address the underlying dispute without sustained diplomacy.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

**Only; did; By; whereas/while; might; unlikely.** Una versión completa es: **Only after the corridor reopened did aid reach the displaced population. By contrast, the rural route remains unsafe, whereas local leaders have requested additional monitoring. A negotiated settlement might well be possible, but it is unlikely to address the underlying dispute without sustained diplomacy.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 19 — Integración final

Reescribe el argumento en un solo párrafo formal. Debes incluir una inversión con **rarely**, una comparación con **whereas**, un resultado con **therefore** o **consequently** y una hipótesis con **might well**.

> International agreements rarely include effective enforcement. Some governments make ambitious promises, but local institutions receive little funding. A rapid improvement is possible, but it is not certain.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

**Rarely do international agreements include effective enforcement. Whereas some governments make ambitious promises, local institutions receive little funding; consequently, implementation remains uneven. A rapid improvement might well be possible if reliable funding were secured, although it cannot be guaranteed.**

La primera frase usa inversión porque *rarely* aparece al principio. *Whereas* contrasta dos realidades completas, *consequently* introduce el resultado y *might well* presenta una posibilidad razonable sin convertirla en predicción segura.

</details>

## Resumen de la unidad

Usa **fronting** para poner en primer plano un tema o una circunstancia. Después de adverbiales negativos como **never, rarely, seldom, not only, not until** y expresiones como **under no circumstances**, invierte el auxiliar y el sujeto. Si no hay auxiliar, añade **do, does o did**.

Usa clefts como **it was X that...** y pseudo-clefts como **what... is...** para enfocar una idea concreta. Usa **whereas, while, by contrast, conversely** e **in contrast** para comparar resultados sin convertir el análisis en una oposición simplista. Recuerda que **despite** necesita un nombre o gerundio, mientras que **although** introduce una cláusula.

Finalmente, el lenguaje especulativo debe reflejar la evidencia. **Might well** expresa una posibilidad razonable, **could conceivably** una posibilidad imaginable, **be likely to** una probabilidad y **be unlikely to** una baja probabilidad. Cuando combines esas formas con colocaciones como *secure humanitarian access*, *ease debt distress* y *reach a negotiated settlement*, podrás hablar de problemas globales con énfasis, equilibrio y rigor propios del nivel C1.
