Este cuaderno reúne los **ejercicios de la Unidad 20 del curso C1** con soluciones. Haz cada bloque sin mirar la respuesta.

### Cómo usar este cuaderno

Responde primero y comprueba después. La explicación está pensada para el estudiante hispanohablante.

## Parte II — Ejercicios

### Ejercicio 1 — Preposiciones de causa

Completa con *owing to / due to / because of / on account of*.

1. ___ supply-chain disruptions, output fell.
2. The downturn was ___ a collapse in consumer confidence.
3. ___ rising energy costs, margins narrowed.
4. The flight was cancelled ___ the strike.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **Owing to / On account of** (más formal) o **Due to / Because of** (válidos). 2. **due to**. 3. **On account of / Owing to** (formal) o **Because of**. 4. **because of / due to**. Todos rigen un sustantivo, nunca una cláusula con verbo.

</details>

### Ejercicio 2 — As a result / consequently

Une las frases con un linker de resultado y puntuación correcta.

1. Demand surged. Prices climbed.
2. The currency depreciated. Imports became dearer.
3. Real wages fell. Spending contracted.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. *Demand surged; as a result, prices climbed.* (o *... . Consequently, prices climbed.*)
2. *The currency depreciated; consequently, imports became dearer.*
3. *Real wages fell, and, as a consequence, spending contracted.*

Los linkers formales se separan con punto y coma o punto, no con coma simple.

</details>

### Ejercicio 3 — Thus / hence / thereby

Elige el linker adecuado (uno de los tres) y la forma correcta.

1. The central bank held rates; ___ (avoid) a sharper slowdown.
2. The data is unreliable; ___ the caution.
3. The firm offshored production, ___ (cut) costs.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. *...; thus avoiding a sharper slowdown.* (*thus* + -ing con sujeto elidido).
2. *...; hence the caution.* (*hence* + sustantivo).
3. *...thereby cutting costs.* (*thereby* + -ing).

</details>

### Ejercicio 4 — Therefore vs so

Reescribe en registro formal sustituyendo *so*.

1. Inflation eased, so the bank cut rates.
2. Demand fell, so prices softened.
3. Yields rose, so investors rotated into bonds.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. *Inflation eased; therefore, the bank cut rates.*
2. *Demand fell; consequently, prices softened.*
3. *Yields rose; therefore, investors rotated into bonds.*

En escritura formal, *therefore* o *consequently* sustituyen a *so*.

</details>

### Ejercicio 5 — Verbos de causa-resultado

Completa con *resulted in / led to / brought about / triggered*.

1. The shock ___ a recession.
2. Looser credit ___ a housing boom.
3. The reforms ___ a gradual recovery.
4. The rate hike ___ a sell-off.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **resulted in**; 2. **led to**; 3. **brought about** (cambio de calado); 4. **triggered** (causa inmediata). Los cuatro contienen ya la relación causal, así que no necesitan *because*. Atención a la voz: estos verbos ponen la **causa** como sujeto y el **efecto** como objeto (*the shock resulted in a recession*), justo al revés que *result from*, que pone el efecto como sujeto (*the recession resulted from the shock*). Confundir ambos es uno de los errores más frecuentes en escritos económicos.

</details>

### Ejercicio 6 — Estructuras con it (acknowledged/known)

Reescribe con *It is widely acknowledged/known that*.

1. Everyone accepts that globalisation has lifted many out of poverty.
2. People know that trade openness carries distributional costs.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. *It is widely acknowledged that globalisation has lifted many out of poverty.*
2. *It is well known that trade openness carries distributional costs.*

El sujeto preparatorio *it* eleva el registro y presenta un consenso sin citarlo.

</details>

### Ejercicio 7 — It has been suggested/argued/claimed that

Presenta cada tesis con la estructura adecuada.

1. Some say the recovery is largely jobless.
2. Economists argue that outsourcing hollows out middle-skill jobs.
3. Critics claim that the stimulus inflated asset prices.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. *It has been suggested that the recovery is largely jobless.*
2. *It is often argued that outsourcing hollows out middle-skill employment.*
3. *It has been claimed that the stimulus inflated asset prices.*

*Suggested* es prudente, *argued* reconoce un debate, *claimed* insinúa escepticismo.

</details>

### Ejercicio 8 — It appears / it seems / it is clear that

Elige el grado de certeza adecuado.

1. The data tentatively shows the downturn has bottomed out. (___ that)
2. The evidence is unmistakable: the policy had unintended consequences. (___ that)
3. Early signs suggest inflation is becoming entrenched. (___ that)

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. *It appears that the downturn has bottomed out.* (inferencia tentativa).
2. *It is clear that the policy had unintended consequences.* (conclusión afirmada).
3. *It seems that inflation is becoming entrenched.* (inferencia preliminar).

</details>

### Ejercicio 9 — It is estimated/reported/believed that

Reescribe con pasiva evaluativa.

1. Analysts estimate that the deficit will widen to 4% of GDP.
2. Reports say that growth stalled in the third quarter.
3. Analysts believe that volatility will persist.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. *It is estimated that the deficit will widen to 4% of GDP.* (o *The deficit is estimated to widen to 4% of GDP.*)
2. *It is reported that growth stalled in the third quarter.*
3. *It is believed that volatility will persist into next year.*

</details>

### Ejercicio 10 — Corrige los errores

1. Owing to the supply chain was disrupted, output fell.
2. Demand surged, consequently prices climbed.
3. The firm offshored production, thereby cut costs.
4. It is widely acknowledge that inflation is easing.
5. Due to rising inflation, so consumers cut spending.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. *Owing to supply-chain disruptions, output fell.*
2. *Demand surged; consequently, prices climbed.*
3. *The firm offshored production, thereby cutting costs.*
4. *It is widely acknowledged that inflation is easing.*
5. *Owing to rising inflation, consumers cut spending.* (sin *so*; o *...; consequently, consumers cut spending.*)

</details>

### Ejercicio 11 — Transformación (CAE-style)

Reescribe manteniendo el sentido con la palabra dada.

1. The shock caused a recession. (RESULTED)
2. Everyone accepts that the recovery is uneven. (ACKNOWLEDGED)
3. The currency fell, and imports became dearer. (CONSEQUENTLY)
4. Analysts estimate that growth will resume. (ESTIMATED)

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. *The shock resulted in a recession.*
2. *It is widely acknowledged that the recovery is uneven.*
3. *The currency fell; consequently, imports became dearer.*
4. *It is estimated that growth will resume.*

</details>

### Ejercicio 12 — Vocabulario económico

Elige el término correcto.

1. Two consecutive quarters of contraction define a ___ (recession / downturn).
2. The total value of goods and services produced is ___ (GDP / trade deficit).
3. Moving production abroad to cut costs is ___ (outsourcing / offshoring) — ambos válidos, pero ___ suele implicar tercerización a un proveedor.
4. When prices rise broadly and persistently, that is ___ (inflation / volatility).
5. A period of strong expansion followed by a sharp fall is a ___ (boom and bust / stagnation) cycle.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **recession** (definición técnica); *downturn* es más vago. 2. **GDP**. 3. **outsourcing** (tercerización a un proveedor) / **offshoring** (traslado al extranjero, que puede ser interno). 4. **inflation**. 5. **boom and bust**.

</details>

### Ejercicio 13 — Colocaciones de tendencia

Empareja verbo y complemento con un adjetivo adecuado.

1. slip into / ease / gain / widen / stall / spike
2. a. recession b. gradually c. momentum d. sharply e. (nothing) f. volatility

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

- *slip into recession* (a); *ease gradually* (b); *gain momentum* (c); *widen sharply* (d); *stall* (e, verbo intransitivo); *spike in response to ...* / *volatility spiked* (f).

Estas colocaciones describen movimientos con precisión y evitan el genérico *go up/down*. Para graduar la intensidad, añade adverbios propios del registro: *sharply, markedly, marginally, modestly, appreciably, negligibly*. Así, *the deficit widened sharply* transmite un deterioro pronunciado, mientras que *it eased marginally* sugiere un cambio apenas perceptible. Elegir el adverbio correcto es lo que separa una descripción genérica de un análisis digno de un informe.

</details>

### Ejercicio 14 — Reading

Lee y responde.

> *It is widely acknowledged that the recovery has been uneven. Owing to supply-chain bottlenecks, inflation spiked in the second quarter; consequently, real wages contracted, thereby dampening consumer spending. It appears, however, that the downturn has bottomed out.*

1. ¿Qué tres relaciones causales encadena el texto?
2. ¿Qué matiz aporta *thereby dampening*?
3. ¿Por qué *It appears* y no *It is clear*?

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. (a) cuellos de botella → inflación; (b) inflación → contracción del salario real; (c) contracción salarial → menor consumo.
2. Presenta la caída del consumo como **subproducto** de la contracción salarial ("por ese medio"), integrando causa y resultado en una sola cláusula.
3. Porque la conclusión de que la recesión tocó fondo es tentativa, basada en indicios aún preliminares; *it is clear* afirmaría una certeza que los datos no justifican del todo.

</details>

### Ejercicio 15 — Listening (paráfrasis)

Oíste: *"They reckon the deficit will hit four percent of GDP."* Reescribe con distancing formal.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

Modelo: *It is estimated that the deficit will reach 4% of GDP.* / *The deficit is estimated to hit 4% of GDP.* / *It is reckoned that the deficit will widen to 4% of GDP.*

</details>

### Ejercicio 16 — Writing

Escribe un párrafo (70–90 palabras) interpretando una tendencia, usando al menos: un linker *owing to*, un *thereby* + -ing, y una estructura *it is estimated that*.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

Modelo: *Owing to persistent supply-chain bottlenecks, inflation spiked in the second quarter, thereby squeezing real wages and dampening consumer spending. Consequently, growth stalled, and it is estimated that the economy will expand by barely 1% this year. It appears, nonetheless, that the downturn has bottomed out, so a modest recovery is likely towards year-end.* (54 palabras; añade una cifra o un matiz para alcanzar el rango.)

Criterios: causa con *owing to*, subproducto con *thereby + -ing*, estimación con *it is estimated*, puntuación correcta de los linkers.

</details>

### Ejercicio 17 — Speaking (monólogo)

En 2 minutos, explica una tendencia económica reciente. Usa al menos un *owing to*, un *consequently* y un *it is widely acknowledged that*.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

Modelo: *It is widely acknowledged that the recent downturn was uneven across regions. Owing to higher energy prices, inflation spiked earlier than expected; consequently, central banks raised rates faster, thereby cooling demand. It appears, however, that the worst has passed, and it is estimated that growth will regain some momentum next year, albeit modestly.*

</details>

### Ejercicio 18 — Consolidación

Identifica cada recurso del texto y su función.

> *It is often argued that outsourcing hollows out middle-skill employment. Owing to automation and offshoring, demand for routine tasks fell; consequently, wage polarisation widened, thereby entrenching inequality. It is estimated that the gap will persist unless policy is overhauled.*

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

- *It is often argued that* → estructura con *it* que presenta una tesis ajena (distancing).
- *Owing to* → preposición de causa + sustantivo.
- *consequently* → linker de resultado con punto y coma.
- *thereby entrenching* → resultado subproducto, *thereby* + -ing.
- *It is estimated that* → pasiva evaluativa con atribución y proyección cuantitativa.

</details>

### Ejercicio 19 — Reescribe el párrafo con linkers formales

Eleva el registro del siguiente párrafo neutro sustituyendo *so/because* por linkers C1 y añadiendo una estructura con *it*.

> *Energy prices went up, so inflation rose. Because wages did not keep pace, consumers spent less, and the economy slowed down. People think the slowdown will be brief.*

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

Modelo: *Owing to rising energy prices, inflation climbed; consequently, as wages failed to keep pace, consumer spending contracted, thereby slowing the economy. It is widely expected that the slowdown will be brief, although it is argued that the risks are tilted to the downside.*

Rúbrica: *so* → *consequently*; *because* → estructura subordinada o *owing to*; añadido *thereby slowing*; *people think* → *it is widely expected/argued that*. La puntuación de los linkers formales debe ser correcta.

</details>

## Resumen de la unidad

- Los **linkers de causa** (*owing to, due to, on account of, because of*) rigen un sustantivo; los **de resultado** (*consequently, as a result, as a consequence, therefore, thus, hence, thereby*) abren una cláusula nueva con punto y coma o punto. **Thereby** va con *-ing*; *hence* admite un sustantivo directo.
- Los **verbos causales** (*result in, lead to, bring about, trigger*) ya contienen la relación causal y aligeran la frase.
- Las **estructuras con it** (*it is widely acknowledged/known that, it has been suggested/argued/claimed that, it appears/seems/is clear that, it is estimated/reported/believed that*) elevan el registro, atribuyen y gradúan la certeza.
- El **vocabulario económico** (*recession, inflation, GDP, trade deficit, emerging markets, outsourcing, volatility, recovery, stagnation*) y sus colocaciones (*slip into recession, ease gradually, gain momentum, widen sharply, stall, spike*) permiten narrar tendencias con la precisión de un analista.
