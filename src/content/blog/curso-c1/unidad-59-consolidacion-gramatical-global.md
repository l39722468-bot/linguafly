La consolidación gramatical es el momento en que los contenidos dispersos del curso se integran en un sistema coherente. A nivel C1, esto significa no solo conocer cada estructura de manera aislada, sino entender sus relaciones, sus intercambios de registro y los matices que distinguen una forma de otra. Esta unidad repasa de manera sistémica las estructuras más exigentes del nivel: los tiempos narrativos, las condicionales mixtas, la inversión emphática, las cláusulas concesivas y los patrones de subordinación compleja.

Cada estructura se presenta en tres niveles. El A2 ofrece la versión más sencilla, el B2 añade complejidad gradual y el C1 presenta la forma avanzada con sus matices de registro y precisión sintáctica.

A2: In English we can talk about the past, the present and the future. We can also say what we think and what we want.

B2: At an intermediate-advanced level, you combine tenses to narrate, hypothesise and concede. You use conditional sentences, concessive clauses and complex subordination to express nuanced relationships between ideas.

C1: C1 grammatical consolidation entails the systemic integration of previously discrete structures into a coherent syntactic repertoire. Mastery resides not in the isolated deployment of individual forms but in the calibrated selection among near-synonymous constructions according to register, emphasis and rhetorical effect, a competence that distinguishes proficient from competent users.

## Parte I — Teoría: Consolidación gramatical global C1

### 1. Los tiempos narrativos: past perfect, past perfect continuous y el uso del simple past

La narración en inglés C1 requiere combinar con precisión el simple past, el past perfect y el past perfect continuous. El past perfect marca acciones anteriores a otro punto del pasado; el past perfect continuous enfatiza la duración o continuidad hasta ese punto. La elección entre ambos matiza el foco narrativo.

A2: We use the past to tell stories. We say what happened first and what happened next.

B2: Narrative tenses combine the simple past, the past perfect and the past perfect continuous. The past perfect marks actions before another past point; the past perfect continuous emphasises duration up to that point.

C1: C1 narrative competence resides in the triadic orchestration of the simple past, the past perfect and the past perfect continuous. The past perfect encodes anteriority relative to a reference point in the past, whilst the past perfect continuous foregrounds the durative or iterative dimension of a process extending up to that reference point. The selection among the three encodes subtle focal distinctions that proficient narrators exploit for rhetorical effect.

### 2. Futuro perfecto y futuro perfecto continuo para la prospectión

El future perfect (will have done) proyecta una acción como completada en un punto futuro; el future perfect continuous (will have been doing) la proyecta como en progreso hasta ese punto. Ambos son indispensables para la especulación temporal del nivel C1 y para describir trayectorias acumulativas.

A2: We can say what will be finished before a time in the future. We can also say how long something will have been happening.

B2: The future perfect projects an action as completed by a future point. The future perfect continuous projects it as ongoing up to that point. Both are useful for speculating about cumulative processes.

C1: The future perfect encodes completion prior to a prospective temporal reference point, whilst the future perfect continuous encodes the durative continuity of a process extending up to that point. Both are indispensable instruments of C1 prospective reasoning, enabling precise articulation of cumulative trajectories and anticipatory states of affairs that simpler future forms cannot capture.

### 3. Condicionales mixtas: combinando tipos

Las condicionales mixtas combinan estructuras de distintos tipos cuando la condición y la consecuencia se sitúan en tiempos distintos. If I had studied medicine (pasado, tipo 3), I would be a doctor now (presente, tipo 2) mezcla ambos. El dominio C1 requiere identificar qué combinación refleja cada relación temporal.

A2: Sometimes the "if" part is about the past and the result is about now. We can mix them.

B2: Mixed conditionals combine structures from different types when the condition and the consequence sit in different times. 'If I had studied medicine, I would be a doctor now' mixes type 3 (past) with type 2 (present). C1 mastery means identifying which combination fits each temporal relationship.

C1: Mixed conditionals interleave conditional architectures when the protasis and the apodosis are temporally dislocated. The type 3/type 2 blend (If I had studied medicine, I would be a doctor now) encodes a counterfactual past condition yielding a counterfactual present consequence, whilst the type 2/type 3 blend (If she were more diligent, she would have finished by now) encodes a present dispositional condition yielding a past counterfactual outcome. C1 mastery demands the diagnostic identification of which temporal configuration each discourse context requires.

### 4. Inversión emphática con adverbios negativos y restrictivos

La inversión emphática desplaza al inicio de la oración un adverbio negativo o restrictivo, seguido de la inversión del auxiliar y el sujeto. Never had she seen such beauty, Rarely do we encounter, Not until he arrived did she relax, Only after the meeting did they understand. Esta estructura confiere énfasis retórico y es marca del registro formal C1.

A2: Sometimes we put a word like "never" at the start to make the sentence stronger.

B2: Emphatic inversion places a negative or restrictive adverb at the front, followed by auxiliary-subject inversion: Never had she seen such beauty. This structure adds rhetorical emphasis and signals a formal register.

C1: Emphatic inversion displaces a negative or restrictive adverb, never, rarely, seldom, not until, only after, to sentence-initial position, triggering auxiliary-subject inversion. The construction confers marked rhetorical prominence upon the displaced element and constitutes a diagnostic feature of the formal C1 register, exploited strategically in expository and literary prose alike to foreground salience.

### 5. Cláusulas concesivas: although, despite, whereas, while y much as

Las cláusulas concesivas expresan contraste o concesión. Although va seguido de una cláusula con sujeto y verbo (Although it rained). Despite e in spite of van seguidos de un sintagma nominal o gerundio (Despite the rain). Whereas y while introducen contraste entre dos proposiciones. Much as es una forma formal y literaria.

A2: We can say "but" in different ways. "Although" and "despite" show that something is surprising.

B2: Concessive clauses express contrast or concession. Although is followed by a clause with subject and verb (Although it rained). Despite and in spite of are followed by a noun phrase or gerund (Despite the rain). Whereas and while introduce contrast between two propositions.

C1: Concessive clauses encode contrast or concession with structurally differentiated complementation patterns. Although and even though select finite clauses, despite and in spite of select non-finite nominals or gerundive complements, whilst whereas and while introduce bipropositional contrast. Much as, a markedly formal and literary variant, selects a finite clause and is characteristic of elevated written registers, the correct distribution of which is a hallmark of C1 syntactic precision.

### 6. Cláusulas de propósito y resultado: so that, in order that, such that

Las cláusulas de propósito (so that, in order that) expresan la finalidad de una acción y suelen llevar verbos modales como can, could, may, might. Las cláusulas de resultado (so... that, such... that) expresan la consecuencia y pueden emplear inversión con so o such al inicio. La distinción entre propósito y resultado es esencial para el registro C1.

A2: We can say why we do things: "I went to the shop so that I could buy bread." We can also say the result: "It was so cold that I stayed home."

B2: Purpose clauses (so that, in order that) express why an action is done and often take modal verbs. Result clauses (so... that, such... that) express consequence. Distinguishing purpose from result is essential for C1 accuracy.

C1: Purpose clauses, introduced by so that and in order that, encode telic relations and characteristically select modal auxiliaries such as can, could, may and might in their complement. Result clauses, encoded by the so... that and such... that correlative constructions, express consequence and admit emphatic inversion when the degree adverbial is fronted. The scrupulous demarcation of purpose from result, and of their respective complementation requirements, is a diagnostic of C1 syntactic control.

### 7. Cláusulas de relativo reducidas y no reducidas

Las cláusulas de relativo reducidas omiten el relativo y el auxiliar be: the book written by Smith (the book which was written by Smith). Las no reducidas (non-defining) aportan información adicional entre comas: My brother, who lives in Madrid, is a doctor. El dominio C1 requiere distinguir cuándo la reducción es posible y cuándo la coma cambia el significado.

A2: We can shorten descriptions: "the book written by Smith" instead of "the book which was written by Smith."

B2: Reduced relative clauses omit the relative pronoun and the auxiliary be: the book written by Smith. Non-defining clauses add extra information between commas: My brother, who lives in Madrid, is a doctor. C1 mastery means knowing when reduction is possible and when the comma changes meaning.

C1: Reduced relative clauses, generated by deleting the relative pronoun and the auxiliary be, yield participial post-modifiers such as the book written by Smith from the book which was written by Smith. Non-defining relatives, set off by commas, supply appositive rather than restrictive information, and the comma's presence or absence can decisively alter semantic import. C1 competence demands diagnostic awareness of the conditions under which reduction is licensed and of the semantic consequences of comma placement.

### 8. Cláusulas nominales con what, whatever y whichever

Las cláusulas nominales introducidas por what, whatever y whichever funcionan como sujeto u objeto. What she said surprised everyone (sujeto). I will support whatever you decide (objeto). La elección entre what y whatever codifica el grado de especificidad o de generalidad, un matiz característico del registro C1.

A2: We can use "what" to talk about a thing: "What she said was interesting."

B2: Nominal clauses with what, whatever and whichever function as subjects or objects: What she said surprised everyone. I will support whatever you decide. The choice between what and whatever encodes specificity versus generality.

C1: Nominal clauses introduced by what, whatever and whichever occupy argument positions as subjects or objects. What she said surprised everyone instantiates a nominal clause in subject position, whilst I will support whatever you decide instantiates one in object position. The alternation between the specific what and the free-choice whatever encodes the speaker's stance regarding the determinacy of the referent, a C1-grade pragmatic distinction.

### 9. Construcciones partitivas: some of which, many of whom

Las construcciones partitivas combinan un cuantificador con un relativo precedido de preposición: the students, many of whom failed, the proposals, some of which were accepted. Estas estructuras permiten integrar información cuantificada en cláusulas no restrictivas y son típicas del registro académico C1.

A2: We can say "many of the students" to talk about part of a group.

B2: Partitive constructions combine a quantifier with a prepositional relative: the students, many of whom failed; the proposals, some of which were accepted. They embed quantified information in non-restrictive clauses and are typical of academic C1.

C1: Partitive constructions fuse a quantifier with a prepositional relative pronoun, yielding structures such as the students, many of whom failed, and the proposals, some of which were accepted. These constructions enable the integration of quantificational information within non-restrictive relatives and are diacritically characteristic of the C1 academic register, where their absence often signals a less proficient writer.

### 10. El uso matizado de before, until, once y as soon as

Las conjunciones temporales before, until, once y as soon as introducen matices distintos. Until enfatiza la continuidad hasta un punto; before marca anterioridad; once indica que una condición se cumple y a partir de ahí cambia la situación; as soon as marca inmediatez. La distinción es crucial para la narración precisa.

A2: We can say "before," "until," and "as soon as" to talk about time and order.

B2: Temporal conjunctions before, until, once and as soon as introduce distinct nuances. Until emphasises continuity up to a point; before marks anteriority; once indicates a condition is met and the situation changes; as soon as marks immediacy. The distinction is crucial for precise narration.

C1: The temporal conjunctions before, until, once and as soon as encode subtly distinct aspectual relations. Until encodes durative continuity terminated at a reference point; before encodes simple anteriority; once encodes a punctual condition the satisfaction of which inaugurates a new state of affairs; as soon as encodes maximal temporal proximity between triggering event and consequence. The calibrated selection among these is indispensable for the fine-grained temporal articulation that distinguishes C1 narrative control.

### 11. Inversión con so y such en cláusulas de resultado

Cuando so o such se desplazan al inicio de la oración para enfatizar el grado, se produce la inversión del auxiliar y el sujeto: So profound was the impact that the policy was revised, Such was the outcry that the minister resigned. Esta inversión confiere fuerza retórica y pertenece al registro formal C1.

A2: We can make a sentence stronger by putting "so" at the start: "So big was the dog that I was afraid."

B2: When so or such is moved to the front for emphasis, auxiliary-subject inversion occurs: So profound was the impact that the policy was revised. This inversion adds rhetorical force and belongs to the formal C1 register.

C1: Fronting the degree adverbials so and such in resultative constructions triggers auxiliary-subject inversion: So profound was the impact that the policy was revised, Such was the outcry that the minister resigned. This inversion confers marked rhetorical emphasis and is a diacritic of the formal C1 register, strategically deployed in argumentative and expository prose to invest a proposition with heightened salience.

### 12. El subjuntivo en inglés: mandative y formulaico

El subjuntivo mandativo aparece tras verbos de sugerencia o exigencia (suggest, demand, insist) en cláusulas con that: I insist that he be present. El subjuntivo formulaico sobrevive en expresiones fijas como be that as it may, suffice it to say, heaven forbid. Ambos son marcas del registro formal C1.

A2: Sometimes after words like "suggest" we use a special form: "I suggest that he be careful."

B2: The mandative subjunctive appears after suggestion or demand verbs in that-clauses: I insist that he be present. The formulaic subjunctive survives in fixed expressions like be that as it may and suffice it to say. Both mark formal C1 register.

C1: The mandative subjunctive, selected by directive predicates such as suggest, demand, insist and require in that-complements (I insist that he be present), encodes obligatory or recommended states of affairs without modal mediation. The formulaic subjunctive persists in fossilised expressions including be that as it may, suffice it to say and heaven forbid. Both constitute diagnostic features of the formal C1 register, their deployment signalling an advanced command of register-sensitive syntax.

### 13. Verbos modales en pasado para especulación y deducción

Los modales en pasado (must have done, might have done, could have done, should have done, can't have done) expresan deducción, especulación o crítica sobre eventos pasados. Must have done indica deducción de certeza; can't have done, deducción de imposibilidad; should have done, crítica retrospectiva. Su uso preciso es central en el C1.

A2: We can guess about the past: "He must have been tired" means I am sure he was tired.

B2: Past modals (must have done, might have done, can't have done, should have done) express deduction, speculation or criticism about past events. Must have done indicates certainty; can't have done indicates impossibility; should have done expresses retrospective criticism.

C1: Past modal configurations, must have done, might have done, could have done, should have done and can't have done, encode epistemic, deontic or evaluative stance toward past eventualities. Must have done encodes deductive certainty; can't have done encodes deductive impossibility; should have done encodes retrospective deontic critique. Their calibrated deployment, distinguishing epistemic from deontic readings, is central to C1 expressive precision in conjectural discourse.

### 14. Conectores discursivos de contraste, adición y reformulación

El registro C1 emplea conectores variados más allá de but y however: nevertheless, nonetheless, conversely, on the contrary, by contrast (contraste); furthermore, moreover, additionally, in addition (adición); in other words, namely, to put it differently, that is to say (reformulación). Su elección codifica el tipo de relación lógica con precisión.

A2: We can use words like "however" and "also" to connect ideas.

B2: C1 uses varied connectors beyond but and however: nevertheless, conversely, on the contrary (contrast); furthermore, moreover, additionally (addition); in other words, namely, to put it differently (reformulation). Their choice encodes the logical relationship precisely.

C1: The C1 register exploits a diversified connector inventory extending well beyond the elementary but and however: nevertheless, nonetheless, conversely, on the contrary and by contrast encode contrastive relations; furthermore, moreover, additionally and in addition encode additive relations; in other words, namely, to put it differently and that is to say encode reformulative relations. The calibrated selection among these encodes the specific logical relation with a granularity unavailable to less proficient users.

### 15. El artículo cero, el y a/an: precisión determinativa

El uso avanzado del artículo distingue contextos donde el artículo cero es obligatorio (institutional reference: go to hospital, at school) de los que requieren the (specific reference) o a/an (classificatory). Las diferencias entre in hospital e in the hospital, o entre go to church y go to the church, codifican distinciones funcionales que el C1 debe dominar.

A2: Sometimes we use "the," sometimes "a," and sometimes no word before a noun. It depends on what we mean.

B2: Advanced article use distinguishes contexts requiring the zero article (go to hospital, at school) from those needing the (specific reference) or a/an (classificatory). The difference between in hospital and in the hospital encodes functional distinctions.

C1: Advanced article deployment hinges on the diagnostic discrimination of contexts licensing the zero article, institutional or atelic reference such as go to hospital and at school, from those requiring the definite article for specific reference or the indefinite article for classificatory reference. The contrast between in hospital and in the hospital, or between go to church and go to the church, encodes functionally consequential distinctions between institutional role and mere locative reference that C1 users must command with reflexive precision.
