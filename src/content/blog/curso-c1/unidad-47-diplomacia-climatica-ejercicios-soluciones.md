## Parte II — Ejercicios

**Ejercicio 1.** Identifica el tipo de condicional y explica su matiz: *If countries ratify the agreement, emissions will peak sooner.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

Es una condicional real (tipo 1): *if + present, will + base form*. Describe una situación posible y su consecuencia probable. El matiz es de optimismo condicional: el hablante considera la ratificación plausible y presenta el pico de emisiones como consecuencia directa y probable. Es la condicional típica de quien quiere motivar la acción presentándola como eficaz y realizable.
</details>

**Ejercicio 2.** Reescribe la frase en una condicional hipotética (tipo 2) con inversión formal: *If the major emitters committed to binding targets, the prospects would improve.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*Were the major emitters to commit to binding targets, the prospects for staying within the 1.5-degree threshold would improve markedly.* — La inversión *were + sujeto + to + infinitivo* eleva el registro hacia lo formal y diplomático, y el tipo 2 comunica que el compromiso es, a juicio del hablante, improbable en el presente.
</details>

**Ejercicio 3.** Reescribe en una condicional contrafactual (tipo 3) con inversión: *If we had acted a decade ago, we would have avoided the worst effects.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*Had we acted a decade ago, the current trajectory would have been markedly less steep, and the worst effects now bearing down on the most vulnerable might have been averted.* — La inversión *had + sujeto + past participle* es propia del registro formal y subraya la imposibilidad de cambiar el pasado, convirtiendo la frase en un juicio sobre responsabilidad histórica.
</details>

**Ejercicio 4.** Completa con la condicional mixta adecuada (contrafactual pasado → presente): *If we ________ (act) earlier, we ________ (be) in a better position now.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*If we had acted earlier, we would be in a better position now.* — La cláusula *if* es contrafactual pasada (*had acted*), y la principal es contrafactual presente (*would be*). Esta mixta es ideal para argumentar que la inacción pasada tiene consecuencias tangibles en el presente, una maniobra retórica frecuente en diplomacia climática.
</details>

**Ejercicio 5.** Distingue *mitigation* y *adaptation* en una frase que muestre su relación.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*While mitigation addresses the root cause by reducing emissions, adaptation addresses the consequences by building resilience to impacts already locked in; both are indispensable, though the balance of investment has historically skewed towards the former, to the detriment of the most vulnerable who face impacts no amount of mitigation can now prevent.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 6.** Reescribe con una estructura de concesión-reserva: *We know they have economic problems. But they still need to decarbonise.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*While we acknowledge the economic constraints facing our partners, we must nevertheless insist that short-term affordability cannot be permitted to override the long-term imperative of decarbonisation, however painful the transition may prove in the near term.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 7.** Reformula la promesa condicional con un modal más diplomático (*should*): *If you ratify the protocol, we will release the funds.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*Should you ratify the protocol, we shall release the pledged funds in full and without further precondition.* — *Should you* es más diplomático que *if you* (sugiere cortesía sin agresividad), y *shall* en británico legal connota promesa formal y obligación, reforzando la credibilidad del compromiso sin elevar innecesariamente el tono de la negociación.
</details>

**Ejercicio 8.** Completa con léxico de equidad climática: *The principle of ________ responsibilities must not become a shield for inaction by emerging economies, even as we insist that historical emitters bear a ________ share of the financial burden.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*common but differentiated* (o *common but differentiated responsibilities and respective capabilities*, CBDR-RC) y *disproportionate* — el principio CBDR-RC es fundacional de la Convención; *disproportionate* capta la idea de que la carga debe pesar más sobre quienes más han contribuido y más pueden pagar.
</details>

**Ejercicio 9.** Reescribe usando vocabulario del proceso negociador: *They couldn't agree on the text about loss and damage, so they made a weaker version and passed it.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*After days of deadlock over the bracketed language on loss and damage, the chair tabled a compromise text that, while watered down from the original draft, nonetheless broke the impasse and allowed the plenary to adopt the outcome document before the summit's close.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 10.** Escribe una frase que integre léxico científico (IPCC, carbon budget) en un argumento diplomático.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The IPCC's latest assessment underscores that the remaining carbon budget for a 1.5-degree pathway is vanishingly small, a finding that, even allowing for uncertainty in the models, leaves little room for the incrementalism that has characterised past rounds of negotiation.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 11.** Reescribe con vocabulario de financiación climática, distinguiendo promesa y desembolso: *They promised money but didn't give it.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The gap between pledged and disbursed climate finance remains a persistent source of distrust, particularly among the most vulnerable states, who argue that promises denominated in the billions mean little if the funds fail to materialise as grants rather than as loans that would compound existing debt burdens.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 12.** Completa con el modal que calibre la obligación: (a) Emissions ________ peak by 2025 (obligación fuerte). (b) Countries ________ enhance their NDCs (recomendación). (c) The fund ________ be operationalised next year (posibilidad).

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

(a) *must* — obligación fuerte, indica necesidad imperativa. (b) *should* — recomendación, sugiere lo deseable sin imponerlo. (c) *may* — posibilidad, indica que es factible pero no garantizado. La elección modal en un texto negociado puede representar semanas de discusión y define el grado real de compromiso que un acuerdo contiene.
</details>

**Ejercicio 13.** Reescribe con vocabulario de loss and damage: *They finally agreed that some damage can't be fixed and set up a fund, but it's not clear if there will be enough money.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The establishment of a loss and damage fund marks a landmark in acknowledging the irreversible harms faced by the most vulnerable, though the gap between symbolic recognition and adequate, predictable financing remains a test of whether the principle of solidarity will be matched by tangible resources.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 14.** Reformula con una condicional alternativa a *if* (*provided that* / *as long as*): *If the verification is strong, the framework could work.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*Provided that verification mechanisms are robust and independently verifiable, the framework could deliver meaningful reductions within the agreed timeframe; as long as compliance remains voluntary, however, its credibility will inevitably be called into question.* — *provided that* y *as long as* son alternativas a *if* que elevan el registro y matizan la condición.
</details>

**Ejercicio 15.** Escribe una amenaza condicional diplomática usando *should you fail*.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*Should the major emitters fail to enhance their contributions in the forthcoming cycle, the prospects for a credible global response will be severely compromised, and the willingness of the most vulnerable states to remain engaged in the process cannot be guaranteed.* — *should you fail* plantea la amenaza con cortesía formal, sin sonar agresiva, permitiendo advertir sin escalar hacia la ruptura.
</details>

**Ejercicio 16.** Completa con vocabulario de compromisos: *The ________ mechanism is designed to ensure that each successive NDC represents a progression, yet the current collective ________ remains substantially short of what the science demands, leaving a persistent ________.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*ratchet*, *ambition*, *gap* — el *ratchet mechanism* exige progreso en cada NDC; *ambition* es el término consagrado para el nivel de compromiso; *gap* (especialmente *emissions gap* o *ambition gap*) describe la distancia entre lo comprometido y lo necesario.
</details>

**Ejercicio 17.** Reescribe esta frase con cohesión y matiz condicional: *There is a gap between promises and action. Finance is short. The system is not good enough. We need more ambition.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*Taken together, the gap between pledged ambition and required action, compounded by the shortfall in disbursed finance, points to a system that, while not without progress, remains substantially inadequate to the scale of the challenge — a finding that, however sobering, must serve as a catalyst for the ambition that the next cycle of negotiation cannot afford to defer.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 18.** Explica en 2-3 frases, en inglés C1, por qué la distinción entre *pledged* y *disbursed* climate finance es políticamente significativa.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The distinction between pledged and disbursed climate finance is politically significant because pledges generate headlines and diplomatic goodwill at the moment they are announced, whereas disbursement determines whether the most vulnerable states actually receive the support they were promised. A persistent gap between the two erodes trust and feeds the perception that climate finance is more a tool of image management than of genuine solidarity, undermining the willingness of developing countries to commit to ambitious mitigation in subsequent negotiating rounds.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 19.** Escribe un párrafo breve (4-5 frases) en inglés C1 que argumente sobre la responsabilidad histórica en el cambio climático, usando al menos dos condicionales (una hipotética y una contrafactual) y al menos dos elementos del léxico de equidad climática.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*While it is true that emerging economies now account for a substantial share of current emissions, the principle of common but differentiated responsibilities reminds us that the climate crisis was disproportionately caused by the cumulative emissions of today's industrialised nations. Had those nations heeded the scientific warnings a generation ago, the trajectory we now face would have been markedly less steep, and the burden falling on the least developed countries would be commensurately lighter. Were the historical emitters to accept a proportionate share of the financial obligation for both mitigation and loss and damage, the prospects for a genuinely equitable global response would improve substantially. To be sure, no framework can undo the emissions already in the atmosphere; yet the willingness of the wealthy to match their historical responsibility with present-day resources remains the single most credible test of whether the diplomatic architecture will deliver justice or merely manage its absence.*
</details>

## Resumen de la unidad

En esta unidad hemos construido el andamiaje lingüístico para participar en la diplomacia climática con la sofisticación que exige el nivel C1. Has dominado las tres familias condicionales —reales, hipotéticas y contrafactuales— junto con sus inversiones formales y alternativas a *if*, herramientas que permiten calibrar el grado de realidad y la formalidad de cualquier escenario negociado. Has incorporado el léxico de la diplomacia ambiental: *mitigation* y *adaptation*, *common but differentiated responsibilities*, *NDCs*, *ratchet mechanism*, *loss and damage*, *climate finance* y la crítica distinción entre *pledged* y *disbursed* funds. Has ejercitado la concesión-reserva como corazón retórico de la negociación y el matiz modal (*must*, *should*, *may*, *shall*) como herramienta para calibrar obligación y posibilidad. Has integrado el léxico científico del IPCC en el argumento diplomático y el vocabulario del proceso negociador, desde *bracketed text* hasta *deadlock*. La destreza central de esta unidad es la condicionalidad matizada: la capacidad de plantear escenarios, formular promesas y amenazas con cortesía, y argumentar sobre responsabilidad histórica y equidad presente sin caer ni en el triunfalismo ni en la fatalidad. Esa precisión retórica es la marca distintiva del hablante C1 cuando se enfrenta a uno de los terrenos donde la gramática y la política se entrelazan con más fuerza.
