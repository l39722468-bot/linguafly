## Parte II — Ejercicios

**Ejercicio 1.** Distingue *misinformation*, *disinformation* y *malinformation* en una frase C1 que muestre la diferencia de intención.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*Whereas misinformation captures the unwitting spread of falsehoods by those who believe them, disinformation denotes their deliberate deployment by those who know them to be false, and malinformation describes the weaponisation of truthful material — three phenomena that a single, undifferentiated charge of "fake news" conflates to the detriment of any precise response.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 2.** Explica el desplazamiento semántico del término *fake news* en una frase C1.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The co-optation of "fake news" as a rhetorical cudgel against accurate but unfavourable reporting has produced a situation in which the term now more often describes the accuser's displeasure than any deficiency in the accused report — a semantic drift that, however unintended, has measurably complicated the task of identifying the very phenomenon the term was coined to name.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 3.** Reescribe con léxico de verificación: *To check if a site is good, you should leave the site and see what other people say about it.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*Lateral reading — leaving the site under evaluation to consult what independent sources say about it — has emerged as a more reliable strategy than vertical reading, because it offloads the burden of verification onto the broader web rather than trusting the site's own self-presentation.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 4.** Completa con léxico del fact-checking: *The verdict of "________" is often more analytically useful than a flat "false," because it captures the common disinformation technique of using accurate elements in a misleading ________.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*missing context* y *arrangement* (o *combination*) — *missing context* es el veredicto que captura el uso engañoso de elementos reales fuera de su contexto; *arrangement* reconoce que la desinformación a menudo reside en la composición, no en los elementos individuales que pueden ser verídicamente reales.
</details>

**Ejercicio 5.** Explica la diferencia entre *debunking* y *prebunking* usando la teoría de la inoculación.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*Whereas debunking seeks to correct a falsehood after it has taken hold, prebunking — grounded in inoculation theory — exposes audiences to a weakened form of the manipulation alongside its refutation before they encounter it, building a cognitive immune response that, much like its medical analogue, is more cost-effective than treating the infection once it has spread.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 6.** Reescribe describiendo la técnica de *truth sandwich* para refutar sin amplificar: *To correct a lie, just say it's a lie and then say the truth.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The truth sandwich, which frames the false claim between two iterations of the accurate account rather than leading with the falsehood, exemplifies an emerging consensus that the structure of a refutation can either mitigate or compound the very amplification it seeks to counter.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 7.** Completa con sesgos cognitivos: *The ________ effect, by which repeated exposure to a claim increases its perceived veracity regardless of its accuracy, explains why mere repetition is itself a disinformation strategy.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*illusory truth* — el *illusory truth effect* es el sesgo por el cual la repetición aumenta la percepción de verdad de una afirmación independientemente de su veracidad real, lo que explica por qué la repetición es una estrategia de desinformación en sí misma y por qué las correcciones que repiten el reclamo falso pueden reforzar perversamente la creencia que pretenden desmontar.
</details>

**Ejercicio 8.** Reescribe con léxico de operaciones de influencia: *There are fake accounts that work together to spread lies and pretend to be normal people.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The detection of coordinated inauthentic behaviour — networks of accounts acting in concert while misrepresenting their identity and purpose, often via sock puppets and troll farms — has become a central diagnostic category in platform governance, though the line between coordinated advocacy and manipulation remains contestable.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 9.** Escribe una frase que explique por qué el fact-checking a menudo no persuade a quienes más lo necesitan, usando *tribal epistemology*.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*Tribal epistemology, in which the truth of a claim is assessed by its alignment with group loyalty rather than by external evidence, renders conventional fact-checking less effective, because the verdict of "false" is itself received as an out-group attack to be dismissed rather than as a correction to be heeded.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 10.** Completa con léxico de regulación de plataformas: *The shift from ex-post ________ to contextual ________ reflects an emerging recognition that removing content can both generate censorship disputes and, paradoxically, amplify it through the ________ effect.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*takedown*, *labelling*, *Streisand* — *takedown* es la retirada de contenido a posteriori; *labelling* es el etiquetado contextual que deja el contenido visible; el *Streisand effect* es la paradoja de que intentar suprimir información genera más visibilidad para ella, un riesgo que el labelling mitiga frente al takedown.
</details>

**Ejercicio 11.** Reescribe con léxico de fuentes y credibilidad: *One person said it, so it might not be true.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*A single-source report relying on an anonymous official, however reputable the outlet, warrants more cautious treatment than a multi-source account with on-the-record corroboration — a distinction that responsible journalism makes explicit and that responsible reading should heed.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 12.** Nombra y explica tres técnicas de desinformación (*out-of-context use*, *deepfake*, *astroturfing*) en frases C1.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*Out-of-context use* emplea material real (una imagen, un vídeo) desprovisto de su contexto original para implicar un acontecimiento distinto, una técnica más insidiosa que la fabricación pura porque los elementos son verificablemente reales aunque el arreglo sea engañoso. *Deepfake* utiliza inteligencia artificial para generar vídeo o audio manipulado en que una persona parece decir o hacer algo que no dijo ni hizo, planteando un reto de verificación que supera la inspección visual casual. *Astroturfing* simula un movimiento espontáneo de base mediante cuentas coordinadas que fingen ser ciudadanos ordinarios, fabricando la apariencia de consenso popular que un debate genuino no habría producido.
</details>

**Ejercicio 13.** Reescribe evitando el catastrofismo y la minimización: *The internet is destroying truth / It's all exaggerated.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The evidence suggests that the information environment has become more contested, with measurable consequences for trust and public deliberation, though the scale, novelty and trajectory of the phenomenon remain matters of ongoing debate — a picture that calls for sustained, clear-eyed engagement rather than either apocalyptic despair or complacent dismissal.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 14.** Completa con léxico de filtros y polarización: *In a(n) ________ chamber, exposure to opinions that reinforce one's own compounds ________ polarisation, in which the other side is perceived not merely as wrong but as morally defective.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*echo* y *affective* — la *echo chamber* (cámara de eco) refuerza las propias opiniones por exposición selectiva; la *affective polarisation* (polarización afectiva) convierte el desacuerdo en hostilidad moral, lo que endurece las posiciones y reduce el espacio para la deliberación racional entre bandos.
</details>

**Ejercicio 15.** Escribe una frase que argumente por qué la transparencia metodológica es la base de la credibilidad del periodismo de verificación.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The credibility of verification journalism rests less on the authority of the outlet than on the transparency of its method — the visible chain from claim to evidence to verdict that allows the reader to audit the reasoning rather than merely accept the conclusion, a procedural virtue that matters all the more in an environment in which institutional authority itself is contested.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 16.** Reformula con léxico de refutación y efecto: *When you correct a lie, sometimes people believe the lie more.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The continued influence effect, by which a corrected falsehood persists in reasoning despite the recipient acknowledging the correction, compounds the risk of the boomerang effect, in which a poorly structured refutation reinforces the very belief it intended to dislodge — twin hazards that make the design of corrections as consequential as their factual content.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 17.** Reescribe esta conclusión con cohesión y matiz: *Fake news is a problem. We have to check things. It's an ongoing thing.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*Ultimately, what this amounts to is not a single battle to be won but an ongoing condition to be managed: the information environment will remain contested, and the task of verification, far from a one-off act, must be a habitual practice embedded in the literacy of every reader — a demand that, however taxing, is the price of an informed public in an age of abundant and weaponised falsehood.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 18.** Explica en 2-3 frases, en inglés C1, por qué *lateral reading* es más fiable que *vertical reading* para evaluar una fuente desconocida.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*Vertical reading, which evaluates a site on its own terms, leaves the reader vulnerable to the site's self-presentation, since anyone can manufacture the surface markers of credibility — professional design, authoritative tone, citation of sources. Lateral reading, by contrast, offloads the burden of verification onto the broader web, consulting what independent and ideally critical sources say about the site, its funders and its track record. The strategy is more reliable precisely because it refuses to trust the source under evaluation as the sole arbiter of its own credibility, a refusal that mirrors the cross-sourcing logic that professional journalists have long applied to their reporting.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 19.** Escribe un párrafo breve (4-5 frases) en inglés C1 que argumente sobre cómo combatir la desinformación, usando al menos tres elementos del léxico de esta unidad (verificación, prebunking, sesgos cognitivos o técnicas de refutación).

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*Combating disinformation effectively requires recognising that the challenge is as much structural as it is factual: the illusory truth effect ensures that mere repetition lends perceived veracity to a claim regardless of its accuracy, which is why corrections that lead with the falsehood risk reinforcing it through the very act of refuting it. For this reason, the truth sandwich, which frames the false claim between two iterations of the accurate account, offers a structurally sounder alternative, while prebunking — grounded in inoculation theory — seeks to build cognitive immunity before exposure rather than treating the infection after it has spread. Compounding the challenge, tribal epistemology can render even the most rigorously transparent fact-check ineffective among audiences who receive the verdict as an out-group attack rather than a good-faith correction. Taken together, these considerations suggest that no single intervention will suffice, and that the response must combine pre-emptive inoculation, structurally careful refutation, platform-level labelling and, above all, a habitual literacy of lateral reading embedded in the public from the earliest education. Ultimately, then, the goal is not to eliminate falsehood — an unattainable aim in any open society — but to cultivate a population sufficiently equipped to navigate an environment in which falsehood will always circulate, and to do so with the proportionate, clear-headed vigilance that the scale of the problem so evidently demands.*
</details>

## Resumen de la unidad

En esta unidad hemos construido el andamiaje lingüístico para analizar y combatir la desinformación con la precisión crítica que exige el nivel C1. Has aprendido a distinguir *misinformation*, *disinformation* y *malinformation* —una diferenciación que el término aglutinador *fake news* confunde— y a reconocer el desplazamiento semántico que ha convertido este último en arma retórica. Has incorporado el léxico de la verificación —*lateral reading*, *reverse image search*, *source triangulation*, *provenance*— y el del *fact-checking* —*claim*, *verdict*, *missing context*, *pants on fire*. Has explorado las técnicas de desinformación, desde el *out-of-context use* al *deepfake* y el *astroturfing*, y las estrategias de respuesta, del *prebunking* y la *inoculation theory* al *truth sandwich* que refuta sin amplificar. Has integrado la psicología cognitiva —*illusory truth effect*, *tribal epistemology*, *echo chamber*— y la gobernanza de plataformas —*takedown*, *labelling*, *coordinated inauthentic behaviour*. Has ejercitado el tono equilibrado que evita el catastrofismo y la conspiración, y la cohesión que estructura el análisis crítico. La destreza central de esta unidad es la verificación como práctica y como análisis: la capacidad de evaluar fuentes, desmontar afirmaciones y argumentar sobre el ecosistema de la desinformación con un léxico técnico que revela las estructuras cognitivas, tecnológicas e institucionales que lo configuran. Esa precisión crítica es la marca distintiva del hablante C1 cuando se enfrenta a uno de los retos definitorios de la información en nuestro tiempo.
