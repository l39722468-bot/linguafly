# Unidad 11 C1: cleft sentences, educación y aprendizaje

En una conversación sencilla podemos decir: *The teacher identified the problem*. En un ensayo o una presentación académica quizá queramos destacar quién lo identificó, qué problema se detectó o cuándo ocurrió. Las **cleft sentences**, también llamadas oraciones escindidas, nos permiten reorganizar la información para que un elemento reciba un foco claro.

- *It was the teacher who identified the problem.*
- *It was the problem that the teacher identified.*
- *What the teacher identified was a gap in the students’ reasoning.*

El hecho general es parecido, pero la perspectiva cambia. En la Unidad 11 C1 aplicamos estas estructuras al tema de **Education and Learning**: autonomía docente, evaluación, pensamiento crítico, aprendizaje autodirigido, educación progresiva y acceso a la universidad. El objetivo es aprender a controlar el foco sin crear frases artificiales.

Las clefts son especialmente útiles al responder preguntas. Si alguien pregunta *Who made the mistake?*, podemos contestar *It was the teacher who made the mistake*. Si pregunta *What did the teacher identify?*, podemos decir *What the teacher identified was a gap in reasoning*. La estructura ayuda a que la respuesta conecte exactamente con la información solicitada.

## Parte I — Teoría

### 1. Qué es el foco informativo

En una oración neutra, varios elementos comparten la atención del lector:

- *The professor changed the assessment because the final exam measured performance under pressure.*

Si queremos corregir una posible interpretación, podemos destacar una parte:

- *It was the professor who changed the assessment, not the students.*
- *It was the assessment that the professor changed, not the curriculum.*
- *It was because the final exam measured performance under pressure that the assessment was changed.*

El foco depende de la pregunta o del contraste. Una cleft no es simplemente una forma “más avanzada” de decir lo mismo. Si no hay una razón para destacar un elemento, la oración normal suele ser más natural. La cleft aporta valor cuando el escritor quiere corregir, contrastar, resumir o responder con precisión.

En educación, el foco puede cambiar la interpretación:

- *The school introduced continuous assessment.*
- *It was the school that introduced continuous assessment.* La escuela, y no otra institución, es el foco.
- *It was continuous assessment that the school introduced.* El tipo de evaluación es el foco.
- *What the school introduced was continuous assessment.* La nueva medida es el foco del comentario.

### 2. It-cleft: estructura básica

La estructura principal es:

> **It + is/was + focus + that/who + clause**

Usamos **is** para una situación presente o general y **was** para un hecho pasado:

- *It is the feedback that shapes the learner’s next step.*
- *It was the feedback that changed her approach.*
- *It is the teacher who notices the pattern first.*
- *It was the teacher who noticed the error.*

**Who** se usa habitualmente para personas y **that** para objetos, ideas, tiempos o lugares:

- *It was the tutor who encouraged independent research.*
- *It was the deadline that created unnecessary pressure.*
- *It was in the library that the students found the relevant source.*
- *It was during the final semester that her confidence improved.*

En el inglés hablado, *who* y *that* pueden variar, pero en escritura académica la elección debe ser clara. No confundas el tiempo de *is/was* con el tiempo de la cláusula posterior. La oración puede combinar un foco presente con una acción pasada si el contexto lo exige:

- *It is the early intervention that helped her regain confidence.*

Sin embargo, muchas veces *it was* resulta más coherente si todo el evento pertenece al pasado:

- *It was the early intervention that helped her regain confidence.*

### 3. Enfocar el sujeto

Para destacar quién realiza la acción, colocamos la persona o grupo después de *it is/was*:

- Orden neutro: *The teacher made the mistake.*
- It-cleft: *It was the teacher who made the mistake, not the students.*

- Orden neutro: *The students challenged the assumption.*
- It-cleft: *It was the students who challenged the assumption.*

La cláusula relativa conserva el orden normal de la oración. No decimos *It was the teacher who did made the mistake*. Después de *who*, usamos el verbo correspondiente: *who made*, *who noticed*, *who had suggested*.

La negación también es posible:

- *It was not the teacher who made the mistake.*
- *It wasn’t the students who requested the change.*

En una pregunta, la cleft mantiene la inversión propia de la pregunta principal:

- *Was it the teacher who made the mistake?*
- *Is it the assessment that needs revision?*

La cleft puede incluir un contraste explícito con *not* o *rather than*:

- *It was the lack of feedback, rather than the difficulty of the task, that discouraged learners.*

### 4. Enfocar el objeto

Para destacar un objeto, usamos **that** o una cláusula que retoma el objeto:

- *It was the final exam that the students found stressful.*
- *It was the research method that the supervisor questioned.*
- *It was the assumption of equal access that the report challenged.*

La oración subyacente es *The students found the final exam stressful*. El objeto aparece en la posición de foco y deja una posición vacía en la cláusula: *that the students found __ stressful*. En inglés formal esta estructura es correcta.

Con ciertos verbos y preposiciones, la preposición puede permanecer al final:

- *It was Oxford that she enrolled at, not Cambridge.*
- *It was the source that the essay referred to.*

En un registro muy formal puede adelantarse:

- *It was at Oxford that she enrolled.*
- *It was to this source that the essay referred.*

Elige la opción más natural para el contexto. No fuerces una preposición delante de *which* si una cleft con *that* produce una oración más clara.

### 5. Enfocar tiempo, lugar y causa

El foco no tiene que ser una persona o un objeto. También puede ser un momento, un lugar o una causa:

- *It was in the first year that the achievement gap became visible.*
- *It was after the policy change that attendance improved.*
- *It was because the test rewarded memorisation that critics opposed it.*
- *It is through sustained practice that learners develop fluency.*

Estas estructuras son útiles en explicaciones históricas y análisis de políticas educativas. La cleft hace que la relación causal o temporal sea visible, pero no garantiza que la causa sea correcta. Si la relación es una interpretación, añade cautela:

- *It may have been the change in curriculum that improved participation.*

### 6. What-clefts o pseudo-clefts

Una **what-cleft** empieza con **what** y contiene una cláusula completa. Su estructura básica es:

> **What + subject + verb + is/was + focus**

- *What matters is sustained practice.*
- *What the teacher noticed was a gap in reasoning.*
- *What surprised the panel was the consistency of the results.*
- *What learners need is meaningful feedback.*

La cláusula con *what* significa “lo que”. No uses una pregunta directa dentro de ella:

- Correcto: *What did the teacher notice?* como pregunta directa.
- Correcto: *What the teacher noticed was a gap in reasoning.* como what-cleft.
- Incorrecto: *What did the teacher notice was a gap in reasoning.*

El verbo dentro de la what-clause va antes del foco y mantiene el orden declarativo. La frase posterior identifica la información que queremos presentar como respuesta.

### 7. What-clefts con tiempos verbales

El tiempo de *is/was* depende del momento del análisis:

- *What matters is whether students can apply the concept.*
- *What mattered was whether students could apply the concept.*
- *What the assessment reveals is a difference in preparation.*
- *What the assessment revealed was a difference in preparation.*

La cláusula interna puede tener cualquier tiempo necesario:

- *What the researchers found was that attendance had improved.*
- *What the teacher is examining is how learners respond to feedback.*
- *What the committee will decide is whether the exam should remain.*

Una what-cleft puede contener una cláusula con *that* o *whether*:

- *What the evidence shows is that teacher autonomy matters.*
- *What remains uncertain is whether continuous assessment reduces anxiety.*

### 8. What-cleft como respuesta y resumen

Las what-clefts son muy útiles para resumir información:

- Pregunta: *What did the Finnish model rely on?*
- Respuesta: *What it relied on was trust in teachers and a commitment to equity.*

- Pregunta: *What concerns the professor about summative assessment?*
- Respuesta: *What concerns her is that a final test captures only a snapshot of performance.*

- Pregunta: *What should the school change?*
- Respuesta: *What the school should change is the balance between testing and feedback.*

También pueden iniciar un párrafo argumentativo:

> *What is often overlooked is that assessment shapes learning behaviour. If students are rewarded only for recalling information, they may devote less time to evaluating evidence or formulating original arguments.*

La estructura crea una transición natural desde el foco hacia la explicación. No abuses de ella en cada frase: una cadena de what-clefts puede resultar monótona.

### 9. It-cleft frente a what-cleft

Ambas estructuras pueden expresar un foco similar, pero su organización retórica es diferente:

- *It was the teacher who identified the error.* Destaca al agente y responde a “¿quién?”.
- *What the teacher identified was the error.* Destaca el contenido y responde a “¿qué?”.

- *It was the final exam that caused anxiety.* Destaca la causa.
- *What caused anxiety was the final exam.* Presenta la causa como respuesta.

- *It was in the library that they found the source.* Destaca el lugar.
- *What they found in the library was the source.* Destaca el objeto encontrado.

Usa **it-cleft** cuando el elemento enfocado contrasta con otro elemento. Usa **what-cleft** cuando quieres presentar una pregunta, una conclusión o un comentario sobre una situación.

### 10. Clefts negativas y contrastivas

Las clefts pueden corregir una suposición:

- *It wasn’t the workload that caused the problem; it was the lack of support.*
- *What the policy changed was not access, but the way access was measured.*
- *It was not memorisation that the teacher wanted to assess, but critical thinking.*

El paralelismo ayuda a que la oposición sea clara. Si el foco aparece en singular, el verbo puede concordar con él:

- *What matters is feedback.*
- *What matter are the practical consequences.*

En usos complejos, algunos hablantes prefieren mantener *what matters is* como fórmula fija y reformular el foco plural: *What matters most is the practical consequences for learners* puede oírse, pero en escritura cuidada es más seguro decir *What matters most are the practical consequences* o *The main issue is the practical consequences*. Mantén la concordancia que tu análisis necesita.

### 11. Clefts en escritura académica

Las clefts pueden reforzar una tesis o una transición:

- *It is the interaction between autonomy and accountability that determines the success of a school reform.*
- *What this comparison demonstrates is that equity and excellence need not be opposites.*
- *It was the lack of longitudinal evidence that limited the original conclusion.*

También ayudan a introducir una limitación:

- *What the study does not establish is whether the improvement will persist.*
- *It is not the size of the effect that matters most, but its relevance to daily learning.*

Una cleft resulta más eficaz cuando el foco tiene una función discursiva. Si solo añade palabras sin organizar la argumentación, una estructura simple puede ser mejor:

- Directa: *Teacher autonomy improves motivation.*
- Con foco necesario: *It is teacher autonomy that the comparison identifies as the key factor.*

### 12. Vocabulario de educación y aprendizaje

- **blended learning:** aprendizaje que combina clases presenciales, recursos digitales y trabajo independiente.
- **pedagogical approach:** enfoque pedagógico.
- **self-directed study:** estudio autodirigido.
- **critical thinking:** pensamiento crítico.
- **scrutinise evidence:** examinar pruebas de forma crítica.
- **shed new light on:** aportar una comprensión nueva o más clara.
- **teacher autonomy:** autonomía docente.
- **equity:** equidad, igualdad justa de oportunidades.
- **academic rigour:** rigor académico.
- **standardised testing:** evaluación estandarizada.
- **summative assessment:** evaluación sumativa al final de un curso o periodo.
- **formative feedback:** retroalimentación durante el proceso de aprendizaje.
- **learning outcome:** resultado de aprendizaje.
- **achievement gap:** brecha de rendimiento o logro.
- **higher education:** educación superior.
- **undergraduate:** estudiante o nivel de grado universitario.
- **curriculum:** plan de estudios.
- **enrolment:** matriculación.
- **retention:** permanencia de estudiantes o empleados.
- **lifelong learning:** aprendizaje permanente.

Colocaciones útiles son **adopt a pedagogical approach**, **combine traditional lectures with self-directed study**, **scrutinise evidence**, **evaluate an argument**, **shed new light on a topic**, **measure learning outcomes**, **provide formative feedback**, **close the achievement gap**, **promote equity**, **maintain academic rigour**, **cultivate critical thinking** y **widen access to higher education**.

### 13. Evaluación y clefts

El contraste entre evaluación sumativa y formativa ofrece contextos naturales:

- *It is the depth of understanding that continuous feedback can reveal.*
- *What a final exam captures is a snapshot of performance under pressure.*
- *It was the absence of formative feedback that made the course difficult to adjust.*
- *What matters is whether students can transfer knowledge to a new problem.*

No presentes una conclusión universal si los datos no la justifican. Una buena frase puede incluir distancia o limitación:

- *What the evidence appears to suggest is that formative feedback supports revision, although its effect depends on how students use it.*

La cleft organiza el foco; *appears to suggest* controla la fuerza de la afirmación; *although* introduce la condición que limita la conclusión.

### 14. Errores frecuentes

- *It was the teacher which made the mistake.* → **It was the teacher who made the mistake.**
- *It was Oxford where she enrolled at.* → **It was Oxford that she enrolled at** o **It was at Oxford that she enrolled.**
- *What did the study show was a gap.* → **What the study showed was a gap.**
- *What the students need it is feedback.* → **What the students need is feedback.**
- *It was the exam that the students were anxious.* → **It was the exam that made the students anxious** o **It was the exam that the students were anxious about.**
- *What matters are sustained practice.* → **What matters is sustained practice.**
- *It was not the teacher who made it, but the student.* → Comprueba el referente: **It was not the teacher who made the mistake, but the student.**
- *The reason why the policy failed was because...* → En registro formal: **The reason why the policy failed was that...**

La revisión final consiste en identificar la pregunta implícita, localizar el foco, comprobar *is/was*, elegir *who/that* y mantener el orden declarativo dentro de la cláusula con *what*.

## Parte II — Ejercicios con soluciones

### Ejercicio 1 — Identifica el foco

Indica qué elemento se destaca.

1. *It was the teacher who made the mistake.*
2. *It was the mistake that the teacher made.*
3. *What the teacher identified was a gap in reasoning.*
4. *It was in the library that the students found the source.*

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **The teacher**, la persona responsable.
2. **The mistake**, el objeto de la acción.
3. **A gap in reasoning**, el contenido identificado.
4. **In the library**, el lugar.

</details>

### Ejercicio 2 — It-cleft básica

Combina las frases destacando el elemento entre paréntesis.

1. The teacher noticed the error. (the teacher)
2. The students found the source. (the source)
3. The policy changed attendance. (the policy)
4. They began the project in September. (in September)

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **It was the teacher who noticed the error.**
2. **It was the source that the students found.**
3. **It was the policy that changed attendance.**
4. **It was in September that they began the project.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 3 — Who o that

Elige el relativo correcto.

1. It was the tutor ___ encouraged independent research.
2. It was the deadline ___ created unnecessary pressure.
3. It was the students ___ challenged the assumption.
4. It was the method ___ the supervisor questioned.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **who**; 2. **that**; 3. **who**; 4. **that**. En clefts, *who* es la opción habitual para personas y *that* para cosas o ideas.

</details>

### Ejercicio 4 — What-clefts

Completa.

1. ___ matters ___ sustained practice.
2. ___ the teacher noticed ___ a gap in reasoning.
3. ___ surprised the panel ___ the consistency of the results.
4. ___ learners need ___ meaningful feedback.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **What matters is**.
2. **What the teacher noticed was**.
3. **What surprised the panel was**.
4. **What learners need is**.

</details>

### Ejercicio 5 — Orden declarativo

Corrige las what-clefts.

1. *What did the study show was a gap in access.*
2. *What does the teacher need is more feedback.*
3. *What did the panel find was unexpected variation.*

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **What the study showed was a gap in access.**
2. **What the teacher needs is more feedback.**
3. **What the panel found was unexpected variation.**

Dentro de una what-clause no usamos el orden de una pregunta directa.

</details>

### Ejercicio 6 — Presente o pasado

Elige **is** o **was**.

1. It ___ the feedback that changed her approach last term.
2. What ___ important now is whether students can apply the concept.
3. It ___ the teacher who notices the pattern first in this course.
4. What ___ most striking in the survey was the gap in access.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **was**; 2. **is**; 3. **is**; 4. **was**. El verbo de la cleft sigue el momento de referencia.

</details>

### Ejercicio 7 — Contraste

Reescribe usando una cleft.

*The problem was not the workload. It was the lack of support.*

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

**It wasn’t the workload that caused the problem; it was the lack of support.** También: **What caused the problem was not the workload, but the lack of support.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 9 — Reading

Lee el texto y responde.

> The Finnish model of education, widely admired for its consistently high outcomes, rests not on standardised testing or competitive ranking, but on deep trust in teachers as autonomous professionals and a firm belief that equity and excellence are not mutually exclusive. What underpins the model is a combination of teacher autonomy, carefully trained professionals and a commitment to support rather than punish struggling learners. 1. What does the model rest on?
2. What underpins it according to the text?
3. What attracted international attention?
4. What concern do critics raise?

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **Trust in teachers and a belief that equity and excellence can coexist.**
2. **Teacher autonomy, trained professionals and support for struggling learners.**
3. **The balance between autonomy, support and expectations, rather than the absence of assessment.**
4. **That creativity and personal development may weaken academic rigour if expectations are unclear.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 10 — Listening guiado

Guion: “What concerns me about purely summative assessment is that it captures a snapshot of performance under pressure rather than the depth of understanding developed over time. It is the lack of regular feedback that makes the system difficult to adjust. What students need is an opportunity to revise their thinking before the final grade is awarded.”

Identifica dos what-clefts y una it-cleft.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

What-clefts: **What concerns me about purely summative assessment is...** y **What students need is...**. It-cleft: **It is the lack of regular feedback that makes the system difficult to adjust.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 11 — Relativo y preposición

Completa o corrige.

1. It was Oxford ___ she enrolled ___, not Cambridge.
2. It was the source ___ the essay referred ___ .
3. It was the teacher ___ the students spoke ___ after class.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **that; at**: *It was Oxford that she enrolled at.* También: **It was at Oxford that she enrolled.**
2. **that; to**: *It was the source that the essay referred to.*
3. **who; to**: *It was the teacher who the students spoke to.* En registro muy formal: *It was to the teacher whom the students spoke*, aunque la versión con preposición final es más natural.

</details>

### Ejercicio 12 — Cleft y registro académico

Reescribe con una cleft: *The study does not establish whether the improvement will persist.*

<details><summary>Modelo</summary>

**What the study does not establish is whether the improvement will persist.** La what-cleft convierte la limitación en el foco del argumento.

</details>

### Ejercicio 13 — Corrige el párrafo

> What did the assessment reveal was a difference in preparation. It was the final exam which caused anxiety, not the feedback. The students needed it was more time to revise.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

**What the assessment revealed was a difference in preparation. It was the final exam that caused anxiety, not the feedback. What the students needed was more time to revise.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 14 — Writing

Escribe 150–180 palabras sobre: **Should final exams be replaced by continuous assessment?** Incluye una it-cleft, una what-cleft, una concesión y vocabulario educativo.

<details><summary>Modelo</summary>

It is the purpose of assessment that should determine its format. If a school wants to measure long-term understanding, continuous assessment may provide more useful evidence than a single final exam. What summative testing captures is a snapshot of performance under pressure, whereas formative feedback can reveal how learners respond to guidance and revise their thinking. Nevertheless, it would be an oversimplification to dismiss examinations entirely. It was the structure of a timed exam that helped some students develop synthesis and time-management skills, although the same structure may disadvantage learners who perform poorly under pressure. A balanced pedagogical approach could combine a final task with regular opportunities to scrutinise evidence, receive feedback and demonstrate progress. What matters most is not whether one method is universally superior, but whether the assessment measures the intended learning outcomes and promotes equity. Schools should therefore use different forms of evidence rather than relying on a single score.

</details>

### Ejercicio 15 — Speaking

Habla durante un minuto sobre el aprendizaje autodirigido. Usa **What matters is**, **It is... that**, **who** y una concesión.

<details><summary>Pista y modelo</summary>

**What matters is whether learners can set realistic goals and evaluate their progress. It is the learner who must take responsibility for regular practice, although teachers still provide structure and formative feedback. It is not the amount of material that guarantees progress, but the quality of attention given to it.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 18 — Mini-test final

Completa el texto.

### Ejercicio 19 — Dos focos, dos respuestas

Responde a cada pregunta usando la estructura indicada. Después explica qué elemento queda en primer plano.

1. **Who changed the assessment?** Usa una it-cleft y destaca *the supervisor*.
2. **What did the supervisor change?** Usa una what-cleft y destaca *the feedback schedule*.
3. **When did attendance improve?** Usa una it-cleft y destaca *after the timetable was revised*.
4. **What remains uncertain?** Usa una what-cleft y destaca *whether the improvement will last*.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **It was the supervisor who changed the assessment.** El foco es la persona responsable.
2. **What the supervisor changed was the feedback schedule.** El foco es el objeto de la acción.
3. **It was after the timetable was revised that attendance improved.** El foco es el momento o la condición temporal.
4. **What remains uncertain is whether the improvement will last.** El foco es la cuestión que todavía no tiene respuesta.

Una it-cleft suele responder a “¿quién, qué, cuándo o dónde?” mediante un elemento que se contrasta. Una what-cleft presenta una pregunta o conclusión como sujeto y coloca la información relevante después de *is/was*. Ambas pueden ser correctas, pero la elección depende de la organización del discurso.

</details>

### Ejercicio 20 — Edición de un párrafo académico

Reescribe este párrafo para que tenga un registro C1 claro y cohesionado. Debes usar una cleft, una what-clause, una concesión y vocabulario de evaluación.

> The exam is bad because it makes students nervous. The school should change it. Some teachers disagree. They say students need exams for their future.

<details><summary>Modelo</summary>

**It is the pressure created by a single high-stakes exam that concerns many students. What the school should consider is whether continuous assessment could provide a fairer picture of learning. Although some teachers argue that examinations prepare students for future demands, the available evidence does not necessarily show that one timed test measures critical thinking or long-term understanding. A blended assessment model could therefore combine academic rigour with formative feedback.**

La versión sustituye *bad* por una descripción precisa, transforma la idea principal en una it-cleft y usa una what-clause para formular la decisión que debe estudiar la escuela. *Although* introduce la postura contraria y *does not necessarily show* evita una generalización excesiva.

</details>

## Resumen de la unidad

Usa **It is/was + foco + that/who + clause** para destacar una persona, cosa, lugar, momento o causa. Usa **What + clause + is/was + foco** para presentar “lo que” importa, lo que se descubrió o lo que sigue sin resolverse.

Recuerda mantener el orden declarativo dentro de la what-clause: *What the study showed was...*, no *What did the study show was...*. Usa *who* para personas y *that* para objetos e ideas, y comprueba que el verbo de la cleft concuerde con el momento de referencia.

En escritura académica, las clefts son más útiles cuando responden a una pregunta implícita o establecen un contraste. Combínalas con *although, whereas, nevertheless* y expresiones de cautela para distinguir resultados, interpretaciones y limitaciones. Cuando unas estas estructuras a colocaciones como *provide formative feedback*, *scrutinise evidence*, *promote equity* y *cultivate critical thinking*, podrás argumentar sobre educación con precisión, cohesión y control propio del nivel C1.
