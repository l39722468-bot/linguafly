Los patrones preposicionales son uno de los territorios donde el inglés C1 se gana o se pierde. La dificultad no reside en reglas lógicas sino en convenciones arbitrarias: cada verbo y cada adjetivo exigen una preposición concreta que no se deduce del significado y que a menudo difiere de la lengua materna, generando interferencias. Esta unidad del Language Lab aísla este fenómeno, lo examina en sus patrones más rentables y lo entrena hasta la automatización, con especial atención a las colocaciones formales que la prosa académica C1 requiere.

Cada concepto se presenta en tres niveles. El A2 ofrece la versión más sencilla, el B2 añade complejidad operativa y el C1 presenta los patrones preposicionales con sus matices de registro, alternancia y colocación.

A2: Some words in English need a small word after them, like "depend on" or "interested in."

B2: Prepositional patterns are conventional, not logical. Each verb and adjective takes a specific preposition that must be memorised: depend on, consist of, result in, rely on, prone to. Interference from your native language causes errors that signal a lower level.

C1: Prepositional patterning constitutes a domain in which C1 proficiency is won or forfeited through the mastery of arbitrary convention. The selection of the complement preposition is irreducible to semantic logic and frequently divergent from the candidate's native language, engendering transfer-induced errors that diagnostically signal sub-C1 competence. This Language Lab unit isolates the phenomenon, scrutinises its most discursively productive patterns and drills them to automaticity, with particular attention to the formal collocations that C1 academic prose demands.

## Parte I — Teoría: Patrones preposicionales de verbo y adjetivo

### 1. La arbitrariedad del patrón preposicional

La elección de la preposición tras un verbo o un adjetivo no se rige por la lógica sino por la convención. Decimos depend on pero en español dependemos de; consist of pero en español consistimos en; result in pero en español resulta en. Estas asociaciones son arbitrarias y deben aprenderse como unidades fijas, porque la interferencia de la lengua materna es la fuente más frecuente de error.

A2: The little word after a verb is not always logical. You must learn it.

B2: The choice of preposition after a verb or adjective is governed by convention, not logic. We say depend on (not depend of), consist of (not consist in, though consist in exists with a different meaning), result in. These associations are arbitrary and must be learned as fixed units.

C1: The complement preposition selected by a verb or adjective is determined by convention rather than semantic logic, rendering the patterns irreducible to inferential acquisition. The English depend on diverges from the Spanish depender de; consist of diverges from consistir en; result in converges superficially but masks deeper distributional divergences. These associations are categorically arbitrary and must be internalised as indivisible lexical units, since cross-linguistic interference constitutes the most prolific source of prepositional error at the C1 threshold.

### 2. Verbos que toman on: depend, rely, build, draw, insist

Un grupo numeroso de verbos C1 toma la preposición on: depend on (depender de), rely on (confiar en), build on (desarrollar a partir de), draw on (recurrir a), insist on (insistir en), focus on (centrarse en), elaborate on (desarrollar, ampliar), deliberate on (deliberar sobre), expand on (ampliar). Su consolidación es fundamental porque aparecen con frecuencia en prosa académica.

A2: Some verbs use "on" after them, like "depend on" and "rely on."

B2: A large group of C1 verbs takes the preposition on: depend on, rely on, build on, draw on, insist on, focus on, elaborate on, deliberate on, expand on. Their consolidation is essential because they appear frequently in academic prose.

C1: A sizeable cohort of C1 verbs selects the preposition on: depend on, rely on, build on, draw on, insist on, focus on, elaborate on, deliberate on and expand on. Their consolidation is foundational, given their diagnostic frequency in academic expository prose, and their mis-prepositioning, such as depend of, constitutes an error whose persistence would conspicuously undermine a candidate's C1 credential.

### 3. Verbos que toman in: result, engage, specialise, culminate

Otro grupo toma in: result in (dar como resultado), engage in (participar en), specialise in (especializarse en), culminate in (culminar en), consist in (consistir en, significar), participate in (participar en), succeed in (tener éxito en), confide in (confiar en alguien). La distinción entre consist of y consist in es crucial: el primero indica composición, el segundo significa consistir en el sentido de equivaler a.

A2: Some verbs use "in," like "result in" and "participate in."

B2: Another group takes in: result in, engage in, specialise in, culminate in, consist in, participate in, succeed in, confide in. The distinction between consist of and consist in is crucial: the first indicates composition, the second means to consist in the sense of to amount to.

C1: A further verbal cohort selects in: result in, engage in, specialise in, culminate in, consist in, participate in, succeed in and confide in. The discrimination between consist of and consist in is pivotal: the former encodes part-whole composition, the latter encodes definitional equivalence, as in leadership consists in inspiring others. Their conflation would generate a semantic distortion diagnostically incompatible with C1 precision.

### 4. Verbos que toman of: consist, dispose, conceive, beware

Los verbos que toman of son menos pero importantes: consist of (estar compuesto de), dispose of (desechar, desprenderse de), conceive of (concebir, imaginar), beware of (cuidado con), acquit of (absolver de), boast of (jactarse de). La interferencia con el español consistir en es una trampa recurrente que el C1 debe erradicar.

A2: A few verbs use "of," like "consist of" and "beware of."

B2: Verbs taking of are fewer but important: consist of, dispose of, conceive of, beware of, acquit of, boast of. Interference with Spanish consistir en is a recurrent trap that C1 must eradicate.

C1: The verbal cohort selecting of, though less populous, is functionally significant: consist of, dispose of, conceive of, beware of, acquit of and boast of. The cross-linguistic interference with the Spanish consistir en constitutes a recurrent trap whose eradication is non-negotiable for C1 certification, the error consist in of being a paradigmatic fossilisation diagnostic of insufficient prepositional automation.

### 5. Verbos que toman with: comply, align, associate, reconcile

Los verbos que toman with incluyen comply with (cumplir con), align with (alinearse con), associate with (asociarse con), reconcile with (reconciliar con), cope with (lidiar con), contend with (hacer frente a), coincide with (coincidir con), part company with (separarse de). Muchos de estos son de registro formal y propios de la prosa académica y administrativa.

A2: Some verbs use "with," like "comply with" and "cope with."

B2: Verbs taking with include comply with, align with, associate with, reconcile with, cope with, contend with, coincide with, part company with. Many of these are formal and typical of academic and administrative prose.

C1: The verbal cohort selecting with encompasses comply with, align with, associate with, reconcile with, cope with, contend with, coincide with and part company with, the majority inhabiting the formal register characteristic of academic and administrative prose. Their correct deployment, particularly comply with and contend with, functions as a registeral marker whose absence would attenuate the impression of C1 formality.

### 6. Adjetivos que toman on, of, with, to: el mapa preposicional adjetival

Los adjetivos C1 también rigen preposiciones específicas: dependent on (dependiente de), independent of (independiente de), consistent with (consistente con), compatible with (compatible con), prone to (propenso a), conducive to (propicio a), averse to (reacio a), reliant on (dependiente de), conversant with (familiarizado con), mindful of (consciente de). Cada adjetivo tiene su preposición fija y cambiarla produce errores de registro.

A2: Adjectives also need small words after them: "dependent on," "prone to."

B2: C1 adjectives also govern specific prepositions: dependent on, independent of, consistent with, compatible with, prone to, conducive to, averse to, reliant on, conversant with, mindful of. Each adjective has its fixed preposition, and changing it produces register errors.

C1: C1 adjectives likewise govern complement prepositions with categorical fixity: dependent on, independent of, consistent with, compatible with, prone to, conducive to, averse to, reliant on, conversant with and mindful of. Each adjective contracts an idiosyncratic prepositional bond whose violation, such as dependent of or prone for, generates an error whose registeral dissonance would diagnostically undermine a candidate's C1 presentation, particularly in written production.

### 7. La trampa de dependent on e independent of

Una de las trampas más frecuentes es la aparente asimetría entre dependent on e independent of. Lógicamente cabría esperar independent on, pero la convención fija independent of. Esta asimiedad refleja la arbitrariedad del patrón preposicional y debe memorizarse sin buscar justificación lógica. Lo mismo ocurre con reliant on pero free of o free from.

A2: We say "dependent on" but "independent of." This is not logical, but it is correct.

B2: One of the most frequent traps is the asymmetry between dependent on and independent of. Logically one might expect independent on, but convention fixes independent of. This asymmetry reflects the arbitrariness of prepositional patterning and must be memorised without seeking logical justification.

C1: Among the most prolific traps is the apparent asymmetry between dependent on and independent of. Logical expectation would project independent on by analogical extension, yet convention categorically fixes independent of. This asymmetry, emblematic of the arbitrary character of prepositional patterning, must be memorised without the quest for rational motivation, a discipline that runs counter to the inferential habits of the proficient learner but that is indispensable for prepositional accuracy at C1.

### 8. Alternancia preposicional con cambio de significado

Algunos verbos cambian de significado según la preposición. Agree with significa estar de acuerdo con una persona u opinión; agree to significa aceptar una propuesta o condición; agree on significa ponerse de acuerdo sobre un tema. Look at significa mirar; look into significa investigar; look after significa cuidar. Reconocer estos matices es una marca distintiva del C1.

A2: Some verbs change meaning with the little word: "agree with" is different from "agree to."

B2: Some verbs change meaning with the preposition. Agree with means to share an opinion; agree to means to accept a proposal; agree on means to reach agreement on a topic. Look at means to watch; look into means to investigate; look after means to care for. Recognising these nuances distinguishes C1.

C1: A subset of verbs undergoes semantic modulation as a function of the complement preposition. Agree with encodes opinion-sharing with a person or proposition; agree to encodes acceptance of a proposal or condition; agree on encodes negotiated convergence on a topic. Similarly, look at encodes visual perception, look into encodes investigative scrutiny, and look after encodes custodial care. The diagnostic recognition of these prepositional semantic nuances is a distinguishing marker of C1 lexical precision, their conflation being a hallmark of insufficient differentiation.

### 9. Verbos sin preposición: el error de la preposición espuria

Un error frecuente es añadir una preposición donde el inglés no la admite. Discuss something (no discuss about something), emphasise something (no emphasise on something), consider something (no consider about something), approach someone (no approach to someone). Esta preposición espuria refleja la interferencia del español y debe erradicarse.

A2: Some verbs do not need a little word after them: "discuss the problem," not "discuss about the problem."

B2: A frequent error is adding a preposition where English does not allow one: discuss something (not discuss about), emphasise something (not emphasise on), consider something (not consider about), approach someone (not approach to). This spurious preposition reflects Spanish interference and must be eradicated.

C1: A high-frequency error consists in the insertion of a preposition where English categorically precludes one: discuss something, not discuss about something; emphasise something, not emphasise on something; consider something, not consider about something; approach someone, not approach to someone. These spurious prepositions are unambiguous artefacts of Spanish transfer and their eradication is a prerequisite for C1 certification, their persistence being a fossilisation diagnostic of sub-C1 syntactic control.

### 10. Patrones preposicionales en la escritura académica

La prosa académica C1 despliega patrones preposicionales formales con frecuencia: to account for a phenomenon, to stem from a cause, to culminate in a result, to be consistent with evidence, to be conducive to innovation, to be reliant on funding, to be averse to risk. Dominar estas colocaciones preposicionales eleva la densidad y la precisión léxica del ensayo académico.

A2: In academic writing, some word combinations sound professional, like "account for" and "consistent with."

B2: C1 academic prose deploys formal prepositional patterns frequently: to account for a phenomenon, to stem from a cause, to culminate in a result, to be consistent with evidence, to be conducive to innovation, to be reliant on funding, to be averse to risk. Mastering these collocations raises lexical density and precision.

C1: C1 academic prose exhibits a diagnostic frequency of formal prepositional collocations: to account for a phenomenon, to stem from a cause, to culminate in a result, to be consistent with evidence, to be conducive to innovation, to be reliant on funding and to be averse to risk. The idiomatic assimilation of these prepositional collocations elevates both the lexical density and the precisional granularity of the academic essay, their conspicuous absence conversely attenuating the impression of scholarly register.

### 11. Verbos de opinión y postura con preposición

Los verbos de opinión y postura rigen preposiciones específicas: to subscribe to a view (suscribir una opinión), to allude to something (aludir a), to point to evidence (indicar), to object to a proposal (oponerse a), to allude to a study (aludir a), to dissent from a view (disentir de), to defer to an authority (deferir a). Su uso preciso en la argumentación académica es esencial.

A2: Some verbs about opinions use specific words: "object to" and "point to."

B2: Opinion and stance verbs govern specific prepositions: to subscribe to a view, to allude to something, to point to evidence, to object to a proposal, to dissent from a view, to defer to an authority. Their precise use in academic argumentation is essential.

C1: Opinion and stance predicates select prepositions with categorical fixity: to subscribe to a view, to allude to something, to point to evidence, to object to a proposal, to dissent from a view and to defer to an authority. Their precise deployment within academic argumentation is indispensable, the substitution of one preposition for another, such as object at for object to, generating a registeral and semantic infraction that would diagnostically compromise C1 credibility.

### 12. Adjetivos de evaluación y crítica con preposición

Los adjetivos evaluativos también rigen preposiciones: critical of (crítico con), indicative of (indicativo de), suggestive of (sugerente de), contemptuous of (despreciativo de), dismissive of (despectivo con), wary of (receloso de), conversant with (familiarizado con), averse to (reacio a). Su uso en reseñas y críticas académicas es recurrente y debe dominarse.

A2: Some describing words use specific words too: "critical of" and "wary of."

B2: Evaluative adjectives also govern prepositions: critical of, indicative of, suggestive of, contemptuous of, dismissive of, wary of, conversant with, averse to. Their use in academic reviews and critiques is recurrent and must be mastered.

C1: Evaluative adjectives likewise govern complement prepositions: critical of, indicative of, suggestive of, contemptuous of, dismissive of, wary of, conversant with and averse to. Their recurrent deployment in academic reviews and critiques necessitates mastery, the prepositional error critical with for critical of being a fossilisation that would conspicuously attenuate the impression of C1 evaluative precision.

### 13. La alternancia entre gerundio y that-clause

Algunos verbos admiten tanto un gerundio como una that-clause tras la preposición o directamente: to insist on leaving / to insist that he leave; to result in the policy being revised / to result in the policy being revised (gerundio); to acknowledge that / to acknowledge doing. La elección codifica matices de estructura y énfasis que el C1 debe manejar.

A2: Some verbs can be followed by an -ing word or by "that."

B2: Some verbs admit both a gerund and a that-clause: to insist on leaving / to insist that he leave; to acknowledge that / to acknowledge doing. The choice encodes structural and emphasis nuances that C1 must handle.

C1: A class of verbs admits both gerundial and finite-clausal complementation: to insist on leaving alternates with to insist that he leave, and to acknowledge doing alternates with to acknowledge that he did. The selection encodes structural and focal nuances, the gerundial variant foregrounding process and the finite variant foregrounding propositional content, whose calibrated manipulation is a diagnostic of C1 syntactic sophistication.

### 14. Errores por interferencia de la lengua materna: catálogo

El catálogo de errores por interferencia es amplio: depend of (de depender de, correcto depend on), consist in (de consistir en, correcto consist of para composición), think in (de pensar en, correcto think about), marry with (de casarse con, correcto marry sin preposición), listen music (de escuchar música, correcto listen to music). Identificar el origen del error acelera su erradicación.

A2: Some mistakes come from your own language. For example, "depend of" is wrong; say "depend on."

B2: The catalogue of interference errors is large: depend of (correct: depend on), consist in for composition (correct: consist of), think in (correct: think about), marry with (correct: marry), listen music (correct: listen to music). Identifying the source of the error accelerates eradication.

C1: The catalogue of transfer-induced errors is extensive: depend of, projected from the Spanish depender de, versus the correct depend on; consist in projected from consistir en versus the correct consist of for compositional readings; think in projected from pensar en versus the correct think about; marry with projected from casarse con versus the correct marry without preposition; listen music projected from escuchar música versus the correct listen to music. The diagnostic identification of the transfer source accelerates the targeted eradication of each fossilisation.

### 15. Estrategias de consolidación de patrones preposicionales

La consolidación exige agrupar los patrones por preposición y por campo semántico, crear tarjetas con verbo o adjetivo, preposición, colocación y frase de ejemplo, y practicar la reformulación activa. La exposición abundante a prosa académica auténtica, donde los patrones aparecen en contexto, es insustituible. La práctica de la traducción inversa, del español al inglés, expone las trampas de interferencia de manera directa.

A2: To learn these patterns, group them and practice a lot. Read a lot of English.

B2: Consolidation requires grouping patterns by preposition and semantic field, creating cards with verb or adjective, preposition, collocation and example sentence, and practising active reformulation. Abundant exposure to authentic academic prose is irreplaceable. Practising translation from Spanish to English exposes interference traps directly.

C1: The consolidation of prepositional patterns necessitates their taxonomic organisation by preposition and semantic field, the construction of flashcards encoding the verb or adjective, its preposition, its canonical collocation and a contextually situated exemplar sentence, and the practice of active reformulation. Abundant immersion in authentic academic prose, wherein the patterns arise in natural context, remains the irreplaceable substrate of acquisition. The practice of inverse translation, from Spanish to English, directly exposes the interference traps whose navigation is constitutive of C1 prepositional mastery.
