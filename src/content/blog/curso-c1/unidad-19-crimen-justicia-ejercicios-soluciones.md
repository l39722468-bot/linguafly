Este cuaderno reúne los **ejercicios de la Unidad 19 del curso C1** con soluciones. Haz cada bloque sin mirar la respuesta.

> **Guía teórica:** [Distancing y Modales de Deducción C1](/blog/curso-c1/unidad-19-crimen-justicia)
> **Más unidades de este nivel:** [Curso C1](/blog/curso-c1)

### Cómo usar este cuaderno

Responde primero y comprueba después. La explicación está pensada para el estudiante hispanohablante.

## Parte II — Ejercicios

### Ejercicio 1 — Distancing léxico

Reescribe introduciendo *allegedly, reportedly, supposedly* o *apparently*.

1. The defendant confessed to a fellow inmate.
2. The company knew about the defect for years.
3. The suspect left the country before the warrant was issued.
4. The jury was not convinced by the closing argument.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. *The defendant **allegedly** confessed to a fellow inmate.*
2. *The company **reportedly** knew about the defect for years.*
3. *The suspect **supposedly** left the country before the warrant was issued.*
4. *The jury **apparently** was not convinced by the closing argument.* (o *...was apparently not convinced...*)

</details>

### Ejercicio 2 — It is claimed / said / reported that

Reescribe con cláusula impersonal.

1. Sources say the evidence was mishandled.
2. People believe the verdict will be appealed.
3. Journalists report that the jury deliberated for only two hours.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. *It is said that the evidence was mishandled.*
2. *It is believed that the verdict will be appealed.*
3. *It is reported that the jury deliberated for only two hours.*

La pasiva impersonal oculta la fuente y sube el registro.

</details>

### Ejercicio 3 — Sujeto + pasiva + to-infinitive

Transforma con el sujente adelantado.

1. It is alleged that the defendant confessed.
2. It was reported that the CEO knew about the fraud.
3. It is believed that the weapon was destroyed.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. *The defendant is alleged to have confessed.*
2. *The CEO was reported to have known about the fraud.*
3. *The weapon is believed to have been destroyed.*

El *to-infinitive* perfecto marca anterioridad respecto al verbo de atribución.

</details>

### Ejercicio 4 — Seem / appear

Reescribe con *seem* o *appear* + to-infinitivo.

1. The witness hesitated.
2. The evidence points to a different suspect.
3. The jury was not convinced.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. *The witness appeared to hesitate.*
2. *The evidence seems to point to a different suspect.*
3. *The jury appeared not to be convinced.* (o *It appeared that the jury was not convinced.*)

</details>

### Ejercicio 5 — Must have / can't have

Completa con la deducción adecuada.

1. The safe was forced open; someone ___ (know) the combination.
2. He ___ (act) alone — the footage shows two figures.
3. She ___ (be) at the scene; she was abroad that night.
4. The documents were shredded; someone ___ (try) to hide the trail.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **must have known**; 2. **can't have acted** (o *couldn't have acted*); 3. **can't have been** (o *couldn't have been*); 4. **must have tried**.

*Must have* = única conclusión lógica; *can't/couldn't have* = imposibilidad lógica.

</details>

### Ejercicio 6 — Might / may / could have

Elige un modal de posibilidad.

1. The defendant ___ (panic) under pressure.
2. The jury ___ (be) influenced by the media coverage.
3. The leak ___ (come) from inside the department.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. *might have panicked*; 2. *may have been influenced*; 3. *could have come*. Los tres son aceptables en cada caso; *may* sugiere mayor probabilidad que *might*; *could have* admite también lectura de posibilidad no realizada. En un sumario judicial conviene reservar *might have* para las conjeturas más especulativas y *may have* cuando ciertos indicios apuntan en esa dirección, dejando *could have* para los escenarios hipotéticos que el abogado plantea como alternativa razonable.

</details>

### Ejercicio 7 — Should / ought to have

Reescribe con expectativa no cumplida.

1. The police didn't secure the scene.
2. The prosecution didn't disclose the evidence earlier.
3. The judge allowed the testimony.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. *The police should have secured the scene.*
2. *The prosecution ought to have disclosed the evidence earlier.*
3. *The judge shouldn't have allowed the testimony.*

*Should/ought to have* marcan lo que era de esperar; el negativo critica lo ocurrido.

</details>

### Ejercicio 8 — Would have (contrafactual)

Completa con *would have* + participio.

1. An experienced officer ___ (notice) the discrepancy.
2. A competent lawyer ___ (object) to the leading question.
3. With stronger evidence, the prosecution ___ (secure) a conviction.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **would have noticed**; 2. **would have objected**; 3. **would have secured**. El *if* está implícito; se deduce lo que habría ocurrido en otras condiciones. Fíjate en que *would have* no afirma lo que pasó, sino lo que cabe esperar de un agente competente, de modo que la frase funciona como estándar implícito contra el que se mide la conducta real del acusado o del profesional.

</details>

### Ejercicio 9 — Corrige los errores

1. He allegedly is guilty.
2. He must to have fled.
3. The police should secured the scene.
4. Reportedly, the verdict is overturned yesterday.
5. It is claimed he is involved last year.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. *He is allegedly guilty.* (o *Allegedly, he is guilty.*)
2. *He must have fled.* (sin *to*)
3. *The police should have secured the scene.*
4. *Reportedly, the verdict was overturned yesterday.*
5. *It is claimed he was involved last year.* (o *...is claimed to have been involved last year.*)

</details>

### Ejercicio 10 — Transformación (CAE-style)

Reescribe manteniendo el sentido con la palabra dada.

1. People say the judge was biased. (CLAIMED)
2. I'm sure the jury was confused. (MUST)
3. It's impossible that he confessed. (CAN'T)
4. Perhaps the witness lied. (MIGHT)

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. *It was claimed that the judge was biased.* (o *The judge was claimed to have been biased.*)
2. *The jury must have been confused.*
3. *He can't have confessed.*
4. *The witness might have lied.*

</details>

### Ejercicio 11 — Vocabulario legal

Elige el término correcto.

1. The person accused in a criminal trial is the ___ (defendant / plaintiff).
2. A verdict of "not guilty" means the court will ___ (convict / acquit).
3. Evidence that implies but does not directly prove is ___ (circumstantial / direct).
4. The standard "beyond ___ doubt" governs criminal verdicts. (reasonable / reasonable)
5. To ask a higher court to review a verdict is to ___ (appeal / indict).

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **defendant** (el *plaintiff* es el demandante en un caso civil); 2. **acquit**; 3. **circumstantial**; 4. **beyond reasonable doubt**; 5. **appeal**.

</details>

### Ejercicio 12 — Colocaciones

Completa con el verbo adecuado (*reach / overturn / plead / file / dismiss / grant*).

1. ___ a verdict 2. ___ a conviction on appeal 3. ___ guilty 4. ___ an appeal 5. ___ the charges 6. ___ bail

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **reach** a verdict; 2. **overturn** a conviction (on appeal); 3. **plead** guilty; 4. **file** an appeal; 5. **dismiss** the charges; 6. **grant** bail.

</details>

### Ejercicio 13 — Distinguir deducción y obligación

Indica si *must* significa obligación (O) o deducción (D).

1. Witnesses must tell the whole truth.
2. He must have left before midnight.
3. The defendant must appear at every hearing.
4. The safe must have been opened with a key.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **Obligación**; 2. **Deducción**; 3. **Obligación**; 4. **Deducción**. Pista: *must have + pp* es siempre deducción; *must + infinitivo* suele ser obligación en presente.

</details>

### Ejercicio 14 — Reading

Lee y responde.

> *The defendant is alleged to have acted under duress, and the prosecution reportedly relied on circumstantial evidence. The jury must have entertained reasonable doubt, for the verdict was acquittal.*

1. ¿Qué dos recursos de distancing aparecen y qué distancia establecen?
2. ¿Qué deduce el autor con *must have entertained*?
3. ¿Por qué es apropiado *reportedly* y no una afirmación directa?

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. *is alleged to have* (atribuye la versión a una fuente, sin asumirla) y *reportedly* (atribuye a información difundida). Ambos separan al autor de la afirmación.
2. Que la única conclusión lógica, dado el veredicto de absolución, es que el jurado albergó duda razonable.
3. Porque la estrategia concreta de la fiscalía no se prueba en el texto; *reportedly* presenta la información como difundida, no como hecho comprobado por el autor. Obsérvese cómo el encadenamiento de tres recursos —atribución, deducción e inferencia causal (*for the verdict was acquittal*)— permite al autor sostener una tesis sin emitir una sola afirmación directa que pudiera ser desmentida por el expediente. Esa es la marca de un comentario judicial maduro.

</details>

### Ejercicio 15 — Listening (paráfrasis)

Oíste: *"They say the judge recused herself at the last minute."* Reescribe con distancing formal.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

Modelo: *It is said that the judge recused herself at the last minute.* / *The judge is said to have recused herself at the last minute.* / *The judge reportedly recused herself at the last minute.*

</details>

### Ejercicio 16 — Writing

Escribe un párrafo (70–90 palabras) sobre un caso, usando al menos: una estructura *is alleged/reported to have*, un *must have* o *can't have*, y un *should have*.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

Modelo: *The defendant is alleged to have coerced the witness, yet the prosecution reportedly failed to disclose this in time. The judge can't have been aware of the breach, or she would have halted the proceedings. In fairness, the defence should have requested the full file earlier, and the court ought to have sanctioned the delay.* (52 palabras; añade un ejemplo para alcanzar el rango.)

Criterios: distancing correcto, deducción modal coherente, *should/ought to have* con matiz crítico, y registro controlado.

</details>

### Ejercicio 17 — Speaking (interacción)

Responde en 2 minutos: *"Should circumstantial evidence ever be enough to convict?"* Usa al menos un *might have*, un *must have* y una expresión de distancing.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

Modelo: *Circumstantial evidence might have to suffice when direct proof is impossible — in financial crime, for instance. But a jury must have felt the case was proven beyond reasonable doubt. Reportedly, many wrongful convictions rest on circumstantial threads, so arguably the standard should be applied with special care.*

</details>

### Ejercicio 18 — Consolidación

Identifica cada recurso del texto y su función.

> *The CEO is reported to have known of the fault for months. He must have judged the risk acceptable. The board, arguably, should have acted sooner; had they done so, the deaths would have been prevented.*

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

- *is reported to have known* → distancing (atribución, sin asumir).
- *must have judged* → deducción (única conclusión lógica).
- *arguably* → hedging/distancing (modula la certeza del juicio).
- *should have acted* → expectativa no cumplida (crítica).
- *had they done so ... would have been prevented* → condicional invertida con *had* (contrafactual).

</details>

### Ejercicio 19 — Reescribe el párrafo con distancing y deducción

Reescribe este párrafo neutro elevando el registro con, al menos, una estructura *is alleged/reported to have*, un *must have* y un *should have*.

> *The company knew the brakes were faulty. They did not recall the cars. People think the executives hid the report. Several accidents happened, and the evidence shows the executives ignored the warnings.*

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

Modelo: *The company is reported to have known that the brakes were faulty, yet it allegedly failed to recall the affected models. The executives are believed to have suppressed the internal report, and they must have grasped the risk, for the warnings were unambiguous. Several accidents ensued; plainly, the board should have issued a recall the moment the first fatality was logged.*

Rúbrica: la atribución (*is reported/believed to have*) sustituye las afirmaciones directas; *allegedly* modula la omisión; *must have grasped* deduce lo que sabían; *should have issued* critica la omisión. Evita presentar como hechos las acusaciones sin prueba.

</details>

## Resumen de la unidad

- El **distancing** separa al hablante de la afirmación: adverbios (*allegedly, reportedly, supposedly, apparently*), cláusulas impersonales (*it is claimed/said/reported that*), estructuras con sujeto adelantado (*is alleged to have*) y *seem/appear to*.
- Los **modales de deducción en pasado** gradúan la certeza: *must have* (certeza positiva), *can't/couldn't have* (imposibilidad), *might/may/could have* (posibilidad), *should/ought to have* (expectativa no cumplida), *would have* (contrafactual).
- En pasado, *must have* es siempre deducción; la obligación pasada usa *had to*. Evita dobles negaciones y mantén la coherencia de tiempos al atribuir.
- El **vocabulario legal** (*alleged, defendant, verdict, circumstantial, prosecute, acquit, conviction, appeal, beyond reasonable doubt*) y sus colocaciones (*reach a verdict, overturn a conviction, plead guilty, file an appeal*) dan precisión a la crónica judicial, donde la cautela gramatical es tan importante como el léxico.
