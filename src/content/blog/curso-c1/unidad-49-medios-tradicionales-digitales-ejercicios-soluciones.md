## Parte II — Ejercicios

**Ejercicio 1.** Distingue *hard news*, *feature* y *op-ed* en una frase que muestre sus diferencias de registro e intención.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*Whereas the hard news piece adheres to the inverted pyramid and confines the journalist's voice to the reporting of verified facts, the feature embraces narrative techniques and explicit authorial presence, and the op-ed dispenses with neutrality altogether to argue a position — three genres that readers calibrate, often unconsciously, to gauge how much of what they are reading is reportage, storytelling and interpretation respectively.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 2.** Explica la diferencia entre *broadsheet*, *tabloid* y *quality press* en inglés C1.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*Broadsheet* designaba originalmente el formato grande de imprenta asociado a la prensa seria; *tabloid* (o *red-top*) designaba el formato pequeño asociado al sensacionalismo popular; *quality press* (o *qualities*) designa la prensa de calidad por su rigor editorial, independientemente del formato físico. La distinción es valativa e institucional, no meramente física: un título puede ser tabloide en formato pero quality en estándares, como ocurrió cuando varios broadsheets adoptaron el formato compacto.
</details>

**Ejercicio 3.** Reescribe con léxico de la transformación económica: *Newspapers don't sell many copies anymore and they don't get much advertising money, so they try to get people to pay online.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The collapse of print circulation, compounded by the erosion of advertising revenue as classifieds migrated online, has forced legacy titles to pivot towards digital subscriptions and metered paywalls in a market where reader willingness to pay remains, at best, uneven.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 4.** Completa con léxico de hipervínculos y lectura hipertextual: *________ reading, in which the reader hops between linked texts rather than progressing linearly, dissolves the boundaries of the article and shifts part of the editorial burden onto the reader, who must now evaluate not only a single text but the coherence and credibility of the ________ of texts it connects to.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*Hyper* (o *Hyper reading*) y *network* — *hyper reading* capta la navegación no lineal entre enlaces; *network* reconoce que el lector evalúa ya no un texto aislado sino un conjunto de textos interconectados, lo que redefine la propia naturaleza de la labor de lectura crítica en el entorno digital.
</details>

**Ejercicio 5.** Reescribe con léxico de redes sociales y economía de la atención: *Social media shows people things that make them react, so they keep looking.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The algorithmic curation of the feed, optimised for engagement rather than accuracy in an attention economy where dwell time is the metric that monetisation follows, tends to amplify content that provokes reaction — a design choice with structural consequences for the kind of information that attains visibility on the platform.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 6.** Identifica y explica la técnica del *curiosity gap* en este titular: *You won't believe what this politician said about climate change.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

El titular emplea la técnica del *curiosity gap* (brecha de curiosidad): oculta deliberadamente la información clave —qué dijo el político— para crear una brecha entre lo que el lector sabe y lo que quiere saber, fabricando el impulso de hacer clic para cerrarla. La fórmula *you won't believe* es emblemática del *clickbait* y optimiza el titular no para informar sino para manufacturar el consumo, alineando los incentivos del titular con el clic y desalineándolos con las necesidades informativas del lector.
</details>

**Ejercicio 7.** Reescribe con léxico de calidad editorial y rigor: *The newspaper mixes news and ads, so people don't know what's real.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The erosion of the church-state separation between newsroom and advertising department, accelerated by the rise of native advertising, risks confusing readers about where reportage ends and paid promotion begins — a blurring that, however commercially expedient, corrodes the trust on which the editorial brand ultimately depends.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 8.** Completa con léxico de comparación tradicional-digital: *The ________ wrought by digital platforms has stripped legacy media of its ________ function, a loss whose democratic implications are ambiguous: it has democratised publication while simultaneously making the ________, rather than the editor, the principal arbiter of what reaches the public.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*disintermediation*, *gatekeeping*, *algorithm* — *disintermediation* capta la eliminación del intermediario editorial; *gatekeeping* es el rol tradicional del editor como filtro; *algorithm* es el nuevo árbitro que sustituye al editor, con implicaciones democráticas ambiguas que el análisis reconoce sin simplificar.
</details>

**Ejercicio 9.** Escribe una frase que analice el *citizen journalism* reconociendo tanto su valor como su riesgo.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*While citizen journalism has expanded the range of voices and the speed of eyewitness reporting, it has also blurred the line between verified and unverified information, placing a heavier burden on the reader — and, arguably, on the professional outlets that amplify such content — to distinguish reportage from raw assertion.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 10.** Reescribe con léxico de crisis de confianza: *People don't trust the media anymore, and politicians call everything they don't like fake news.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The weaponisation of the term "fake news" as a catch-all charge against unfavourable coverage has compounded a pre-existing trust deficit, corroding the shared informational baseline on which public discourse depends and leaving readers to navigate an environment in which the very distinction between verified reporting and fabricated content is itself politically contested.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 11.** Completa con léxico de propiedad y concentración mediática: *The ________ of media ownership in the hands of a small number of conglomerates raises structural questions about ________, since the editorial line of a title cannot be wholly insulated from the commercial and political interests of its ________.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*concentration*, *pluralism*, *proprietor* — la concentración de propiedad amenaza el pluralismo mediático; el *proprietor* (propietario o magnate) es la figura cuyos intereses, por robusta que sea la separación formal, pueden influir en la línea editorial del medio que posee.
</details>

**Ejercicio 12.** Reescribe evitando la generalización sobre *the media* como bloque homogéneo: *The media is biased and bad.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*To speak of "the media" as a monolith obscures the considerable variation across titles, genres and ownership structures; the record is in fact mixed, with notable examples of rigorous investigative journalism running alongside documented instances of sensationalism and commercial pressure shaping coverage — a variation that any serious analysis must respect if it is to avoid the very simplification it would rightly criticise in the outlets under examination.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 13.** Escribe una frase que analice un formato digital (*live blog* o *newsletter*) con léxico específico.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The live blog, with its rolling updates and embedded multimedia, exemplifies the shift from the finished article to the continuously refreshed stream, a format that trades the polish of the printed page for the immediacy that the digital reader has come to expect — and that, in doing so, redefines the very unit of journalistic production from the discrete article to the ongoing thread.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 14.** Distingue *on the record*, *off the record* y *on background* en frases C1.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*On the record* significa que la información puede citarse con atribución nominal a la fuente. *Off the record* significa que la información no puede publicarse en absoluto, sirviendo solo para orientar al periodista. *On background* (o *not for attribution*) significa que la información puede publicarse pero sin identificar a la fuente nominalmente, citándola genéricamente (p. ej., *a senior official said*). Estas distinciones gobiernan la relación periodista-fuente y son esenciales para entender cómo se construyen las noticias a partir de fuentes que controlan su propia atribución.
</details>

**Ejercicio 15.** Reformula con un tono analítico que evite el sensacionalismo al describir el clickbait: *Clickbait is evil and destroys journalism.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The curiosity-gap headline, engineered to withhold just enough information to compel the click, exemplifies a logic in which the headline is optimised not to inform but to manufacture the impulse to consume — a logic that, when generalised across an outlet, systematically misaligns the incentives of the headline writer with the informational needs of the reader, however commercially rational it may appear at the level of the individual article.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 16.** Completa con léxico de la economía de la atención: *In an attention economy where ________ is the metric that monetisation follows, the pressure to produce ________, frictionless content exerts a subtle but persistent pull away from the long, demanding formats that investigative journalism requires.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*dwell time*, *sticky* — *dwell time* (tiempo de permanencia) es la métrica que sigue la monetización; *sticky* describe el contenido que retiene al lector. Juntos, captan cómo la presión por contenido pegajoso y fácil de consumir tira contra los formatos largos y exigentes del periodismo de calidad.
</details>

**Ejercicio 17.** Reescribe esta conclusión con cohesión y matiz: *Digital media changed everything. Editors don't decide anymore. Algorithms decide. This affects democracy.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*Ultimately, the migration to digital has not so much replaced the editorial function as relocated it: from the editor's desk to the algorithm's code, a shift that, however invisible to the reader, has profound implications for the kind of public conversation the media sustains — and, by extension, for the health of the democracy it is meant to inform.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 18.** Explica en 2-3 frases, en inglés C1, por qué la separación entre redacción y publicidad (*church-state separation*) es importante para la credibilidad de un medio.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The church-state separation between newsroom and advertising department matters because it insulates editorial decisions from commercial pressure, ensuring that coverage is determined by news value rather than by the interests of advertisers. When that separation erodes — as it does with the rise of native advertising, which blends promotional content into editorial formats — readers can no longer take for granted that what they are reading is reportage rather than paid persuasion, and the trust on which the outlet's long-term credibility depends begins to corrode.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 19.** Escribe un párrafo breve (4-5 frases) en inglés C1 que analice críticamente la transición de la prensa tradicional a la digital, usando al menos tres elementos del léxico de esta unidad (géneros, modelo económico, hipervínculos o economía de la atención).

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The migration of readership from print to digital platforms has upended the economic model of legacy media, whose reliance on advertising revenue has been eroded by the attention economy of social networks, in which dwell time rather than news value determines what reaches the public eye. Compounding this, the disintermediation wrought by those platforms has stripped legacy titles of their gatekeeping function, relocating editorial authority from the editor's desk to the algorithm's code — a shift whose democratic implications are ambiguous at best. To be sure, the digital transition has brought genuine gains: live blogs and interactive features have expanded the toolkit of journalism, and hyper reading has given the active reader unprecedented capacity to triangulate across sources. Yet the same transition has also incentivised clickbait and curiosity-gap headlines that optimise the title for the click rather than the reader for the information, a misalignment that, however rational at the level of the individual article, is corrosive when generalised across an outlet. Ultimately, then, the question is not whether digital journalism can match the editorial standards of the quality press — in scattered examples it already does — but whether the economic model that sustains those standards can survive a transition in which the metrics of success are increasingly decoupled from the metrics of journalistic quality.*
</details>

## Resumen de la unidad

En esta unidad hemos construido el andamiaje lingüístico para analizar la transformación de los medios con la precisión crítica que exige el nivel C1. Has aprendido a distinguir géneros mediáticos —*hard news*, *feature*, *op-ed*, *editorial*— y a situarlos en la tipología de la prensa tradicional, de *broadsheet* a *quality press*. Has incorporado el léxico de la crisis económica —*print circulation*, *paywall*, *native advertising*, *attention economy*— y el de las prácticas digitales —*live blog*, *data journalism*, *clickbait*, *curiosity gap*. Has explorado la lectura hipertextual y los hipervínculos sociales como redefinidores de la experiencia lectora, las redes sociales como ecosistema con incentivos algorítmicos y la crisis de confianza con su *weaponisation of fake news*. Has ejercitado la comparación crítica entre medios tradicionales y digitales, el análisis de la propiedad mediática y el tono analítico que evita tratar a *the media* como bloque homogéneo. La destreza central de esta unidad es la crítica precisa: la capacidad de juzgar los medios no con generalidades morales sino con un léxico técnico que revela las estructuras económicas, editoriales y tecnológicas que los configuran. Esa precisión crítica es la marca distintiva del hablante C1 cuando se enfrenta a uno de los terrenos donde el lenguaje y el poder informativo se entrelazan con más fuerza.
