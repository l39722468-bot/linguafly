La formación de palabras, o word formation, es el motor morfológico del inglés. A partir de una raíz, los afijos generan sustantivos, adjetivos, verbos y adverbios con significados precisos, y la conversión de categoría permite que una misma forma funcione en distintas clases gramaticales. A nivel C1, dominar este proceso no es un lujo sino una necesidad: la sección use of English del CAE lo evalúa directamente, y la escritura académica exige la precisión nominal que solo la word formation adecuada confiere. Esta unidad del Language Lab aísla el fenómeno y lo entrena en sus patrones más rentables.

Cada concepto se presenta en tres niveles. El A2 ofrece la versión más sencilla, el B2 añade complejidad operativa y el C1 presenta la formación de palabras con sus matices de precisión nominal y registro.

A2: We can make new words by adding small parts. "Happy" + "ness" = "happiness."

B2: Word formation creates new words through affixes and category conversion. Suffixes like -tion, -ment and -ness form nouns; prefixes like un- and in- form negatives. C1 mastery means generating the correct noun, adjective or verb from a root with precision.

C1: Word formation, the morphological engine of English, generates nominals, adjectivals, verbals and adverbials from a root via affixation and category conversion. At C1, its mastery is not optional but obligatory: the CAE use of English component assesses it directly, and academic writing demands the nominal precision that only calibrated word formation confers. This Language Lab unit isolates the phenomenon and drills its most discursively productive patterns to automaticity.

## Parte I — Teoría: Formación de palabras y precisión nominal

### 1. La arquitectura morfológica de la palabra

Una palabra inglesa se compone de raíz (root), prefijo y sufijo. La raíz porta el significado léxico; los afijos modifican la categoría o el significado. Understandable se descompone en under (prefijo) + stand (raíz) + able (sufijo). Comprender esta arquitectura permite analizar palabras desconocidas y generar las formas correctas a partir de una raíz dada, destreza central del C1.

A2: A word can have a main part and small parts before and after it.

B2: An English word comprises a root, a prefix and a suffix. The root carries the lexical meaning; affixes modify the category or meaning. Understandable decomposes into under + stand + able. Understanding this architecture lets you analyse unknown words and generate correct forms.

C1: The morphological architecture of an English word comprises a root, which ports the lexical semantics, together with prefixes and suffixes which modulate category or meaning. The item understandable decomposes into the prefix under, the root stand and the suffix able. Internalising this architecture enables the decomposition of unfamiliar lexemes and the generative production of the correct derivative from a given root, a competence central to C1 morphological agility.

### 2. Sufijos nominalizadores: -tion, -ment, -ness, -ity

Los sufijos nominalizadores transforman verbos o adjetivos en sustantivos. -tion/-sion (creation, decision), -ment (development, achievement), -ness (happiness, awareness), -ity (complexity, validity), -ance/-ence (importance, existence), -ship (relationship, leadership), -hood (childhood, likelihood), -al (refusal, proposal), -er/-or (researcher, director). Su dominio permite nominalizar con precisión.

A2: We can make nouns by adding endings like "-tion" or "-ness."

B2: Nominalising suffixes transform verbs or adjectives into nouns: -tion/-sion (creation, decision), -ment (development), -ness (happiness), -ity (complexity), -ance/-ence (importance), -ship (relationship), -hood (childhood), -al (refusal), -er/-or (researcher). Their mastery enables precise nominalisation.

C1: Nominalising suffixes effect the categorial transposition of verbs or adjectives into nouns: -tion/-sion (creation, decision), -ment (development, achievement), -ness (happiness, awareness), -ity (complexity, validity), -ance/-ence (importance, existence), -ship (relationship, leadership), -hood (childhood, likelihood), -al (refusal, proposal) and -er/-or (researcher, director). Their calibrated deployment enables the precise nominalisation that is a diagnostic of C1 academic prose, where nominal density outranks verbal proliferation.

### 3. Sufijos adjetivizadores: -able, -ous, -ive, -ful, -less

Los sufijos adjetivizadores forman adjetivos a partir de sustantivos o verbos. -able/-ible (reliable, visible), -ous (hazardous, courageous), -ive (effective, productive), -ful (meaningful, useful), -less (meaningless, useless), -al (cultural, accidental), -ic (strategic, economic), -y (risky, wealthy). La elección del sufijo correcto es convencional y debe memorizarse por raíz.

A2: We can make describing words with endings like "-able" or "-ful."

B2: Adjectivising suffixes form adjectives from nouns or verbs: -able/-ible (reliable, visible), -ous (hazardous), -ive (effective), -ful (meaningful), -less (meaningless), -al (cultural), -ic (strategic), -y (risky). The correct suffix is conventional and must be memorised by root.

C1: Adjectivising suffixes generate adjectives from nominal or verbal bases: -able/-ible (reliable, visible), -ous (hazardous, courageous), -ive (effective, productive), -ful (meaningful, useful), -less (meaningless, useless), -al (cultural, accidental), -ic (strategic, economic) and -y (risky, wealthy). The selection of the correct suffix is conventionally determined and must be memorised per root, since the distribution is idiosyncratic and not predictable from semantics, a memorisation burden intrinsic to C1 morphological mastery.

### 4. Sufijos verbales: -ise, -ate, -en, -ify

Los sufijos verbales forman verbos a partir de sustantivos o adjetivos. -ise/-ize (realise, emphasise), -ate (activate, formulate), -en (widen, strengthen), -ify (simplify, justify). La diferencia entre -ise (británico) e -ize (americano) es ortográfica pero convencional. Estos sufijos son rentables en la prosa académica, donde la verbalización densifica la expresión.

A2: We can make verbs with endings like "-ise" or "-en."

B2: Verbal suffixes form verbs from nouns or adjectives: -ise/-ize (realise, emphasise), -ate (activate, formulate), -en (widen, strengthen), -ify (simplify, justify). The difference between -ise (British) and -ize (American) is orthographic but conventional. These suffixes are useful in academic prose.

C1: Verbal suffixes generate verbs from nominal or adjectival bases: -ise/-ize (realise, emphasise), -ate (activate, formulate), -en (widen, strengthen) and -ify (simplify, justify). The alternation between -ise (British) and -ize (American) is orthographically conventional rather than semantically motivated. These suffixes are discursively productive in academic prose, where verbalisation contributes to the lexical density that characterises the C1 register.

### 5. Prefijos negativos: un-, in-, dis-, non-, mis-

Los prefijos negativos invierten o matizan el significado. un- (unhappy, unlikely), in-/im-/il-/ir- (invisible, impossible, illegal, irregular), dis- (disagree, dissatisfied), non- (non-negotiable, non-existent), mis- (misunderstand, misrepresent). La distribución alomórfica de in- (im- ante p/b, il- ante l, ir- ante r) es convencional y debe respetarse.

A2: We can make words negative with beginnings like "un-" or "dis-."

B2: Negative prefixes invert or nuance meaning: un- (unhappy), in-/im-/il-/ir- (invisible, impossible, illegal, irregular), dis- (disagree), non- (non-negotiable), mis- (misunderstand). The allomorphic distribution of in- (im- before p/b, il- before l, ir- before r) is conventional and must be respected.

C1: Negative prefixes effect semantic inversion or attenuation: un- (unhappy, unlikely), in-/im-/il-/ir- (invisible, impossible, illegal, irregular), dis- (disagree, dissatisfied), non- (non-negotiable, non-existent) and mis- (misunderstand, misrepresent). The allomorphic distribution of the in- allomorph, im- before bilabial onsets, il- before lateral onsets and ir- before rhotic onsets, is phonologically conditioned yet conventionally fixed, and its violation, such as irpossible for impossible, constitutes an orthographic error diagnostically incompatible with C1 competence.

### 6. Prefijos de grado e intensidad: over-, under-, out-, sub-

Los prefijos de grado matizan la intensidad o el exceso. over- (overestimate, overcomplicated), under- (underestimate, underdeveloped), out- (outperform, outnumber), sub- (substandard, subcommittee), super- (superhuman, supervise). Su uso en prosa académica permite expresar matices cuantitativos con precisión y concisión.

A2: Some beginnings change how much something is: "over-" means too much, "under-" means too little.

B2: Degree prefixes nuance intensity or excess: over- (overestimate), under- (underestimate), out- (outperform), sub- (substandard), super- (superhuman). Their use in academic prose expresses quantitative nuances with precision and concision.

C1: Degree prefixes modulate quantitative or intensive magnitude: over- (overestimate, overcomplicated), under- (underestimate, underdeveloped), out- (outperform, outnumber), sub- (substandard, subcommittee) and super- (superhuman, supervise). Their deployment in academic prose enables the concise expression of quantitative nuances that would otherwise require periphrastic formulation, a concision that is itself a marker of C1 lexical economy.

### 7. Prefijos de actitud y postura: pro-, anti-, counter-, co-

Los prefijos de actitud expresan postura o alineación. pro- (pro-European, proactive), anti- (anti-war, antisocial), counter- (counter-argument, counterproductive), co- (coexist, cooperate), mis- (misaligned), pre- (predetermine). En la argumentación académica, estos prefijos compactan relaciones de postura que de otro modo requerirían circunloquios.

A2: Some beginnings show what side you are on: "pro-" means for, "anti-" means against.

B2: Attitude prefixes express stance or alignment: pro- (pro-European), anti- (anti-war), counter- (counter-argument), co- (coexist), pre- (predetermine). In academic argumentation, these prefixes compact stance relations that would otherwise need circumlocution.

C1: Attitudinal prefixes encode stance or alignment: pro- (pro-European, proactive), anti- (anti-war, antisocial), counter- (counter-argument, counterproductive), co- (coexist, cooperate) and pre- (predetermine, precondition). In academic argumentation, these prefixes compactly encode relational stances that would otherwise demand periphrastic exposition, a lexical economy that contributes to the informational density diagnostic of C1 scholarly register.

### 8. Conversión de categoría: la productividad del inglés

La conversión consiste en cambiar la categoría gramatical de una palabra sin alterar su forma. to google (sustantivo a verbo), a run (verbo a sustantivo), to clean (adjetivo a verbo), a must (verbo modal a sustantivo). Es muy productiva en inglés y una marca de fluidez idiomática en C1, donde permite expresar ideas con economía.

A2: Sometimes we use the same word as a noun or a verb, like "a run" and "to run."

B2: Conversion changes a word's grammatical category without altering its form: to google (noun to verb), a run (verb to noun), to clean (adjective to verb), a must (modal to noun). It is very productive in English and a marker of idiomatic fluency at C1.

C1: Conversion effects categorial transposition without morphological alteration: to google instantiates noun-to-verb conversion, a run instantiates verb-to-noun conversion, to clean instantiates adjective-to-verb conversion, and a must instantiates modal-to-noun conversion. Its productivity in English is exceptionally high, and its idiomatic deployment constitutes a marker of C1 fluency, enabling the economical expression of ideas that a more morphologically conservative language would require periphrastic means to convey.

### 9. Nominalización en prosa académica: densidad informativa

La nominalización, la conversión de verbos o cláusulas en sustantivos, es un rasgo distintivo de la prosa académica inglesa. The decision was made to revise the policy se densifica en the policy revision decision. The fact that the data were analysed carefully se densifica en the careful analysis of the data. Esta densidad nominal es lo que distingue el registro académico C1 del coloquial.

A2: In academic writing, we can use nouns instead of long phrases to be shorter.

B2: Nominalisation, converting verbs or clauses into nouns, is a distinctive feature of academic English. The decision was made to revise the policy becomes the policy revision decision. The fact that the data were analysed carefully becomes the careful analysis of the data. This nominal density distinguishes C1 academic register from colloquial.

C1: Nominalisation, the conversion of verbal or clausal constructions into nominal counterparts, constitutes a diacritic feature of English academic prose. The clausal the decision was made to revise the policy condenses into the nominal the policy revision decision, and the clausal the fact that the data were analysed carefully condenses into the careful analysis of the data. This nominal density is what distinguishes the C1 academic register from the colloquial, and its calibrated deployment is a reliable index of scholarly registeral control.

### 10. Compuestos: noun + noun y adjetivo + noun

La formación de compuestos, dos palabras unidas para formar una nueva, es muy productiva en inglés. Noun + noun (decision-making process, research findings, policy framework), adjetivo + noun (long-term effect, high-quality evidence). Los compuestos densifican la expresión y son característicos de la prosa académica C1, donde aparecen con frecuencia diagnóstica.

A2: We can join two words to make a new one, like "research findings."

B2: Compounding, joining two words to form a new one, is very productive in English. Noun + noun (decision-making process, research findings, policy framework), adjective + noun (long-term effect, high-quality evidence). Compounds densify expression and are characteristic of C1 academic prose.

C1: Compounding, the lexical fusion of two bases into a novel unit, is exceptionally productive in English. Noun-plus-noun compounding (decision-making process, research findings, policy framework) and adjective-plus-noun compounding (long-term effect, high-quality evidence) densify expression and are diacritically characteristic of C1 academic prose, where their diagnostic frequency furnishes a reliable index of registeral elevation and lexical sophistication.

### 11. Errores frecuentes en word formation: sufijos espurios

Los errores más comunes incluyen sufijos espurios: importance no importancy, development no developement, happiness no happyness, reliable no relyable. La ortografía de los sufijos tiene irregularidades (y se convierte en i ante -ness: happy → happiness) que deben memorizarse. El C1 no puede permitirse estos errores en escritura formal.

A2: Some word endings are spelled in special ways. "Happy" becomes "happiness," not "happyness."

B2: Common errors include spurious suffixes: importance not importancy, development not developement, happiness not happyness, reliable not relyable. Suffix spelling has irregularities (y becomes i before -ness: happy → happiness) that must be memorised. C1 cannot afford these errors in formal writing.

C1: The most prevalent word-formation errors involve spurious suffixal spellings: importance, not importancy, development, not developement, happiness, not happyness, and reliable, not relyable. Suffixal orthography harbours idiosyncratic irregularities, such as the y-to-i mutation ante -ness (happy → happiness), whose memorisation is non-negotiable. The commission of such errors in formal writing is diagnostically incompatible with C1 certification, their persistence signalling a morphological control beneath the target threshold.

### 12. Word formation en el CAE: la sección use of English

La sección use of English del CAE incluye ejercicios de word formation donde se da una raíz y se debe generar la forma correcta según el contexto. La estrategia exige analizar la categoría requerida por el hueco (sustantivo, adjetivo, adverbio, verbo), aplicar el sufijo o prefijo adecuado y verificar la ortografía. Practicar con cronómetro es esencial.

A2: In the CAE exam, you get a word and change it to fit the sentence.

B2: The CAE use of English includes word formation exercises where a root is given and the correct form must be generated. The strategy requires analysing the category needed by the gap (noun, adjective, adverb, verb), applying the correct suffix or prefix and checking spelling. Timed practice is essential.

C1: The CAE use of English component incorporates word-formation items in which a root is supplied and the contextually correct derivative must be generated. The strategic protocol demands the analysis of the categorial slot required by the gap, nominal, adjectival, adverbial or verbal, the application of the diacritically correct affix and the verification of orthographic accuracy. Timed practice is indispensable, since the examination rewards both accuracy and velocity, and the candidate must internalise the derivational pathways to automaticity.

### 13. Adverbios en -ly y sus irregularidades

La mayoría de los adverbios se forman con -ly: carefully, effectively, significantly. Pero hay irregularidades: los adjetivos terminados en -y cambian a -ily (happy → happily), los terminados en -ble cambian a -bly (probable → probably), y algunos adjetivos no admiten -ly (fast, hard, early son ya adverbios). Conocer estas reglas evita errores ortográficos.

A2: Many adverbs end in "-ly," like "carefully."

B2: Most adverbs are formed with -ly: carefully, effectively. But there are irregularities: adjectives ending in -y change to -ily (happy → happily), those ending in -ble change to -bly (probable → probably), and some adjectives do not take -ly (fast, hard, early are already adverbs).

C1: The majority of English adverbs are -ly derivatives: carefully, effectively, significantly. The formation, however, harbours systematic irregularities: adjectival bases terminating in -y undergo y-to-i mutation ante the suffix (happy → happily), those terminating in -ble contract to -bly (probable → probably), and a delimited class of adjectives, fast, hard and early, function adverbially without morphological marking. Internalising these rules precludes the orthographic errors whose commission would attenuate the impression of C1 morphological control.

### 14. Falsos amigos morfológicos: sufijos que engañan

Ciertos sufijos generan falsos amigos morfológicos. -ly no siempre forma adverbios: lonely es adjetivo, likely es adjetivo o adverbio. -ful no siempre significa lleno de: dreadful significa terrible, no lleno de terror. -ish puede indicar aproximación (greenish) o actitud (childish). El C1 debe reconocer estos matices para no interpretar literalmente.

A2: Sometimes word endings do not mean what you think. "Lonely" is not an adverb.

B2: Some suffixes generate morphological false friends. -ly does not always form adverbs: lonely is an adjective, likely is an adjective or adverb. -ful does not always mean full of: dreadful means terrible. -ish can indicate approximation (greenish) or attitude (childish). C1 must recognise these nuances.

C1: A subset of suffixes generates morphological false friends whose literal interpretation would produce semantic distortion. The -ly suffix does not invariably derive adverbs: lonely is adjectival and likely is ambiguously adjectival or adverbial. The -ful suffix does not invariably encode repletion: dreadful denotes terribleness rather than fullness of dread. The -ish suffix ambigates between approximation (greenish) and attitudinal characterisation (childish). The C1 user must recognise these non-compositional nuances to preclude literal misinterpretation.

### 15. Estrategias de consolidación de la word formation

La consolidación exige practicar la derivación sistemática: partir de una raíz y generar todas las formas posibles (noun, adjective, verb, adverb), crear tarjetas con las familias de palabras y practicar con ejercicios tipo CAE cronometrados. La lectura abundante de prosa académica, donde las formas derivadas aparecen en contexto, es insustituible. La atención a la ortografía de los sufijos es crucial.

A2: To learn word formation, practice making different words from one root.

B2: Consolidation requires systematic derivation practice: starting from a root, generate all possible forms (noun, adjective, verb, adverb), create cards with word families, and practise with timed CAE-style exercises. Abundant reading of academic prose is irreplaceable. Attention to suffix spelling is crucial.

C1: The consolidation of word formation necessitates systematic derivational practice: commencing from a root, the learner generates all categorially admissible derivatives, nominal, adjectival, verbal and adverbial, constructs flashcards encoding word families and practises with timed CAE-prototype exercises. Abundant immersion in academic prose, wherein derived forms arise in natural context, remains the irreplaceable substrate of acquisition. Scrupulous attention to suffixal orthography is pivotal, since orthographic error is the most prevalent manifestation of incomplete morphological automation at the C1 threshold.

En esta unidad del Language Lab has entrenado la formación de palabras, el motor morfológico del inglés C1.
