## Parte II — Ejercicios con soluciones

### Ejercicio 1 — Secuencia básica

Elige **past simple**, **past continuous** o **past perfect**.

1. The curator ___ (open) the storage room and called the conservator.
2. The technicians ___ (install) the lights when the fire alarm went off.
3. The museum discovered that someone ___ (move) the sculpture.
4. The critic ___ (take) notes while the orchestra was playing.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **opened** — evento principal en secuencia.
2. **were installing** — actividad en progreso interrumpida.
3. **had moved** — acción anterior al descubrimiento.
4. **was taking** — actividad simultánea en desarrollo.

</details>

### Ejercicio 2 — Past perfect continuous

Completa con **had been + -ing**.

1. By the time the exhibition opened, the curators ___ (work) on the labels for weeks.
2. The extras ___ (stand) in the rain for nearly an hour when the director shouted “Cut!”.
3. The biennale ___ (post) record attendance figures until travel restrictions affected travel.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **had been working**.
2. **had been standing**.
3. **had been posting**.

En cada frase, la actividad duraba hasta otro momento pasado.

</details>

### Ejercicio 3 — Simple frente a continuo

Explica el matiz.

1. *The team had restored three paintings.*
2. *The team had been restoring paintings throughout the winter.*
3. *The director had edited the final scene.*
4. *The director had been editing the final scene for several days.*

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. Destaca resultado y cantidad.
2. Destaca la actividad prolongada.
3. Presenta la edición como completada.
4. Presenta el proceso y su duración.

</details>

### Ejercicio 4 — Interrupciones

Completa usando past simple o past continuous.

1. The musicians ___ (rehearse) when the lights ___ (fail).
2. While the dancers ___ (perform), a camera ___ (fall).
3. The doors ___ (open) after the audience ___ (take) their seats.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **were rehearsing; failed**.
2. **were performing; fell**.
3. **opened; had taken** o **opened; took**, según cuánto quieras enfatizar la anterioridad. Con *after*, la secuencia ya es clara.

</details>

### Ejercicio 5 — Historic present

Transforma el resumen al historic present.

*The protagonist entered the theatre, noticed the empty seats and found a hidden letter.*

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

**The protagonist enters the theatre, notices the empty seats and finds a hidden letter.**

El presente histórico da viveza al resumen de la trama. La fecha de estreno puede seguir en pasado: *The film was released in 1998; the protagonist enters...*.

</details>

### Ejercicio 6 — Was about to

Completa.

1. The trustees ___ ___ ___ announce the shortlist when the leak went viral.
2. The actor ___ ___ ___ leave the stage when the audience began applauding.
3. The museum ___ ___ ___ reopen in May. (*due to*)

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **were about to**.
2. **was about to**.
3. **was due to**.

</details>

### Ejercicio 7 — Nominalisation

Convierte la palabra entre paréntesis en un sustantivo.

1. The committee’s ___ (decide) changed the programme.
2. The ___ (restore) of the theatre took two years.
3. The critic’s ___ (interpret) divided the audience.
4. The artist’s ___ (innovate) influenced younger designers.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **decision**.
2. **restoration**.
3. **interpretation**.
4. **innovation**.

</details>

### Ejercicio 8 — Colocaciones

Elige el verbo correcto: **mount, curate, preserve, restore, attract**.

1. ___ an exhibition
2. ___ a collection
3. ___ cultural heritage
4. ___ a painting
5. ___ a diverse audience

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **mount an exhibition**.
2. **curate a collection**.
3. **preserve cultural heritage**.
4. **restore a painting**.
5. **attract a diverse audience**.

</details>

### Ejercicio 9 — Reading

Lee y responde.

> By the time the new gallery opened, the curators had been negotiating loans with museums across Europe for almost a year. Several works had arrived earlier, but the central installation had not been completed because one component was still in transit. While technicians were assembling the lighting system, the artist revised the arrangement of the final room. The result was an exhibition that appeared spontaneous, although its apparent simplicity depended on months of planning. In her review, the critic describes how the installation transforms ordinary materials into a meditation on memory.

1. How long had the curators been negotiating?
2. Why had the central installation not been completed?
3. What were the technicians doing when the artist revised the room?
4. Why did the exhibition appear spontaneous?
5. What does the installation transform?

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **For almost a year.**
2. **Because one component was still in transit.**
3. **They were assembling the lighting system.**
4. **Because its apparent simplicity hid months of planning.**
5. **It transforms ordinary materials into a meditation on memory.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 10 — Listening guiado

Guion: “When I arrived at the cinema, the audience was already taking their seats. The director had given an introduction, and the projectionist was about to start the film when the sound system failed. The technicians had been testing it all afternoon, but they had not identified the fault.”

Identifica cada forma verbal y su función.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

**Was taking** presenta una actividad en curso; **had given** muestra una acción anterior; **was about to start** expresa inminencia; **had been testing** destaca duración anterior; **had not identified** presenta un resultado no conseguido antes del fallo.

</details>

### Ejercicio 11 — Reescritura formal

Combina las frases mediante nominalisation.

*The museum decided to expand the exhibition. Attendance had increased significantly.*

<details><summary>Modelo</summary>

**The museum’s decision to expand the exhibition followed a significant increase in attendance.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 12 — Evita una cadena pesada

Haz más clara esta frase:

*The implementation of the renovation of the entrance caused the disruption of the movement of visitors.*

<details><summary>Modelo</summary>

**Renovating the entrance disrupted visitor movement.** También es posible: **The renovation of the entrance disrupted the movement of visitors.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 13 — Corrección de errores

Corrige el párrafo.

> By the time the concert began, the orchestra rehearsed for three hours. The conductor had been arrived late, and the musicians were waited impatiently. The hall’s renovation had caused a significant improve in acoustics.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

**By the time the concert began, the orchestra had been rehearsing for three hours. The conductor had arrived late, and the musicians were waiting impatiently. The hall’s renovation had caused a significant improvement in acoustics.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 14 — Matiz narrativo

Elige la opción que presenta mejor el fondo de la escena: *The audience waited / was waiting when the doors opened.*

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

**The audience was waiting when the doors opened.** El continuo presenta la situación en desarrollo y el past simple introduce el evento que la modifica.

</details>

### Ejercicio 15 — Registro académico

Reescribe con una nominalisation y una colocación cultural: *The artist preserved old techniques and this influenced young designers.*

<details><summary>Modelo</summary>

**The artist’s preservation of traditional techniques influenced a new generation of designers.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 16 — Writing

Escribe 140–170 palabras sobre esta pregunta: **How can museums make cultural heritage relevant to younger audiences?** Incluye dos narrative tenses, dos nominalisations y tres colocaciones de arte.

<details><summary>Modelo</summary>

Museums can make cultural heritage more relevant by connecting preservation with active participation. When one city museum reopened its main gallery, the curators had been developing digital resources for several months. Their decision to combine historical objects with interactive explanations attracted a younger audience without reducing the significance of the collection. While visitors were exploring the exhibition, they could compare traditional techniques with contemporary design. This juxtaposition encouraged discussion rather than passive observation. The museum’s restoration of the building also created an opportunity to explain how conservation works in practice. By the end of the first season, the institution had attracted several school groups and had received positive feedback from teachers. These results suggest that cultural heritage becomes more accessible when museums curate meaningful experiences, preserve the original context and invite visitors to interpret what they see. Digital tools are useful, but the quality of the explanation remains more important than novelty.

</details>

### Ejercicio 17 — Speaking

Habla durante un minuto sobre una obra, película o concierto. Usa past perfect, past perfect continuous, historic present y una nominalisation.

<details><summary>Pista y modelo</summary>

**Before I saw the film, I had read several reviews. The director had been developing the project for years, and that preparation was visible in the final result. In the opening scene, the protagonist enters an empty station and discovers a forgotten suitcase. The film’s use of silence creates a strong sense of anticipation.**

</details>

### Ejercicio 18 — Mini-test final

Completa el texto.

> By the time the festival opened, the organisers ___ (work) on the programme for months. Several performers ___ (arrive) already, but the headline act ___ (be) about to cancel when the technical team solved the problem. The festival’s ___ (expand) attracted a wider audience, and a later ___ (analyse) showed that younger visitors had valued the workshops.

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

**had been working; had already arrived; was about to; expansion; analysis.** La primera forma muestra duración anterior; la segunda, anterioridad y resultado; la tercera, inminencia pasada; las dos últimas son nominalisations.

</details>

### Ejercicio 19 — Contraste de perspectiva

Completa cada par con el tiempo que mejor expresa la intención indicada.

1. Resultado: *The theatre ___ (restore) three times before the festival opened.*
2. Proceso: *The theatre ___ (restore) for three years before the festival opened.*
3. Evento principal: *The critic ___ (enter) the gallery and immediately recognised the artist’s style.*
4. Fondo: *The critic ___ (walk) through the gallery when she noticed the missing label.*
5. Anterioridad: *The curator discovered that the label ___ (contain) an inaccurate date.*
6. Acción inminente: *The trustees ___ (about / announce) the shortlist when the leak appeared.*

<details><summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **had restored** — importa el número de restauraciones completadas.
2. **had been restoring** — importa la duración del proceso.
3. **entered** — es el evento principal de la secuencia.
4. **was walking** — la actividad estaba en curso cuando ocurrió el descubrimiento.
5. **had contained** — el contenido de la etiqueta precede al descubrimiento, aunque en algunos contextos el past simple también sería posible.
6. **were about to announce** — la decisión era inminente y fue interrumpida.

La elección del aspecto crea una perspectiva distinta incluso cuando el referente es el mismo. El simple cuantifica o avanza la historia; el continuo muestra actividad; el perfecto ordena antecedentes.

</details>

### Ejercicio 20 — Edición de una reseña

Reescribe este párrafo para que funcione como una reseña cultural coherente. Usa historic present para resumir la película, past simple para la información de producción, una nominalisation y un past perfect para el antecedente.

> The film released in 2004. The director had rejected the first script. In the opening scene, the protagonist entered a station and she found a suitcase. The film used silence very effectively. This created a feeling of suspense.

<details><summary>Modelo</summary>

**The film was released in 2004, after the director had rejected the first script. In the opening scene, the protagonist enters a deserted station and finds a suitcase. The film’s use of silence creates a powerful sense of suspense, while the decision to delay the explanation of the suitcase keeps the viewer engaged.**

La frase inicial establece el contexto en pasado. El resumen de la trama cambia al historic present: *enters* y *finds*. **The film’s use of silence** y **the decision to delay** son nominalisations que condensan las acciones sin perder claridad.

</details>

### Ejercicio 21 — Respuesta razonada

Explica en 60–80 palabras por qué esta frase resulta más informativa que su versión corta:

*By the time the exhibition opened, the curators had been negotiating loans for almost a year.*

Versión corta: *The exhibition opened. The curators negotiated loans.*

<details><summary>Modelo</summary>

La primera frase establece un punto de referencia claro con *by the time*, muestra que las negociaciones empezaron antes y expresa su duración con *had been negotiating*. La versión corta presenta dos hechos, pero no indica si las negociaciones continuaron hasta la apertura ni cuánto esfuerzo exigieron. El past perfect continuous conecta proceso, duración y resultado narrativo en una sola estructura.

</details>

## Resumen de la unidad

Para narrar con precisión, usa **past simple** para los eventos principales, **past continuous** para el fondo o una acción interrumpida, **past perfect** para lo anterior a otro momento pasado y **past perfect continuous** para una actividad que llevaba tiempo desarrollándose. Usa **historic present** cuando resumes una obra con intención de viveza y **was about to** para acciones inminentes en el pasado.

La nominalisation te permite convertir acciones en conceptos y construir un registro más compacto: *the decision to expand*, *the restoration of the theatre*, *the interpretation of the work*. Pero la densidad no es un objetivo en sí mismo. Alterna sustantivos y verbos para que el lector entienda con rapidez quién hizo qué y cuándo.

La pregunta final siempre es la misma: ¿quieres contar una acción, mostrar su duración, ordenar sus antecedentes o analizarla como concepto? Cuando eliges el tiempo y la forma nominal en función de esa intención, tu inglés gana precisión, ritmo y verdadero control C1.
