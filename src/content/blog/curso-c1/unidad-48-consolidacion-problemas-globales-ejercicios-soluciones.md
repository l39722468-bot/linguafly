## Parte II — Ejercicios

**Ejercicio 1.** Reformula la siguiente generalización vaga en una afirmación de síntesis matizada y anclada en evidencia: *Everything is connected and it's all getting worse.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The available evidence suggests that climate stress, migration pressure and institutional fragility interact in ways that, while not deterministic, meaningfully elevate the risk of cascading crises in already vulnerable regions — a systemic dynamic that no single-lens analysis can adequately capture.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 2.** Escribe una frase que plantee un problema a través de dos lentes distintas (legal y económica) usando la técnica de lentes múltiples.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*From a legal standpoint, the question of climate-induced displacement turns on the absence of refugee status for those fleeing environmental collapse; from an economic standpoint, it turns on the distribution of costs between host communities and the international community — two lenses that, rather than competing, together expose the inadequacy of any framework that addresses one dimension while neglecting the other.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 3.** Completa con léxico de interconexión: *Inequality ________ political instability, which in turn undermines institutional capacity, which further ________ inequality — a ________ cycle in which cause and effect become difficult to ________.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*compounds* (o *exacerbates*), *entrenches* (o *reinforces*), *vicious*, *disentangle* — *compound* y *entrench* son verbos precisos de interconexión negativa; *vicious cycle* capta la retroalimentación autodestructiva; *disentangle* reconoce la dificultad analítica de separar causa y efecto en sistemas complejos.
</details>

**Ejercicio 4.** Reescribe usando un conector de síntesis (*taken together* / *viewed holistically*): *The evidence from climate science, migration studies and conflict research shows that environmental, demographic and political stresses reinforce each other.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*Taken together, the evidence from climate science, migration studies and conflict research points to a system in which environmental, demographic and political stresses are mutually reinforcing — a picture that no single discipline can render in full and that therefore demands integrated, multi-causal reasoning.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 5.** Reescribe la afirmación categórica con causalidad compleja y multicausal: *Climate change causes conflict.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*While climate stress is a contributing factor in conflict, its effect is mediated by political vulnerability and economic precarity, such that the same environmental shock can produce open violence in one context and manageable adjustment in another — a contingency that flat, mono-causal claims of "climate causes conflict" cannot capture.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 6.** Completa con léxico de gobernanza global: *The climate regime exemplifies a ________ problem: each state's incentive to ________ on others' efforts undermines the cooperation needed to avert a ________ that would harm all.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*collective action*, *free-ride*, *tragedy of the commons* — el problema de acción colectiva, el incentivo de free-rider y la tragedia de los comunes son los tres conceptos que capturan el dilema estructural de la cooperación climática internacional.
</details>

**Ejercicio 7.** Escribe una frase que integre evidencia cuantitativa y cualitativa sobre pobreza, mostrando sus limitaciones respectivas.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*While the aggregate figures document a decline in extreme poverty, the qualitative accounts from specific communities reveal a more uneven picture, in which national-average statistics obscure localised reversals and the erosion of livelihoods that those very statistics are not calibrated to capture.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 8.** Reescribe con una concesión encadenada (*while it is true that..., it is equally true that...*): *Poverty has gone down, but inequality has gone up.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*While it is true that global poverty has declined, it is equally true that within-country inequality has widened; and even as we celebrate the aggregate gains, we must recognise that the distribution of those gains has been uneven enough to call into question the sustainability of the very model that produced them.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 9.** Critica el marco de la *securitisation* de la migración en una frase C1.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*Securitising migration — framing it primarily as a security threat — both distorts the phenomenon by downplaying its economic and humanitarian dimensions and risks producing policy responses that compound the very instability they purport to address, a framing whose costs may well outweigh its putative benefits.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 10.** Completa con léxico de marcos analíticos: *A ________ approach would foreground the claims of the displaced, whereas a ________ analysis might privilege the fiscal impact on host communities — a divergence that itself reveals the ________ nature of the choice between frameworks.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*rights-based*, *cost-benefit*, *value-laden* — el enfoque basado en derechos pone a las personas en el centro; el análisis coste-beneficio pondera impactos fiscales; y *value-laden* reconoce que la elección entre marcos no es técnica sino ética.
</details>

**Ejercicio 11.** Reescribe con un tono equilibrado que evite tanto el catastrofismo como el optimismo ingenuo: *Global cooperation is a total failure / Global cooperation is working great.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The record of global cooperation is neither the unmitigated failure that its critics describe nor the steady progress that its cheerleaders claim, but rather a more complex story of incremental gains in some domains running parallel to deepening crises in others — a mixed picture that calls for neither despair nor complacency but for sustained, clear-eyed engagement.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 12.** Escribe una frase que use la metáfora sistémica (*house of cards* o *web*) y reconozca sus límites.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The metaphor of a "house of cards" captures the fragility of interdependent global systems, yet it can also overstate the inevitability of collapse and understate the resilience that adaptive institutions can provide — a caveat that should temper any systemic reading with attention to agency and contingency.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 13.** Reformula con vocabulario del nexo clima-migración-conflicto: *Climate change makes people move and sometimes fight.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*Framing climate change as a "threat multiplier" rather than a direct cause captures the way environmental stress interacts with pre-existing political and economic vulnerabilities to tip fragile situations towards displacement and, in some cases, conflict — a formulation that avoids both climate determinism and the dismissal of climate as irrelevant.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 14.** Completa con léxico de marcos y crítica de reduccionismo: *________ accounts that attribute conflict solely to ethnicity tend to overlook the economic and institutional ________ that shape whether ethnic divisions become violent, thereby producing explanations that are analytically ________.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*Mono-causal* (o *Reductionist*), *mediators* (o *factors*), *incomplete* (o *inadequate*) — *mono-causal* critica el reduccionismo; *mediators* reconoce las variables intermedias; *incomplete* evalúa la explicación resultante como insuficiente para capturar la complejidad real del fenómeno.
</details>

**Ejercicio 15.** Escribe una frase de conclusión integradora que use un conector de síntesis y reconozca la interconexión estructural.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*Ultimately, what emerges from synthesising these strands is not a single policy prescription but a recognition that the challenges are structurally interconnected, and that responses calibrated to one domain in isolation will, however well-intentioned, tend to generate unintended consequences in others — a finding that, sobering as it is, at least clarifies the shape of the integrated reasoning that the scale of the problem demands.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 16.** Reescribe con hedging y anclaje en evidencia: *Globalisation destroys local cultures.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*While evidence from several case studies suggests that globalisation can contribute to the erosion of local cultural practices, the relationship appears to be mediated by domestic policy and community agency, such that the same exposure to global flows can produce cultural homogenisation in one context and a flourishing hybridisation in another — a contingency that sweeping claims of cultural destruction fail to capture.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 17.** Distingue *necessary but not sufficient condition* de *contributing factor* en una frase sobre desigualdad y conflicto.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*Whereas inequality is a contributing factor in conflict — elevating the risk without guaranteeing the outcome — the presence of weak institutional mediation appears to be a necessary but not sufficient condition for that risk to translate into actual violence, a distinction that matters for any policy aimed at interrupting the chain.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 18.** Explica en 2-3 frases, en inglés C1, por qué la síntesis interdisciplinar es más adecuada que el análisis monocausal para los problemas globales.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*The defining feature of global problems is their interconnection: climate stress feeds migration, migration strains institutions, institutional fragility enables conflict, and conflict destroys development — a cascade in which each node is both cause and effect. Any analysis that isolates a single variable will therefore not merely be incomplete but actively misleading, since it will miss the feedback loops that drive the system. Only an interdisciplinary synthesis that tracks these interactions can produce diagnoses faithful to the structure of the problem and, by extension, responses proportionate to its complexity.*
</details>

**Ejercicio 19.** Escribe un párrafo breve (4-5 frases) en inglés C1 que sintetice las interconexiones entre cambio climático, migración y derechos humanos, usando al menos dos conectores de síntesis y al menos un elemento del léxico de interconexión.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

*Viewed holistically, the relationship between climate change, migration and human rights illustrates a compounding dynamic in which environmental stress displaces populations whose movement, in the absence of adequate legal protection, exposes them to rights violations that existing frameworks — calibrated to persecution, not to ecological collapse — are ill-equipped to address. Taken together, these strands reveal not three separate crises but a single interlocking one, in which a gap in any node cascades through the others. Compounding this, the institutional architecture remains largely siloed: climate policy, refugee law and development assistance are designed and administered as if the phenomena they address were unconnected, which they manifestly are not. To be sure, integrated responses are harder to design and politically more demanding than sectoral ones; yet the cost of persisting in analytical isolation is, by every available measure, far higher than the cost of attempting the synthesis that the structure of the problem so evidently demands.*
</details>

## Resumen de la unidad

En esta unidad de consolidación hemos forjado la destreza transversal más exigente del nivel C1: la síntesis interdisciplinar. Has aprendido a moverte entre lentes analíticas —legal, económica, política, ética— sin perder el hilo argumentativo, y a integrarlas en un discurso que reconoce las interconexiones sin reducirlas a explicaciones monocausales. Has incorporado el léxico de interconexión y retroalimentación —*compounds*, *catalyst*, *vicious cycle*, *feedback loop*, *threat multiplier*— y los conectores de síntesis —*taken together*, *viewed holistically*, *in the round*— que elevan el análisis del nivel parcial al integrador. Has ejercitado la técnica de lentes múltiples, las concesiones encadenadas, la crítica de marcos reduccionistas como la *securitisation*, y la integración de evidencia cuantitativa y cualitativa. Has reflexionado sobre la causalidad compleja, los límites de la metáfora sistémica y el tono equilibrado que evita tanto el catastrofismo como el optimismo ingenuo. La destreza central de esta unidad es la integración madura: la capacidad de mostrar cómo los problemas globales se tejen entre sí, cómo las intervenciones aisladas generan consecuencias no intencidas y cómo el análisis responsable debe navegar esa complejidad sin renunciar al juicio. Esa elegancia sintética es la culminación del bloque temático y la marca distintiva del hablante C1 cuando se enfrenta a los grandes retos que definen nuestro tiempo.
