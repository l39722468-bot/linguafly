## Lesson 1 — Grammar

**Goal:** choose **me llamo** vs **soy**, conjugate **soy / eres** only, and reject English calques (*Soy llamado*, *Soy 25*, compulsory capital *Yo*).

### Exercises 1–5 — Choose the correct form

1. Hola, ___ Ana.  
   a) me llamo b) soy llamado c) mi llamo

2. ¿___ Pablo?  
   a) Eres b) Soy c) Llamo

3. — ¿Cómo te llamas? — ___ Marta.  
   a) Soy llamado b) Yo Soy c) Me llamo

4. No ___ Carlos. Me llamo Pedro.  
   a) eres b) soy c) llamo

5. — Mucho gusto. — ___.  
   a) Igualmente b) Good night c) Soy 25

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

1. **a) me llamo** — This is the name formula. × *Soy llamado* copies English *I am called*. × *Mi llamo* mixes the possessive *mi* into the chunk.  
2. **a) Eres** — The question is about *tú*. **Eres** is *ser* for *you*. **Soy** would mean *I am Pablo*, which is the wrong person. **Llamo** without *me / te* is not the A1 chunk.  
3. **c) Me llamo** — The question *¿Cómo te llamas?* asks for the name formula, not a passive. × *Yo Soy* adds a capital *Yo* in the middle of a turn and treats *soy* as the name line.  
4. **b) soy** — You are talking about yourself: [[No soy Carlos.|I am not Carlos.]] *No* sits immediately before the verb. × *No eres Carlos* would describe the listener.  
5. **a) Igualmente** — The reply to [[Mucho gusto.|Nice to meet you.]] is this formula. *Good night* is English. *Soy 25* is the age calque (Unit 3 uses *tener*, not *ser*).

</details>

### Exercises 6–10 — Fill the gap

Use **one** word or chunk: *soy / eres / me llamo / hola / adiós*.

6. ___, buenos días. ¿Cómo te llamas?  
7. — ¿Eres Ana López? — Sí, ___ Ana López.  
8. — ¿Te llamas Ruiz? — No, ___ García.  
9. Tú ___ Pablo, ¿no?  
10. ___, Ana. Hasta luego.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

6. **Hola** — A greeting can stack: [[Hola, buenos días.|Hello, good morning.]] You need the all-purpose hello before the time-of-day line. *Adiós* is a goodbye, so it cannot open the exchange.  
7. **soy** — The question used **eres** (*tú*). The answer about yourself uses **soy**. Do not answer *me llamo* here: the question was identity confirmation (*¿Eres…?*), not *¿Cómo te llamas?*  
8. **me llamo** — You are giving the real surname after a yes/no name check. [[No, me llamo García.|No, my name is García.]] *Soy García* is possible for identity, but the stimulus is the *llamar(se)* pair, so keep the name chunk.  
9. **eres** — *Tú* takes **eres**. Dropping *tú* is also fine (*¿Eres Pablo, no?*), but the gap after *Tú* is the verb.  
10. **Adiós** — The second half is already a leave-taking (*Hasta luego*). *Hola* cannot close. You can also hear *Hasta luego* twice; the gap wants the other goodbye from the unit list.

</details>

### Exercises 11–15 — Rewrite (repair the calque)

Write one natural Unit 1 sentence. Do **not** add a city or a job.

11. × *Soy llamado Ana.*  
12. × *I am Ana* → Spanish identity line (not the name formula).  
13. × *Nice to meet you.* (do not build a clause)  
14. × *Yo Soy Pablo* (middle of a conversation, no contrast).  
15. × *Señor Pablo, buenos días.* (title + first name)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

11. **Me llamo Ana.** — English *I am called* is a passive. Spanish first meetings do not use *ser* + *llamado* for a first name.  
12. **Soy Ana.** — Identity with *ser*, *yo* dropped. **Me llamo Ana** is also correct Spanish, but this item asked for the identity line, not the name formula.  
13. **Mucho gusto.** — A1 does not build *Es bueno conocerte*. The formula is enough. A woman may say **Encantada**; a man **Encantado**. If you are not sure of gender agreement, stay with *mucho gusto*.  
14. **Soy Pablo.** — Spanish *yo* is lower case and usually omitted. Keep *Yo soy Pablo* only when you contrast (*Yo soy Pablo, no Carlos*).  
15. **Buenos días, señor López.** — Titles take the **apellido**, not the first name. First name stands alone: *Hola, Pablo.*

</details>

## Lesson 2 — Vocabulary

**Goal:** the Unit 1 lexical set in sentences, with gender (*el / la*). Sixteen items from the theory article: greetings, titles, *nombre / apellido*, the two verb chunks, *mucho gusto*, *igualmente*.

### Exercises 1–5 — Choose the word that fits

1. Morning greeting to a teacher: ___ , señor López.  
   a) Buenas noches b) Buenos días c) Hasta luego

2. Evening *arrival* at a restaurant at 21:00: ___.  
   a) Good night b) Buenos días c) Buenas noches

3. Leave-taking, informal and frequent: ___.  
   a) Hasta luego b) Señor c) El nombre

4. The word for *surname*: ___ apellido.  
   a) el b) la c) los

5. Which line is the social hello, not a form?  
   a) Mi nombre es Ana. b) Me llamo Ana. c) Soy 25.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

1. **b) Buenos días** — Morning. *Buenas noches* is evening. *Hasta luego* is goodbye.  
2. **c) Buenas noches** — Unlike English *Good night*, Spanish **buenas noches** can greet when you arrive in the evening. Do not write the English phrase.  
3. **a) Hasta luego** — Neutral-informal goodbye. *Señor* is a title; *el nombre* is “the first name.”  
4. **a) el** — [[el apellido|surname]] is masculine. *La apellido* is a gender error.  
5. **b) Me llamo Ana.** — *Mi nombre es* is for forms and hotel desks. *Soy 25* is the age calque, out of this unit.

</details>

### Exercises 6–10 — Gender and the right noun

Write **el** or **la** plus the noun, then a short example sentence from the unit.

6. *first name* →  
7. *Mr* →  
8. *Mrs / Ms* →  
9. *surname* →  
10. Which noun does **not** belong with a first meeting in Unit 1: *nombre, apellido, profesor, señor*? Why?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

6. **el nombre** — [[El nombre es Ana.|The first name is Ana.]] On a form you may see *Nombre y apellidos*. In a handshake line you still prefer *Me llamo Ana.*  
7. **el señor** — [[Buenos días, señor López.|Good morning, Mr López.]] Not *señor Pablo*.  
8. **la señora** — [[Buenas tardes, señora Ruiz.|Good afternoon, Mrs Ruiz.]] Feminine title.  
9. **el apellido** — [[El apellido es García.|The surname is García.]] Many Spanish speakers have two surnames; you only need to recognise that fact here.  
10. **profesor** does not belong. Jobs are Unit 8 (*ser* + profession, without *un/una*). Unit 1 vocabulary is names, titles, and greetings. If you wrote *Soy profesor*, you left the unit.

</details>

### Exercises 11–15 — Collocations: build the turn

Complete with a Unit 1 chunk (*Me llamo / ¿Cómo te llamas? / Mucho gusto / Igualmente / ¿Eres*).

11. Hola. ___ Ana.  
12. ___? — Me llamo Pablo.  
13. Me llamo Marta. ___.  
14. ___. — Igualmente.  
15. ___ Ana López? — Sí, soy Ana López.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

11. **Me llamo** — Name formula after *Hola*. *Soy Ana* is possible but this gap trains the *llamar(se)* chunk.  
12. **¿Cómo te llamas?** — The only Unit 1 question that asks for a name. Write both marks: **¿** and **?**.  
13. **Mucho gusto.** — Close after you give your name.  
14. **Mucho gusto.** — The stimulus already has the reply *Igualmente*, so the first speaker used the formula.  
15. **¿Eres** — Identity check with *ser*, *tú*. Not *¿Me llamo Ana López?* (that would be asking about yourself).

</details>

## Lesson 3 — Reading

**Goal:** understand a first-meeting dialogue that recycles *hola, me llamo, soy / eres, mucho gusto*, and a surname. No city, no job, no age.

### Text

**En la clase de español**

Ana: Hola, buenos días.  
Pablo: Buenos días. ¿Cómo te llamas?  
Ana: Me llamo Ana. ¿Y tú?  
Pablo: Me llamo Pablo. Mucho gusto.  
Ana: Igualmente. ¿Eres Pablo Ruiz?  
Pablo: Sí, soy Pablo Ruiz. ¿Eres Ana López?  
Ana: Sí, soy Ana López.  
Pablo: Hasta luego, Ana.  
Ana: Adiós, Pablo.

(98 words of Spanish if you count the names; well inside A1. Gloss once: [[¿Y tú?|And you?]])

### Questions 1–10

1. What time-of-day greeting do they use?  
2. Who speaks first?  
3. How does Ana give her name (which formula)?  
4. How does Pablo close the first meeting?  
5. Which form of *ser* does Ana use to confirm who she is?  
6. True or false: Pablo’s surname is López.  
7. True or false: Ana says *Soy llamada Ana.*  
8. What leave-taking does Pablo use?  
9. What leave-taking does Ana use?  
10. Inference (stay in A1): is this *tú* or a form you have not learned yet? How do you know?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

1. **Buenos días** — Both speakers; it is a morning class. *Hola* is also there, stacked with *buenos días* in Ana’s first line.  
2. **Ana** — She opens with *Hola, buenos días.*  
3. **Me llamo Ana.** — Name formula, not *Mi nombre es* and not *Soy llamada*. She also later says *Soy Ana López* to confirm identity.  
4. **Mucho gusto.** — After he gives his name. Ana replies **Igualmente.**  
5. **soy** — [[Sí, soy Ana López.|Yes, I am Ana López.]]  
6. **False** — He is **Pablo Ruiz**. López is Ana’s apellido.  
7. **False** — That calque does not appear. She uses *me llamo* and *soy*.  
8. **Hasta luego** — Informal, frequent goodbye.  
9. **Adiós** — The other Unit 1 goodbye. Both are correct leave-takings.  
10. ***Tú.*** — Evidence: *¿Cómo te llamas?*, *¿Y tú?*, *¿Eres…?* The *usted* form (*se llama*, *es*) is Unit 9. Nothing in the dialogue is *usted*.

</details>

## Lesson 4 — Listening

**Goal:** catch the target forms in a short first meeting.

*Transcript practice (audio to follow).* Read the transcript aloud twice, then cover it and answer. When the MP3 exists, listen first without reading.

### Transcript

**Marta:** Hola.  
**Luis:** Hola. ¿Cómo te llamas?  
**Marta:** Me llamo Marta. ¿Eres Luis?  
**Luis:** Sí, soy Luis. Mucho gusto.  
**Marta:** Igualmente. Hasta luego.  
**Luis:** Adiós.

### Questions 1–10

1. How many greetings with *Hola* do you hear?  
2. Who asks the name question?  
3. What is Marta’s first name?  
4. Does Marta use *me llamo* or *mi nombre es*?  
5. Which *ser* form does Marta use in her question?  
6. Which *ser* form does Luis use in his answer?  
7. How does Luis close the meeting?  
8. How does Marta take leave?  
9. How does Luis take leave?  
10. Is there any origin, job, or age in this dialogue?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

1. **Two** — Each speaker says [[Hola.|Hello.]] once. There is no *buenos días* here; *hola* is enough.  
2. **Luis** — [[¿Cómo te llamas?|What’s your name?]]  
3. **Marta** — Given with the name formula.  
4. ***Me llamo.*** — The social line. *Mi nombre es* does not appear.  
5. **eres** — [[¿Eres Luis?|Are you Luis?]] *Tú* + *ser*.  
6. **soy** — [[Sí, soy Luis.|Yes, I am Luis.]]  
7. **Mucho gusto.** — Formula, not a clause.  
8. **Hasta luego.**  
9. **Adiós.**  
10. **No.** — If you “heard” *de Madrid* or *profesor* or *veinticinco*, you invented Unit 3–8 material. The tape (and the transcript) only has greeting, name, *soy / eres*, and goodbye.

</details>

## Lesson 5 — Writing

**Goal:** introduce yourself by name in about four lines (the Unit 1 can-do: ~20 seconds spoken, 4 written lines).

### 1. Situation

You meet a classmate on the first morning of a Spanish class. Write **four lines** in Spanish:

- a greeting  
- your name with **me llamo**  
- identity with **soy**  
- a close with **mucho gusto**

Do not write where you are from, what you do, or how old you are.

### 2. Planning box — words you must use

Tick them off as you write: *Hola* or *Buenos días* · *Me llamo* · *Soy* · *Mucho gusto*

Optional extras (still Unit 1): *¿Cómo te llamas?* · *Igualmente* · *el señor / la señora* + apellido

### 3. Sentence repair (do these before you write the four lines)

Repair each one. One sentence each.

1. × *Soy llamado Alex.*  
2. × *Buenos días señor Alex.*  
3. × *Soy 30.*  
4. × *Nice to meet you, me llamo Alex.*  
5. × *Yo Soy Alex de Londres.* (two problems: pronoun + origin)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

1. **Me llamo Alex.** — Name formula.  
2. **Buenos días, señor López.** — Title + **apellido**. If you only know the first name: **Buenos días, Alex.** Comma after the greeting is natural.  
3. Leave age out of Unit 1. If you needed a line, the later form is *Tengo treinta años* (Unit 3) — **not** *Soy 30.* For today’s task, drop the line entirely.  
4. **Me llamo Alex. Mucho gusto.** — Do not mix an English clause into the Spanish turn.  
5. **Soy Alex.** — Drop capital *Yo* and drop *de Londres* (Unit 4). Origin is *ser de*, later.

</details>

### 4. Model answer + commentary

**Model (four lines):**

> Buenos días.  
> Me llamo Alex.  
> Soy Alex.  
> Mucho gusto.

**Commentary.** Line 1 is a morning greeting; *Hola* would also score. Line 2 is the name formula English speakers underuse if they cling to *My name is* → *Mi nombre es*. Line 3 is identity *ser*, *yo* omitted; it is not a job and not an age. Line 4 is the close; *Encantado / Encantada* is fine if the ending matches **you**. A slightly longer (still A1) version you may add after the model, not instead of it:

> Hola, me llamo Alex. ¿Cómo te llamas?  
> Mucho gusto.

That extra question is production of the *tú* chunk. It still contains no city, no *profesor*, and no number of years.

**Self-check before you stop**

- Silent *h* in *Hola* when you read it aloud.  
- *ll* in *me llamo* like the *y* in *yes*.  
- Written **¿** on every question.  
- No *Soy llamado*, no *Soy 25*, no *Soy de…*, no *Soy profesor*.

## After you finish

Return to any item you missed and reread the matching section of the [theory guide](/blog/curso-espanol-a1/unidad-1-greetings-names-ser). Then say the twenty-second introduction out loud without reading.

When the name is automatic, continue to **Unit 2** (alphabet, stress, *¿ ?*), where you will spell *Ana* and *López*.

*Official inventory practised here:* PCIC functions 5.1–5.5, 5.21; notion 3.1.1; grammar 12 (*ser* of identity, *yo / tú* only). No past, no imperative paradigm, no *ir a*.
