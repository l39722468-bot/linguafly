English speakers walk into a first Spanish conversation with a ready-made script: *Hello. My name is… I am… Nice to meet you.* That script is excellent English. It is a poor map of Spanish. The language does not build a first meeting out of a single verb that covers *am* in every sense, and it does not treat *My name is* as a possessive plus *to be*. If you translate the English line word for word, you get sentences that Spanish speakers do not say when they meet someone: *Soy llamado Ana*, *Mi nombre es* as a handshake line, or *Soy 25* when you meant your age.

This is **Unit 1 of the A1 Spanish course** (*Greetings, names, and* ser *with yo / tú*). By the end you will greet someone, give your name, confirm who you are with **soy** and **eres**, and close with **mucho gusto** — in about twenty seconds, without dragging in origin, job, or age.

The official repertoire this unit opens is small on purpose. In the Instituto Cervantes *Plan curricular* (PCIC / NRE), first contact sits in **functions 5.1–5.5 and 5.21** (greet, introduce yourself, take leave), **specific notion 3.1.1** (name and surnames), and **grammar inventory 12** (*ser* of identity). A1 does not hand you the whole verb *ser*, and it does not hand you the English-style “thirty-second bio.” Stay inside this box and the next nine units will add age, origin, and work in the right order.

## What you will be able to do

After this unit you can:

- Greet and take leave with [[Hola.|Hello.]], [[Buenos días.|Good morning.]], [[Buenas tardes.|Good afternoon.]], [[Buenas noches.|Good evening / good night.]], [[Adiós.|Goodbye.]], and [[Hasta luego.|See you later.]].
- Give your first name with [[Me llamo…|My name is…]] and ask [[¿Cómo te llamas?|What’s your name?]]
- Use **ser** only with **yo** and **tú**: [[Soy Ana.|I am Ana.]] / [[Eres Pablo.|You are Pablo.]]
- Close a first meeting with [[Mucho gusto.|Nice to meet you.]] and [[Igualmente.|Likewise.]]
- Spot the typical English-speaker calques (*Soy llamado*, capital *Yo*, *Nice to meet you* copied word for word, *Mi nombre es* as the default hello).

You will **not** yet say where you are from, what you do, or how old you are. Those are Units 4, 8, and 3. If you add them now, you practise the wrong verbs.

## Grammar

Two tools do the work of English *I am / my name is* in this first meeting: the name formula **me llamo**, and the identity verb **ser** in the two persons you need today.

### 1. Greetings and leave-takings

Spanish has a neutral hello and a set of time-of-day greetings. **Hola** is always available. The *buenos / buenas* set is the polite default with people you do not know well, in shops, and in class.

| Spanish | English | When |
|---------|---------|------|
| **Hola** | Hello / hi | Any time; informal or neutral |
| **Buenos días** | Good morning | Morning, roughly until midday |
| **Buenas tardes** | Good afternoon | Afternoon until evening |
| **Buenas noches** | Good evening / good night | Evening: greeting **or** goodbye |
| **Adiós** | Goodbye | Neutral leave-taking |
| **Hasta luego** | See you later | Informal, very frequent |

[[Hola, Ana.|Hello, Ana.]]

[[Buenos días, señor López.|Good morning, Mr López.]]

[[Buenas tardes, señora Ruiz.|Good afternoon, Mrs Ruiz.]]

[[Adiós, Pablo.|Goodbye, Pablo.]]

[[Hasta luego.|See you later.]]

English *Good night* is almost only a farewell before sleep. Spanish **buenas noches** is broader: you can say it when you *arrive* at a restaurant at 9 p.m. That is the first English–Spanish mismatch of the unit, and it is worth repeating aloud so it does not feel like a mistake.

A compact informal opener is the chunk [[Hola, ¿qué tal?|Hi, how’s it going?]] Treat **¿qué tal?** as a formula for now. You do not need the verb *estar* yet.

Titles sit before the surname: **señor** (*Mr*), **señora** (*Mrs / Ms* in many adult contexts). You will hear **señores** for a couple. You do not need **don / doña** at A1.

### 2. The name formula: *me llamo*

English *My name is Ana* is possessive + *to be*. Spanish **me llamo** is literally “I call myself.” The verb is *llamar(se)*; at A1 you learn it as a **fixed first-person chunk**, plus the question for *tú*.

| Function | Spanish | English |
|----------|---------|---------|
| Give your name | **Me llamo** Ana. | My name is Ana. |
| Ask a peer | **¿Cómo te llamas?** | What’s your name? |
| Confirm | **¿Te llamas** Ana? | Is your name Ana? |
| Yes | **Sí, me llamo** Ana. | Yes, my name is Ana. |
| No | **No, me llamo** Marta. | No, my name is Marta. |

[[Me llamo Ana.|My name is Ana.]]

[[Me llamo Carlos.|My name is Carlos.]]

[[¿Cómo te llamas?|What’s your name?]]

[[Me llamo María López.|My name is María López.]]

[[¿Te llamas Pablo?|Is your name Pablo?]]

[[No, me llamo Pedro.|No, my name is Pedro.]]

Three details English speakers miss:

1. **There is no *soy* inside *me llamo*.** You do not say *Soy me llamo* or *Me soy llamo*. One formula, then the name.
2. **The name is not introduced with *a*.** × *Me llamo a Ana.*
3. **Spanish does not capitalise *yo*.** English *I* is always capital; Spanish *yo* is capital only at the start of a sentence. In this unit you will often drop *yo* altogether.

**Mi nombre es Ana** is grammatical. Adults use it on forms, at a hotel desk, or when they spell a full official name. It is not the handshake line. If you make it your default, you sound as if you are filling in a box. Train **me llamo** first.

Full names in the Spanish-speaking world often have **two surnames** (*dos apellidos*): father’s, then mother’s. You do not need to explain the system yet. You do need the words: [[el nombre|first name]] and [[el apellido|surname]]. On a form you may see *Nombre y apellidos*. For this unit, giving a first name is enough: [[Me llamo Ana.|My name is Ana.]]

### 3. Identity with *ser*: only *soy* and *eres*

English *I am Ana* and *You are Pablo* use *to be*. Spanish **ser** is the identity verb here — “who someone is,” not where they are, not how they feel, and not how old they are.

| Person | Spanish | English | Typical spoken form |
|--------|---------|---------|---------------------|
| yo | **soy** | I am | **Soy** Ana. |
| tú | **eres** | you are | **¿Eres** Ana? |

[[Soy Ana.|I am Ana.]]

[[Yo soy Carlos.|I am Carlos.]]

[[Eres Marta.|You are Marta.]]

[[Tú eres Pablo.|You are Pablo.]]

[[¿Eres Ana?|Are you Ana?]]

[[Sí, soy Ana.|Yes, I am Ana.]]

[[No, no soy Ana.|No, I am not Ana.]]

Subject pronouns are **optional**. Spanish is not English: you do not need *yo* in every line. **Soy Ana** is the default. Add **yo** when you contrast: *Yo soy Ana, no Marta.* Add **tú** when you contrast or when you want the question to land clearly: *¿Tú eres Pablo?*

Negation is **no** immediately before the verb: [[No soy Ana.|I am not Ana.]] [[No eres Pablo.|You are not Pablo.]] Do not copy English *I am not* with a word for *am* sitting after *not* in a different slot: × *Soy no Ana.*

Yes/no questions at A1 are often just inverted intonation plus the written marks **¿ ?**: [[¿Eres Ana?|Are you Ana?]] You will train those marks properly in Unit 2. For now, put **¿** at the start as well as **?** at the end. English speakers who write *Eres Ana?* are spelling in English.

**What you are not conjugating today.** *Él es / ella es / usted es / nosotros somos / vosotros sois / ellos son* belong to **Unit 7**. Formal **usted** belongs to **Unit 9**. If you jump there now, you will mix identity *ser* with later uses of the same verb.

**What *ser* is not doing today.** English *I am* also covers age (*I am 25*), origin (*I am from Leeds*), profession (*I am a teacher*), and location (*I am in Madrid*). Spanish splits those. Age is **tener** (Unit 3). Origin is **ser de** (Unit 4). Profession is *ser* without *un/una* (Unit 8). Location is **estar** (Unit 29). If you say *Soy 25* or *Soy profesor* in Unit 1, you are practising a future unit with the wrong support.

### 4. Closing the first meeting: *mucho gusto*

English *Nice to meet you* is a full clause. Spanish A1 uses a short formula:

[[Mucho gusto.|Nice to meet you.]]

[[Encantado.|Pleased to meet you. (speaker masculine)]]

[[Encantada.|Pleased to meet you. (speaker feminine)]]

[[Igualmente.|Likewise.]]

**Mucho gusto** is the safest all-purpose close. **Encantado / encantada** agrees with the **speaker**, not the listener: a woman says *encantada* even if she is meeting a man. English has no such agreement, so this is a new habit. If you are not sure, say **mucho gusto**.

A complete A1 first meeting, about twenty seconds:

[[Hola, me llamo Ana. ¿Cómo te llamas?|Hello, my name is Ana. What’s your name?]]

[[Me llamo Pablo. Mucho gusto.|My name is Pablo. Nice to meet you.]]

[[Igualmente.|Likewise.]]

## How English and Spanish differ here

This contrast is the pedagogical core of Unit 1. English packs greeting, name, identity, origin, and job into one *to be* script. Spanish A1 unpacks the script and refuses several of the English pieces until later.

**1. “My name is” is not *ser*.**  
× *Soy llamado Ana.* (calque of *I am called*)  
× *Mi nombre es Ana* as the default hello.  
→ [[Me llamo Ana.|My name is Ana.]]

**2. “I am Ana” is *ser*, but a tiny slice of *ser*.**  
English *I am* looks like one switch. Spanish *soy* here only identifies. It does not locate you, date you, or employ you.  
→ [[Soy Ana.|I am Ana.]]  
Not: × *Soy 25.* Not: × *Soy de Londres* (save for Unit 4). Not: × *Soy un profesor* (the article is wrong later anyway).

**3. Capital *I* trains a false *Yo*.**  
Every English sentence with *I* shouts the pronoun. Spanish writes **yo** in lower case and often omits it.  
→ [[Soy Ana.|I am Ana.]]  
Not: × *Yo Soy Ana* with a capital on both words in the middle of a sentence.

**4. *Nice to meet you* is not a clause you build.**  
× *Es bueno conocerte* as an A1 first line (you do not have that grammar).  
× *Niza to meet you* (the English phrase borrowed raw).  
→ [[Mucho gusto.|Nice to meet you.]]

**5. *Good night* is not a greeting in English; *buenas noches* can be.**  
Arrive at 21:00: [[Buenas noches.|Good evening.]]  
Leave to sleep: also [[Buenas noches.|Good night.]]  
English *Good evening* vs *Good night* is a split Spanish does not copy one-to-one.

**6. Titles and surnames.**  
English *Mr / Mrs* + surname maps onto **señor / señora** + apellido. Do not put the title before a first name in the English way (*Señor Pablo* is not the A1 target). [[Buenos días, señor López.|Good morning, Mr López.]]

## Vocabulary

Learn these **sixteen** items **in sentences**, not as a naked list. Gender is marked with *el / la* where there is a noun. Recycle them in the dialogue below; do not add origin or job words.

| Spanish | Gender / extras | Example | English |
|---------|-----------------|---------|---------|
| hola | invariable | Hola, Ana. | hello |
| los buenos días | masculine plural as a greeting | Buenos días, señor. | good morning |
| las buenas tardes | feminine plural as a greeting | Buenas tardes, señora. | good afternoon |
| las buenas noches | feminine plural as a greeting | Buenas noches. | good evening / good night |
| adiós | invariable | Adiós, Carlos. | goodbye |
| hasta luego | chunk | Hasta luego. | see you later |
| el señor | masculine | El señor López. | Mr |
| la señora | feminine | La señora Ruiz. | Mrs / Ms |
| el nombre | masculine | Mi nombre es Ana. *(forms)* | first name / name |
| el apellido | masculine | Mi apellido es López. | surname |
| me llamo | chunk (yo) | Me llamo Ana. | my name is |
| ¿cómo te llamas? | chunk (tú) | ¿Cómo te llamas? | what’s your name? |
| soy | *ser*, yo | Soy Ana. | I am |
| eres | *ser*, tú | ¿Eres Pablo? | you are |
| mucho gusto | chunk | Mucho gusto. | nice to meet you |
| igualmente | invariable | Igualmente. | likewise |

Reuse in full sentences:

[[El nombre es Ana.|The first name is Ana.]]

[[El apellido es García.|The surname is García.]]

[[Buenos días, señor.|Good morning, sir.]]

[[Buenas tardes, señora.|Good afternoon, ma’am.]]

[[Me llamo Ana García.|My name is Ana García.]]

[[Mucho gusto. Igualmente.|Nice to meet you. Likewise.]]

Two extra recognition items (you will hear them; you do not need to build them yet): **encantado / encantada** (speaker-gendered “pleased to meet you”) and **¿qué tal?** (informal “how’s it going?”). Keep them as chunks.

## Pronunciation and spelling

Unit 2 is the sound system. Unit 1 only fixes the sounds inside this script so you do not freeze on *hola* and *me llamo*.

**Silent *h*.** [[Hola.|Hello.]] starts with a vowel. There is no English *h* as in *hello*. If you puff air, you sound like you are saying a different language. Think of English *hour*, not *house*. The stress is on the first syllable: *HO-la*.

**The *ll* in *me llamo*.** In almost all of the Spanish-speaking world, **ll** sounds like the *y* in English *yes* (this is *yeísmo*). Say *me yamo*. You do not need a separate “Castilian *ll*” at A1. Stress: *me LLA-mo* (the double *l* syllable is the strong one).

**The *ñ* in *señor / señora*.** [[Señor.|Mr]] has *ñ*, roughly the *ny* in English *canyon*. Not *senior*. Stress on the last syllable: *se-ÑOR*. **Señora** is *se-ÑO-ra*.

**Vowels stay pure.** Spanish *a e i o u* do not glide like English *name* or *I*. *Ana* is *AH-nah*, not *Ay-nuh*. *Me* is a clean *meh*, not English *me*.

**Question marks come in pairs.** [[¿Cómo te llamas?|What’s your name?]] begins with **¿**. Leaving it out is an English spelling habit. You will drill this in Unit 2; start correctly today.

**Listen-and-repeat set** (audio files will be added later; use your own voice now):

1. Hola.  
2. Buenos días.  
3. Buenas tardes.  
4. Buenas noches.  
5. Me llamo Ana.  
6. ¿Cómo te llamas?  
7. Soy Ana.  
8. ¿Eres Pablo?  
9. Mucho gusto.  
10. Hasta luego.

Say each line twice: once looking, once with the page covered.

## Model dialogue

A first meeting in a language class. Read it aloud. Then cover the English column and say the Spanish.

**Ana:** [[Hola, buenos días.|Hello, good morning.]]  
**Pablo:** [[Buenos días. ¿Cómo te llamas?|Good morning. What’s your name?]]  
**Ana:** [[Me llamo Ana. ¿Y tú?|My name is Ana. And you?]]  
**Pablo:** [[Me llamo Pablo. Mucho gusto.|My name is Pablo. Nice to meet you.]]  
**Ana:** [[Igualmente. ¿Eres Pablo Ruiz?|Likewise. Are you Pablo Ruiz?]]  
**Pablo:** [[Sí, soy Pablo Ruiz. ¿Eres Ana López?|Yes, I am Pablo Ruiz. Are you Ana López?]]  
**Ana:** [[Sí, soy Ana López.|Yes, I am Ana López.]]  
**Pablo:** [[Hasta luego, Ana.|See you later, Ana.]]  
**Ana:** [[Adiós, Pablo.|Goodbye, Pablo.]]

The dialogue is nine turns, inside A1: greetings, *me llamo*, *soy / eres*, a surname for recognition, and a goodbye. There is no city, no job, no age, no *usted*.

**Comprehension** (answers in the [workbook](/blog/curso-espanol-a1/unidad-1-greetings-names-ser-ejercicios-soluciones); preview here):

1. What is Ana’s first name?  
2. How does Pablo close the first meeting?  
3. Which form of *ser* does Ana use to confirm who she is?

**Speaking (20 seconds).** Stand up and say, without reading:

> Hola, me llamo [your name]. ¿Cómo te llamas?  
> Then, as if you heard an answer: Mucho gusto.

**Writing model (do not go beyond this).** Four lines is enough for Unit 1:

> Hola.  
> Me llamo Alex.  
> Soy Alex.  
> Mucho gusto.

If you add *I am a student* or *I am from…*, you have left the unit.

## English-speaker pitfalls

1. × *Soy llamado Ana.* → [[Me llamo Ana.|My name is Ana.]]  
   English *I am called* is a passive. Spanish does not use *ser* + *llamado* for a first name.

2. × *Yo Soy Ana* (capital *Yo* in the middle of a sentence, and a compulsory pronoun). → [[Soy Ana.|I am Ana.]]  
   Keep *yo* for contrast. Write it in lower case unless it starts the sentence.

3. × *Nice to meet you* said in English, or × *Es nice meet you*. → [[Mucho gusto.|Nice to meet you.]]  
   Do not build a clause. Use the formula. Reply: [[Igualmente.|Likewise.]]

4. × *Mi nombre es Ana* as every hello. → [[Me llamo Ana.|My name is Ana.]]  
   Save *mi nombre es* for forms. The social line is *me llamo*.

5. × *Soy 25.* → wait for Unit 3: age is **tener**, not *ser*.  
   Identity *soy* is for *who*, not *how old*.

6. × *Hola* with an English *h*. → [[Hola.|Hello.]] with a silent *h*.  
   If you say it like English *hola* in *holiday*, you have added a consonant Spanish does not want here.

7. × *Señor Pablo* as a title + first name. → [[Señor López.|Mr López.]]  
   Title plus **apellido**. First name stands alone: [[Hola, Pablo.|Hello, Pablo.]]

8. × Filling the English four-step intro (greeting + name + origin + job).  
   That is the English-course Unit 1 shape. In Spanish A1, origin is Unit 4 and job is Unit 8. Today: greeting + name + *soy / eres*.

## FAQs

**How do I say “My name is” in Spanish?**  
[[Me llamo Ana.|My name is Ana.]] is the line you want in a first meeting. *I am called* does not become *Soy llamado*. *Mi nombre es* is for documents. Add identity with [[Soy Ana.|I am Ana.]] if you need to confirm who you are.

**What is the difference between *Me llamo Ana* and *Soy Ana*?**  
*Me llamo* is the name formula (what you are called). *Soy* is *ser* of identity (who you are). You can use both in the same conversation. You cannot use *soy* for age, and you should not bolt on a city or a job in this unit.

**When do I use *Hola* versus *Buenos días*?**  
*Hola* is the flexible hello. *Buenos días / Buenas tardes / Buenas noches* mark the time of day and sound more situationally polite. *Buenas noches* can greet in the evening, unlike English *Good night*.

**How do I conjugate *ser* with I and you?**  
[[Soy.|I am.]] and [[Eres.|You are.]] That is the whole productive paradigm for Unit 1. Drop the pronoun unless you contrast. The rest of *ser*, and *usted*, come later.

**Where do I practise?**  
In the [answered workbook for Unit 1](/blog/curso-espanol-a1/unidad-1-greetings-names-ser-ejercicios-soluciones), then in [Unit 2](/blog/curso-espanol-a1/unidad-1-greetings-names-ser), where you will spell the names you have just learned.
