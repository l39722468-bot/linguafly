La **Unidad 52** consolida la **passive voice** en todos los tiempos, la **passive reporting** (*It is believed that… / The suspect is believed to have…*) y el **reported speech** (*He said that… / She claimed to have…*) en el contexto de la **justicia y los derechos humanos**. Estas estructuras son esenciales para expresar quién realiza una acción cuando el foco está en el receptor, o para atribuir información a fuentes sin comprometer la neutralidad. En este artículo encontrarás teoría completa con ejemplos del ámbito legal y ejercicios progresivos para dominar estas construcciones.

## Sección 1 — Passive Voice: passive en todos los tiempos

La **passive voice** se usa cuando el receptor de la acción es más importante que quien la realiza, o cuando el agente es desconocido, irrelevante o deliberadamente ocultado. En contextos legales y de derechos humanos, la passive es extremadamente frecuente porque los informes judiciales, las resoluciones de tribunales y los comunicados de prensa suelen centrarse en los hechos y sus víctimas, no en los agentes.

### Estructura general

**Subject + to be (en el tiempo correspondiente) + past participle (V3)**

### Present simple passive

Se usa para hechos generales, procedimientos habituales o leyes vigentes.

- *The defendant **is charged** with fraud.* — El acusado es acusado de fraude.
- *Human rights **are protected** by the constitution.* — Los derechos humanos son protegidos por la constitución.
- *Evidence **is collected** at the crime scene.* — La evidencia es recolectada en la escena del crimen.

### Past simple passive

Describe acciones completadas en el pasado, como veredictos o sentencias ya dictadas.

- *The law **was passed** in 2015.* — La ley fue aprobada en 2015.
- *The suspect **was arrested** on Monday.* — El sospechoso fue arrestado el lunes.
- *Three witnesses **were called** to testify.* — Tres testigos fueron llamados a declarar.

### Present perfect passive

Enfatiza el resultado actual de una acción pasada. Muy útil en informes legales actualizados.

- *The evidence **has been examined** by the court.* — La evidencia ha sido examinada por el tribunal.
- *The verdict **has been announced**.* — El veredicto ha sido anunciado.
- *Several amendments **have been made** to the bill.* — Se han hecho varias enmiendas al proyecto de ley.

### Future simple passive

Se usa para decisiones futuras, predicciones legales o pronunciamientos pendientes.

- *The verdict **will be announced** tomorrow.* — El veredicto será anunciado mañana.
- *The accused **will be sentenced** next week.* — El acusado será sentenciado la próxima semana.
- *New regulations **will be enforced** from January.* — Nuevas regulaciones serán aplicadas desde enero.

### Modal passive

Expresa posibilidad, obligación, permiso o capacidad en forma pasiva.

- *The sentence **can be reduced** with good behaviour.* — La sentencia puede ser reducida con buen comportamiento.
- *Corruption **must be investigated** thoroughly.* — La corrupción debe ser investigada a fondo.
- *The prisoner **may be released** on parole.* — El prisionero puede ser liberado en libertad condicional.

### Past modal passive (modal + have been + V3)

Habla de obligaciones, posibilidades o críticas sobre el pasado.

- *The rights **should have been respected**.* — Los derechos deberían haber sido respetados.
- *The case **could have been resolved** sooner.* — El caso podría haber sido resuelto antes.
- *More evidence **might have been gathered**.* — Más evidencia podría haber sido recopilada.

### Conditional passive

Expresa situaciones hipotéticas o consecuencias supuestas.

- *The case **would be dismissed** without evidence.* — El caso sería desestimado sin evidencia.
- *The defendant **would be acquitted** if new proof emerged.* — El acusado sería absuelto si surgiera nueva prueba.

### Perfect conditional passive

Habla de hipótesis sobre resultados que no ocurrieron.

- *The sentence **would have been reduced** if the defendant had cooperated.* — La sentencia habría sido reducida si el acusado hubiera cooperado.
- *Justice **would have been served** if the trial had been fair.* — La justicia habría sido servida si el juicio hubiera sido justo.

### Errores comunes con la passive voice

| # | Error | Corrección | Explicación |
|---|-------|-----------|-------------|
| 1 | *The law **was passed** by government in 2015.* (sin artículo) | *The law was passed by **the** government in 2015.* | Cuando el agente va con "by", se necesita el artículo definido si se refiere a una entidad concreta. |
| 2 | *The evidence **has been examine** by the court.* | *The evidence has been **examined** by the court.* | Se necesita el past participle (V3), no el infinitivo. |
| 3 | *Human rights **are protect** by the constitution.* | *Human rights **are protected** by the constitution.* | Falta el pasado participio; "protect" → "protected". |
| 4 | *The verdict **will announced** tomorrow.* | *The verdict **will be announced** tomorrow.* | En future passive se necesita "will + be + V3". |
| 5 | *The suspect **was arrest** on Monday.* | *The suspect **was arrested** on Monday.* | Falta el pasado participio; "arrest" → "arrested". |

## Sección 2 — Passive Reporting

La **passive reporting** atribuye información a una fuente general o impersonal sin comprometer al hablante. Es fundamental en periodismo, informes legales y comunicados oficiales. En inglés se construye de dos formas principales:

### Estructura impersonal

**It + is/was + reported/said/believed/claimed/thought/expected + that + clause**

- *It is said that the suspect fled the country.* — Se dice que el sospechoso huyó del país.
- *It was reported that three people were injured.* — Se reportó que tres personas resultaron heridas.
- *It is believed that the crime was premeditated.* — Se cree que el crimen fue premeditado.

### Estructura personal (con "to")

**Subject + is/was + said/believed/reported + to + infinitive / to have + V3**

Cuando la acción es pasada, se usa **to have + past participle**.

- *The suspect **is said to have fled** the country.* — Se dice que el sospechoso huyó del país.
- *The man **is believed to be** connected to the crime.* — Se cree que el hombre está conectado con el crimen.
- *The victim **was reported to have died** at the scene.* — Se reportó que la víctima murió en el lugar.

### Passive reporting en diferentes tiempos

| Tiempo | Estructura | Ejemplo |
|--------|-----------|---------|
| Present simple | It is said that… | *It is said that the trial will be fair.* |
| Past simple | It was reported that… | *It was reported that the jury reached a verdict.* |
| Present perfect | It has been claimed that… | *It has been claimed that evidence was tampered with.* |
| Future | It will be expected that… | *It will be expected that the defendant cooperates.* |
| Past perfect | It had been alleged that… | *It had been alleged that the contract was signed under duress.* |

### Verbos de reporting habituales en passive reporting

- **said** — dicho
- **believed** — creído
- **reported** — reportado
- **claimed** — alegado
- **expected** — esperado
- **thought** — pensado
- **known** — sabido
- **alleged** — alegado
- **suspected** — sospechado
- **confirmed** — confirmado

### Errores comunes con la passive reporting

| # | Error | Corrección | Explicación |
|---|-------|-----------|-------------|
| 1 | *It is believed **that him** to be guilty.* | *It is believed **that he is** guilty.* / *He is believed **to be** guilty.* | No se pueden mezclar ambas estructuras. Usar "that + clause" o "to + infinitive". |
| 2 | *The man is said **having fled** the country.* | *The man is said **to have fled** the country.* | Después de "to" se usa el infinitivo perfecto, no el gerundio. |
| 3 | *It was reported **him** to have been arrested.* | *It was reported **that he had been** arrested.* / ***He** was reported to have been arrested.* | El sujeto después de "it was reported" va en clause con "that", o como sujeto principal. |
| 4 | *It is **say** that the law is effective.* | *It is **said** that the law is effective.* | Se necesita el past participle, no el infinitivo base. |
| 5 | *He is believed **be** innocent.* | *He is believed **to be** innocent.* | Falta la preposición "to" antes del infinitivo. |

## Sección 3 — Reported Speech

El **reported speech** (estilo indirecto) transforma una cita directa en una cláusula subordinada. En contextos legales, es indispensable para declaraciones de testigos, sentencias judiciales y comunicados de abogados.

### Backshift de tiempos

Cuando el verbo de reporting está en pasado, los tiempos de la cita directa se desplazan hacia atrás (backshift):

| Directo | Indirecto |
|---------|-----------|
| "I **am** innocent." | He said he **was** innocent. |
| "I **was** at home." | She said she **had been** at home. |
| "I **have seen** the evidence." | He said he **had seen** the evidence. |
| "I **will testify**." | She said she **would testify**. |
| "I **can help**." | He said he **could help**. |
| "I **must leave**." | She said she **had to leave**. |

### Verbos de reporting

En derecho y derechos humanos, los verbos de reporting más frecuentes son:

- **say** — decir
- **tell** — decir (a alguien)
- **claim** — alegar
- **alleg** — alegar (formal)
- **insist** — insistir
- **deny** — negar
- **suggest** — sugerir
- **report** — reportar

### Reporting de preguntas

- Preguntas con auxiliary: *He asked **whether** the suspect **had been** arrested.*
- Preguntas sin auxiliary: *She asked **where** the evidence **was**.*

### Reporting de órdenes y peticiones

- Orden: *The judge ordered **that the jury be sequestered**.* / *The judge told them **to leave** the courtroom.*
- Petición: *The lawyer requested **that the case be dismissed**.*

### Errores comunes con el reported speech

| # | Error | Corrección | Explicación |
|---|-------|-----------|-------------|
| 1 | *He said that he **is** innocent.* | *He said that he **was** innocent.* | El backshift exige cambiar "is" a "was" cuando el verbo de reporting está en pasado. |
| 2 | *She told **that** she had seen the evidence.* | *She said that she had seen the evidence.* / *She **told me** that she had seen the evidence.* | "Tell" requiere un objeto indirecto; "say" no lo necesita. |
| 3 | *The witness claimed **to not have been** present.* | *The witness claimed **not to have been** present.* | La negación va antes del infinitivo: "not to + V". |
| 4 | *He asked me **that** where I was.* | *He asked me **where I was**.* | En preguntas indirectas no se usa "that" ni se duplica la interrogación. |
| 5 | *The lawyer insisted **to see** the documents.* | *The lawyer insisted **on seeing** the documents.* / *The lawyer insisted **that he be allowed** to see the documents.* | "Insist" no va seguido de infinitivo directo. Se usa "on + gerundio" o "that + clause". |

## Sección 5 — Reading: A landmark human rights case

<audio controls preload="none" src="/audio/blog/curso-b2/unit-52/reading-landmark-case.mp3" title="🔊 Reading: A Landmark Human Rights Case"></audio>

In 2010, a landmark case **was brought** before the International Criminal Court. The case **was filed** by a group of human rights organisations alleging that war crimes **had been committed** during an armed conflict. The evidence **was collected** over several years by independent investigators and **has been presented** to the court in a series of hearings.

It **is believed** that the crimes **were planned** at the highest levels of government. Several high-ranking officials **are said to have ordered** attacks on civilian populations. It **was reported** that thousands of civilians **were displaced** as a result.

After a lengthy trial, the court **ruled** that the evidence was sufficient to proceed. It **is expected** that the verdict **will be announced** early next year. Human rights groups have called the proceedings a milestone in international justice, and it **is hoped** that the case **will set** a precedent for future prosecutions.

*(Approx. 150 words)*

## Sección 6 — Listening y diálogo

<audio controls preload="none" src="/audio/blog/curso-b2/unit-52/listening-courtroom-dialogue.mp3" title="🔊 Listening: Courtroom Dialogue"></audio>

**Context:** A courtroom scene where a judge addresses the defendant's lawyer.

> **Judge:** It has been alleged that your client was present at the scene. Is that correct?
>
> **Lawyer:** My client has been wrongly accused, Your Honour. He is believed to have been at work at the time.
>
> **Judge:** The prosecution claims that evidence places him at the location. Were the security records examined?
>
> **Lawyer:** Yes, Your Honour. The security records have been reviewed and it is now confirmed that the timestamps were altered. It is thought that the footage was tampered with.
>
> **Judge:** Very well. The defence will be allowed to present its findings. The trial will resume on Monday.
>
> **Lawyer:** Thank you, Your Honour.

Este diálogo ilustra passive voice (*were examined, have been reviewed*), passive reporting (*it has been alleged, is believed to have been, it is thought that*) y reported speech en un contexto judicial real.

## Sección 7 — Writing: Reports and legal documents

La passive voice y la passive reporting son esenciales en la redacción formal de informes legales, resoluciones judiciales y documentos de derechos humanos.

### Consejos clave

1. **Usa passive cuando el agente es desconocido o irrelevante:** *The victim was found unconscious.* (no sabemos quién la encontró).
2. **Usa passive reporting para atribuir información sin compromiso:** *It is alleged that…* en vez de *People say that…*
3. **Mantén coherencia temporal:** Si el informe describe hechos pasados, usa past passive; si describe la situación actual, usa present passive.
4. **Evita el estilo periodístico informal:** Usa *It has been reported that* en lugar de *People have been saying that*.
5. **Cita testigos con reported speech:** *The witness stated that she had seen the defendant leaving the building.*
6. **Combina passive y reported speech:** *The documents were said to have been destroyed.*

## Sección 8 — Speaking

Para discutir temas de justicia y derechos humanos en inglés B2:

1. **Prepara chunks de passive reporting:** *It is believed that…, The suspect is said to have…, It was reported that…*
2. **Usa passive para sonar objetivo:** En lugar de *They arrested him*, di *He was arrested*. Esto es más formal y neutral.
3. **Cita fuentes con reported speech:** *According to the report, the law was passed unanimously.*
4. **Practica opiniones con condicionales pasivos:** *More could have been done to prevent the crisis.*
5. **Vocabulario temático:** Domina los términos de la tabla de vocabulario para sonar preciso y profesional.

## Sección 9 — Errores cruzados y estrategia de examen

| # | Error | Corrección | Tipo de error |
|---|-------|-----------|---------------|
| 1 | *The law is said **pass** last year.* | *The law is said **to have been passed** last year.* | Passive reporting + tiempo pasado |
| 2 | *He said that he **will** testify.* | *He said that he **would** testify.* | Falta backshift en reported speech |
| 3 | *It was believed **him to be** guilty.* | *It was believed **that he was** guilty.* | Mezcla incorrecta de estructuras |
| 4 | *The verdict **will announced** tomorrow.* | *The verdict **will be announced** tomorrow.* | Falta "be" en future passive |

## Sección 10 — Del reconocimiento a la producción B2

- **Lee informes legales y artículos de prensa** en inglés y subraya todas las construcciones pasivas y de reporting.
- **Escucha debates parlamentarios o juicios** disponibles en YouTube para identificar passive reporting en tiempo real.
- **Escribe resúmenes de noticias legales** usando al menos 5 construcciones passive/reporting por párrafo.
- **Practica speaking** simulando una entrevista periodística o una declaración ante un tribunal.

## Sección 11 — Resumen rápido de teoría

| Estructura | Forma | Ejemplo |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| Present simple passive | am/is/are + V3 | *Rights are protected by law.* |
| Past simple passive | was/were + V3 | *The law was passed in 2015.* |
| Present perfect passive | have/has been + V3 | *Evidence has been examined.* |
| Future simple passive | will be + V3 | *The verdict will be announced.* |
| Modal passive | modal + be + V3 | *The sentence can be reduced.* |
| Past modal passive | modal + have been + V3 | *Rights should have been respected.* |
| Conditional passive | would be + V3 | *The case would be dismissed.* |
| Perfect conditional passive | would have been + V3 | *The sentence would have been reduced.* |
| Passive reporting (impersonal) | It is said that… | *It is said that he fled.* |
| Passive reporting (personal) | Subject + is said to… | *He is said to have fled.* |
| Reported speech (statements) | Subject + said (that)… | *He said (that) he was innocent.* |
| Reported speech (questions) | Subject + asked whether/if… | *She asked whether the law applied.* |
| Reported speech (orders) | Subject + ordered/told + to V | *The judge ordered him to leave.* |

## Checklist

- [ ] La passive voice se forma con **subject + to be (tiempo) + V3**.
- [ ] En cada tiempo verbal, el verbo "be" se conjuga según el tiempo correspondiente.
- [ ] La passive reporting impersonal usa **It is/was + V3 + that + clause**.
- [ ] La passive reporting personal usa **Subject + is/was + V3 + to + infinitive**.
- [ ] Para acciones pasadas en passive reporting se usa **to have + V3**.
- [ ] El reported speech requiere **backshift** cuando el verbo de reporting está en pasado.
- [ ] "Tell" necesita un objeto indirecto; "say" no.
- [ ] Las preguntas indirectas usan **whether/if** (preguntas sí/no) o wh- words.
- [ ] Las órdenes se reportan con **ordered/told + object + to + verb**.
- [ ] En writing formal, la passive y passive reporting crean un tono objetivo y profesional.
