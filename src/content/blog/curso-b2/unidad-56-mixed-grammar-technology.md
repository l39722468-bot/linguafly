## 1. Mixed Conditionals in Technology

Los **mixed conditionals** combinan tiempos diferentes en la cláusula if y la cláusula principal. Son esenciales para hablar de tecnología porque constantemente mezclamos pasado, presente y futuro.

### Tipo 1: Pasado → Presente

Usamos esta estructura cuando una condición hipotética del pasado tiene un resultado en el presente.

**Estructura:** `If + past perfect, ... would + infinitive`

- *If AI had been developed earlier, healthcare **would be** more advanced now.*
- *If cybersecurity had been prioritised in the 1990s, data breaches **wouldn't be** so common today.*
- *If engineers had invested more in renewable energy tech, we **wouldn't face** so many environmental challenges now.*

### Tipo 2: Presente → Pasado

Usamos esta estructura cuando una condición hipotética del presente tiene un resultado en el pasado.

**Estructura:** `If + past simple, ... would have + past participle`

- *If we **invested** more in cybersecurity, breaches **wouldn't have increased** last year.*
- *If the company **had** better infrastructure, the server **wouldn't have crashed** during the launch.*
- *If developers **tested** their code more thoroughly, that bug **wouldn't have affected** millions of users.*

### Consejo práctico

Para identificar qué mixed conditional usar, pregunta: **¿El resultado es presente o pasado?** Si es presente → usa `would + infinitive`. Si es pasado → usa `would have + past participle`.

## 2. Passive Voice and Passive Reporting in Tech News

El **passive voice** es frecuente en noticias tecnológicas porque el foco está en la acción, no en quién la realiza. El **passive reporting** se usa para atribuir información a fuentes generales.

### Passive voice — Estructuras

| Tiempo | Estructura | Ejemplo tech |
|--------|-----------|--------------|
| Present simple | am/is/are + past participle | *The data **is encrypted** before transmission.* |
| Past simple | was/were + past participle | *The algorithm **was designed** to detect fraud.* |
| Present perfect | has/have been + past participle | *Several vulnerabilities **have been discovered** this quarter.* |
| Future | will be + past participle | *New features **will be rolled out** next month.* |
| Modal | modal + be + past participle | *This software **must be updated** immediately.* |

### Passive reporting

El passive reporting permite informar sin comprometer la fuente. Es muy usado en periodismo tecnológico y reportes corporativos.

**Estructuras comunes:**

- *It is believed that the data **was compromised** by an internal employee.*
- *It is reported that the company **has raised** $50 million in funding.*
- *It is thought that the outage **was caused** by a cyberattack.*
- *It is claimed that the new AI **can outperform** human doctors.*

### Diferencia entre passive voice y passive reporting

El **passive voice** se centra en la acción: *The server was hacked.* El **passive reporting** atribuye la información a una fuente vaga: *It is believed that the server was hacked.* Ambos son pasivos, pero el reporting tiene una capa adicional de distancia con la información.

## 3. Modals of Deduction for Tech Speculation

Los **modals of deduction** permiten especular sobre causas, consecuencias y estados de la tecnología con diferentes grados de certeza.

### Escala de certeza

| Nivel | Modal | Ejemplo tech |
|-------|-------|--------------|
| Certeza (~95%) | must | *The system **must have crashed** due to the overload.* |
| Probabilidad (~75%) | should / ought to | *The update **should have fixed** the security flaw.* |
| Posibilidad (~50%) | might / could | *The breach **might have been** caused by a phishing attack.* |
| Imposibilidad (~5%) | can't | *The breach **can't have been accidental** — too much data was stolen.* |

### Modals en contextos tecnológicos

- *The new feature **could revolutionise** how we use smartphones.*
- *AI **might eventually replace** certain types of manual labour.*
- *This app **must have been** designed with accessibility in mind.*
- *The company's security **can't have been** adequate — the breach was massive.*

### Consejo práctico

Cuando escuches o leas una noticia tecnológica, fíjate en qué modal usa el autor para expresar certeza o duda. Esto te ayuda a entender la postura del hablante y a usar los modals correctamente en tus propios textos.

## 4. Phrasal Verbs for Technology

Los **phrasal verbs** son frecuentes en conversaciones sobre tecnología. Aquí tienes los más importantes para el contexto B2.

| Phrasal verb | Significado | Ejemplo |
|-------------|-------------|---------|
| **roll out** | lanzar, implementar progresivamente | *The company will **roll out** the new update next week.* |
| **scale up** | ampliar, aumentar capacidad | *We need to **scale up** our servers to handle the traffic.* |
| **phase out** | eliminar gradualmente | *They plan to **phase out** older devices by 2027.* |
| **plug in** | conectar | *Just **plug in** the USB and the software will install automatically.* |
| **sync up** | sincronizar | *Make sure your phone **syncs up** with the cloud before travelling.* |
| **back up** | respaldar | *Always **back up** your data before updating the system.* |
| **run into** | encontrar (un problema) | *We **ran into** some compatibility issues during testing.* |
| **come up with** | idear, inventar | *The team **came up with** an innovative solution to the problem.* |
| **break down** | averiarse, fallar | *The server **broke down** during peak hours.* |
| **set up** | configurar, instalar | *It took hours to **set up** the new network infrastructure.* |

### Consejo práctico

Los phrasal verbs tech suelen ser separables: *The update was rolled out gradually* / *They rolled the update out gradually.* Cuando el objeto es un pronombre, siempre va entre el verbo y la partícula: *They rolled it out.*

## 5. Linkers and Cleft Sentences in Technical Writing

Los **linkers** conectan ideas y dan fluidez a textos técnicos. Los **cleft sentences** enfatizan información específica.

### Linkers esenciales para tecnología

| Linker | Función | Ejemplo |
|--------|---------|---------|
| **furthermore** | añadir información | *The system is fast. **Furthermore**, it is highly secure.* |
| **however** | contrastar | *The app is free. **However**, it contains ads.* |
| **consequently** | consecuencia | *The server was overloaded. **Consequently**, it crashed.* |
| **in addition** | añadir | *The software is reliable. **In addition**, it is open source.* |
| **nevertheless** | concesión | *The product had bugs. **Nevertheless**, it was a success.* |
| **as a result** | resultado | *Demand surged. **As a result**, the company expanded its team.* |

### Cleft sentences

Las cleft sentences reestructuran una oración para enfatizar un elemento. Son muy útiles en textos técnicos y académicos.

**Estructura:** `It + be + elemento enfatizado + that/who + resto de la oración`

- *It was **the algorithm** that identified the security flaw.* (énfasis en qué descubrió el fallo)
- *It is **data privacy** that concerns users the most.* (énfasis en qué preocupa)
- *It was **during the launch** that the system crashed.* (énfasis en cuándo ocurrió)
- *It was **the development team** who resolved the issue.* (énfasis en quién)

### Diferencia entre cleft y oración normal

- Normal: *The team resolved the issue during the launch.*
- Cleft: *It was **the team** who resolved the issue.* (énfasis en quién)
- Cleft: *It was **during the launch** that the issue was resolved.* (énfasis en cuándo)

## 7. Reading: A Tech Startup's Story

Lee el siguiente texto sobre una empresa emergente tecnológica.

> **From Garage to Global: The Rise of NovaTech**
>
> NovaTech was founded in 2019 by two university graduates who wanted to make cybersecurity accessible to small businesses. In the early days, the founders had little funding and worked from a garage. However, their idea was innovative: a subscription-based firewall service powered by artificial intelligence.
>
> By 2021, the company had scaled up significantly. Over 10,000 small businesses had signed up for the service, and NovaTech had raised $12 million in venture capital. It was the AI-driven detection system that attracted most investors, as it could identify threats in real time.
>
> In 2023, the company rolled out a major update that included machine learning capabilities. Although the update was praised by experts, some users ran into compatibility issues with older operating systems. Fortunately, the development team came up with a patch within 48 hours.
>
> Today, NovaTech serves clients in over 30 countries. The founders believe that if they hadn't taken the risk of starting the company, thousands of small businesses would still lack affordable cybersecurity protection.

**Vocabulario clave del texto:**

| Palabra | Significado |
|---------|------------|
| venture capital | capital de riesgo |
| subscription-based | basado en suscripción |
| detection system | sistema de detección |
| compatibility issues | problemas de compatibilidad |
| affordable | asequible |

## 9. Writing — Technical Reports

Un **technical report** en inglés sigue una estructura formal. Aquí tienes la plantilla y los linkers esenciales.

### Estructura de un reporte técnico

1. **Title** — Descriptive and clear
2. **Executive Summary** — Brief overview of findings
3. **Introduction** — Context and objectives
4. **Methodology** — How data was gathered
5. **Findings** — Results and data analysis
6. **Conclusions** — What the findings mean
7. **Recommendations** — Actionable next steps

### Linkers para reportes

| Sección | Linkers recomendados |
|---------|---------------------|
| Executive Summary | *In summary, ...* / *Overall, ...* |
| Findings | *Furthermore, ...* / *In addition, ...* / *It was observed that ...* |
| Conclusions | *Consequently, ...* / *As a result, ...* / *Therefore, ...* |
| Recommendations | *It is recommended that ...* / *It is suggested that ...* / *Nevertheless, ...* |

### Ejemplo de cleft sentence en un reporte

- Normal: *The firewall prevented the attack.*
- Cleft: *It was **the firewall** that prevented the attack.* (énfasis en el elemento clave)

## 11. Common Errors — Errores Comunes

Evita estos errores frecuentes al usar las estructuras B2 de esta unidad.

| # | Estructura | Error | Corrección | Explicación |
|---|-----------|-------|-----------|-------------|
| 1 | Mixed conditional | *If AI had developed earlier, healthcare **would have been** more advanced now.* | *If AI had developed earlier, healthcare **would be** more advanced now.* | El resultado es presente, no pasado: usa `would + infinitive`. |
| 2 | Passive voice | *The data has been compromise by hackers.* | *The data **has been compromised** by hackers.* | El pasado participio de "compromise" es "compromised". |
| 3 | Passive reporting | *It is believe that the system was hacked.* | *It is **believed** that the system was hacked.* | El participio es "believed", no "believe". |
| 4 | Modals of deduction | *The server must crashed due to overload.* | *The server **must have crashed** due to overload.* | Después de "must" para deduction en pasado, usa `have + past participle`. |
| 5 | Phrasal verb | *They rolled out the update it gradually.* | *They rolled the update out gradually.* | El pronombre va entre el verbo y la partícula: `rolled it out`. |
| 6 | Linker | *The software is fast. Furthermore, it is cheap. Furthermore, it is easy to use.* | *The software is fast. **Furthermore**, it is cheap. **In addition**, it is easy to use.* | No repitas el mismo linker dos veces seguidas. |
| 7 | Cleft sentence | *It was during the launch when the system crashed.* | *It was during the launch **that** the system crashed.* | En cleft sentences se usa "that", no "when". |
| 8 | Mixed conditional | *If we invested more, breaches **wouldn't increase** last year.* | *If we invested more, breaches **wouldn't have increased** last year.* | El resultado es pasado (last year): usa `would have + past participle`. |
