## 1. Mixed Conditionals in Health

Los *mixed conditionals* combinan tiempos diferentes en la condición y el resultado. En salud son especialmente útiles para hablar de consecuencias presentes de acciones pasadas o de resultados hipotéticos que afectan al presente.

### Type 1 — Past condition → Present result

> *If the patient **had followed** the prescription, she **would be** better now.*

La condición es pasada (no siguió la receta) y el resultado es presente (todavía no está bien).

### Type 2 — Present condition → Past result

> *If the doctor **were** more thorough, he **would have caught** the symptoms earlier.*

El doctor no es minucioso (presente) y por eso no detectó los síntomas a tiempo (pasado).

### Type 3 — Past condition → Past result (but emphasising present relevance)

> *If they **had developed** the vaccine sooner, thousands of lives **would have been saved**.*

### Other health examples

| Conditional | Example |
|---|---|
| Type 1 (past → present) | *If I **had eaten** less processed food, I **wouldn't have** high blood pressure now.* |
| Type 2 (present → past) | *If the NHS **were** better funded, the waiting lists **would have been** shorter last year.* |
| Mixed inversion | *Had the patient reported the pain, the diagnosis **would have been** earlier.* |

> **Consejo:** En exámenes B2, la clave es identificar **cuándo** ocurre la condición y **cuándo** ocurre el resultado. Si no coinciden en tiempo, necesitas un *mixed conditional*.

## 2. Passive Voice in Medical Reports

La voz pasiva es omnipresente en textos médicos porque el foco está en la acción y el paciente, no en quién la realiza.

### Structures

| Tense | Active | Passive |
|---|---|---|
| Past Simple | *The nurse administered the treatment.* | *The treatment **was administered** by the nurse.* |
| Present Perfect | *Doctors have diagnosed the condition.* | *The condition **has been diagnosed**.* |
| Past Perfect | *They had performed the surgery.* | *The surgery **had been performed**.* |
| Future Simple | *They will carry out more tests.* | *More tests **will be carried out**.* |
| Modals | *You should take the medication.* | *The medication **should be taken**.* |

### Health-specific examples

> *The patient **has been diagnosed** with Type 2 diabetes.*<br>
> *A new vaccine **is being tested** in clinical trials.*<br>
> *The operation **was carried out** by a specialist surgeon.*<br>
> *Three doses of the medication **should be taken** daily.*

> **Clave B2:** En informes médicos se omite frecuentemente el agente (*by the doctor*) porque es obvio por el contexto.

## 3. Passive Reporting in Health News

La *passive reporting* se usa en noticias de salud para presentar información sin atribuirla a una fuente concreta.

### Common structures

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| *It is believed that...* | ***It is believed that** the outbreak originated in a wet market.* |
| *...is believed to have...* | *The virus **is believed to have** mutated several times.* |
| *It is estimated that...* | ***It is estimated that** 1 in 3 adults suffers from hypertension.* |
| *...is thought to be...* | *Stress **is thought to be** a major factor in heart disease.* |
| *It is reported that...* | ***It is reported that** the new treatment has a 90% success rate.* |
| *...was reported to have...* | *The patient **was reported to have** made a full recovery.* |

### Combining with other structures

> *The vaccine, **which is believed to be** effective against the new variant, **will be administered** next week.*<br>
> ***It is estimated that** if people **exercised** regularly, the rate of obesity **would drop** significantly.*

## 4. Modals of Obligation and Deduction in Healthcare

### Obligation

| Modal | Strength | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *must* | Strong obligation | *Patients **must** take the full course of antibiotics.* |
| *have to* | External obligation | *You **have to** fast before the blood test.* |
| *should* | Advice | *You **should** reduce your salt intake.* |
| *ought to* | Slightly weaker advice | *You **ought to** see a specialist about that.* |
| *mustn't* | Prohibition | *You **mustn't** drink alcohol with this medication.* |
| *don't have to* | No obligation | *You **don't have to** stay overnight for this procedure.* |

### Deduction

| Modal | Certainty | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *must* | Near certain | *She **must be** exhausted after the surgery.* |
| *might / may* | Possible | *The symptoms **might indicate** a viral infection.* |
| *can't* | Impossible | *It **can't be** a fracture — the X-ray is clear.* |
| *could* | Theoretical possibility | *This treatment **could** revolutionise cancer care.* |

### Perfect modals (past deduction)

> *The doctor **might have recommended** a different approach.*<br>
> *She **must have forgotten** to take her medication.*<br>
> *He **can't have been** following the diet — his cholesterol is still high.*

## 5. Wish / Regret in Health Context

### Structure

| Form | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *wish* + past simple | Present regret | *I **wish** I **exercised** more often.* |
| *wish* + past perfect | Past regret | *I **wish** I **had started** exercising earlier.* |
| *wish* + would | Complaint / annoyance | *I **wish** the doctor **would** listen to my concerns.* |
| *regret* + -ing | Past action you're sorry about | *I **regret** not going to the doctor sooner.* |
| *If only...* | Stronger wish | ***If only** I had quit smoking years ago.* |

### Health examples

> *Many patients **wish** they **had sought** medical advice sooner.*<br>
> *I **regret** not taking my mental health seriously earlier.*<br>
> ***If only** the government **invested** more in preventive care.*<br>
> *She **wishes** the hospital **would** keep her better informed.*

## 6. Phrasal Verbs for Health

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *bring up* | Mention / introduce (a topic) | *She **brought up** her symptoms during the consultation.* |
| *carry out* | Perform (tests, procedures) | *The lab **carried out** a full blood analysis.* |
| *cut down on* | Reduce consumption | *He was advised to **cut down on** saturated fat.* |
| *go through* | Experience / endure | *She **went through** months of chemotherapy.* |
| *look into* | Investigate | *The NHS is **looking into** alternative treatments.* |
| *set up* | Establish / create | *They **set up** a new mental health clinic in the area.* |
| *take over* | Assume responsibility | *A specialist will **take over** the patient's care.* |
| *work out* | Exercise | *She **works out** at the gym three times a week.* |
| *come down with* | Become ill with | *He **came down with** the flu last week.* |
| *build up* | Increase gradually | *You need to **build up** your stamina slowly.* |

## 7. Linkers in Health Writing

### Cause / Effect

> *Due to a poor diet, the patient developed high cholesterol.*<br>
> *As a result of regular exercise, her blood pressure improved.*<br>
> *The increase in obesity is largely caused by sedentary lifestyles.*<br>
> *Consequently, the NHS has introduced new screening programmes.*

### Contrast

> *Although the treatment was effective, it had significant side effects.*<br>
> *Despite the advances in medicine, cancer remains difficult to cure.*<br>
> *However, not all patients respond to the same medication.*<br>
> *Nevertheless, prevention is always better than cure.*

### Addition

> *In addition to medication, patients should adopt a healthier diet.*<br>
> *Furthermore, mental health is just as important as physical health.*<br>
> *Moreover, regular check-ups can detect problems early.*<br>
> *Equally important is the role of sleep in overall wellbeing.*

> **Tip para writing B2:** Varía los linkers. No repitas *however* en cada párrafo. Alterna con *nevertheless*, *on the other hand*, *conversely*.

## 9. Reading — A Public Health Campaign

<Image src={healthCampaign} alt="Campaña de salud pública" />

> **Read the text about a new public health initiative.**

A major public health campaign has been launched across the UK to tackle the rising rates of obesity and Type 2 diabetes. The initiative, which is being funded by the Department of Health, aims to encourage healthier eating habits and increased physical activity among adults and children alike.

Health experts believe that if the government were to invest more in preventive measures, the long-term burden on the NHS would be significantly reduced. It is estimated that obesity-related conditions cost the NHS over £6 billion every year. As a result, the campaign focuses on three key areas: improving nutrition in school meals, making safe cycling routes more accessible, and providing free wellness checks in local communities.

Although the campaign has been welcomed by many healthcare professionals, some critics have pointed out that it does not go far enough. "We need stricter regulations on junk food advertising," said Dr. Sarah Mitchell, a public health specialist. "Without tackling the root causes, we're simply putting a plaster on a wound."

Despite these concerns, early reports suggest that the initiative is already having a positive impact. Attendance at free wellness checks has increased by 40% in the first month, and several schools have reported that children are choosing healthier options in the canteen.

> **Listen to a conversation between a patient (Emma) and her GP (Dr. Khan).**

**Dr. Khan:** Good morning, Emma. What seems to be the problem?

**Emma:** Well, Doctor, I've been feeling really tired lately, and I've been getting headaches almost every day.

**Dr. Khan:** I see. How long has this been going on?

**Emma:** About three weeks now. I thought it would go away, but it hasn't.

**Dr. Khan:** Have you been sleeping properly?

**Emma:** Not really. I've been working late, and I suppose I haven't been eating very well either.

**Dr. Khan:** Right. I'm going to refer you to a specialist, but first I'd like to carry out some blood tests. It's possible that you're deficient in iron or vitamin D.

**Emma:** Should I be worried?

**Dr. Khan:** It's nothing to panic about. If it were something serious, the symptoms would be more severe. But it's best to check. In the meantime, I'd recommend cutting down on caffeine and making sure you get at least seven hours of sleep.

**Emma:** I wish I had come in sooner, to be honest.

**Dr. Khan:** Well, the important thing is that you're here now. Let's get those tests done, and we'll take it from there.

## 11. Writing — Health Reports

En B2, los informes de salud requieren un tono formal, voz pasiva y passive reporting.

### Estructura típica

1. **Introduction:** Purpose and background
2. **Findings:** Data and observations (passive voice)
3. **Conclusions:** Deductions (modals)
4. **Recommendations:** Obligation and advice (should, must)

### Example extract

> *This report examines the effects of remote working on employee wellbeing. It is estimated that 60% of remote workers have reported increased levels of stress. The findings suggest that if companies were to implement structured wellness programmes, mental health issues could be significantly reduced. It is recommended that employees should be offered regular check-ups and access to counselling services.*

### Common health topics in B2 speaking exams

- Describe a healthy / unhealthy lifestyle
- Talk about your own health habits
- Discuss the pros and cons of a national health service
- Give advice about a health problem
- Discuss whether certain treatments should be free

### Useful phrases

> *I wish people would take their health more seriously.*<br>
> *If everyone exercised regularly, the rate of chronic disease would fall.*<br>
> *It is widely believed that prevention is better than cure.*<br>
> *You ought to consider cutting down on sugar.*<br>

## 13. Common Errors

| ❌ Error | ✅ Correct | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| *If I would have gone to the doctor...* | *If I **had gone** to the doctor...* | En conditionals, nunca se usa *would* en la cláusula condicional. |
| *The patient has been diagnosed from cancer.* | *The patient has been diagnosed **with** cancer.* | El verbo *diagnose* se combina con *with*. |
| *She must to take the medicine.* | *She **must take** the medicine.* | Los modales nunca llevan *to* infinitive. |
| *I wish I didn't smoke.* | *I wish I **didn't** smoke.* (OK for present) OR *I wish I **hadn't started** smoking.* (for past) | Asegúrate de que el tiempo del *wish* refleje si es presente o pasado. |
| *It is believe that...* | ***It is believed** that...* | Passive reporting necesita el participio (*believed*), no el infinitivo. |
| *He might have recommended different.* | *He might have recommended **something different**.* | En inglés, el adjetivo va después del pronombre indefinido. |
| *Despite of the side effects...* | ***Despite** the side effects...* / ***In spite of** the side effects...* | *Despite* no lleva *of*. *In spite of* sí. |
| *The symptoms was very mild.* | *The symptoms **were** very mild.* | *Symptoms* es plural. |

## 14. Resumen Rápido

| Structure | Key form | Health context |
|---|---|---|
| Mixed conditionals | past → present / present → past | Hypothetical health outcomes |
| Passive voice | was/have been + past participle | Medical reports and procedures |
| Passive reporting | It is believed that... / ...is thought to have... | Health news and research |
| Modals of obligation | must / should / have to / mustn't | Medical advice and rules |
| Modals of deduction | must / might / can't + base / have + pp | Diagnosing and guessing |
| Wish / regret | wish + past simple / past perfect / would | Reflecting on health choices |
| Phrasal verbs | bring up, carry out, cut down on... | Everyday health conversations |
| Linkers | although, due to, as a result of... | Health writing and reports |
