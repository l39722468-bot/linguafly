## Repaso B2 Unidades 51–54: Gramática, Vocabulario y Globalización

La **Unidad 55** consolida la gramática y el vocabulario de **U51–54**. La lección viva combina wish/if only, third conditional, mixed conditionals, passive voice, passive reporting, reported speech, modals of obligation/deduction, phrasal verbs, linkers of contrast/reason/addition y collocations de política y relaciones internacionales.

## 1. Un repaso de decisiones, no de etiquetas

Un error habitual al repasar es memorizar reglas aisladas en lugar de reconocer **qué decisión comunicativa** produce cada estructura. En B2, el examinador no busca que sepas nominar la estructura; busca que sepas **usarla en contexto**. Cada herramienta gramatical que hemos visto responde a una función concreta:

| Función comunicativa | Estructura (U51–54) | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| Expresar lamento o deseo irreal | Wish / If only + past perfect | *If only we had invested earlier.* |
| Hipótesis sobre el pasado | Third conditional | *We would have grown faster if the tariff had been lower.* |
| Escenario que mezcla pasado y presente | Mixed conditional | *If the policy had changed, we would be in a better position now.* |
| Destacar la acción, no el agente | Passive voice (todos los tiempos) | *New sanctions were imposed last week.* |
| Reportar fórmulas oficiales | Passive reporting | *It is believed that the economy will slow down.* |
| Trans ideas de terceros | Reported speech | *The minister said that unemployment had risen.* |
| Obligación / deducción | Modals (must, have to, should, might) | *They must have known about the risk.* |
| Acción verbal cotidiana | Phrasal verbs | *The government has set up a new committee.* |
| Conectar ideas | Linkers (although, despite, in addition) | *Although growth slowed, investment rose.* |
| Combinar palabras frecuentes | Collocations | *draw a conclusion, reach an agreement* |

Lee esta tabla como un mapa de decisiones, no como una lista para memorizar. Cuando hablas o escribes, la pregunta no es «¿qué estructura es?» sino **«¿qué quiero comunicar y qué herramienta me lleva allí?»**.

## 2. U51: Wish/If Only y Third Conditional — contexto de economía

En **U51** aprendimos a expresar deseos sobre el pasado (wish/if only + past perfect) y a construir hipótesis completas sobre situaciones pasadas (third conditional). Ambos apuntan al pasado, pero con matices distintos.

### 2.1 Wish / If only + past perfect — lamento

Se usa para **lamentar** algo que ya pasó o **desear** que el pasado hubiera sido diferente. El tono es subjetivo, personal.

- *I wish I **had saved** more money before the crisis.* (Lamento personal)
- *If only the government **had acted** sooner.* (Frustración / reproche)
- *She wishes she **hadn't invested** in that company.* (Arrepentimiento)

### 2.2 Third conditional — hipótesis sobre el pasado

El third conditional construye una **realidad alternativa** completa: si X hubiera pasado, Y habría sido el resultado. No hay juicio emocional; es una hipótesis lógica.

- *If the central bank **had raised** rates, inflation **would have dropped**.*
- *If trade barriers **had not existed**, the company **would have expanded** into Asia.*
- *Had the agreement been signed earlier, we **would have avoided** the losses.*

### 2.3 Mixed conditional — pasado → presente

Mezcla un if-clause en pasado perfect con un resultado en condicional simple. Es decir: una decisión del pasado que afecta **ahora**.

- *If the reforms **had been implemented**, the economy **would be** stronger today.*
- *If we **had signed** the deal, we **wouldn't be** in this situation now.*

### 2.4 Contraste: wish vs. third conditional

| | Wish / If only | Third conditional |
|---|---|---|
| **Función** | Lamento, deseo emocional | Hipótesis lógica, causa-efecto |
| **Tono** | Subjetivo, a menudo frustrado | Analítico, neutral |
| **Resultado** | No especifica qué habría cambiado | Especifica el resultado alternativo |
| **Ejemplo** | *I wish I had negotiated harder.* | *If I had negotiated harder, I would have got a better deal.* |

> **Pista de función:** Si la frase suena a quejido → wish. Si suena a análisis → third conditional.

## 3. U52: Passive Voice, Passive Reporting y Reported Speech — contexto de justicia

**U52** giró en torno a **quién hace la acción y quién la recibe**, y a cómo reportamos lo que otros dicen o creen.

### 3.1 Passive voice — todos los tiempos

La voz pasiva se construye con **be + past participle** y se usa cuando el **receptor** de la acción es más importante que el agente, o cuando el agente es desconocido/irrelevante.

| Tiempo | Ejemplo (justicia) |
|---|---|
| Present simple | *Laws **are enforced** by the authorities.* |
| Present continuous | *The case **is being investigated** right now.* |
| Past simple | *The verdict **was announced** yesterday.* |
| Past continuous | *The evidence **was being examined** when the appeal was filed.* |
| Present perfect | *Several charges **have been brought** against the defendant.* |
| Past perfect | *The documents **had been destroyed** before the audit.* |
| Future simple | *A new inquiry **will be launched** next month.* |
| Modals | *The sentence **could be reduced** on appeal.* |

### 3.2 Passive reporting — fórmulas institucionales

Las construcciones de reporting pasivo son **it + passive + that / to infinitive**. Son habituales en noticias, informes y contextos formales.

- ***It is believed** that the suspect has fled the country.*
- ***It is reported** that the trial will begin in September.*
- ***He is said to have left** the country before the warrant was issued.*
- ***She is thought to be** the key witness.*

### 3.3 Reported speech — transformación completa

El reported speech requiere **backshift** de tiempos y ajustes de pronombres y deícticos.

| Directo | Indirecto (backshift) |
|---|---|
| "I **am** innocent," he said. | He said he **was** innocent. |
| "The trial **will start** Monday," the lawyer stated. | The lawyer stated the trial **would start** the following Monday. |
| "We **have reached** a verdict," the foreman announced. | The foreman announced they **had reached** a verdict. |
| "I **was** at the scene," she claimed. | She claimed she **had been** at the scene. |

> **Pista de función:** Si la fuente es una institución o un periódico → passive reporting. Si estás citando a alguien directamente → reported speech.

## 4. U53: Modals of Obligation/Deduction y Phrasal Verbs — contexto de carrera profesional

**U53** combinó modales (obligación y deducción) con phrasal verbs de uso frecuente en el ámbito laboral.

### 4.1 Modals of obligation

| Modal | Fuerza | Ejemplo (carrera) |
|---|---|---|
| **must** | Obligación interna, regla firme | *You **must** submit the report by Friday.* |
| **have to** | Obligación externa, regla institucional | *We **have to** follow the company's compliance policy.* |
| **should** | Recomendación, consejo profesional | *You **should** update your CV before the interview.* |
| **mustn't** | Prohibición | *You **mustn't** share confidential data.* |
| **don't have to** | No hay obligación | *You **don't have to** wear a tie on casual Fridays.* |

### 4.2 Modals of deduction

| Modal | Certeza | Ejemplo (deducción) |
|---|---|---|
| **must** | Alta certeza (90%+) | *She **must be** the new project manager — everyone is following her instructions.* |
| **might / may** | Posibilidad media | *He **might have missed** the deadline because of the meeting.* |
| **can't** | Imposibilidad | *They **can't have finished** the audit already — it only started yesterday.* |

### 4.3 Phrasal verbs — ámbito profesional

Los phrasal verbs son una barrera clásica para hablantes no nativos. En B2 debes conocer los más frecuentes y reconocer si son **separables** o **inseparables**.

**Separables** (el pronombre va entre el verbo y la partícula):
- *carry out* → *The team carried out the analysis.* → *They carried **it** out.*
- *set up* → *We set up the meeting.* → *We set **it** up.*
- *bring up* → *She brought up the issue.* → *She brought **it** up.*
- *work out* → *Can you work out the budget?* → *Can you work **it** out?*

**Inseparables** (el pronombre va después de la partícula):
- *come across* → *I came across an interesting report.*
- *run into* → *We ran into a problem with the software.*
- *look into* → *The manager will look into the complaint.*
- *stick to* → *We need to stick to the original plan.*

> **Pista de función:** Si el contexto exige obligación → must / have to / should. Si exige deducción → must / might / can't + perfecto. Si es una acción cotidiana verbal → phrasal verb.

## 5. U54: Linkers y Collocations — contexto de geopolítica

**U54** cerró el bloque con conectores discursivos y collocations del campo de relaciones internacionales.

### 5.1 Linkers de contraste

- ***Although** the sanctions were imposed, trade continued to grow.*
- ***Despite** the political tensions, both countries signed the agreement.*
- ***However**, the long-term effects remain unclear.*
- ***Even though** the economy slowed, employment figures improved.*
- ***Nevertheless**, the government proceeded with the reforms.*

### 5.2 Linkers de causa y razón

- ***Because of** the energy crisis, manufacturing output declined.*
- ***Due to** rising interest rates, foreign investment has decreased.*
- ***As a result**, the central bank was forced to intervene.*
- ***Owing to** the conflict, supply chains were disrupted.*
- ***Since** the agreement was signed, bilateral trade has doubled.*

### 5.3 Linkers de adición

- ***In addition**, the treaty includes environmental provisions.*
- ***Moreover**, the cultural exchange has strengthened diplomatic ties.*
- ***Furthermore**, the economic data supports the decision.*
- ***Also**, both nations have committed to renewable energy targets.*
- ***What's more**, the partnership extends to defence cooperation.*

### 5.4 Collocations — relaciones internacionales

| Collocation | Significado | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| **forge an alliance** | crear una alianza | *The two nations forged a strong alliance.* |
| **reach an agreement** | llegar a un acuerdo | *They finally reached an agreement on tariffs.* |
| **impose sanctions** | imponer sanciones | *The UN imposed sanctions on the regime.* |
| **conduct diplomacy** | llevar a cabo diplomacia | *The country conducted quiet diplomacy behind the scenes.* |
| **tackle a crisis** | abordar una crisis | *Leaders gathered to tackle the economic crisis.* |
| **draw a conclusion** | llegar a una conclusión | *The committee drew the conclusion that reform was needed.* |
| **lift restrictions** | levantar restricciones | *The government lifted travel restrictions last month.* |
| **broker a peace deal** | negociar un acuerdo de paz | *The mediator brokered a historic peace deal.* |

### The Hidden Costs of Globalisation

Globalisation has transformed the world economy over the past four decades, yet its consequences are far more complex than most policymakers anticipated. While trade liberalisation has lifted millions out of poverty, it has also **created** deep inequalities between and within nations.

Economists **have pointed out** that the benefits of global trade are unevenly distributed. If developing countries **had received** fairer terms of trade in the 1990s, the gap between rich and poor nations **would not have widened** as dramatically. This is a third conditional that reflects a genuine frustration among development experts.

In many cases, governments **were pressured** by international institutions to adopt structural adjustment programmes. These policies **were supposed to** stimulate growth, but they **were later found** to have increased unemployment and reduced public services. The passive voice is used here because the focus is on what happened to the populations, not on who imposed the policies.

**It is now widely recognised** that the free market model has significant limitations. **It is argued** that without stronger regulation, globalisation **can** lead to exploitation of workers and environmental degradation. The passive reporting structures here signal institutional consensus rather than personal opinion.

Some economists **must have underestimated** the social costs of rapid liberalisation. If they **had studied** the effects on local industries more carefully, better safeguards **could have been** implemented. The modal of deduction "must have" expresses a strong inference about past negligence.

**Despite** the criticisms, globalisation **has brought** undeniable advantages: access to technology, cultural exchange, and economic interdependence that **in addition** reduces the likelihood of armed conflict. **Moreover**, the spread of democratic values has been partly facilitated by global media and communication networks.

The challenge now is not to reverse globalisation but to **tackle** its negative effects more effectively. Leaders **should** invest in education, healthcare, and social safety nets so that the benefits are shared more equitably. They **have to** acknowledge that unregulated markets **cannot** solve every problem.

In conclusion, the story of globalisation is one of trade-offs. If we **had approached** it from the beginning with a focus on inclusiveness rather than efficiency alone, we **would be** living in a more balanced world today — a mixed conditional that captures both the regret of the past and the reality of the present.

## 9. Errores cruzados y estrategia

Los errores más frecuentes en B2 no son de una sola unidad: aparecen **cruzados** entre varias. Aquí tienes los ocho errores más comunes y cómo evitarlos.

| # | Error frecuente | Corrección | Unidad |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | *I wish I **would have saved** more money.* | *I wish I **had saved** more money.* | U51 |
| 2 | *If the law **was passed**, we **would have benefited**.* | *If the law **had been passed**, we **would have benefited**.* | U51+U52 |
| 3 | *The report **was wrote** by the team.* | *The report **was written** by the team.* | U52 |
| 4 | *She said that she **will** attend the summit.* | *She said that she **would** attend the summit.* (backshift) | U52 |
| 5 | *You **must to** submit the application.* | *You **must** submit the application.* | U53 |
| 6 | *He **can't have knew** about the decision.* | *He **can't have known** about the decision.* | U53 |
| 7 | *Although **of** the difficulties, we succeeded.* | *Although we faced difficulties, we succeeded.* / *Despite the difficulties, we succeeded.* | U54 |
| 8 | *They reached **to** an agreement.* | *They reached an agreement.* (sin preposición) | U54 |

**Estrategia:** Antes de escribir o hablar, clasifica mentalmente la estructura que necesitas en una de estas categorías:

1. **Lamento / deseo** → wish / if only
2. **Hipótesis pasada completa** → third conditional
3. **Pasado que afecta al presente** → mixed conditional
4. **Acción sin agente** → passive
5. **Fuente institucional** → passive reporting
6. **Cita de tercero** → reported speech
7. **Obligación / deducción** → modals
8. **Acción verbal diaria** → phrasal verb

## 10. Del reconocimiento a la producción B2

The rapid pace of globalisation has fundamentally reshaped the world of work. While some economists argue that it has created unprecedented opportunities, others point out that it has also generated significant challenges for workers in both developed and developing countries.

In the past three decades, international trade has expanded dramatically. Manufacturing jobs that were once based in Europe and North America have been outsourced to countries with lower labour costs. Although this has boosted economic growth in emerging markets, it has led to unemployment and social instability in many industrial regions. If governments had invested more in retraining programmes, many displaced workers would have found new opportunities more quickly.

Technology has further accelerated these changes. Artificial intelligence and automation are expected to replace millions of jobs within the next decade. It is estimated that up to 30% of current jobs could be automated by 2030. However, new industries are also being created, and it is believed that the net effect on employment will be broadly neutral — provided that workers are equipped with the right skills.

The gig economy is another consequence of globalisation. Platforms like Uber and Deliveroo have enabled millions of people to work flexibly, but critics argue that this has come at the cost of job security and workers' rights. Many gig workers must pay their own taxes, insurance, and sick leave — obligations that were traditionally handled by employers.

Governments must strike a balance between embracing economic openness and protecting their citizens. They should invest in education and reskilling, impose fair taxation on multinational corporations, and ensure that trade agreements include strong labour protections. If the benefits of globalisation are not shared more equitably, social unrest is likely to grow.

In conclusion, the future of work depends on the choices that policymakers make today. If we had prioritised inclusive growth from the beginning, we would not be facing such deep divisions in our societies. The challenge is clear: we have to build a global economy that works for everyone, not just for the privileged few.

Dear Sir or Madam,

I am writing regarding the recent changes in trade policy. __________ the government's intention to protect local industry, these measures have had unintended consequences. __________, many small businesses have been unable to adapt. __________ the new tariffs, import costs have risen sharply. __________, some exporters have benefited from reduced competition. __________, a more balanced approach is needed.

**Exercise W8.** Write a short opinion piece (100–120 words) on whether globalisation benefits everyone. Use at least:

## Tip

> **Consejo de estudio:** Cuando repases estas unidades, no intentes memorizar todas las estructuras a la vez. Elige una familia gramatical (condicionales, pasiva, modales o conectores) y practica esa familia durante 15 minutos. Luego cambia. La repetición espaciada — estudiar, esperar 24 horas y volver a practicar — es la forma más efectiva de consolidar lo aprendido a largo plazo.

## Preguntas frecuentes

### ¿Qué gramática se repasa en la Unidad 55?

Integra U51–54: wish/if only + past perfect, third conditional, mixed conditional, passive all tenses, passive reporting, reported speech, modals of obligation/deduction, phrasal verbs, linkers y collocations.

### ¿Cómo debo estudiar el repaso?

Clasifica por familia gramatical antes de completar. Identifica la pista de función: lamento, hipótesis, atribución, obligación, deducción, contraste, causa o adición.

### ¿También se repasa vocabulario?

Sí. Integra economy, justice, career, politics e international relations de U51–54.

### ¿Cuánto tiempo debo dedicar al repaso?

Al menos dos sesiones de 20 minutos con un intervalo de 24–48 horas para consolidar la retención a largo plazo.

### ¿Dónde practico la Unidad 55?
