La **Unidad 10 del curso B2** cierra el primer módulo del curso integrando todo lo trabajado en las cuatro unidades anteriores: [wish e if only](/blog/curso-b2/unidad-6-wish-if-only-feelings), [would rather, would prefer e it's time](/blog/curso-b2/unidad-7-would-rather-family), [mixed conditionals](/blog/curso-b2/unidad-8-mixed-conditionals-travel) y [participle clauses](/blog/curso-b2/unidad-9-participle-clauses-environment). En lugar de presentar gramática nueva, esta unidad se centra en algo igual de importante en el nivel B2: **reconocer qué estructura usar** cuando varias parecen posibles a primera vista, y no confundir unas con otras.

Este tipo de repaso integrado es exactamente lo que se exige en la parte de *use of English* de exámenes como el Cambridge B2 First: frases sueltas, sin contexto gramatical explícito, en las que debes decidir tú mismo/a qué tiempo o estructura corresponde. Por eso, en vez de repetir explicaciones ya vistas, esta guía combina las cuatro estructuras en ejemplos mezclados, un texto de lectura y un diálogo que las usan todas juntas, tal como aparecerían en una conversación o un texto real.

También repasarás el vocabulario temático de las cuatro unidades — **feelings, family, travel y environment** — reutilizando las imágenes y el enfoque de cada unidad para reforzar la memoria visual y semántica antes de avanzar al siguiente módulo del curso, centrado en relative clauses.

## Qué aprenderás en la Unidad 10 (B2)

Al terminar este repaso serás capaz de:

- Reconocer y producir correctamente **wish / if only** en presente y en pasado.

## Repaso de gramática: las cuatro estructuras

### 1. Wish / If only (Unidad 6)

Recuerda la pregunta clave: **¿el deseo es sobre el presente o sobre el pasado?** Si es sobre el presente, usa **wish + past simple / were** (*I wish I had more time*) o **wish + could** (*I wish I could play better*). Si es sobre el pasado, usa **wish / if only + past perfect** (*If only I had booked the tickets earlier*). Y si te quejas del comportamiento de otra persona, usa **wish + would** (*I wish my brother would help more at home*).

<audio controls preload="none" src="/audio/blog/curso-b2/unit-10/review-wish.mp3" title="🔊 Repaso: wish / if only"></audio>

### 2. Would rather / Would prefer / It's time (Unidad 7)

Recuerda que **would rather** va con **infinitivo sin *to*** cuando hablas de tu propia preferencia (*I would rather travel by train*) y con **sujeto + pasado** cuando hablas de la preferencia sobre otra persona (*I would rather you came early*). **Would prefer**, en cambio, siempre lleva **to + infinitivo** (*She would prefer to celebrate with family*). **It's (high) time + pasado** expresa urgencia sobre algo que ya deberíamos estar haciendo (*It is time we reduced our carbon footprint*).

<audio controls preload="none" src="/audio/blog/curso-b2/unit-10/review-rather.mp3" title="🔊 Repaso: would rather / would prefer"></audio>

<audio controls preload="none" src="/audio/blog/curso-b2/unit-10/review-time.mp3" title="🔊 Repaso: it's time"></audio>

### 3. Mixed conditionals (Unidad 8)

Traza mentalmente una línea de tiempo: si la **causa** está en el pasado y el **resultado** se siente ahora (o pronto), usa **if + past perfect, would + infinitivo** (*If we had left earlier, we would be at the museum now*). Si la causa es una característica **estable del presente** y el resultado ya ocurrió en el pasado, usa **if + past simple/were, would have + participio** (*If I were taller, I would have joined the team*).

<audio controls preload="none" src="/audio/blog/curso-b2/unit-10/review-mixed.mp3" title="🔊 Repaso: mixed conditionals"></audio>

### 4. Participle clauses (Unidad 9)

Pregúntate primero si el sujeto **hace** la acción (-ing: *Walking along the river, they collected litter*) o la **recibe** (-ed: *Encouraged by the campaign, they recycle more*). Si una acción terminó **antes** que otra, usa **having + participio** (*Having read the guide, they chose the eco-friendly tour*). Y comprueba siempre que el sujeto de la cláusula reducida coincide con el de la cláusula principal.

<audio controls preload="none" src="/audio/blog/curso-b2/unit-10/review-participle.mp3" title="🔊 Repaso: participle clauses"></audio>

### 5. Cómo no confundir las cuatro estructuras

Estas cuatro estructuras se parecen porque todas usan formas de **pasado** con un significado que no es realmente pasado, pero cada una responde a una pregunta distinta. Wish/if only responde a "¿qué deseo o lamento?". Would rather/prefer/it's time responde a "¿qué prefiero o qué ya debería estar pasando?". Mixed conditionals responde a "¿qué condición produce qué resultado, y en qué momento?". Y participle clauses no expresa deseo ni condición, sino que **reduce** una oración más larga en una sola cláusula compacta. Antes de construir la frase, identifica primero qué tipo de idea quieres comunicar, y la estructura correcta se vuelve mucho más evidente.

## Errores típicos al mezclar las cuatro estructuras

| ❌ Incorrecto | ✅ Correcto | Unidad |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| I wish I have booked earlier. | I wish I **had booked** earlier. | 6 — wish + past perfect |
| I would rather to travel by bus. | I would rather **travel** by bus. | 7 — rather sin *to* |
| If we leave early, we would be there now. | If we **had left** early, we would be there now. | 8 — mixed: past perfect en la condición |
| Having read, we chose the tour. | Having **read** the guide, we chose the tour. | 9 — having + participio + complemento |
| It is time we reduce emissions. | It is time we **reduced** emissions. | 7 — it's time + pasado |
| If only they take the early bus! | If only they **had taken** the early bus! | 6 — if only + past perfect |
| Encourage by the campaign, they recycle more. | **Encouraged** by the campaign, they recycle more. | 9 — participio pasado |
| I'd rather you drive so fast. | I'd rather you **didn't drive** so fast. | 7 — negación con rather |

## Reading: Olivia's module review

![Ejemplos de repaso combinados](/blog/curso-b2/unit-10/review-examples.png)

<audio controls preload="none" src="/audio/blog/curso-b2/unit-10/reading-mix.mp3" title="🔊 Escucha las frases clave del reading"></audio>

> Olivia's family is reviewing everything they learned during the first module of their English course, and she has decided to plan a trip that puts all four structures into practice. She wishes she had listened to the travel podcast before the trip, because now she keeps mixing up dates and destinations. She would rather take the train than fly for short distances, even though her partner would prefer to fly whenever possible. If they had left home earlier this morning, they would not be queuing at the station right now — a classic mixed conditional connecting a past decision with a present consequence. Having joined a local green group last year, the whole family now recycles more carefully and thinks twice before buying single-use plastics. Walking to the station together, Olivia realised that grammar, like family life, works best when every part fits naturally with the others. It is high time, she thought, that they talked more openly about their feelings and their plans for the next trip.

**Comprueba tu comprensión:** localiza en el texto un ejemplo de wish, uno de would rather, uno de mixed conditional y uno de participle clause.

## Diálogo: mezclando las cuatro estructuras

<audio controls preload="none" src="/audio/blog/curso-b2/unit-10/dialogue-mix.mp3" title="🔊 Diálogo: Olivia mixes U6–9"></audio>

> **A:** I wish I had booked the tickets earlier — we missed the concert.
> **B:** That's a shame. I would rather travel by train than drive, though.
> **A:** If we had taken the early bus, we would be at the museum now.
> **B:** True. Having read the guide, we chose the eco-friendly tour instead.
> **A:** Good choice. It is time we reduced our carbon footprint as a family.
> **B:** I agree. Disturbed by all the plastic on the beach last year, I started recycling properly.
> **A:** I wish more people would do the same.
> **B:** If only everyone thought about it as much as you do!
> **A:** Well, it's high time we all made small changes.
> **B:** Walking home now, let's plan our next eco-friendly trip together.

## Ejercicios de repaso

### Ejercicio 1 — Identifica la estructura

Indica si cada frase es **wish**, **rather/prefer/time**, **mixed** o **participle**.

1. I wish I had booked the tickets earlier.
2. I would rather travel by train than fly.
3. If we had left earlier, we would be there now.
4. Having read the guide, we chose the eco tour.
5. It is time we reduced our footprint.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **wish** · 2. **rather** · 3. **mixed** · 4. **participle** · 5. **time**
</details>

### Ejercicio 2 — Completa con la forma correcta

1. I wish I ___ (book) the tickets earlier.
2. I would rather ___ (travel) by train than fly.
3. If we had left earlier, we ___ (be) at the museum now.
4. ___ (read) the guide, we chose the eco tour.
5. It is time we ___ (reduce) our carbon footprint.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **had booked** · 2. **travel** · 3. **would be** · 4. **Having read** · 5. **reduced**
</details>

### Ejercicio 3 — Corrige el error

1. *I wish I have booked earlier.*
2. *I would rather to travel by bus.*
3. *If we leave early, we would be there now.*
4. *Having read, we chose the tour.*
5. *It is time we reduce emissions.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. I wish I **had booked** earlier.
2. I would rather **travel** by bus.
3. If we **had left** early, we would be there now.
4. Having **read the guide**, we chose the tour.
5. It is time we **reduced** emissions.
</details>

4. ___ by the campaign, they recycle more. → *Encourage / Encouraged / Encouraging*
5. I'd rather you ___ drive so fast. → *don't / didn't / not*

<summary>Ver solución</summary>

## Tip del profesor

En el repaso, no te limites a completar huecos: antes de escribir la respuesta, **nombra en voz alta la estructura** (wish, rather/prefer/time, mixed o participle). Este pequeño paso de "etiquetar" antes de responder es lo que realmente evita que mezcles wish con mixed conditionals, o would rather con would prefer — el tipo de confusión que más puntos resta en el writing de los exámenes B2.
