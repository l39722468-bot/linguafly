Ya dominas la pasiva en los ocho tiempos verbales tras la [Unidad 16](/blog/curso-b2/unidad-16-passive-all-tenses-heritage). Ahora toca combinarla con los **modal verbs** para hablar de obligaciones, recomendaciones y críticas: **must/should + be + past participle**. Además, aprenderás una estructura clave del inglés cotidiano, la **causativa have something done**, perfecta para hablar de servicios que otra persona hace por ti (revisar el equipo, reparar un traje de neopreno, inspeccionar una cuerda). Todo esto lo practicarás con vocabulario de **adventure & extreme sports**: expediciones, escalada, paracaidismo y kayak.

Este bloque de gramática es extremadamente útil en contextos de seguridad, normativa y responsabilidad —desde manuales de instrucciones hasta noticias sobre accidentes— por lo que aparece constantemente en exámenes de nivel B2 como el First Certificate.

## 1. Modal passive: modal + be + past participle

El modal passive combina cualquier verbo modal (*must, should, can, could, might, may, have to, ought to*) con la voz pasiva. La fórmula es sencilla y no cambia nunca:

```
sujeto + MODAL + be + past participle (V3)
```

> Gear **must be tested** before every expedition.  
> Harnesses **must be inspected** before each climb.  
> This kind of equipment **should be replaced** every two years.  
> The route **can be dangerous** if the weather changes suddenly.  
> Beginners **might be injured** without proper training.

<audio controls preload="none" src="/audio/blog/curso-b2/unit-17/must-be-tested.mp3" title="🔊 Gear must be tested before every expedition"></audio>

<audio controls preload="none" src="/audio/blog/curso-b2/unit-17/must-be-inspected.mp3" title="🔊 Harnesses must be inspected before each climb"></audio>

Nota que, igual que en la Unidad 16, el past participle **no cambia**: solo cambia el modal que colocas delante de *be*. Si ya sabes decir *you must check the gear* (activa), pasar a pasiva es tan sencillo como convertir *check* en *be checked*: *the gear must be checked*.

### Distintos matices según el modal

| Modal + be + V3 | Matiz | Ejemplo |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **must be** | obligación fuerte / norma | Gear **must be tested** before use. |
| **have to be** | obligación externa | The route **has to be approved** by a guide. |
| **should be** | recomendación | Ropes **should be replaced** regularly. |
| **can/could be** | posibilidad | The climb **can be risky** in winter. |
| **might/may be** | posibilidad más incierta | The summit **might be closed** due to snow. |

## 2. *Should have been + V3*: crítica sobre el pasado

Cuando algo **debería haber ocurrido** en el pasado y **no ocurrió**, usamos **should have been + past participle**. Es una de las estructuras más rentables del nivel B2 porque combina crítica, arrepentimiento y voz pasiva en una sola fórmula:

> The safety report **should have been published** earlier — the team lost valuable time.  
> The harness **should have been checked** before the accident.  
> Those warnings **should have been taken** more seriously.

<audio controls preload="none" src="/audio/blog/curso-b2/unit-17/should-have-been.mp3" title="🔊 The safety report should have been published earlier"></audio>

Compara los tres niveles de tiempo y significado:

| Estructura | Cuándo | Ejemplo |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| must be + V3 | obligación ahora/futuro | The gear **must be tested** now. |
| should be + V3 | recomendación general | The gear **should be tested** regularly. |
| should have been + V3 | crítica del pasado | The gear **should have been tested**, but it wasn't. |

En español, *should have been* equivale a "debería haberse hecho" o "tendría que haberse hecho", con ese matiz de reproche que en inglés se transmite solo con el orden de las palabras: *should* + *have* + *been* + participio.

## 3. Have something done: la estructura causativa

Cuando **otra persona realiza una acción por ti** —normalmente porque la pagas, la encargas o simplemente no la haces tú mismo— usamos la estructura causativa **have + objeto + past participle**:

```
sujeto + have/get + objeto + past participle
```

> I **am having** my parachute **serviced** next week.  
> She **had** her wetsuit **repaired** yesterday after a long dive.  
> He **has** his climbing ropes **checked** every six months.

<audio controls preload="none" src="/audio/blog/curso-b2/unit-17/having-serviced.mp3" title="🔊 I am having my parachute serviced next week"></audio>

<audio controls preload="none" src="/audio/blog/curso-b2/unit-17/had-repaired.mp3" title="🔊 She had her wetsuit repaired yesterday"></audio>

<audio controls preload="none" src="/audio/blog/curso-b2/unit-17/has-ropes-checked.mp3" title="🔊 He has his climbing ropes checked every six months"></audio>

Fíjate en el orden: **have + objeto + participio**, nunca *have + participio + objeto*. Compara:

| ❌ Orden incorrecto | ✅ Orden correcto |
| :--- | :--- |
| *I have serviced my parachute* (esto significa que TÚ lo revisaste) | *I have my parachute serviced* (otra persona lo revisa) |
| *She had repaired her wetsuit* (esto es past perfect activo: ella misma lo reparó) | *She had her wetsuit repaired* (alguien lo reparó para ella) |

*Have* funciona en cualquier tiempo verbal: *I have my gear checked* (presente), *I had my gear checked* (pasado), *I am having my gear checked* (en curso), *I will have my gear checked* (futuro). El participio al final se mantiene siempre igual.

### *Get* como alternativa informal

En inglés hablado, *get* sustituye a menudo a *have* sin cambiar el significado, con un registro algo más coloquial:

> I **got** my harness **checked** yesterday. *(= I had my harness checked)*  
> We need to **get** the ropes **replaced** before the next trip.

### Bonus: *need + -ing* = *need to be + V3*

Otra forma muy natural de expresar una obligación pasiva es **need + verbo en -ing**, que significa exactamente lo mismo que **need to be + past participle**. Es habitual cuando hablamos del estado de un objeto que requiere mantenimiento:

> This rope **needs replacing**. = This rope **needs to be replaced**.  
> The harness **needs checking** before the trip. = The harness **needs to be checked** before the trip.

Ambas versiones son igual de correctas y aparecen constantemente en manuales de mantenimiento de equipo, así que merece la pena reconocer las dos formas aunque solo uses una de ellas al hablar.

## 5. Reading: Leo before the expedition

![Adventure in context](/blog/curso-b2/unit-17/adventure-scene.png)

> Leo has been organising extreme sports expeditions for almost ten years, and he never takes safety for granted. All climbing gear must be tested before every expedition, without exception. Last year, one of his colleagues was injured because a harness should have been inspected but nobody checked it in time — a mistake Leo never wants to repeat. This week, he is having his parachute serviced before a jump scheduled for Saturday, and he had his wetsuit repaired after a long dive at the coast. Every rope at base camp must be inspected by a certified inspector, and ropes should be replaced every two years regardless of how they look. Leo also has his kayak checked twice a season, because he knows that even experienced thrill-seekers can get injured if equipment fails. "The adrenaline is great," he always says, "but it should never come before safety."

## 6. Diálogo: Checking the gear

> **Ana:** And what about your wetsuit? Didn't it tear last week?  
> **Leo:** Yes, but I had it repaired on Monday.

> **Leo:** They must be inspected by the safety officer before we use them.

> **Leo:** That's right — it should have been checked, but nobody did it.  
> **Ana:** That's exactly why we should never skip these steps.  
> **Leo:** Agreed. Safety should always come before adrenaline.

## 7. Errores típicos de hispanohablantes

| ❌ Incorrecto | ✅ Correcto | Explicación |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *Gear must tested before the climb.* | *Gear **must be tested** before the climb.* | Falta **be** entre el modal y el participio. |
| *The report should have published earlier.* | *The report **should have been published** earlier.* | Falta **been**: sin él no es pasiva. |
| *I am having my parachute service.* | *I am having my parachute **serviced**.* | El verbo va en participio (V3), no en infinitivo. |
| *I have serviced my parachute* (queriendo decir que otro lo hizo) | *I **have** my parachute **serviced**.* | Con *have + serviced* dices que lo hiciste tú mismo. |
| *She had repaired her wetsuit* (queriendo decir causativa) | *She **had** her wetsuit **repaired**.* | Orden: have + objeto + participio, no have + participio. |
| *Harnesses must inspect before climbs.* | *Harnesses **must be inspected** before climbs.* | Falta la voz pasiva completa: **be** + V3. |
| *He has checked his ropes every six months* (activa, cuando fue otro quien lo hizo) | *He **has** his ropes **checked** every six months.* | La causativa necesita *have + objeto + V3*. |
| *It should be checked already* (para crítica del pasado) | *It **should have been checked** already.* | Crítica del pasado → should have been, no should be. |

## 9. Practica en voz alta

<audio controls preload="none" src="/audio/blog/curso-b2/unit-17/practice-u17.mp3" title="🔊 Practice: modal passive and have something done"></audio>

> All climbing gear must be tested before the expedition. The safety report should have been published earlier. I am having my parachute serviced next week. She had her wetsuit repaired yesterday. He has his climbing ropes checked every six months. Harnesses must be inspected before each climb.

### Mini-diálogos para memorizar

> **A:** Is the equipment ready for tomorrow?  
> **B:** Almost — the ropes need replacing, so I'm getting them changed today.

> **A:** What happened to your wetsuit?  
> **B:** It tore last week, so I had it repaired on Monday.

> **A:** Whose fault was the accident?  
> **B:** Honestly, the harness should have been inspected. Nobody checked it in time.

Cambia solo el objeto (*rope, wetsuit, harness, parachute*) y el verbo (*replace, repair, check, service*): la estructura se mantiene igual.

## Tip del profesor

Pregúntate siempre: ¿**quién** hace la acción? Si nadie importa o es obvio → **modal + be + V3** (*gear must be tested*). Si **tú pagas o encargas** a otra persona que la haga → **have/get + objeto + V3** (*I had my gear tested*). Y si algo **no se hizo** en el pasado y debería haberse hecho → **should have been + V3**.

## Lección 1 — Gramática

**Objetivo:** must/should be + V3; should have been + V3; have/get + object + V3

### Ejercicios 1–5 — Completa

1. Gear ___ before the climb. (obligation) → *must be tested / must test / must testing*
2. The report ___ earlier. (past criticism) → *should have been published / should publish / should be publish*
