Este cuaderno reúne los **ejercicios de la Unidad 47 del curso B2** (*Cleft Sentences + Academic Writing & Reports*). Practicarás reconocimiento, transformación y producción con tareas cerradas o criterios verificables. Cada actividad indica qué debes escribir, qué opciones puedes usar o qué información debes localizar.

> **Guía teórica:** [Cleft Sentences & Academic Writing B2](/blog/curso-b2/unidad-47-cleft-sentences-academic-writing)<br>
> **Practica en el curso:** [Unidad 47 — Cleft Sentences & Academic Writing](/curso-b2/unit-47)

Trabaja en este orden: responde sin mirar, abre la solución, identifica la causa de cada error y repite la frase correcta en voz alta. En writing y speaking, compara primero la estructura obligatoria y después el contenido.

**Recuerda antes de empezar:**
- Enfoca una persona con **It was X who...** y una cosa o circunstancia con **It was X that...**.
- Una what-cleft sigue **What + clause + is/was + focus**.
- La cleft conserva el hecho básico; cambia qué información recibe contraste.
- En U47, la what-clause toma **is/was** singular en el patrón evaluado.

## Lección 1 — Gramática y chunks

**Objetivo:** seleccionar y construir correctamente it-clefts y what-clefts para enfocar personas, cosas, circunstancias y hallazgos.

No respondas por parecido visual. Localiza la pista de significado o tiempo y comprueba la cadena completa: modal, auxiliar, infinitivo, participio, objeto y concordancia.

### Ejercicios 1–5 — It-clefts: persona y cosa

Transforma el mensaje neutro y enfoca exactamente el elemento indicado.

1. Maya revised the report. (enfoca Maya)
   Opciones: *It was Maya who revised the report. · It was Maya which revised the report.*
2. The introduction needed revision. (enfoca introduction)
   Opciones: *It was the introduction that needed revision. · It was the introduction who needed revision.*
3. The methodology matters now. (enfoca methodology)
   Opciones: *It is the methodology that matters now. · It was methodology who matters now.*
4. The students were concerned. (enfoca students)
   Opciones: *It was the students who were concerned. · It were the students who was concerned.*
5. Corrige: *It was Professor Khan which wrote the conclusion.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **It was Maya who revised the report.** — *Who* enfoca una persona.
2. **It was the introduction that needed revision.** — *That* introduce el resto tras una cosa.
3. **It is the methodology that matters now.** — *Now* favorece *is* y el foco es una cosa.
4. **It was the students who were concerned.** — El marco usa *it was* y la relativa concuerda con *students*.
5. **It was Professor Khan who wrote the conclusion.** — La persona enfocada toma *who*.

</details>

### Ejercicios 6–10 — Circunstancias y not until

Coloca tiempo, lugar o razón dentro del marco *it was...that*.

6. We found the source in the library. (enfoca lugar)
   Opciones: *It was in the library that we found the source. · It was the library who we found.*
7. We rushed because of the deadline. (enfoca razón)
   Opciones: *It was because of the deadline that we rushed. · What rushed was the deadline.*
8. Academic writing changed in the twentieth century. (enfoca tiempo)
   Opciones: *It was in the twentieth century that academic writing changed. · It was when the century which changed.*
9. They finished the draft only on Friday.
   Opciones: *It wasn't until Friday that they finished the draft. · It wasn't Friday who they finished.*
10. Corrige: *It was in 2021 when the study began.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

6. **It was in the library that we found the source.** — La preposición permanece con el foco de lugar.
7. **It was because of the deadline that we rushed.** — La razón completa ocupa el foco.
8. **It was in the twentieth century that academic writing changed.** — La transformación objetivo usa *that* después del foco temporal.
9. **It wasn't until Friday that they finished the draft.** — *It wasn't until...that* destaca el retraso.
10. **It was in 2021 that the study began.** — U47 selecciona *that* tras la circunstancia enfocada.

</details>

### Ejercicios 11–15 — What-clefts

Completa la cláusula nominal, el verbo *be* y el foco sin añadir un *it* redundante.

11. Clear evidence matters most.
   Opciones: *What matters most is clear evidence. · What it matters most is evidence.*
12. His reaction surprised me.
   Opciones: *What surprised me was his reaction. · What did surprise me it was his reaction.*
13. The report shows significant progress.
   Opciones: *What the report shows is significant progress. · What shows the report are progress.*
14. We need to revise the outline.
   Opciones: *What we need to do is revise the outline. · What we need do are revising.*
15. Corrige: *What the essay argues it is that access must improve.*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

11. **What matters most is clear evidence.** — La what-clause funciona como sujeto.
12. **What surprised me was his reaction.** — El hecho es pasado y no aparece *it*.
13. **What the report shows is significant progress.** — Orden normal dentro de la what-clause.
14. **What we need to do is revise the outline.** — La acción enfocada sigue al marco completo.
15. **What the essay argues is that access must improve.** — No se duplica el sujeto con *it*.

</details>

## Lección 2 — Vocabulario

**Objetivo:** comprender y usar vocabulario de *Academic Writing & Reports* en situaciones concretas.

Aprende cada palabra con su colocación. Las alternativas cercanas obligan a decidir por definición y contexto, no por parecido.

### Ejercicios 1–5 — Traducción precisa

Elige el único significado que corresponde a la palabra.

1. thesis statement
   Opciones: *idea central que defenderá el texto · lista completa de fuentes · versión preliminar*
2. outline
   Opciones: *plan previo de la estructura · cita literal · conclusión definitiva*
3. draft
   Opciones: *versión que todavía se revisará · fuente primaria · tabla final*
4. argumentation
   Opciones: *conexión de afirmaciones, razones y evidencia · formato de portada · resumen de una frase*
5. bibliography
   Opciones: *lista de fuentes · sección de resultados · nota informal*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

1. **idea central que defenderá el texto**
2. **plan previo de la estructura**
3. **versión que todavía se revisará**
4. **conexión de afirmaciones, razones y evidencia**
5. **lista de fuentes**

</details>

### Ejercicios 6–10 — Elige por definición

Completa cada definición con una de las tres opciones.

6. The opening section that states purpose and context is the ___.
   Opciones: *introduction · appendix · citation*
7. The final section that synthesises the report is the ___.
   Opciones: *conclusion · methodology · abstract source*
8. Words copied exactly from a source form a ___.
   Opciones: *quotation · paraphrase · outline*
9. A reference identifying a source in the text is a ___.
   Opciones: *citation · thesis · register*
10. Language suited to scholarly communication uses a ___.
   Opciones: *formal register · casual draft · personal bibliography*

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

6. **introduction**
7. **conclusion**
8. **quotation**
9. **citation**
10. **formal register**

</details>

### Ejercicios 11–15 — Completa el chunk

Escribe la expresión exacta que completa la situación; todas pertenecen al inventario de la unidad.

11. Use evidence to ___.
12. Express the source's idea in new wording and attribution: ___.
13. Before drafting, students should ___.
14. After feedback, the writer must ___.
15. Every borrowed idea requires the writer to ___.

<details>
<summary>Ver solución</summary>

11. **support a claim**
12. **paraphrase the source**
13. **formulate a thesis statement**
14. **revise the draft**
15. **cite the source**

</details>

## Lección 3 — Reading: A report that needed a clearer focus

**Objetivo:** comprender la idea global, localizar datos y reconocer los objetivos en contexto.

> Professor Khan reviewed a student report on access to university education. It was the introduction that needed the most revision because it contained no precise thesis statement. What the evidence showed was a clear difference between rural and urban applicants, but the first draft hid that finding beneath several general claims. It was Maya, the student's supervisor, who suggested a new outline. What she recommended was moving the central finding to the opening paragraph. It was after the methodology section that the student added a short discussion of limitations. The student paraphrased two sources accurately, quoted one definition and included every citation in the bibliography. What finally impressed Professor Khan was the report's logical argumentation, not its length. It wasn't until Friday that the revised draft was submitted.

What I want you to do is revise the outline before rewriting the introduction. It wasn't until the final page that I found your main argument. What impressed me was your careful paraphrasing. ## Lección 5 — Writing y Speaking

**Objetivo:** producir mensajes controlados con it-clefts y what-clefts para enfocar personas, cosas, circunstancias y hallazgos.

### Ejercicios 1–10 — Writing con modelo

Respeta la extensión y usa todas las expresiones indicadas. Cada tarea incluye una respuesta o un modelo completo: puedes cambiar los detalles, pero conserva las estructuras evaluadas.

1. Enfatiza a Maya: *Maya revised the report.*
2. Enfatiza la sección: *The methodology caused confusion.*
3. Transforma: *Clear evidence matters most.*
4. Corrige: *What surprised me it was the conclusion.*
5. Enfatiza el retraso: *They submitted only on Friday.*
6. Escribe 35–45 palabras con dos it-clefts y *thesis statement*.
7. Redacta 35–45 palabras con dos what-clefts y *evidence*.
8. Escribe 45–55 palabras enfocando persona, lugar, tiempo y razón.
9. Escribe 50–60 palabras con *outline, draft, paraphrase, cite, bibliography*.
10. Escribe 100–120 palabras como comentario de tutor. Incluye cuatro it-clefts, cuatro what-clefts y diez términos U47.

<details>
<summary>Ver modelos de writing</summary>

1. **It was Maya who revised the report.**
2. **It was the methodology that caused confusion.**
3. **What matters most is clear evidence.**
4. **What surprised me was the conclusion.**
5. **It wasn't until Friday that they submitted.**
6. Modelo: **It was the introduction that** lacked focus, and **it was Maya who** proposed a solution. She placed a precise **thesis statement** in the opening paragraph so readers could identify the report's central claim before examining its evidence.
7. Modelo: **What the table shows is** a gradual decline. **What the writer must explain is** why that pattern matters. Reliable **evidence** supports the interpretation, while careful wording prevents the report from claiming more than the available data can demonstrate.
8. Modelo: **It was Dr Lewis who** collected the data. **It was in the library that** Maya found the missing source. **It was on Friday that** she revised the bibliography, and **it was because of an incorrect citation that** the supervisor requested one final change before accepting the report.
9. Modelo: Begin with an **outline** that orders each claim and its evidence. In the first **draft**, mark every borrowed idea. **Paraphrase** accurately instead of replacing only a few words, and **cite** the original author. Before submission, compare all in-text references with the **bibliography** and correct any missing publication details before sending it.
10. Modelo: **What your report needs is** a clearer **thesis statement**. **It is the introduction that** should define the question, and **it was the second paragraph that** first presented useful **evidence**. **What the table shows is** a gradual trend, not a sudden change. **It was Dr Lewis who** collected those data, so **cite** her study and add it to the **bibliography**. **What strengthens your argumentation is** the careful way you **paraphrase** each source. **It is the conclusion that** currently introduces a new claim; move that point into the main discussion. **What I recommend is** revising the **outline** before editing the **draft**. Preserve the **formal register**, verify each **quotation** and submit the revised **report** on Monday.

</details>

### Ejercicios 11–15 — Speaking con guion y pistas

Prepara durante 30 segundos y habla sin leer. Después compara tus estructuras con el guion o las pistas concretas.

11. Pronuncia cuatro focos con it-clefts.
12. Explica it-cleft / what-cleft en 30 segundos.
13. Role-play de feedback académico.
14. Contrasta oración neutra y foco.
15. Presenta un informe durante 60 segundos con ocho clefts y ocho términos U47.

<details>
<summary>Ver guiones y pistas de speaking</summary>

11. Guion modelo: **It was Maya who wrote it. It was the introduction that changed. It was in May that we began. It was because of the deadline that we rushed.**
12. Guion modelo: An **it-cleft** selects a specific element. A **what-cleft** opens a category and identifies it after *be*: **What matters is evidence**.
13. A: Which section needs work? B: **It is the methodology that** needs revision. A: What is the priority? B: **What matters most is** clearer evidence.
14. Guion modelo: **Dr Lewis collected the data** is neutral. **It was Dr Lewis who collected the data** contrasts Dr Lewis with another possible researcher.
15. Pistas: cuatro it-clefts · cuatro what-clefts · introduction · thesis statement · outline · draft · evidence · citation · bibliography · conclusion.

</details>

## Cómo revisar tus resultados

Clasifica cada fallo como **significado**, **forma**, **tiempo**, **concordancia**, **orden** o **vocabulario**. Copia la corrección mínima y crea una frase nueva con el mismo patrón. Después vuelve a intentarlo sin opciones.

Para consolidar, repite las expresiones mañana, dentro de tres días y una semana después. Reduce el apoyo en cada revisión: primero opciones, después una pista en español y finalmente una situación de 45 segundos.
